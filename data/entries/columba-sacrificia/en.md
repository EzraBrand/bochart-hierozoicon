# Part 2, Book 1, chapter 5: The sacrifices of doves and turtledoves (vol. 2, pp. 557–559)

### ‎[II, 557] Chapter V.

### ‎<i>On the sacrifices of doves and turtledoves.</i>

‎The <i>Jews</i> sought doves not only for eating but also for sacrifices, which were of many kinds. Thus <i>Lev 1:14</i> deals with a voluntary burnt offering of turtledoves or young doves. In <i>Levit. V</i>, 7. one who had not rebuked another who blasphemed, or had himself sworn rashly, or had defiled himself by touching an unclean thing, is commanded to offer a pair of turtledoves or doves, one for a sin offering, the other for a burnt offering. In <i>Lev 12:6.</i> God requires of a woman after childbirth, once the days of her purification are completed, a yearling lamb for a burnt offering and a young dove or a turtledove for a sin offering; or, if she cannot offer a lamb, two turtledoves or two young doves. You may see the same offerings prescribed also for one who has been healed of leprosy, <i>Levit. XIV</i>, 22; likewise for one who has suffered from a discharge, when he has been cleansed of his flux, <i>Levit. XV</i>, 13. 14; and ἐν τῷ τῆς αἱμοῤῥοούσης καθαρισμῷ [en tō tēs haimorrhoousēs katharismō], <i>Levit. XV</i>, 28. 29; and in the purification of the <i>Nazirite</i> who has defiled himself by contact with a dead body, <i>Num. VI</i>, 10.

‎In these sacrifices note, first, that God preferred doves and turtledoves to other birds because of the purity, chastity, simplicity, gentleness and the other shadows of virtues that have been observed in them. 2. That adult turtledoves, but only young doves, were offered on the altar, because turtledoves are best at a mature age, doves at a tender age. 3. That most of these sacrifices were only those of the poorer people, as is expressly noted in <i>Levit. V</i>, 7. and <i>XII, 6.</i> So gather even from this how poor the holy Virgin, the mother of <i>Christ</i>, was, who after her purification offered not a lamb but turtledoves or young doves, <i>Luc. II</i>, 24. 4. That sacrifices of this kind were already in use before the Law is clear from the example of <i>Abraham</i>, who was commanded to offer to God, together with a three-year-old heifer, she-goat and ram, a turtledove and a גוזל <i>gozal</i>, that is, a young bird of the dove kind: namely, so that this sacrifice might embrace all the kinds of victims that were afterwards established in the Law. This I think the simplest explanation, although many seek various mysteries here. Some would have these five animals signify the gifts of <i>Christ</i>; others, those for whom God had destined the land of <i>Canaan</i>, and the virtues required in them; others, finally, what the condition of <i>Abraham</i>'s descendants would be. For by the ox, the goat and the lamb, which are land animals and accustomed to live under the yoke of men, [col. 26] they say is signified the time when they would live abased and humble and in servitude to the <i>Egyptians</i>; by the turtledove, which is solitary, the sojourn of the <i>Jews</i> in the desert; and by the dove, which is gregarious and lives in cultivated places, their dwelling in <i>Canaan.</i> 5. That the gentiles too, through a certain perverse κακοζηλίᾳ [kakozēlia], adopted sacrifices of doves. <i>Propertius</i>, book 4, in the <i>Elegy</i> to the <i>Procuress</i>: [II, 558] <i>“But take, o queen</i> Venus, <i>the throat of a ringed dove,</i> <i>cut before your hearths for its desert.”</i>

‎<i>Ovid</i>, book 1 of the <i>Fasti</i>:

‎<i>“Therefore often the white dove, a wife taken from her husband,</i> <i>is burned on the hot hearths.”</i>

‎Yet the eating of doves seems to have been forbidden to the initiates of <i>Venus</i>, as may be gathered from these lines of <i>Martial</i>, book 13, <i>Epigr.</i> 66:

‎<i>“Do not violate tender doves with too hard a tooth,</i> <i>if the rites of the</i> Cnidian <i>Goddess have been handed down to you.”</i>

‎The rite by which the <i>Jews</i> slaughtered turtledoves and doves is taught briefly by <i>Moses</i> in <i>Levit. I,</i> 15. 16. 17. and <i>V,</i> 8., and at greater length by the <i>Talmudists</i> in <i>Zebachim</i> ch. 6, fol. 64, and by <i>Maimonides</i> [=Moses Maimonides (Rambam), Mishneh Torah] in the book <i>On the offering of sacrifices</i> ch. 6, §§ 20, 21, 22, 23, and ch. 7, §§ 6, 7, 8. Much could be drawn from these that is not altogether commonplace, if that were our present business. But here it will suffice to have noted that the <i>Hebrews</i> slaughtered other victims with a blade, but birds with the fingernail alone, by which, with the head bent back, their throat was torn open. In <i>Hebrew</i> this is מלק, which the <i>Greeks</i> render ἀποκνίζειν [apoknizein], that is, <i>to tear with the nail</i>, or <i>to cut off</i>, Lev 1:15 and V, 8., the only places where it occurs. The <i>Jerusalem</i> Targum has עום, and <i>Jonathan</i> [=Targum Jonathan] חום, which is the same, ח and ע being interchanged. In <i>Arabic</i> the same is حذم [ḥadhama] or خذم [khadhama], which they render <i>to cut</i> and <i>to tear apart</i>. Thus خذمت الصقر [khadhamat al-ṣaqr], <i>the hawk tore with its talons</i>, in <i>Alcamus</i> [=al-Qāmūs al-muḥīṭ, the Arabic dictionary of al-Fīrūzābādī]. <i>Kimchi</i> [=David Kimḥi (Radak), Sefer ha-Shorashim], in his <i>Lexicon</i> under the word מלק, describes the rite of tearing the bird thus: ואר״זל כיצד מולקין חטאת העוף נותן שתי גפיה בין שתי אצבעותיו ושתי רגליה בין שתי אצבעותיו והיה מותח צוארה על שתי אצבעותיו והיה מולק בצפורן ממול עורף עד שמגיע לסימנים וקוצץ אותן או רובן ואינו מבדיל שנאמר ולא יבדיל אבל בעולה העוף שאמר ומלק את ראשו ולא אמר ולא יבדיל הוא מולק ומבדיל הראש בעצמו הגוף בעצמו <i>Our masters of blessed memory said: How was the sin offering of a bird torn? He (the priest) placed its wings between two fingers and its feet between two fingers, and stretched its neck over his two fingers, and cut with his nail opposite the nape until he reached the signs</i> (in the throat), <i>which he severed, or the greater part of them, yet so that he did not divide it, because it is said</i> Lev 5:8 „<i>And he shall not divide it</i>“. <i>But in the burnt offering of a bird, because it is said,</i> „<i>He shall wring off its head</i>“, <i>and it is not added,</i> „<i>And he shall not divide it</i>“, Lev 1:15, <i>he cut so that the head was separated from the trunk of the body.</i> So he, from the passage of the <i>Talmud</i> we have cited. See also <i>Maimonides</i>, who asserts that this was מעבודות קשות שבמקדש, <i>one of the most laborious services of the Sanctuary</i>, in the book <i>On the offering of sacrifices</i> ch. 7, § 8 <sup>R1</sup>.

‎[II, 559] So that victims should not be lacking, in the first part of the temple, which was called פרבר <i>parbar</i>, <i>1 Chr 26:18</i>, oxen, sheep and doves were sold, <i>John 2:14.</i> Accordingly in <i>Maimonides</i>' treatise <i>On the vessels of the Sanctuary</i> ch. 7, § 9 (among the officers of the temple, whom the <i>Hebrews</i> called ממוני המקדש and the <i>Greeks</i> στρατηγοὺς τοῦ ἱεροῦ [stratēgous tou hierou], <i>Luke 22:52</i>) there is listed הממונה שעל הקינים, <i>the officer over the nests</i>, that is, <i>over the birds</i>. And he is described thus: הדא שפוסקין עמו שימכור הקנים לקורבנות כך וכך בסלע וכל מי שהוא חייב תורים או שני בני יונה יביא דמיהם למקדשי [col. 27] וזה הממונה נותן הקנים לבעלי הקורבנות ועושה חשבון עם הגוברים ונותנין לו משלושים יום לשלושים יום, <i>He is the one with whom they contract that he provide birds for the offerings, so many or so many for a shekel. And whoever was obliged to offer either turtledoves or young doves brought the price to the sanctuary. And this officer gave the birds to those whose duty it was to offer, and valued them with the treasurers; and, once he had agreed on the price, they paid him within three days.</i> And a little later: וכן קן שנמצא פסול או שנפסל קודם שיקרוב נותן אחר תחתיו, <i>And if any bird was found unfit, or became unfit</i> (through ceremonial defilement) <i>before it was offered, he gave another in its place.</i> That these arrangements were devised for gain by greedy and avaricious priests, <i>Jerome</i> teaches in these words on <i>Matthew</i> ch. 21: <i>According to the commandments of the Law, in the temple of the Lord, the most confined in the whole world, with the people flocking there from almost all the regions of</i> the Jews, <i>innumerable victims were sacrificed, especially on feast days: bulls, rams and he-goats, while the poor, so as not to be without a sacrifice, offered young doves and turtledoves. It often happened that those who had come from afar had no victims. The priests therefore devised a way to make a prey of the people: they sold all the animals needed for the sacrifices, so that they might both sell to those who had none and themselves receive back again what had been bought.</i> Hence <i>Christ</i>, rightly and justly angered against these profiteers, <i>overturned the seats of those who sold doves</i>, <i>Matth. XXI,</i> 12. and <i>Marc. XI,</i> 15. <i>And to those who sold doves he said, Take these things hence. Do not make my father's house a house of trade</i>, John 2:16.
