# Book 4, chapter 4: Leta is a venomous lizard like the stellio (vol. 2, pp. 497–500)

### ‎[II, 497] Chapter IV. [col. 1073]

### ‎<i>It is proved that the word</i> לטאה letaa <i>signifies a lizard, similar to the stellio, extremely venomous and clinging to the ground, which in Arabic is called</i> وحرة [waḥra] vvachara.

‎Some of the more recent writers try to interpret the <i>Hebrew</i> לטאה <i>letaa</i> from the <i>Greek</i> language, in which λὼτ [lōt] is the <i>mole</i>. <i>Hesychius</i>: Λώτ· ἀσπάλαξ, μῦς τυφλὸς ὁ τὴν γῆν τρυπῶν [Lōt· aspalax, mys typhlos ho tēn gēn trypōn]. And λάταξ [latax] is an amphibious animal, akin to the beaver and the otter, of which <i>the Philosopher</i> speaks in <i>History</i>, book 8, chapter 5. Thus in their view לטאה <i>letaa</i> would be either the <i>mole</i> or the <i>latax</i>. Others prefer the <i>shrew-mouse</i>, into which Λητὼ [Lētō], that is, <i>Latona</i>, is fabled to have been changed, on account of the allusion between the words Λητὼ [Lētō] and <i>letaa</i>, which would be the μυγάλη [mygalē]. And it is indeed true that <i>Latona</i> is fabled to have been changed into a shrew-mouse for fear of <i>Typhon</i>. <i>Antoninus Liberalis</i>, <i>Metam.</i> 28: Ἀπόλλων μὲν ἐγένετο ἱέραξ, Ἑρμῆς δὲ ἴβις· Ἄρης δὲ λεπιδωτὸς ἰχθύς· Ἄρτεμις δὲ αἴλουρος [Apollōn men egeneto hierax, Hermēs de ibis; Arēs de lepidōtos ichthys; Artemis de ailouros] (not σίλουρος [silouros], as our editions wrongly have it). Τράγῳ δὲ εἰκάζεται Διόνυσος· ἔλλῳ δ᾽ Ἡρακλῆς· βοῒ δ᾽ Ἥφαιστος· μυγάλῃ δὲ Λητώ [Tragō de eikazetai Dionysos; ellō d᾽ Hēraklēs; boi d᾽ Hēphaistos; mygalē de Lētō]. But it does not follow from this that the word <i>letaa</i> signifies the <i>shrew-mouse</i>, any more than the <i>mole</i> or the <i>latax</i> because λὼτ [lōt] and λάταξ [latax] sound alike. For the mole has already been dealt with in the preceding verse. And the <i>latax</i> cannot be counted among the reptiles of the earth (any more than the beaver, discussed above) <sup>a)</sup>, since it lives rather in the water and has hair partly like that of the seal. It is better, therefore, to follow the ancients, who also number this animal among the species of lizards. Thus for the <i>Greeks</i> it is καλαβώτης [kalabōtēs] or ἀσκαλαβώτης [askalabōtēs], and for Jerome the <i>stellio</i>; for <i>Jonathan</i> [=Targum Jonathan] שממיתא <i>semamitha</i>, which is the [II, 498] very same thing; for the <i>Syrian</i> ܨܪܘܪܝܬܐ [צרוריתא] <i>tseruritha</i>, that is, the <i>salamander</i>, as they claim; and for the <i>Arabs</i> العظاة [al-ʿaẓāh] <i>aladha</i> or العظاية [al-ʿaẓāya] <i>aladhaja</i>, the <i>lizard</i>. So also <i>Solomon</i> [Rashi], <i>Kimchi</i> [=David Kimḥi (Radak), Sefer ha-Shorashim] and the <i>Pomarium</i>. That the <i>Talmudists</i> also took it so is clear from the tractate <i>Oholoth</i> (ch. 1, fol. 45b), where certain beasts and birds are said to quiver after the head has been cut off, כגון הזנב של הלטאה שהיא מפרכסת, <i>like the tail</i> of the letaa, <i>which</i> (when cut off) <i>still keeps moving</i>. To this the words in the <i>Porta coeli</i> allude: ויש מב״ח אם יכרתו מזנבותיהם יתנועעו זמן רב אחר כן כזנב הלטאה וזנבות השרצים, <i>There are certain animals whose tails, if cut off, move for some time, like the tail</i> of the letaa <i>and of other reptiles</i>. Of the same kind is what <i>Aelian</i>, <i>History</i>, book 2, chapter 23, and <i>Phile</i>, chapter 53, assert: that when a lizard is cut in two by the blow of a rod, each part lives and crawls on two feet, and sometimes they grow together again. I would not, however, lightly believe what is said in the <i>Porta coeli</i> about tails so cut off, כי הן יגדלו אחרי כריתותם, <i>that they grow after being cut off</i>, since it is held as established that once divided they live only a very short time.

‎So far, then, it is established that the <i>letaa</i> belongs to the lizard kind, since the ancients so determine with great agreement. But to define the species is not for just anyone. The Syrian explains it as the <i>salamander</i>. Of the more recent writers some prefer the <i>stellio</i>, others the <i>lizard</i>. The [col. 1074] former is followed by <i>Pagninus</i> [=Santes Pagnino, Latin Bible (1528) and Thesaurus linguae sanctae], <i>Arias</i> [=Benito Arias Montano, the interlinear Latin of the Antwerp Polyglot], <i>Junius</i> [=Franciscus Junius, of the Tremellius-Junius Latin Bible], <i>Castalio</i> [=Sebastian Castellio, Latin Bible (1551)]; the latter by <i>Munster</i>, <i>Schindler</i>, the <i>Genevans</i>, the <i>English</i>, the <i>Italians</i>. I understand a species of lizard which in <i>Arabic</i> is called وحرة [waḥra] <i>wachra</i> or <i>wachara</i>. Concerning it the <i>Arabs</i> note chiefly three things: 1. That it is similar to the stellio. 2. That it clings very closely to the ground. 3. That whatever food it treads on is infected with its venom. These three settle the matter, as we shall show later. But first the <i>Arabs</i> must be heard. <i>Giauhari</i> [=al-Jawharī, al-Ṣiḥāḥ]: الوحرة دويبة حمرا تلتصق بالارض كالعظا [al-waḥra duwaybba ḥamrā taltaṣiqu bi-l-arḍ ka-l-ʿaẓā] Alvvachra, <i>a small red animal, clinging to the ground, like a lizard</i>. Damir [=al-Damīrī, Ḥayāt al-ḥayawān]: هي دويبة شبيهة بسام ابرص تلتصق بالارض او ضرب من العظا لا تطا طعاما ولا سرابا الا سميته [hiya duwaybba shabīha bi-sām abraṣ taltaṣiqu bi-l-arḍ aw ḍarb min al-ʿaẓā lā taṭaʾ ṭaʿāman wa-lā sarāban illā sammathu] <i>It is a small animal, similar</i> to the <i>sammabras</i> (or <i>greater stellio</i>), <i>clinging to the ground, or a species of lizard, infecting with its venom whatever food or drink it treads on</i>. In the same place others say: وقيل هي علي شكل سام ابرص [wa-qīla hiya ʿalī shakl sām abraṣ] <i>It is said to be of the form of the</i> sammabras. And <i>Alcamus</i> [=al-Qāmūs al-muḥīṭ, the Arabic dictionary of al-Fīrūzābādī]: الوحرة وزغة كسام ابرص او ضرب من العظا لا تطا شي لا سميته [al-waḥra wazagha ka-sām abraṣ aw ḍarb min al-ʿaẓā lā taṭaʾ shay lā sammathu] Alvvachara <i>is a</i> guezga, <i>like the</i> sammabras, <i>or a species of lizard, which treads on nothing that it does not infect</i>. Hence وحر [waḥira] <i>vvachara</i> is a verb meaning <i>to infect with venom</i>. Accordingly they say: وحر الصدر عشه ووسواسه [waḥira al-ṣadr ʿishshuhu wa-waswāsuhu] [II, 499] <i>His deceit and his insinuation infected the breast.</i> Likewise: الغل اللازق به كما تلزق الوحرة بالارض [al-ghill al-lāziq bihi kamā talzaqu al-waḥra bi-l-arḍ] <i>Deceit clings to him, as the</i> alvvachara <i>clings to the ground.</i>

‎Now I shall prove without any difficulty that this, whatever it is, fits the <i>letaa</i>. 1. That this kind of lizard is similar to the stellio, or even a species of it, is suggested by the fact that the most ancient of the interpreters, the <i>Greek</i>, the <i>Chaldee</i> and the <i>Roman</i>, render it <i>stellio</i>. 2. That it clings to the ground is gathered from the name itself. For in <i>Arabic</i> the verb لطا [laṭā] <i>lataa</i>, or لطئ [laṭiʾa] <i>latia</i>, properly means <i>to cling</i>, not to anything at all, but <i>to the ground.</i> It is never taken otherwise. Hence <i>Damir</i>, in the chapter قبرة [qubbara] <i>Kobbara</i>, or <i>On the Lark: It is its nature that noise does not frighten it; and sometimes it is crushed by a millstone</i>, ولطي بالارض حتي يتجاوزها الحجر [wa-laṭiya bi-l-arḍ ḥattā yatajāwazahā al-ḥajar] <i>and it clings to the ground until the stone has passed over it.</i> And in the chapter عنكبوت [ʿankabūt], or <i>On the Spider</i>, فانا اراد صيد الذباب لطي بالارض [fa-nā arāda ṣayd al-dhubāb laṭiya bi-l-arḍ] <i>And when it wants to hunt flies, it clings to the ground.</i> Thus לטאה <i>letaa</i>, derived from it, properly signifies the animal that clings to the ground. This is what <i>Giauhari</i> and <i>Damir</i> note in the definition of the <i>vvachara</i>. 3. Finally, that the <i>letaa</i> is a venomous animal, and one to be guarded against above all, is taught by the enlarged <i>Lamed</i> at the beginning of the name. For it is not written לטאה, but לטאה. Hence <i>Buxtorf</i> [=Johannes Buxtorf the Elder, Synagoga Judaica and Lexicon Chaldaicum Talmudicum et Rabbinicum] in the <i>Commentarius Masorethicus</i>, ch. 14: <i>The small Masora notes an enlarged</i> La- [col. 1075] med <i>in the word</i> והלטאה, Lev 11:30, <i>which some render lizard, others</i> stellio. <i>The enlarged letter indicates that among the other reptiles this reptile is to be guarded against most of all.</i>

‎As for the rest, what this animal is called in <i>Greek</i> no one could easily say, unless one thinks it to be a species of <i>salamander</i>. For some of the ancients compare the salamander with the stellio, as similar to it, or even a species of it. <i>Aëtius, Tetrab.</i> 4, Serm. 1, ch. 52: <i>The salamander is an animal similar to the stellio.</i> The <i>Scholiast</i> of Nicander on the <i>Alexipharmaca</i>: Ἡ σαλαμάνδρα, εἶδος ἀσκαλαβώτου [Hē salamandra, eidos askalabōtou]. 2. The same animal is said to infect whatever it treads on. <i>Pliny</i>, in the place cited above: <i>If it has crept onto a tree, it infects all the fruit with poison.</i> Likewise: <i>If a crust of bread is baked over wood or a foot touched by it, the poisoning is the same.</i> To this belongs what the people of <i>Narbonne</i> keep saying, according to <i>Gesner</i> [=Conrad Gesner, Historia animalium]: <i>If a Blanda</i> (that is, a <i>salamander</i>) <i>is found in a heap of wheat, the whole is infected, so much that even hens feeding on it die.</i> Accordingly الوحرة [al-waḥara] <i>alvvachara</i>, to which the <i>Arabs</i> attribute these same things, might seem to be the same as the salamander, except that the colour does not agree. For in the <i>wachara</i> it is red, in the <i>salamander</i> variegated. Therefore the <i>wachara</i> is another kind of <i>Arabian</i> lizard, unknown to us, yet similar in [II, 500] many respects to the stellio and the salamander. Hence it is that לטאה <i>letaa</i>, which we take to be the <i>vvachara</i> of the <i>Arabs</i>, is rendered ܣܡܢܕܪܐ [סמנדרא] <i>salamandra</i> by the <i>Syriac</i>, and <i>stellio</i> by most of the other ancients <sup>R1</sup>.
