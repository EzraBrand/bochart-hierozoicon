II, 497 Chapter IV. col. 1073 ¶

Probatur, voce לטאה letaa Lacertam significari, stellioni similem, maxime venenatam et terrae adhaerentem, quae Arabice وحرة vvachara dicitur.

It is proved that the word לטאה letaa signifies a lizard, similar to the stellio, extremely venomous and clinging to the ground, which in Arabic is called وحرة [waḥra] vvachara. ¶

Hebraeum לטאה letaa conantur quidam recentiorum ex Graeca lingua interpretari, qua λὼτ est talpa. Hesychius Λώτ· ἀσπάλαξ, μῦς τυφλὸς ὁ τὴν γῆν τρυπῶν. Et λάταξ animal amphibium, castori et lutrae congener, de quo Philosophus Historiae libri octavi capite quinto. Itaque ex illorum mente לטאה letaa, aut talpa esset, aut latax. Alii murem araneum malunt, in quem Λητὼ, id est, Latona, mutata fuisse fingatur, propter allusionem vocum Λητὼ et letaa, quae sit μυγάλη. Atque illud quidem verum est, fingi Latonam, Typhonis metu, in murem araneum fuisse mutatam. Antonius Liberalis, Metam. 28. Ἀπόλλων μὲν ἐγένετο ἱέραξ, Ἑρμῆς δὲ ἴβις· Ἄρης δὲ λεπιδωτὸς ἰχθύς· Ἄρ-τεμις δὲ αἴλουρος (non σίλουρος, ut perperam habent nostrae editiones). Τρά-γῳ δὲ εἰκάζεται Διόνυσος· ἔλλῳ δ᾽ Ἡρακλῆς· βοῒ δ᾽ Ἥφαιστος· μυγάλῃ δὲ Λητώ. Sed non inde sequitur, voce letaa murem araneum significari: ut neque talpam, aut latacem; quia alludunt λὼτ et λάταξ. De talpa enim versu praecedente jam actum. Et latax in terrae reptilibus computari non potest (ut neque castor, de quo supra) a), quia in aquis potius vivit, et pilos habet, ex parte similes vituli marini pilis. Praestat igitur sequi veteres, qui hoc quoque animal numerant in lacertorum speciebus. Ita Graecis est καλαβώτης, vel ἀσκα-λαβώτης, et Hieronymo stellio: et Ionathani שממיתא semamitha; quod id II, 498 ipsum est: et Syro ܨܪܘܪܝܬܐ tseruritha, id est, salamandra, ut volunt: et Arabibus العظاة aladha, vel العظاية aladhaja, lacertus. Ita etiam Selomoni, et Kimchio, et Pomario. Quomodo etiam sumsisse Talmudicos constat ex tractatu Oholoth (cap. 1. fol. 45. b.), ubi pecudes quaedam et aves, post caput amputatum palpitare leguntur, כגון הזנב של הלטאה שהיא מפרכסת, quomodo cauda τῆς letaae, quae (abscissa) adhuc se motitat. Quo haec alludunt in Porta coeli, ויש מב״ח אם יכרתו מזנבותיהם יתנועעו זמן רב אחר כן כזנב הלטאה וזנבות השרצים, Sunt quaedam animalia, quorum caudae, si abscindantur, moventur aliquamdiu, ut cauda letaae et aliorum reptilium. Quale est, quod lacertae, virgae ictu bifariam divisi, singulas partes vivere, et duobus pedibus repere, et nonnunquam iterum coalescere asserunt Aelianus Historiae libri secundi capite vigesimo tertio, et Phile capite quinquagesimo tertio. Non tamen temere crediderim, quod habetur in Porta coeli de caudis, ita abscissis, כי הן יגדלו אחרי כריתותם, eas post abscissionem crescere, cum non nisi tantillum temporis divisas vivere pro comperto habeatur.

Some of the more recent writers try to interpret the Hebrew לטאה letaa from the Greek language, in which λὼτ [lōt] is the mole. Hesychius: Λώτ· ἀσπάλαξ, μῦς τυφλὸς ὁ τὴν γῆν τρυπῶν [Lōt· aspalax, mys typhlos ho tēn gēn trypōn]. And λάταξ [latax] is an amphibious animal, akin to the beaver and the otter, of which the Philosopher speaks in History, book 8, chapter 5. Thus in their view לטאה letaa would be either the mole or the latax. Others prefer the shrew-mouse, into which Λητὼ [Lētō], that is, Latona, is fabled to have been changed, on account of the allusion between the words Λητὼ [Lētō] and letaa, which would be the μυγάλη [mygalē]. And it is indeed true that Latona is fabled to have been changed into a shrew-mouse for fear of Typhon. Antoninus Liberalis, Metam. 28: Ἀπόλλων μὲν ἐγένετο ἱέραξ, Ἑρμῆς δὲ ἴβις· Ἄρης δὲ λεπιδωτὸς ἰχθύς· Ἄρτεμις δὲ αἴλουρος [Apollōn men egeneto hierax, Hermēs de ibis; Arēs de lepidōtos ichthys; Artemis de ailouros] (not σίλουρος [silouros], as our editions wrongly have it). Τράγῳ δὲ εἰκάζεται Διόνυσος· ἔλλῳ δ᾽ Ἡρακλῆς· βοῒ δ᾽ Ἥφαιστος· μυγάλῃ δὲ Λητώ [Tragō de eikazetai Dionysos; ellō d᾽ Hēraklēs; boi d᾽ Hēphaistos; mygalē de Lētō]. But it does not follow from this that the word letaa signifies the shrew-mouse, any more than the mole or the latax because λὼτ [lōt] and λάταξ [latax] sound alike. For the mole has already been dealt with in the preceding verse. And the latax cannot be counted among the reptiles of the earth (any more than the beaver, discussed above) a), since it lives rather in the water and has hair partly like that of the seal. It is better, therefore, to follow the ancients, who also number this animal among the species of lizards. Thus for the Greeks it is καλαβώτης [kalabōtēs] or ἀσκαλαβώτης [askalabōtēs], and for Jerome the stellio; for Jonathan [=Targum Jonathan] שממיתא semamitha, which is the II, 498 very same thing; for the Syrian ܨܪܘܪܝܬܐ [צרוריתא] tseruritha, that is, the salamander, as they claim; and for the Arabs العظاة [al-ʿaẓāh] aladha or العظاية [al-ʿaẓāya] aladhaja, the lizard. So also Solomon [Rashi], Kimchi [=David Kimḥi (Radak), Sefer ha-Shorashim] and the Pomarium. That the Talmudists also took it so is clear from the tractate Oholoth (ch. 1, fol. 45b), where certain beasts and birds are said to quiver after the head has been cut off, כגון הזנב של הלטאה שהיא מפרכסת, like the tail of the letaa, which (when cut off) still keeps moving. To this the words in the Porta coeli allude: ויש מב״ח אם יכרתו מזנבותיהם יתנועעו זמן רב אחר כן כזנב הלטאה וזנבות השרצים, There are certain animals whose tails, if cut off, move for some time, like the tail of the letaa and of other reptiles. Of the same kind is what Aelian, History, book 2, chapter 23, and Phile, chapter 53, assert: that when a lizard is cut in two by the blow of a rod, each part lives and crawls on two feet, and sometimes they grow together again. I would not, however, lightly believe what is said in the Porta coeli about tails so cut off, כי הן יגדלו אחרי כריתותם, that they grow after being cut off, since it is held as established that once divided they live only a very short time. ¶

Constat igitur hactenus, letaam esse in lacertorum genere, cum magno consensu veteres ita statuant. Sed speciem definire non est cujusvis. Syrus salamandram exponit. Recentiorum alii stellionem, alii lacertam malunt. Quo- col. 1074 rum prius sequuntur Pagninus, Arias, Iunius, Castalio: posterius Munsterus, Schindlerus, Genevenses, Angli, Itali. Ego lacertae speciem intelligo, quae Arabice وحرة wachra, vel wachara dicitur. De ea scilicet Arabes tria potissimum observant: 1. Stellioni similem esse. 2. Terrae maxime adhaerere. 3. Veneno ejus cibos infici, quoscumque calcat. Quae tria rem conficiunt, ut postmodum docebimus. Sed Arabes prius audiendi. Giauhari, الوحرة دويبة حمرا تلتصق بالارض كالعظا Alvvachra, animalculum rubrum, terrae adhaerens, ut lacerta. Damir, هي دويبة شبيهة بسام ابرص تلتصق بالارض او ضرب من العظا لا تطا طعاما ولا سرابا الا سميته Est animalculum, simile τῷ sammabras (seu stellioni majori) terrae adhaerens, aut lacertae species, quemcunque cibum aut potum calcet, veneno suo inficiens. Ibidem alii, وقيل هي علي شكل سام ابرص Dicitur esse formae sammabras. Et Alcamus, الوحرة وزغة كسام ابرص او ضرب من العظا لا تطا شي لا سميته Alvvachara est guezga, sicut sammabras, aut lacertae species, quae nihil calcat, quod non inficiat. Inde وحر vvachara verbum est veneno inficere. Proinde dicunt, وحر الصدر عشه ووسواسه II, 499 Pectus infecit dolus ejus, et suggestio ejus. Item, الغل اللازق به كما تلزق الوحرة بالارض Dolus ipsi adhaeret, ut alvvachara adhaeret terrae.

So far, then, it is established that the letaa belongs to the lizard kind, since the ancients so determine with great agreement. But to define the species is not for just anyone. The Syrian explains it as the salamander. Of the more recent writers some prefer the stellio, others the lizard. The col. 1074 former is followed by Pagninus [=Santes Pagnino, Latin Bible (1528) and Thesaurus linguae sanctae], Arias [=Benito Arias Montano, the interlinear Latin of the Antwerp Polyglot], Junius [=Franciscus Junius, of the Tremellius-Junius Latin Bible], Castalio [=Sebastian Castellio, Latin Bible (1551)]; the latter by Munster, Schindler, the Genevans, the English, the Italians. I understand a species of lizard which in Arabic is called وحرة [waḥra] wachra or wachara. Concerning it the Arabs note chiefly three things: 1. That it is similar to the stellio. 2. That it clings very closely to the ground. 3. That whatever food it treads on is infected with its venom. These three settle the matter, as we shall show later. But first the Arabs must be heard. Giauhari [=al-Jawharī, al-Ṣiḥāḥ]: الوحرة دويبة حمرا تلتصق بالارض كالعظا [al-waḥra duwaybba ḥamrā taltaṣiqu bi-l-arḍ ka-l-ʿaẓā] Alvvachra, a small red animal, clinging to the ground, like a lizard. Damir [=al-Damīrī, Ḥayāt al-ḥayawān]: هي دويبة شبيهة بسام ابرص تلتصق بالارض او ضرب من العظا لا تطا طعاما ولا سرابا الا سميته [hiya duwaybba shabīha bi-sām abraṣ taltaṣiqu bi-l-arḍ aw ḍarb min al-ʿaẓā lā taṭaʾ ṭaʿāman wa-lā sarāban illā sammathu] It is a small animal, similar to the sammabras (or greater stellio), clinging to the ground, or a species of lizard, infecting with its venom whatever food or drink it treads on. In the same place others say: وقيل هي علي شكل سام ابرص [wa-qīla hiya ʿalī shakl sām abraṣ] It is said to be of the form of the sammabras. And Alcamus [=al-Qāmūs al-muḥīṭ, the Arabic dictionary of al-Fīrūzābādī]: الوحرة وزغة كسام ابرص او ضرب من العظا لا تطا شي لا سميته [al-waḥra wazagha ka-sām abraṣ aw ḍarb min al-ʿaẓā lā taṭaʾ shay lā sammathu] Alvvachara is a guezga, like the sammabras, or a species of lizard, which treads on nothing that it does not infect. Hence وحر [waḥira] vvachara is a verb meaning to infect with venom. Accordingly they say: وحر الصدر عشه ووسواسه [waḥira al-ṣadr ʿishshuhu wa-waswāsuhu] II, 499 His deceit and his insinuation infected the breast. Likewise: الغل اللازق به كما تلزق الوحرة بالارض [al-ghill al-lāziq bihi kamā talzaqu al-waḥra bi-l-arḍ] Deceit clings to him, as the alvvachara clings to the ground. ¶

Iam hoc, quidquid est, letaae convenire, nullo negotio probavero. 1. Id enim lacertae genus stellioni esse simile, aut etiam ejus speciem, suadet, quod interpretum antiquissimi, Graeci, Chaldaei et Romani stellionem explicant. 2. Terrae adhaerere, colligitur ex ipso nomine. Nam Arabice verbum لطا lataa, vel لطئ latia, proprie adhaerere sonat, non ubivis, sed terrae. Nusquam aliter sumitur. Hinc Damir in capite قبرة Kobbara, seu De Alauda, E natura ejus est, ut strepitus eam non terreat: et quandoque lapide molari conteratur, ولطي بالارض حتي يتجاوزها الحجر et terrae adhaereat, donec eam lapis pertransierit. Et in capite عنكبوت seu De Aranea, فانا اراد صيد الذباب لطي بالارض Et, cum vult venari muscas, terrae adhaeret. Itaque לטאה letaa, inde deducta, proprie id animal significat, quod terrae adhaeret. Quod in vvacharae definitione observant Giauhari et Damir. 3. Letaam denique venenatum esse animal, et a quo maxime cavendum, docet majusculum Lamed initio nominis. Neque enim scribitur לטאה, sed לטאה. Unde Buxtorfius in Commentario Masorethico cap. 14. La- col. 1075 med majusculum Masora parva notat in voce והלטאה, Levit. XI, 30. quam alii lacertam, alii stellionem reddunt. Litera autem majuscula denotat, inter reliqua reptilia vel maxime ab hoc reptili esse cavendum.

Now I shall prove without any difficulty that this, whatever it is, fits the letaa. 1. That this kind of lizard is similar to the stellio, or even a species of it, is suggested by the fact that the most ancient of the interpreters, the Greek, the Chaldee and the Roman, render it stellio. 2. That it clings to the ground is gathered from the name itself. For in Arabic the verb لطا [laṭā] lataa, or لطئ [laṭiʾa] latia, properly means to cling, not to anything at all, but to the ground. It is never taken otherwise. Hence Damir, in the chapter قبرة [qubbara] Kobbara, or On the Lark: It is its nature that noise does not frighten it; and sometimes it is crushed by a millstone, ولطي بالارض حتي يتجاوزها الحجر [wa-laṭiya bi-l-arḍ ḥattā yatajāwazahā al-ḥajar] and it clings to the ground until the stone has passed over it. And in the chapter عنكبوت [ʿankabūt], or On the Spider, فانا اراد صيد الذباب لطي بالارض [fa-nā arāda ṣayd al-dhubāb laṭiya bi-l-arḍ] And when it wants to hunt flies, it clings to the ground. Thus לטאה letaa, derived from it, properly signifies the animal that clings to the ground. This is what Giauhari and Damir note in the definition of the vvachara. 3. Finally, that the letaa is a venomous animal, and one to be guarded against above all, is taught by the enlarged Lamed at the beginning of the name. For it is not written לטאה, but לטאה. Hence Buxtorf [=Johannes Buxtorf the Elder, Synagoga Judaica and Lexicon Chaldaicum Talmudicum et Rabbinicum] in the Commentarius Masorethicus, ch. 14: The small Masora notes an enlarged La- col. 1075 med in the word והלטאה, Lev 11:30, which some render lizard, others stellio. The enlarged letter indicates that among the other reptiles this reptile is to be guarded against most of all. ¶

De caetero hoc animal quomodo Graece vocetur, nemo facile dixerit, nisi quis putet, esse salamandrae speciem. Nempe cum stellione salamandram veterum nonnulli conferunt, tanquam illi similem, aut etiam ejus speciem. Aëtius Tetrab. 4. Serm. 1. cap. 52. Salamandra animal est simile stellioni. Nicandri Scholiastes in Alexipharmaca, Ἡ σαλαμάνδρα, εἶδος ἀσκαλαβώτου. 2. Eadem inficere legitur, quidquid calcat. Plinius ubi supra: Si arbori irrepsit, omnia poma inficit veneno. Item, Si contacto ab ea ligno, vel pede, crusta panis incoquatur, idem veneficium est. Quo pertinet, quod Narbonenses apud Gesnerum dictitant: Si Blanda (id est, salamandra) in acervo tritici reperiatur, totum infici, adeo, ut vel gallinae vescentes inde pereant. Proinde الوحرة alvvachara, cui Arabes haec eadem tribuunt, videri queat, idem esse cum salamandra, nisi quod non convenit color. Is enim in wachara ruber est, in salamandra varius. Itaque wachara aliud est Arabicae lacertae genus, nobis ignotae, similis tamen, in II, 500 multis, stellioni et salamandrae. Unde est, quod לטאה letaa, quam putamus esse Arabum vvacharam, a Syro ܣܡܢܕܪܐ salamandra, et a veterum plerisque aliis stellio redditur (1).

As for the rest, what this animal is called in Greek no one could easily say, unless one thinks it to be a species of salamander. For some of the ancients compare the salamander with the stellio, as similar to it, or even a species of it. Aëtius, Tetrab. 4, Serm. 1, ch. 52: The salamander is an animal similar to the stellio. The Scholiast of Nicander on the Alexipharmaca: Ἡ σαλαμάνδρα, εἶδος ἀσκαλαβώτου [Hē salamandra, eidos askalabōtou]. 2. The same animal is said to infect whatever it treads on. Pliny, in the place cited above: If it has crept onto a tree, it infects all the fruit with poison. Likewise: If a crust of bread is baked over wood or a foot touched by it, the poisoning is the same. To this belongs what the people of Narbonne keep saying, according to Gesner [=Conrad Gesner, Historia animalium]: If a Blanda (that is, a salamander) is found in a heap of wheat, the whole is infected, so much that even hens feeding on it die. Accordingly الوحرة [al-waḥara] alvvachara, to which the Arabs attribute these same things, might seem to be the same as the salamander, except that the colour does not agree. For in the wachara it is red, in the salamander variegated. Therefore the wachara is another kind of Arabian lizard, unknown to us, yet similar in II, 500 many respects to the stellio and the salamander. Hence it is that לטאה letaa, which we take to be the vvachara of the Arabs, is rendered ܣܡܢܕܪܐ [סמנדרא] salamandra by the Syriac, and stellio by most of the other ancients R1. ¶
