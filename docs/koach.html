II, 493 Chapter III. col. 1069

Probatur, כח coach esse ورل guaril, lacertorum in Arabia maximum et validissimum.

It is proved that כח coach is the ورل [waral] guaril, the largest and strongest of the lizards in Arabia.

Sequitur כח coach, quem in lacertorum genere ponunt veteres fere quotquot sunt. Graeci et Hieronymus chamaeleontem reddunt. Uterque Arabs editus حردون chardaun, id est, crocodilum terrestrem, vel ejus speciem. Ita etiam R. Ionas et David Kimchi. Lacertam R. Selomo. Hebraeam vocem retinent Chaldaei et Samaritanus, sola terminatione mutata. Sed magno errore Syrus ܚܘܠܕܐ chulda: et Arabes manuscripti الخلد alchuld, id est, talpam, hic habent. E recentioribus Pagninus, Munsterus, Castalio, Angli, Itali reddunt chamaeleontem: Arias et Iunius lacertam: Petrus Robertus testudinem: Pomarius limacem: Iudaei quidam (apud Gesnerum) sciurum: Genevenses crocodilum. Nos iis accedimus, qui lacertae genus intelligunt; quia de lacertis hic agitur, et sic sumunt plerique veteres, iique antiquissimi. Quo semel concesso, facile erit statuere, Hebraeo nomine כח coach significari الورل id est, Arabum guaril, quomodo barbari scriptores vocant. Patet id ex ipso nomine, quod a robore sumtum est. Alibi enim Scripturae locis plus centum כח coach pro robore sumitur. Et Arabice كوح iisdem literis significat, in pugna superiorem esse. Itaque in lacertorum genere vero consentaneum est, כח coach illum appellari, qui robore maxime valet, et in pugna reliquis praecellit. Is autem haud dubie est الورل alvvarlo, seu guaril, cujus robur maxime eminet in pugna contra serpentes et crocodilum terrestrem. Quod ne quisquam in dubium vocet, en ea de re disertissima scriptorum Arabum testimonia! Alkazuinius, هو عدو للضب وللحية يدخل بيتها وياكلها وليس شي من الحيوان اقوي علي قتل الحيات منه ولا يحتفر لنفسه بل يغتصب كل شي II, 494 بيته لان اي بيت دخله هرب ساكنه وبغتصب بيت الحية كما يغتصب الحية بيت ساير الاجناس Infensus est crocodilo terrestri, et serpenti, cujus latibulum ingre- col. 1070 ditur, ut eum voret. Neque ullum est animal eo fortius ad serpentes occidendos. Neque latibulum sibi ipsi fodit: sed aliorum omnium latibula invadit. Nam quod- cunque latibula adeat, ejus incola fugam capessit. Quin serpentis lustrum invadit, quomodo aliorum generum (malim الاحناش ساير aliorum reptilium) lustra invadit serpens. Abdol- latif, ex quo Damir: وبينه وبين الضب عداوة فيغلب الورل الضب ويقتله لكنه لا ياكله كما يفعل بالحيه وهو لا يتخذ بيتا لنفسه ولا يحفر حجرا بل يخرج الضب من حجره صارعا ويستولي عليه وانه كان اكثر براثن منه واقوي لكن الظلم يمنعه ولهذا يضرب به المثل في الظلم ويكفي في ظلمه انه يغصب الحية حجرها ويبلعها وربما قتل فوجد في بطنه الحية العظيمة وهو لا يتبلعها حتي يشدخ راسها Inter eum et τὸν dhab (seu crocodilum terrestrem) gravis est inimicitia. Sed guaril est superior, et eum occidit. Nec tamen eum comedit, quo- modo serpentes solent. Neque domum sibi deligit, aut latibulum fodit. Sed croco- dilum terrestrem ex proprio domicilio colluctando extrudit, et ei praevalet. Quam- vis crocodilus unguium viribus praepolleat; tamen hic violentia potior est. Unde est, quod in proverbium cessit ejus violentia. Quae in illo tanta est, ut sufficiat ad serpentem e lustro suo extrudendum ac devorandum. Ac proinde in occisi ventre magnum serpentem nonnunquam reperire est, quem non vorat, nisi prius fracto capite. Proverbia de eo multa sunt apud Arabes, in quibus haec notanda sunt: اجبر من الورل Ferocior (aut superbior) τῷ guaril. Et اظلم من الورل Iniquior (aut violentior) τῷ guaril: ad quod supra allusit Abdollatif. Et اسرع من تلظم الورل Citius quam guaril cibum in ore versat. Quo pertinet, quod scribit Algiahid, eum serpentem avide comedere, vel II, 495 اكلا نريعا id est ad verbum, veloci et praecipiti comestione (1). Ex col. 1071 Ex hac descriptione apparet, nomen כח coach, a robore sumtum, huic lacertae apprime convenire (2).

Next comes כח coach, which almost all the ancients, as many as there are, place in the lizard family. The Greeks and Jerome render it chameleon. Both printed Arabic versions have حردون [ḥardūn] chardaun, that is, the land crocodile, or a species of it. So also R. Jonah and David Kimchi. R. Solomon has lizard. The Chaldee versions and the Samaritan keep the Hebrew word, with only the ending changed. But the Syriac, by a great error, has ܚܘܠܕܐ [חולדא] chulda here, and the manuscript Arabic versions الخلد [al-khuld] alchuld, that is, mole. Among the more recent translators, Pagninus, Münster, Castellio, the English and the Italians render it chameleon; Arias and Junius [=Franciscus Junius, of the Tremellius-Junius Latin Bible] lizard; Petrus Robertus tortoise; Pomarius slug; certain Jews (in Gesner [=Conrad Gesner, Historia animalium]) squirrel; the Genevans crocodile. We side with those who understand a kind of lizard, because lizards are the subject here, and this is how most of the ancients take it, and the most ancient of them. Once this is granted, it will be easy to establish that the Hebrew name כח coach signifies الورل [al-waral], that is, the guaril of the Arabs, as the barbarous writers call it. This is clear from the name itself, which is taken from strength. For in more than a hundred other passages of Scripture כח coach is used for strength. And in Arabic كوح [kawḥ], with the same letters, means to be superior in a fight. It is therefore entirely fitting that, within the lizard family, the one called כח coach should be the one that is most powerful in strength and surpasses the rest in fighting. And that is undoubtedly الورل [al-waral] alvvarlo, or guaril, whose strength stands out above all in fighting against serpents and the land crocodile. Lest anyone call this into doubt, here are the most explicit testimonies of the Arabic writers on the matter! Alkazuini: هو عدو للضب وللحية يدخل بيتها وياكلها وليس شي من الحيوان اقوي علي قتل الحيات منه ولا يحتفر لنفسه بل يغتصب كل شي II, 494 بيته لان اي بيت دخله هرب ساكنه وبغتصب بيت الحية كما يغتصب الحية بيت ساير الاجناس [huwa ʿaduww li-l-ḍabb wa-li-l-ḥayya yadkhulu baytahā wa-yaʾkuluhā wa-laysa shayʾ min al-ḥayawān aqwā ʿalā qatl al-ḥayyāt minhu wa-lā yaḥtafiru li-nafsihi bal yaghtaṣibu kull shayʾ baytahu li-anna ayy bayt dakhalahu haraba sākinuhu wa-byaghtaṣibu bayt al-ḥayya kamā yaghtaṣibu al-ḥayya bayt sāyir al-ajnās] It is hostile to the land crocodile and to the serpent, whose hiding place it enters in order to devour it. And no animal is stronger than it at killing serpents. col. 1070 Nor does it dig a hiding place for itself, but it invades the hiding places of all others. For whatever hiding place it approaches, its occupant takes flight. Indeed, it invades the serpent's den, just as the serpent invades the dens of other kinds (I would prefer الاحناش ساير [sāyir al-aḥnāsh], of other reptiles). Algiahid on the same animal: وهو يقوي علي الحيات وياكلها اكلا ذريعا ويخرجها من حجرها ويسكن فيه فهو اظلم من ظالم [wa-huwa yaqwā ʿalā al-ḥayyāt wa-yaʾkuluhā aklan dharīʿan wa-yukhrijuhā min juḥrihā wa-yaskunu fīhi fa-huwa aẓlam min ẓālim] It prevails over serpents and eats them greedily, and drives them out of their hiding places in order to occupy them, and it is more violent than the violent. Abdollatif, followed by Damir [=al-Damīrī, Ḥayāt al-ḥayawān]: وبينه وبين الضب عداوة فيغلب الورل الضب ويقتله لكنه لا ياكله كما يفعل بالحيه وهو لا يتخذ بيتا لنفسه ولا يحفر حجرا بل يخرج الضب من حجره صارعا ويستولي عليه وانه كان اكثر براثن منه واقوي لكن الظلم يمنعه ولهذا يضرب به المثل في الظلم ويكفي في ظلمه انه يغصب الحية حجرها ويبلعها وربما قتل فوجد في بطنه الحية العظيمة وهو لا يتبلعها حتي يشدخ راسها [wa-baynahu wa-bayna al-ḍabb ʿadāwa fa-yaghlibu al-waral al-ḍabb wa-yaqtuluhu lākinnahu lā yaʾkuluhu kamā yafʿalu bi-l-ḥayya wa-huwa lā yattakhidhu baytan li-nafsihi wa-lā yaḥfiru juḥran bal yukhriju al-ḍabb min juḥrihi ṣāriʿan wa-yastawlī ʿalayhi wa-innahu kāna akthar barāthin minhu wa-aqwā lākin al-ẓulm yamnaʿuhu wa-li-hādhā yuḍrabu bihi al-mathal fī al-ẓulm wa-yakfī fī ẓulmihi annahu yaghṣibu al-ḥayya juḥrahā wa-yablaʿuhā wa-rubbamā qutila fa-wujida fī baṭnihi al-ḥayya al-ʿaẓīma wa-huwa lā yatabalaʿuhā ḥattā yashdakha raʾsahā] Between it and the τὸν [ton] dhab (or land crocodile) there is fierce enmity. But the guaril is superior and kills it. Yet it does not eat it, as it does with serpents. Nor does it choose a dwelling for itself or dig a hiding place. Rather, it thrusts the land crocodile out of its own home by wrestling, and prevails over it. Although the crocodile excels in the strength of its claws, this one is superior in violence. Hence its violence has become proverbial. It is so great in it that it suffices to thrust a serpent out of its den and devour it. Accordingly, a large serpent is sometimes found in the belly of one that has been killed; it does not swallow it without first crushing its head. There are many proverbs about it among the Arabs, of which these are worth noting: اجبر من الورل [ajbar min al-waral] Fiercer (or prouder) than the τῷ [tō] guaril. And اظلم من الورل [aẓlam min al-waral] More unjust (or more violent) than the τῷ [tō] guaril, to which Abdollatif alluded above. And اسرع من تلظم الورل [asraʿ min talaẓẓum al-waral] Quicker than the guaril turns food over in its mouth. To this belongs what Algiahid writes, that it eats the serpent greedily, or II, 495 اكلا نريعا [aklan narīʿan], that is, literally, with a swift and headlong eating R1. col. 1071 From this description it appears that the name כח coach, taken from strength, suits this lizard exceedingly well R2.

Si quis de lacerti genere, in his terris ignoto, plura scire aveat, illum describit Ibno-saida, دابة علي خلق الضب الا انه اعظم منه animal naturae τοῦ dhab, nisi quod est majus. Et Abdollatif, فاما الورل وهو الحردون Guaril utique idem est, quod chardaun. Sed contra Algiahid apud Damirem tradit, ان الحردون غير الورل ووصفه دابة تكون غالبا بارض مصر موشاة بالوان كثير ولها كف ككف الانسان مقسومة اصابعها الي الانامل Chardaun a guaril esse diversum, quem describit animal in Aegypto frequens, multis distinctum coloribus, manus habens, quales hominis, et digitos in articulos divisos. Et Alkazuinius, هو العظيم من شكل الوزغ وسام ابرص الطويل الذنب الصغير الراس وهو سريع السير خفيف الحركة Maximus est lacertorum, qui formam habent guezgae et sam-mabras (sunt stellionum species, quarum illa minor, haec major), longae caudae, parvi capitis, celeris incessus, et motus expediti. Avicenna quoque, postquam illum cum stellionibus contulit, caudamque et caput eodem modo descripsit, quo Alkazuinius, وهو غير الضب والضب لا يكون او قل ما يكون الا في البادية وراسه وبدنه وذنبه خالف الورل وربما قاربة في طبايعه Et alius est a dhab (seu terrestri crocodilo).

If anyone wishes to know more about this kind of lizard, unknown in these lands, Ibno-saida describes it: دابة علي خلق الضب الا انه اعظم منه [dābba ʿalā khalq al-ḍabb illā annahu aʿẓam minhu] an animal of the nature of the dhab, except that it is larger. And Abdollatif: فاما الورل وهو الحردون [fa-ammā al-waral wa-huwa al-ḥardūn] Guaril is indeed the same as chardaun. But on the contrary Algiahid, in Damir, reports ان الحردون غير الورل ووصفه دابة تكون غالبا بارض مصر موشاة بالوان كثير ولها كف ككف الانسان مقسومة اصابعها الي الانامل [anna al-ḥardūn ghayr al-waral wa-waṣafahu dābba takūn ghāliban bi-arḍ Miṣr muwashshāh bi-alwān kathīr wa-lahā kaff ka-kaff al-insān maqsūma aṣābiʿuhā ilā al-anāmil] that chardaun is different from guaril; he describes it as an animal common in Egypt, marked with many colours, having hands like those of a man, and fingers divided into joints. And Alkazuinius: هو العظيم من شكل الوزغ وسام ابرص الطويل الذنب الصغير الراس وهو سريع السير خفيف الحركة [huwa al-ʿaẓīm min shakl al-wazagh wa-sāmm abraṣ al-ṭawīl al-dhanab al-ṣaghīr al-raʾs wa-huwa sarīʿ al-sayr khafīf al-ḥaraka] It is the largest of the lizards that have the form of the guezga and the sam-mabras (these are species of stellio, the former smaller, the latter larger), with a long tail, a small head, a swift gait and nimble movement. Avicenna too, after comparing it with the stellios and describing its tail and head in the same way as Alkazuinius, says: وهو غير الضب والضب لا يكون او قل ما يكون الا في البادية وراسه وبدنه وذنبه خالف الورل وربما قاربة في طبايعه [wa-huwa ghayr al-ḍabb wa-al-ḍabb lā yakūn aw qall mā yakūn illā fī al-bādiya wa-raʾsuhu wa-badanuhu wa-dhanabuhu khālaf al-waral wa-rubbamā qārabahu fī ṭabāʾiʿihi] And it is different from the dhab (or land crocodile).

II, 496 Nam dhab nunquam est, aut valde raro est alibi, quam in deserto. Et capite, et corpore, et cauda differt a guaril. Naturae tamen illi vicinae est. Unde Al-camus, الورل دابة كالضب والعظيم من اشكال الوزغ طويل الذنب صغير الراس Guaril animal, ut dhab magnum, in iis, quae guezgae forma sunt, longae caudae, parvi capitis. Sunt, qui ex crocodili semine nasci putant, quomodo scincum: sed eos refellit Damir, cum quia cute et pelle multum differunt, tum quia statura est valde dispar: والورل لا يزيد قدره علي ذراعين او ذراع ونصف والتمساح عشرة col. 1072 اذرع واكثر Guaril enim non crescit supra duos cubitos, aut unum et semi, cum crocodilus crescat supra decem cubitos et amplius. Idem animal describitur ut valde libidinosum. Abdollatif, فليس في الحيوان اكثر سفاد منه Nullum est animal, quod coëat frequentius. Et, si creditur Alcamus, شحمه يعظم الذكر دلكا. Quod qui potest intelligat. Idem de carnis esu, لحمه حار جدا يسمن بقوة Caro ejus calidissima est, et maxime pinguefacit. Et Alkazuinius, لحمه وشحمه سمن طبقات الناس Ejus caro et adeps mulieres inprimis pinguefacit. Ita etiam Avicenna et Damir. Addunt, in eadem carne vim esse, qua e corpore avellit السلي والشوك surculos et spinas. Pilos, brachio mulieris alligatos, conceptum impedire. Excitari membra torpentia, si combustae pellis cinere, cum olei faecibus misto, illinantur. Stercus albugines, lentigines et verrucas amovere. Sanguinem denique visum roborare et acuere. Quod idem scribunt de crocodilo terrestri.

II, 496 For the dhab is never, or very rarely, found anywhere but in the desert. And in head, body and tail it differs from the guaril. Yet it is close to it in nature. Hence Alcamus [=al-Qāmūs al-muḥīṭ, the Arabic dictionary of al-Fīrūzābādī, which Bochart cites like an author]: الورل دابة كالضب والعظيم من اشكال الوزغ طويل الذنب صغير الراس [al-waral dābba ka-al-ḍabb wa-al-ʿaẓīm min ashkāl al-wazagh ṭawīl al-dhanab ṣaghīr al-raʾs] Guaril is an animal like the dhab, large among those which have the form of the guezga, with a long tail and a small head. Some think it is born from the seed of the crocodile, like the skink; but Damir refutes them, both because they differ much in skin and hide, and because their size is very unequal: والورل لا يزيد قدره علي ذراعين او ذراع ونصف والتمساح عشرة col. 1072 اذرع واكثر [wa-al-waral lā yazīd qadruhu ʿalā dhirāʿayn aw dhirāʿ wa-niṣf wa-al-timsāḥ ʿashara adhruʿ wa-akthar] For the guaril does not grow beyond two cubits, or one and a half, whereas the crocodile grows beyond ten cubits and more. The same animal is described as very lustful. Abdollatif: فليس في الحيوان اكثر سفاد منه [fa-laysa fī al-ḥayawān akthar sifādan minhu] There is no animal that copulates more often. And, if Alcamus is to be believed, شحمه يعظم الذكر دلكا [shaḥmuhu yuʿaẓẓim al-dhakar dalkan]. Let him understand this who can. He says the same about eating its flesh: لحمه حار جدا يسمن بقوة [laḥmuhu ḥārr jiddan yusamminu bi-quwwa] Its flesh is very hot and fattens greatly. And Alkazuinius: لحمه وشحمه سمن طبقات الناس [laḥmuhu wa-shaḥmuhu samman ṭabaqāt al-nās] Its flesh and fat fatten women above all. So also Avicenna and Damir. They add that the same flesh has a power by which it draws out of the body السلي والشوك [al-salā wa-al-shawk] splinters and thorns; that its hairs, tied to a woman's arm, prevent conception; that numb limbs are revived if smeared with the ash of its burnt skin mixed with the dregs of oil; that its dung removes white spots of the eye, freckles and warts; and finally that its blood strengthens and sharpens the sight. They write the same of the land crocodile.

Quaeri potest, an hic lacertus sit idem cum lacerto viridi: quod videbuntur multa suadere. 1. Quod lacertus viridis eorum est maximus, qui apud nos sunt. Erasmus in Dialogo De Amicitia: Italia magnas habet lacertas, et virides. Gesnerus: Majores fere duplo sunt communibus lacertis. 2. Lacerti virides sunt ὀφιομάχοι. Erasmus ibidem, Hoc animal natura et homini amicum est, et serpentibus inimicum. Et paullo post, Quodam die vidi praegrandem et mire viridem, in ostio cavi decertantem cum serpente. Primum mirabamur, quid esset rei. Nam serpens nobis non erat conspicuus. Italus admonebat, in antro esse hostem. Paullo post venit ad nos lacerta, velut ostendens sua vulnera, ac remedium flagitans, etc. Serpens alterum latus paene totum eroserat, et ex viridi rubrum fecerat. Itaque ad has lacertas id referendum, quod in Plinio legitur, lacertas cum serpentibus pugnare. Unde illi nomen ὀφιονίκος a recentioribus inditum.

It may be asked whether this lizard is the same as the green lizard, which many things will seem to suggest. 1. The green lizard is the largest of those found among us. Erasmus in the Dialogue On Friendship: Italy has large lizards, and green ones. Gesner: They are almost twice as large as common lizards. 2. Green lizards are ὀφιομάχοι [ophiomachoi]. Erasmus in the same place: This animal is by nature both friendly to man and hostile to serpents. And a little later: One day I saw a very large and wonderfully green one fighting with a serpent at the mouth of a hole. At first we wondered what was going on, for the serpent was not visible to us. An Italian told us that the enemy was in the cave. A little later the lizard came to us, as if showing its wounds and begging for a remedy, etc. The serpent had gnawed away almost the whole of one side and had turned it from green to red. So what we read in Pliny, that lizards fight with serpents, should be referred to these lizards. Hence the more recent writers gave it the name ὀφιονίκος [ophionikos].

II, 497 Pandectarius: OFIONICOS Graece, id est, lacerta. 3. Ut esu τοῦ guaril Arabes pinguescere se scribunt: ita phtisicis lacertam viridem esse utilissimam, et incredibiliter prodesse tradunt Plinius et Marcellus. 4. Utriusque stercora, faciei il-lita, maculas delere leguntur. Tamen guaril a lacerto viridi distinguit color. Varium enim describit Algiahid. Praeterea videtur guaril, qualis ab Arabibus describitur, lacertam viridem viribus et animi ferocia longe antecellere. Puto igitur, τὸν guaril lacerti genus esse, Arabiae peculiare, et lacertum viridem ita illi similem, ut sit

II, 497 The Pandectarius: OFIONICOS in Greek, that is, lizard. 3. Just as the Arabs write that they grow fat by eating the guaril, so Pliny and Marcellus report that the green lizard is most useful for consumptives and benefits them incredibly. 4. The dung of both, smeared on the face, is said to remove blemishes. Yet colour distinguishes the guaril from the green lizard, for Algiahid describes it as variegated. Moreover the guaril, as the Arabs describe it, seems far to surpass the green lizard in strength and fierceness of spirit. I therefore think that the guaril is a kind of lizard peculiar to Arabia, and that the green lizard is so similar to it that it is

Proximus huic, longo sed proximus intervallo.

Next to it, but next by a long interval.

R1 Rosenmüller, II, 495 In Leo Africanus, Descript. Africae book 9, ch. 53, this lizard is called Guaral. An animal, he says, similar to the aforementioned [Dub, ضبّ [ḍabb]] but somewhat larger; it likewise carries venom in its head and tail, and when these parts have been cut off the Arabs are accustomed to eat it; otherwise it is ugly and of a foul colour. But ورل [waral] must not be confused with وَرَنٌ [waranun], which in Giggeius [=Antonio Giggei, Thesaurus linguae Arabicae (Milan 1632)] is rendered a species of reptile, but which, on Forskål's testimony, is the name of the Nile lizard; see Descriptiones Animalium, quae in itinere Orientali observavit Petrus Forskal, p. 13.

(2) Rosenmüller, II, 495 Sed recte monet MICHAELIS in Supplemm. P. IV. p. 1221. plura se offerre etyma, a Bocharto non expensa, ad quae il-lius lacertae nomen possit referri. Prorsus incertam esse hanc rationem, meris etymologiis expiscari eorum nominum, quae animalia indicant, significationes, jam saepius monuimus. Videtur nomen כח unum esse ex iis, de quorum significatione definienda plane est desperandum, cum destituamur tam reliquarum dialectorum, quam eorum locorum adminiculis, ex quibus hujus animalis natura et indoles ita cognoscatur, ut de eo certi quidquam possit statui.

R2 Rosenmüller, II, 495 But Michaelis rightly points out in the Supplemm. vol. 4, p. 1221, that several further etymologies present themselves, not considered by Bochart, to which the name of that lizard could be referred. We have already noted quite often that this method, fishing out the meanings of the names that denote animals by etymologies alone, is wholly uncertain. The name כח seems to be one of those whose meaning we must utterly despair of determining, since we lack the aid both of the other dialects and of passages from which the nature and character of this animal might be known well enough for anything certain to be established about it.
