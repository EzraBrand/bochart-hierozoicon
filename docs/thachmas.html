II, 830 col. 231 CAP. XV.

II, 830 col. 231 Chapter 15. ¶

Probatur, תחמס thachmas esse struthionem marem.

It is proved that תחמס thachmas is the male ostrich. ¶

Ut apud Graecos ἅρπη, ἁρπετὸς, ἁρπυία rapacium avium nomina sunt, a ra- col. 232 piendo ita dicta: sic inter aves immundas, in Lege prohibitas, תחמס thachmas, esse creditur ex numero rapacium; quia חמס chamas, unde thachmas, rapere est, et vim inferre. Ita fere statuunt Hebraei recentiores. Kimchius, נקרא כן שחומס שאר העופות וגוזל אותם, Ita vocatur, quia reliquis avibus vim infert, et eas rapit. Aquinas תחמס שם עוף דורס, Thachmas, nomen avis rapacis. Pomarius, עוף דורסים פלקוני בלעז או כדומה, Avis e genere rapacium, falco vulgari lingua, aut alia, illi similis. Proinde Arias accipitrem interpretatur. Cui sententiae patrocinari visi sunt ex veteribus Ionathan, et Ierosolymitanus interpres, qui hic habent חטפיתא chataphitha, vel חטופיתא chatupphitha, quod avis rapacis nomen esse, statuunt plerique Lexicographi; quia חטף est rapere. Itaque ex Hierosolymitana Paraphrasi confirmat Kimchius, quod dixerat, תחמס thachmas, esse avem rapacem. Seniores γλαῦκα, et Hieronymus noctuam interpretatur. Quam versionem Fullerus maxime probat Miscellaneorum libri sexti capite nono, et confirmare nititur ex etymo vocabuli; quia noctuae proprium est vim inferre, et pati, caeteris avibus in eam certatim involantibus. Forte etiam in Onkelo ציצא tsitsa, noctua est, ut Graece τυτώ. Hesychius, Τυ-τώ· ἡ γλαύξ. Ex ציצא tsitsa, factum τυτώ· ut τύρος ex צור tsor; et τάνις ex צען tsoan. Vel ציצא tsitsa et τυτὼ vocabula sunt ὀνοματοποιητικὰ, quae II, 831 noctuae boatum videntur imitari. Quo pertinet Plauti jocus in Menaechmis Actu quarto.

Just as among the Greeks ἅρπη [harpē], ἁρπετὸς [harpetos], ἁρπυία [harpyia] are names of birds of prey, so called from seizing col. 232, so among the unclean birds forbidden in the Law, תחמס thachmas is believed to be one of the birds of prey, because חמס chamas, from which thachmas comes, means to seize and to do violence. This is roughly the view of the more recent Hebrews. Kimchi [=David Kimḥi (Radak), Sefer ha-Shorashim]: נקרא כן שחומס שאר העופות וגוזל אותם, It is so called because it does violence to the other birds and seizes them. Aquinas [=Philippe d'Aquin, Dictionarium hebraeo-chaldaeo-talmudico-rabbinicum (Paris 1629)]: תחמס שם עוף דורס, Thachmas, the name of a bird of prey. Pomarius [=David de Pomis, Ṣemaḥ David (Venice 1587)]: עוף דורסים פלקוני בלעז או כדומה, A bird of the raptor kind, falcon in the vernacular, or another similar to it. Accordingly Arias [=Benito Arias Montano, the interlinear Latin of the Antwerp Polyglot] renders it hawk. Among the ancients, Jonathan [=Targum Jonathan] and the Jerusalem translator seemed to support this view; they have here חטפיתא chataphitha, or חטופיתא chatupphitha, which most lexicographers hold to be the name of a bird of prey, because חטף means to seize. So Kimchi confirms from the Jerusalem Paraphrase what he had said, that תחמס thachmas is a bird of prey. The Elders render it γλαῦκα [glauka], and Jerome little owl. Fuller [=Nicholas Fuller, Miscellanea theologica / sacra (1612–22)] strongly approves this version in Miscellanea book 6, chapter 9, and tries to confirm it from the etymology of the word, since it is characteristic of the little owl both to do violence and to suffer it, as the other birds vie in flying at it. Perhaps also in Onkelos [=Targum Onkelos] ציצא tsitsa is the little owl, as in Greek τυτώ [tytō]. Hesychius: Τυτώ· ἡ γλαύξ [Tytō: hē glaux]. From ציצא tsitsa came τυτώ [tytō], as τύρος [tyros] from צור tsor, and τάνις [tanis] from צען tsoan. Or ציצא tsitsa and τυτὼ [tytō] are onomatopoeic words, ὀνοματοποιητικὰ [onomatopoiētika], which II, 831 seem to imitate the hooting of the little owl. To this belongs the joke of Plautus in the Menaechmi, Act 4. ¶

M. Quis is Menaechmus est? P. TU istic inquam? M. Egone? P. TU, etc. M. Egon' dedi? P. TU istic, inquam. Vin' afferri noctuam, Quae TUTU usque dicat? Nam nos jam defossi sumus.

M. Who is this Menaechmus? P. YOU, I say. M. I? P. YOU, etc. M. Did I give it? P. YOU there, I say. Shall I have a little owl brought, to keep saying TUTU at you? For we are already done for. ¶

Itaque etiam Arabum طوط taut, noctuam explicare malim, quam cum Giggeio vespertilionem (1). Eos hic consulto praetereo, qui תחמס, harpen, aut har- pyam, aut cymindim, aut nycticoracem reddunt; cum, quia tanti non sunt, tum, quia nullas, quod sciam, interpretationis suae rationes afferunt praeter hanc solam, scilicet nomen avis תחמס thachmas, a rapina deduci.

Therefore I would also rather explain the Arabs' طوط [ṭwṭ] taut as little owl than, with Giggeius [=Antonio Giggei, Thesaurus linguae Arabicae (Milan 1632)], as bat R1. Here I deliberately pass over those who render תחמס as harpe, or harpy, or cymindis, or nycticorax: both because they are not of much weight, and because, as far as I know, they offer no reasons for their interpretation except this one alone, namely that the name of the bird תחמס thachmas is derived from robbery. ¶

Verum hoc fuit τὸ πρῶτον ψεῦδος, quod plerosque interpretes in errorem egit transversos, quod putarunt, חמס chamas, et חטף chataph, esse prorsus synonyma. Ego, ut vicinae fateor esse significationis, ita ejusdem esse pernego. Proprie חטף chataph, est rapere; חמס chamas, inique agere. Neque enim עד חמס a), toties in sacro textu recte verteris testem rapinae, sed testem iniquita- tis, id est, iniquum, et falsum. Sic חמסו תורה b), ne reddas rapuerunt Le- gem, sed inique egerunt adversus Legem. Et alibi In pupillum, et viduam אל תחמסו c), ne injuste agatis. Item, Qui laedit sapientiam, חומס נפשו d) in seipsum inique agit. Itaque in Psalmis passim חמס, et עמל, et עולה, et תוך, et רע, et רשע copulantur ut synonyma. Et Septuaginta חמס nunquam reddunt ἁρπαγὴν, sed ἀδίκημα, ἀδικίαν, ἀθεσίαν, ἀνομίαν, ὄνειδος, ὕβριν. Et vero sicubi חמס, rapere est, aut vim inferre, ita sumitur ex consequente dun- col. 233 taxat; quia per vim aliquid extorquere contra jus, et fas, hominum iniquorum est. Quocirca nihil necesse est, ut תחמס, sit avis rapax, Hebraeis עוף דורס, cum probatum sit, nomine חמס proprie significari, quod impium, et iniquum est, etiam in eo, qui vacat crimine rapacitatis. Itaque quaecunque avis dicatur esse תחמס thachmas, nomini respondebit satis, si probetur, esse iniqua, et impia, II, 832 etiamsi non numeretur in censu rapacium. Avium porro nulla est, quae nomine impietatis tam male audiat, quam struthio. Caeteris ova fovere, et pullos educare curae est: haec ova sua derelinquit in terra, ut in pulvere calefiant; nec cogitat futurum, ut illa pes conterat, et agri bestia comprimat. Duram se exhibet erga pullos suos, ac si non essent sui; Iob. XXXIX, 17. 18. 19. Proinde Ieremias Thren. IV, 3. similis immanitatis a struthione sumit exemplum, ut jam attigimus obiter, et uberius docebimus in expositione verborum Iobi.

But this was the τὸ πρῶτον ψεῦδος [to prōton pseudos] that led most interpreters astray: they thought that חמס chamas and חטף chataph were wholly synonymous. I admit they are of neighbouring meaning, but I flatly deny that they mean the same. Properly חטף chataph is to seize; חמס chamas, to act unjustly. For you would not correctly render עד חמס a), so frequent in the sacred text, as witness of robbery, but witness of iniquity, that is, unjust and false. Likewise חמסו תורה b) you should not render they have seized the Law, but they have acted unjustly against the Law. And elsewhere: Against the orphan and the widow אל תחמסו c), do not act unjustly. Again: He who offends against wisdom חומס נפשו d) acts unjustly against himself. So in the Psalms חמס is everywhere joined as a synonym with עמל, עולה, תוך, רע and רשע. And the Septuagint never renders חמס as ἁρπαγὴν [harpagēn], but as ἀδίκημα [adikēma], ἀδικίαν [adikian], ἀθεσίαν [athesian], ἀνομίαν [anomian], ὄνειδος [oneidos], ὕβριν [hybrin]. And indeed, wherever חמס means to seize or to do violence, it is so taken only by consequence col. 233, because extorting something by force against law and right is the conduct of unjust men. Therefore there is no need for תחמס to be a bird of prey, in Hebrew עוף דורס, since it has been proved that the word חמס properly signifies what is impious and unjust, even in one free of the crime of rapacity. So whatever bird is said to be תחמס thachmas will answer well enough to the name, if it is proved to be unjust and impious, II, 832 even if it is not counted among the birds of prey. Moreover, no bird has so bad a name for impiety as the ostrich. The others take care to brood their eggs and rear their young; this one leaves her eggs on the ground, to be warmed in the dust, and does not consider that a foot may crush them and a beast of the field trample them. She is harsh toward her young, as if they were not hers; Job 39:17–18. 19. Accordingly Jeremiah, Lam 4:3, takes an example of similar cruelty from the ostrich, as we have already touched on in passing and will explain more fully in expounding the words of Job. ¶

Hoc porro nomine ab Arabibus struthio vocatur ظليم dtalim, id est, impius, iniquus. Quod struthionis nomen, etsi minus frequens, quam نعام naam, de quo supra, tamen in Avicenna occurrit libro secundo Canonis; et in Abenbitare, qui ordine alphabetico agunt De animalibus, plantis, lapidibus, fossilibus, etc. quae ad medicinam pertinent. Et in Damire, praeter caput De النعام τῶ naam, in quo struthionis naturam plenius explicat, est et caput aliud De الظليم τῶ dtalim, cujus hoc est initium: الظليم ذكر النعام وكنيته ابو البيض وابو ثلاثين وابو الصحاري وجمعه ظلمان Dtalim est mas struthionis, cujus cognomina sunt, pater ovorum, et pater triginta (supple ovorum) et pater desertorum. Plurale est dtulman. Ejusdem vocabuli meminit Ibnocholcan, in quo عار الظليم arra dtalim, id est, clamavit struthio mas. Meminit et Nasrius (scriptor ὀνειροκριτικὸς, quem legi manu-scriptum Oxoniae) lib. 3. cap. 98. sect. 21. Et Pandectarius Arabs, Talim, id est, struzo masculus. Etiam in Lexico Alcamus passim occurrit, quod pauca haec exempla doceant e multo pluribus: الظليم الذكر من النعام Dtalim mas, de struthione. يرفا الظليم النافر Iarpha, id est, dtalim (seu struthio mas) fugiens. عمود من الظليم رجلاه Columnae de dtalim, id est, pedes ejus. اسقف الاعوج العنق من الظلمان Ascaph, qui distorto est collo, de dtulman, seu struthionibus masculis. In quem morbum saepe incidunt, quia, cum in arenis adverso vento fugiunt, caput avertunt. الاصنع الظليم متقشر الساق Asna, dtalim (seu struthio mas) cruribus excoriatis, nempe dum fugit cum impetu per confragosa loca, الحاضب الظليم اغتلم فاخمرت ساقاه Alhadib, dtalim (seu struthio mas), cum furit, et rubent crura ejus. Quod illi quotannis accidit, col. 234 quo tempore dactyli rubescere incipiunt. جفلت الظليم حركته وطردته Excitavit τὸν dtalim, et impulit illum, ut fit a venatoribus. Possem II, 833 afferre hujus generis plura decuplo. Sed timeo, ne lectori sim taediosus. Et haec plus satis docent, ظليم dtalim esse struthionem. Quod nomen cum de mare proprie dicatur, tamen et foeminae tribui videtur καταχρηστικῶς. Ita enim Alcamus, توحوح الظليم فوق البيض رومها واظهر ولوعها بها Dtalim (seu struthio) thevvachvvacha super ova, id est, ea impense amavit, et docuit, quantum ea deperiret. Et الزاجل zagil eidem est ما يسيل من دبر الظليم ايام تحضينها بيضها quod fluit ex tergo τοῦ dtalim, dum ova sua fovet.

Further, by this name the Arabs call the ostrich ظليم [ẓalīm] dtalim, that is, impious, unjust. This name of the ostrich, though less frequent than نعام [naʿām] naam, discussed above, nevertheless occurs in Avicenna [=Ibn Sīnā, al-Qānūn fī l-ṭibb (the Canon)], book 2 of the Canon, and in Abenbitar [=Ibn al-Bayṭār], who treat in alphabetical order Of the animals, plants, stones, minerals, etc. that pertain to medicine. And in Damir [=al-Damīrī, Ḥayāt al-ḥayawān], besides the chapter On النعام [al-naʿām] the naam, in which he explains the nature of the ostrich more fully, there is also another chapter On الظليم [al-ẓalīm] the dtalim, which begins thus: الظليم ذكر النعام وكنيته ابو البيض وابو ثلاثين وابو الصحاري وجمعه ظلمان [al-ẓalīm dhakar al-naʿām wa-kunyatuhu abū al-bayḍ wa-abū thalāthīn wa-abū al-ṣaḥārī wa-jamʿuhu ẓulmān] Dtalim is the male of the ostrich, whose surnames are: father of eggs, and father of thirty (supply eggs) and father of the deserts. The plural is dtulman. The same word is mentioned by Ibnocholcan, in whom عار الظليم [ʿāra al-ẓalīm] arra dtalim, that is, the male ostrich cried out. It is mentioned also by Nasrius (a writer on the interpretation of dreams, ὀνειροκριτικὸς [oneirokritikos], whom I read in manuscript at Oxford), book 3, ch. 98, sect. 21. And by the Arabic Pandectarius: Talim, that is, the male struzo. It also occurs everywhere in the Lexicon of Alcamus [=al-Qāmūs al-muḥīṭ, the Arabic dictionary of al-Fīrūzābādī], as these few examples out of many more may show: الظليم الذكر من النعام [al-ẓalīm al-dhakar min al-naʿām] Dtalim, the male, of the ostrich. يرفا الظليم النافر [yarfā al-ẓalīm al-nāfir] Iarpha, that is, the dtalim (or male ostrich) in flight. عمود من الظليم رجلاه [ʿamūd min al-ẓalīm rijlāhu] The columns of the dtalim, that is, its feet. اسقف الاعوج العنق من الظلمان [asqaf al-aʿwaj al-ʿunuq min al-ẓulmān] Ascaph, one with a twisted neck, among the dtulman, or male ostriches. They often fall into this ailment because, when they flee across the sands against the wind, they turn their heads away. الاصنع الظليم متقشر الساق [al-aṣnaʿ al-ẓalīm mutaqashshir al-sāq] Asna, a dtalim (or male ostrich) with skinned legs, namely when it flees headlong through rough places. الحاضب الظليم اغتلم فاخمرت ساقاه [al-ḥāḍib al-ẓalīm ightalama fa-ikhmarrat sāqāhu] Alhadib, a dtalim (or male ostrich), when it is in rut and its legs turn red. This happens to it every year, col. 234 at the time when the dates begin to redden. جفلت الظليم حركته وطردته [jaffaltu al-ẓalīm ḥarraktuhu wa-ṭaradtuhu] He roused the dtalim, and drove it on, as hunters do. I could II, 833 bring forward ten times as many of this kind. But I fear I would weary the reader. And these show more than enough that ظليم [ẓalīm] dtalim is the ostrich. Although this name is properly said of the male, it nevertheless seems to be given also to the female by catachresis, καταχρηστικῶς [katachrēstikōs]. For thus Alcamus: توحوح الظليم فوق البيض رومها واظهر ولوعها بها [tawaḥwaḥa al-ẓalīm fawqa al-bayḍ rawmuhā wa-aẓhara walūʿahā bihā] The dtalim (or ostrich) thevvachvvacha over the eggs, that is, loved them intensely, and showed how desperately it doted on them. And الزاجل [al-zājil] zagil is for him ما يسيل من دبر الظليم ايام تحضينها بيضها [mā yasīlu min dubr al-ẓalīm ayyām taḥḍīnihā bayḍahā] what flows from the rear of the dtalim while it is brooding its eggs. ¶

Haec ideo observo, quia nomini Arabico ظليم dtalim plane respondet Hebraeum תחמס thachmas, hactenus ignoratum. Nam, ut ظليم dtalim fit a ظلم dtalima, quod iniquum esse et opprimere significat: ita תחמס thachmas a verbo חמס chamas, ejusdem significationis, cujus Arabicum ظلم dtalima. Itaque in Pentateucho fere quoties occurrit חמס in sacro textu, fere toties occurrit ظلم in Arabum paraphrastis. Sic Gen. VI, 11. Hebr. Terra plena erat חמס, iniquitate, Arabs Erpenianus بالظلم. Ita etiam versu decimo tertio, ubi et alter Arabs Parisiis editus تملات ظلما. Et capitis decimi sexti versu quinto, ubi Hebraea חמסי עליך, Injuria mea super te, id est, Injuria, quae mihi fit, in te recidet, Arabs uterque habet ظلمي عليك. Et Gen. XLIX, 5. Hebr. כלי חמס, vasa iniquitatis, uterque reddit آلة الظلم. Ut Exod. XXIII, 1. et Deut. XIX, 16. עד חמס, testis iniquitatis, in utroque est شاهد ظلم. Cujusmodi exempla videas etiam in aliis Scripturae libris. Sic Psal. VII, 17. Super verticem ejus descendat iniquitas ejus. Hebr. חמסו, Arab. ظلمه. Psal. XI, 5. Qui amat iniquitatem. Hebr. חמס, Arab. الظلم. Psal. XXV, 19. Oderunt me שנאת חמס, odio iniquitatis: Arab. ظلما, inique. Vide et Psal. XXVII, 12. et LVIII, 2. et LXXII, 14. et LXXIII, 6. et CXL, 2. 5. 12. Ita etiam in Prophetis. Ut Esa. LX, 18. Non audietur amplius חמס, iniquitas in terra tua: Arab. ظلم. Ezech. XLV, 9. Amovete חמס, iniquitatem: Arab. الظلم. Amos III, 10. Qui thesaurizant חמס, iniquitatem: Arab. ظلما. Ion. III, 8. Quisque se convertat a via sua mala, ומן החמס, et ab iniquitate, quae est in manibus suis: Arab. ومن الظلم. Hab. I, 2. Clamo ad te חמס, oppressionem (id est, vim) patiens, ut Hieronymus: Arab. مظلوم oppressus. Cum igitur Arabicum ظلم sit plane idem, quod Hebraeum חמס, col. 235 II, 834 ut patuit ex tot exemplis, inde valde probabili conjectura elicitur, etiam ظليم idem esse, quod תחמס; et תחמס idem, quod ظليم, hoc est utraque voce struthionem significari, et quidem marem. Quam probabilis, et, paene dicam, necessaria sit haec ratio, sciunt omnes, quibus harum linguarum genius est perspectus. Omnino igitur, ut in Mose בת היענה est struthio foemina, ita vox proxima התחמס est struthio mas. Neque id novum est, ut sexus animalium sic distinguatur. Sic Avicenna et Abenbitar, ut notavimus, de نعام naam et ظليم dtalim agunt, id est, de struthione foemina et mare, separatis capitibus. Et in ipso Mose Gen. XXXII, 15. Iacob Esavo obtulisse legitur capras et hircos, oves et arietes. Et Gen. XLV, 23. Iosephus patri mittit asinos decem et asinas totidem. At, inquies, cur in struthione sexus distinguitur potius, quam in aquila, corvo, etc. Ratio est in promptu. Cum נשר totum aquilarum, et עורב totum corvorum genus contineat, Mosi suffecit prohibere נשר et עורב. Sed struthionis nomen genericum Hebraei nullum habent. Nam תחמס solos mares significat, et בת יענה solas foeminas, ut et רננים in Iobo. Itaque, ne, horum unum prohibens, videretur alterum concedere, necesse habuit utrumque diserte prohibere (2).

I note this because the hitherto unidentified Hebrew תחמס thachmas corresponds exactly to the Arabic name ظليم [ẓalīm] dtalim. For as ظليم [ẓalīm] dtalim comes from ظلم [ẓalama] dtalima, which means to be unjust and to oppress, so תחמס thachmas comes from the verb חמס chamas, which has the same meaning as the Arabic ظلم [ẓalama] dtalima. Accordingly, in the Pentateuch, nearly every time חמס occurs in the sacred text, ظلم [ẓulm] occurs in the Arabic paraphrasts. Thus in Gen 6:11 the Hebrew reads: The earth was full of חמס, iniquity; the Erpenianus [=the Arabic version edited by Thomas Erpenius (Pentateuch 1622; New Testament 1616)] has بالظلم [bi-l-ẓulm]. So too in verse 13, where the other Arabic version, published in Paris, also has تملات ظلما [tamallaʾat ẓulman]. And in chapter 16, verse 5, where the Hebrew has חמסי עליך, My wrong be upon you, that is, the wrong done to me will fall back upon you, both Arabic versions have ظلمي عليك [ẓulmī ʿalayka]. And in Gen 49:5, for the Hebrew כלי חמס, instruments of iniquity, both render آلة الظلم [ālat al-ẓulm]. Likewise in Exod 23:1 and Deut 19:16, עד חמס, a witness of iniquity, is in both شاهد ظلم [shāhid ẓulm]. Examples of this kind may also be seen in the other books of Scripture. Thus Ps 7:17. Let his iniquity come down upon his own pate. Hebrew חמסו, Arabic ظلمه [ẓulmuhu]. Ps 11:5. He who loves iniquity. Hebrew חמס, Arabic الظلم [al-ẓulm]. Ps 25:19. They hate me שנאת חמס, with a hatred of iniquity: Arabic ظلما [ẓulman], unjustly. See also Ps 27:12 and LVIII, 2. and LXXII, 14. and LXXIII, 6. and CXL, 2. 5. 12. So also in the Prophets. Thus Isa 60:18. There shall no more be heard חמס, iniquity in your land: Arabic ظلم [ẓulm]. Ezek 45:9. Remove חמס, iniquity: Arabic الظلم [al-ẓulm]. Amos 3:10. Who store up חמס, iniquity: Arabic ظلما [ẓulman]. Jonah 3:8. Let everyone turn from his evil way, ומן החמס, and from the iniquity that is in his hands: Arabic ومن الظلم [wa-min al-ẓulm]. Hab 1:2. I cry out to you חמס, suffering oppression (that is, violence), as Jerome has it: Arabic مظلوم [maẓlūm], oppressed. Since, then, the Arabic ظلم [ẓulm] is exactly the same as the Hebrew חמס, col. 235 II, 834 as so many examples have shown, a highly probable conjecture follows: that ظليم [ẓalīm] is also the same as תחמס, and תחמס the same as ظليم [ẓalīm]; that is, that both words denote the ostrich, and specifically the male. How probable, and I might almost say necessary, this reasoning is, all who understand the genius of these languages know. It is entirely certain, therefore, that as in Moses בת היענה is the female ostrich, so the next word, התחמס, is the male ostrich. Nor is it anything new for the sexes of animals to be distinguished in this way. Thus Avicenna and Abenbitar, as we have noted, treat نعام [naʿām] naam and ظليم [ẓalīm] dtalim, that is, the female and the male ostrich, in separate chapters. And in Moses himself, Gen 32:15, we read that Jacob offered Esau she-goats and he-goats, ewes and rams. And in Gen. XLV, 23., Joseph sends his father ten male asses and as many she-asses. But, you will say, why is the sex distinguished in the ostrich rather than in the eagle, the raven, etc.? The reason is ready to hand. Since נשר comprises the whole genus of eagles, and עורב the whole genus of ravens, it was enough for Moses to prohibit נשר and עורב. But the Hebrews have no generic name for the ostrich. For תחמס denotes only the males, and בת יענה only the females, as does רננים in Job. Therefore, lest by prohibiting one of these he should seem to permit the other, he had to prohibit both expressly R2. ¶

Meanwhile, lest you be surprised that the interpreters went different ways in explaining this word, here is another similar example in the word חטפיתא, by which the Chaldeans render תחמס! For all the lexicographers, relying on the etymology of the word, hold that it signifies a bird of prey. For חטף means to seize. Therefore חטפיתא, plainly derived from it, was believed to be a bird of the class τῶν γαμψωνύχων [tōn gampsōnychōn]; this is a very grave error. For the Chaldeans' חטפיתא chatphitha, or חטופיתא chatophitha, is the very same as the Arabs' خطاف [khuṭṭāf] chottaph, since in both the so-called radical letters are the same. Accordingly, in this very passage, for the Chaldee חטפיתא the Arabs have خطاف [khuṭṭāf] chottaph in three different paraphrases, of which two are printed and the third is in manuscript. Further, that the Arabic خطاف [khuṭṭāf] chottaph, whose plural is خطاطيف [khaṭāṭīf] chatatiph, is the swallow is clear from Avicenna and Abenbitar. They cite the passage of Dioscorides on the swallow near the end of his second book, Χελιδόνος νεοσσοὺς τοὺς ἐκ τῆς πρώτης νεοσσοποιΐας αὐξομένης τῆς Σελήνης ἀνατεμὼν, εὑρήσεις ἐν τῇ γαστρὶ λίθους [Chelidonos neossous tous ek tēs prōtēs neossopoiias auxomenēs tēs Selēnēs anatemōn, heurēseis en tē gastri lithous], etc., in Arabic thus, in the chapter خطاف [khuṭṭāf] cottaph: قال ديسقوريدوس II, 835 في الثاني اذا اخذ فرخه في زيادة القمر وكان اول ما افرخ وشق وجد فيه حصايان [qāla Dīsqūrīdūs fī al-thānī idhā ukhidha farkhuhu fī ziyādat al-qamar wa-kāna awwal mā afrakha wa-shuqqa wujida fīhi ḥaṣāyān] Dioscorides said in the second book: if its chick is taken while the moon is waxing, and is from the first brood, and is cut open, stones are found in it, etc. Or واخذ من الحصا الموجودة في جوفه حصاتان [wa-ukhidha min al-ḥaṣā al-mawjūda fī jawfihi ḥaṣātān] And let two stones be taken from the stones that are found in its belly, etc., as Abenbitar has it. See the rest there, for there is no time to copy out more. But what, you will say, does the swallow have in common with birds of prey, that it should take its name from seizing? To me it makes no difference: whatever the reason it is so called, it is nevertheless certain that it is so called. But the origin of the name is also clear enough, since the swallow is carnivorous and lives only by seizing prey. Hence Isidore, Origins, book 12, chapter 7: The swallow is so called because it does not take its food sitting, but SEIZES its food in the air and eats it. And the Philosopher more than once calls it σαρκοφάγον [sarkophagon]. Thus in History of Animals, book 6, chapter 5: Ἡ δὲ χελιδὼν δὶς νεοττεύει μόνον II, 836 τῶν σαρκοφάγων [Hē de chelidōn dis neotteuei monon tōn sarkophagōn]; The swallow alone of carnivorous animals nests twice. And in book 8, chapter 3: Εἰσὶ δὲ καὶ τῶν μὴ γαμψωνύχων ἔνιοι σαρκοφάγοι, οἷον ἡ χελιδών [Eisi de kai tōn mē gampsōnychōn enioi sarkophagoi, hoion hē chelidōn]. Pliny rendered this in book 10, chapter 24: The swallow is the only bird that feeds on flesh, among those which do not have hooked talons. And Plutarch, Symposiacs, book 8, Problem 7, comparing these two symbols of Pythagoras, Χελιδόνας οἰκίᾳ μὴ δέχεσθαι, μηδὲ γαμψωνύχων οἴκοι τρέφειν [Chelidonas oikia mē dechesthai, mēde gampsōnychōn oikoi trephein], Swallows are not to be admitted into the house, nor birds with hooked talons to be reared in the home, asserts that they rest on the same reason: Σαρκοφάγος γάρ ἐστι (ἡ χελιδὼν) καὶ μάλιστα τοὺς τέττιγας ἱεροὺς καὶ μουσικοὺς ὄντας ἀποκτίννυσι, καὶ σιτεῖται [Sarkophagos gar esti (hē chelidōn) kai malista tous tettigas hierous kai mousikous ontas apoktinnysi, kai siteitai]; For the swallow is carnivorous, he says, and above all kills and devours cicadas, which are sacred and musical. Moreover, Pliny observes of the swallow, in the place cited above, that the speed of its winding flight is so swift that for this reason it is not exposed to the attacks of other birds. Perhaps those who named it from seizing had this in view. For it is well known that in almost all languages verbs of seizing also refer to speed. Thus in Hebrew Kimchi and Mardochaeus explain חטיפה, seizure, as לקיחה במהירות, a snatching with speed. And the Talmudists in Moed col. 237 Katan ch. 3, fol. 28a: מת פתאום זו היא מיתא חטופא, If someone dies suddenly, this is a snatched death, that is, headlong and swift. Like that saying of Plutarch in the Consolation to Apollonius: Ἐὰν δὲ ταχέως ἀποθάνῃ, ὀδύρονται λέγοντες, Ἀνηρπάσθη [Ean de tacheōs apothanē, odyrontai legontes, Anērpasthē]. If he dies suddenly, they lament, saying, He has been snatched away. R1 Rosenmüller, II, 831 This very view has recently also found favour with Oedmann [=Samuel Ödmann, Vermischte Sammlungen aus der Naturkunde], who in vol. 3, Chapter 4, p. 45 ff. sought to prove that תחמס is strix Otus Linn. (die mittlere Ohreule, der kleine Schuhu). He relied above all on this argument: that خمش [khamasha] in the language of the Arabs means to wound with the claws, and that this fits very well that bird which, as Hasselquist [=Fredrik Hasselquist, Iter Palaestinum (1757)] reports (Travels p. 291), when in the evening it finds the windows open, enters houses and kills infants left without a guard. How flimsy this argument is, everyone will see for himself.

(2) Rosenmüller, II, 834 De vocabulo תחמס dignus quoque est, qui conferatur I. D. MICHAELIS in Supplemm. P. III. pag. 825. seqq. Probabilis et ipsi est Bocharti sententia. Sed ab adsentiendo prohibet ipsum maxime hoc, quod contra Mosis morem sit, utriusque sexus in animalibus vetitis mentionem facere. Cui dubitationi jam Bochartus ipse occurrit tam hoc ipso loco, quam supra sub fin. Cap. praeced.

R2 Rosenmüller, II, 834 On the word תחמס, J. D. Michaelis [=J. D. Michaelis (1717–91), orientalist: Supplementa ad lexica hebraica; Fragen an eine Gesellschaft gelehrter Männer] in the Supplemm. vol. 3, p. 825 ff. also deserves to be compared. To him too Bochart's opinion seems probable. But what chiefly prevents him from assenting is that it is contrary to Moses' custom to mention both sexes among the forbidden animals. Bochart himself has already met this doubt, both in this very passage and above, toward the end of the preceding Chapter.
