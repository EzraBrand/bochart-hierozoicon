II, 770 col. 185 Chapter 5. ¶

Probatur, פרס peres esse Ossifragam.

It is proved that פרס peres is the ossifrage. ¶

In censu immundarum avium פרס peres est ordine secunda. Graeci τὸν γρύπα explicant. Unde gryphum Hieronymus, Pagninus et Arias. Et ex Hebraeis Pomarius, qui Perusiam, Tuscorum oppidum, ex eo vult sumsisse nomen, ac proinde פרס peres, seu gryphem habere in insignibus. Gryphem tamen nunquam ὑποχείριον ἀνθρώπων γεγονέναι, vere observat Origenes lib. 4. in Celsum. Et de gryphibus quidquid afferunt nugacissimi scriptores, Ctesias et Aristeas Proconnesius, jure merito rejiciunt ut fabulosum Plinius, Arrianus et Pausanias, ut alibi docemus. Itaque non potest stare haec versio, nisi gryphis nomine aliud intelligatur, quam vulgo, γρυπαίετος nimirum, id est, aquila rostro insigniter adunco. Id enim proprie γρυπότης. Nempe in rostro eadem esse solet, quae in unguibus aduncitas. Porro nomine פרס peres avem significari maxime ungulatam, ex eo puto conjecisse vetustos illos interpretes, quod Hebraeum פרסה parsa, pro ungula iisdem fere literis scribitur. Sed conjectura haec minus placet; quia פרסה parsa nonnisi quadrupedum est ungula: ut avium צפרן tsipporen, Chaldaeis טפר tephar, Dan. IV, 30.

In the list of unclean birds פרס peres is second in order. The Greeks render it τὸν γρύπα [ton grypa]. Hence Jerome, Pagninus [=Santes Pagnino, Latin Bible (1528) and Thesaurus linguae sanctae] and Arias [=Benito Arias Montano, the interlinear Latin of the Antwerp Polyglot] have "griffin". So too, among the Hebrews, Pomarius [=David de Pomis, Ṣemaḥ David (Venice 1587)], who holds that Perusia, a town of the Etruscans, took its name from it, and therefore has the פרס peres, or griffin, in its arms. Yet Origen, book 4 Against Celsus, rightly observes that the griffin has never ὑποχείριον ἀνθρώπων γεγονέναι [hypocheirion anthrōpōn gegonenai]. And whatever those most frivolous writers Ctesias and Aristeas of Proconnesus relate about griffins, Pliny, Arrian and Pausanias quite rightly reject as fabulous, as we show elsewhere. This rendering, therefore, cannot stand, unless by the name gryps [griffin] something other than the common meaning is understood, namely γρυπαίετος [grypaietos], that is, an eagle with a remarkably hooked beak. For that is properly γρυπότης [grypotēs]. The beak, of course, usually has the same hookedness as the talons. Further, I think those ancient interpreters conjectured that the name פרס peres signifies a bird with especially large talons because the Hebrew פרסה parsa, "hoof", is written with almost the same letters. But this conjecture pleases me less, because פרסה parsa is only the hoof of quadrupeds, while that of birds is צפרן tsipporen, in Chaldee טפר tephar, Dan 4:30. ¶

Samaritano פרס peres est ࠁࠆࠀࠄ bezaa, accipiter: Munstero quoque et Schindlero. Sed Ionathani עוזא uza, de qua sequenti capite. Arabibus عقاب okab, μελαναίετος. Iudaeis in Germania בלאבֿוז blafus, quasi cyanopodem dicas. Ita vocant falconis genus a coeruleis pedibus, de quo Albertus, Nonum genus falconum, et jam declinans ab excellentia nobilium falconum, est id, col. 186 II, 771 cujus pedes hyacinthini, sive azurini sunt, etc. Ab Italis apud Gesnerum zafiro dicitur a sapphirino colore pedum. Id sequutus Deodatius in Italica versione falconem reddidit, et in Gallica girofalcum. Cujus versionis prima origo jam dicetur. A prasino deducta vox Gallica vetus pers primo viridem, deinde etiam coeruleum colorem significavit. Quod cum animadvertissent Iudaei, qui in Gallia olim frequentes habitabant, inde collegerunt insulsi homines, avem פרס peres saltem ex parte coerulei esse coloris, quasi nostrum pers pro coeruleo ab Hebraeo esset factum. Quod quidem est perabsurdum. Sed illis hominibus insanire solenne est. Nec minus ridiculo errore eam ipsam vocem פרס peres fecisse Perusiae nomen (Tuscorum oppido veteri, cujus praeter Ptolemaeum, Silius, Appianus, Eutropius, Stephanus atque alii meminerunt), nuper asserebat David de Pomis.

For the Samaritan, פרס peres is ࠁࠆࠀࠄ [בזאה] bezaa, the hawk; so too for Münster and Schindler. But for Jonathan [=Targum Jonathan] it is עוזא uza, of which in the next chapter. For the Arabs it is عقاب [ʿuqāb] okab, the melanaeetus. For the Jews in Germany it is בלאבֿוז blafus, as if you were to say "blue-foot". So they call a kind of falcon from its blue feet, of which Albertus says: The ninth kind of falcons, already falling short of the excellence of the noble falcons, is the one col. 186 II, 771 whose feet are hyacinthine or azure, etc. By the Italians, according to Gesner [=Conrad Gesner, Historia animalium], it is called zafiro from the sapphire colour of its feet. Following this, Diodati rendered it "falcon" in his Italian version, and "gerfalcon" in the French. The first origin of this rendering will now be explained. The old French word pers, derived from prasinus [leek-green], first meant a green, then also a blue colour. When the Jews, who once lived in large numbers in France, noticed this, those witless men inferred that the bird פרס peres is at least partly blue in colour, as if our pers for blue were formed from the Hebrew. This is utterly absurd. But raving is customary with those men. And by no less ridiculous an error David de Pomis [=David de Pomis, Ṣemaḥ David (Venice 1587), a Hebrew-Latin-Italian dictionary] recently asserted that this very word פרס peres produced the name of Perusia (an old town of the Etruscans, mentioned besides Ptolemy by Silius, Appian, Eutropius, Stephanus and others). ¶

Ex eodem grege non nemo apud Munsterum פרס peres avem esse statuit, quae in sola Perside reperiatur; quasi פרס peres alludat ad Hebraeum Persidis vocabulum פרס paras. Sed et haec conjectura duabus de causis inanis est. 1. Quia Persidis nomen Mose recentius est annis circiter nongentis: Cyri nempe tempore natum, qui primus populares suos equitare docuit. Unde פרסין, vel פרשין, Persae vel equites dici coeperunt, qui olim Cuthaei, vel Cissii, Susiani et Elamitae, ut probavimus in nostro Phaleg. 2. Quia Moses non prohibuisset Iudaeis avem, Persidi peculiarem, quae a Iudaea immensum distat. Interim agnoscendum est, quandam avem a Perside nomen habuisse, quam Aristophanes in Avibus vocat Περσικὸν ὄρνιν. Sed scribit Scholiastes, quosdam τὸν ἀλεκτρυόνα, alios τὸν ταῶ pavonem intelligere; quarum avium utraque munda, cum peres sit immunda.

From the same flock, someone cited by Münster decided that the פרס peres is a bird found only in Persia, as if פרס peres alluded to the Hebrew word for Persia, פרס paras. But this conjecture too is empty, for two reasons. 1. Because the name of Persia is about nine hundred years more recent than Moses: it arose in the time of Cyrus, who first taught his countrymen to ride. Hence those who were formerly Cuthaeans, or Cissians, Susians and Elamites began to be called פרסין or פרשין, Persians or horsemen, as we proved in our Phaleg. 2. Because Moses would not have forbidden the Jews a bird peculiar to Persia, which is immensely distant from Judaea. Meanwhile it must be acknowledged that a certain bird had its name from Persia, which Aristophanes in the Birds calls Περσικὸν ὄρνιν [Persikon ornin]. But the Scholiast writes that some understand τὸν ἀλεκτρυόνα [ton alektryona], others τὸν ταῶ [ton taō], the peacock; both of these birds are clean, whereas the peres is unclean. ¶

Iam scire est operae pretium, cur פרס, peres, Iunius ossifragam, Castalio anatariam interpretetur, quae sunt aquilarum species. Verbum פרס paras, unde פרס peres, primo dividere et distribuere, deinde etiam frangere significat; quia res frangendo dividuntur. Quod respexit Castalio, quia de anataria aquila scribit Plinius lib. 10. cap. 3. Ingenium est ei testudines raptas frangere e sublimi jaciendo, quae sors interemit poetam Aeschylum. Sed Iunius, quasi פרס peres a distribuendo dicatur, hanc suae versionis rationem affert in Notis: Ossifraga nomen habet a distribuendo, quod huic animanti sic tribuit Plinius: Ejectos ab aquilis cognatum genus ossifragae excipiunt, et educunt cum suis. In quibus tamen Plinii verbis de distributione ne γρῦ quidem, nisi καθ᾽ ἀκολούθησιν· quia cum ossifraga foetus alienos cum suis alit, necesse est, ut alimenta inter utrosque partiatur. Nec ossifragae nomen est a distribuendo, sed ab ossium fractione, quod omnes sciunt.

Now it is worth knowing why Junius [=Franciscus Junius, of the Tremellius-Junius Latin Bible] renders פרס, peres, "ossifrage", and Castalio [=Sebastian Castellio, Latin Bible (1551)] anataria [duck-eagle], which are species of eagles. The verb פרס paras, from which פרס peres comes, first means to divide and to distribute, then also to break, because things are divided by breaking. Castalio had this in view, because Pliny, book 10, ch. 3, writes of the duck-eagle: It has the habit of breaking tortoises it has seized by dropping them from on high, a fate which killed the poet Aeschylus. But Junius, as if פרס peres were named from distributing, gives this reason for his rendering in his Notes: The ossifrage has its name from distributing, because Pliny attributes this to the creature: The related kind, the ossifrages, take in the young cast out by the eagles and rear them with their own. Yet in these words of Pliny there is not even a γρῦ [gry] about distribution, except καθ᾽ ἀκολούθησιν [kath᾽ akolouthēsin]; for since the ossifrage feeds strange young with its own, it must divide the food between both. Nor is the name of the ossifrage from distributing, but from the breaking of bones, as everyone knows. ¶

II, 772 Aliorum sententiis sic explicatis, nostram quoque hic afferemus, et idoneis argumentis statuminare conabimur. Ac primo quidem iis accedo, qui פרס peres aquilae speciem esse volunt. In quibus Graeci, Hieronymus, Chaldaei, Arabes et plerique recentiorum. Huc facit, quod apud Macedones, qui ex Orientis spoliis non solum opes, sed et linguam suam auxerunt, aquila dicebatur παραὸς, allusione manifesta ad Hebraeum פרס peres. Hesychius, Παραός· ἀετὸς ὑπὸ Μακεδόνων· Paraos aquila Macedonibus. Praeterea, ut ex omnium consensu נשר genuinam aquilam sonat, ita vocibus, in Mose proxime sequentibus, nempe פרס et עזניה, alias aquilarum species, Iudaeis notas, designari, valde verisimile est. Neque tutum est se opponere tam multis interpretibus, qui sic statuunt. Hoc igitur firmum maneat, פרס esse in aquilarum speciebus. Sed de specie jam inquirendum, quae aliunde exsculpi non potest, quam ex vocis origine, cum de ea praeter nudum nomen nihil quidquam habeamus. Et recte col. 187 notat Aben Ezra, sumendam esse originem ex his Es. LVIII, 7. פרוס לרעב לחמך Frange esurienti panem tuum. Qua nota significatum voluit aquilae genus esse a frangendo sic appellatae. Sed, ut res sit manifestior, expende, Le-ctor, haec Mich. III, 3. quae in hoc argumento nondum fuisse citata, mirum est: ואת עצמותיהם פצחו ופרשו כאשר בסיר וכבשר בתוך קלחת, Et ossa eorum ruperunt, et fregerunt tanquam in olla, et sicut carnem in medio lebetis. Ex quibus constat, Verbum פרס (vel פרש per Sin Semol, quod idem est) etiam de fractione ossium usurpari. Inde igitur nemo est, qui non colligat, פרס peres esse ossifragam, hoc est, illam aquilae speciem, quae a frangendis ossibus Romana lingua ossifraga, vel ossifragus appellatur: et Arabica vel simpliciter كاسر id est, fractor, vel كاسر اعظام fractor ossium (1). Itaque Iunius ossifragam recte reddidit, quamvis illum latuerit hujus versionis vera ratio. Et fortasse eodem pertinet, quod pro פרס Onkelos et Hierosolymitanus ha-bent ער, vel עריא. Quippe voces ער et עריא, inversis radicalibus, deduci potuere ex verbis רעע, vel רעה, quae frangere et conterere significant. De ossifraga sic Lucretius lib. 5. versu 1077.

II, 772 Having thus set out the opinions of others, we shall here also present our own and try to support it with suitable arguments. First, I side with those who hold that פרס peres is a species of eagle. Among them are the Greeks, Jerome, the Chaldeans, the Arabs and most of the more recent writers. In favour of this is the fact that among the Macedonians, who enriched from the spoils of the East not only their wealth but also their language, the eagle was called παραὸς [paraos], in manifest allusion to the Hebrew פרס peres. Hesychius: Παραός· ἀετὸς ὑπὸ Μακεδόνων· [Paraos; aetos hypo Makedonōn;] Paraos, the eagle among the Macedonians. Moreover, just as by universal agreement נשר denotes the true eagle, so it is very probable that the words immediately following in Moses, namely פרס and עזניה, designate other species of eagles known to the Jews. Nor is it safe to oppose so many interpreters who hold this view. Let it stand firm, then, that פרס is among the species of eagles. But now we must inquire into the species, which can be extracted from nothing else than the origin of the word, since apart from the bare name we have nothing whatever about it. And Aben Ezra [=Abraham ibn Ezra] rightly col. 187 notes that the origin is to be taken from these words of Es. LVIII, 7: פרוס לרעב לחמך Break your bread for the hungry. By this note he meant to indicate that it is a kind of eagle so called from breaking. But to make the matter clearer, consider, reader, these words of Mic 3:3, which it is surprising have not yet been cited in this discussion: ואת עצמותיהם פצחו ופרשו כאשר בסיר וכבשר בתוך קלחת, And they broke their bones and shattered them as in a pot, and as flesh in the midst of a cauldron. From this it is evident that the verb פרס (or פרש with Sin Semol, which is the same) is also used of the breaking of bones. From this, then, there is no one who would not conclude that פרס peres is the ossifrage, that is, that species of eagle which from breaking bones is called in the Roman tongue ossifraga or ossifragus [bone-breaker], and in Arabic either simply كاسر [kāsir], that is, breaker, or كاسر اعظام [kāsir aʿẓām] breaker of bones R1. Thus Junius rightly rendered it ossifrage, although the true reason for this rendering escaped him. And perhaps it bears on the same point that for פרס Onkelos [=Targum Onkelos] and the Jerusalem Targum have ער or עריא. For the words ער and עריא, with the radicals inverted, could have been derived from the verbs רעע or רעה, which mean to break and to crush. Of the ossifrage Lucretius writes thus, book 5, line 1077: ¶

Postremo genus alituum, variaeque volucres, Accipitres, atque ossifragae. — — —

Lastly the race of winged things, and the various birds, hawks and ossifrages — — — ¶

And Pliny uses it throughout, as in book 10, ch. 3, where he speaks of the haliaeetus: That which is born from them has its kind among the ossifrages. And a little later: Some add a kind of eagle which they call the bearded eagle; the Tuscans, however, call it the ossifrage. And again, where he speaks of the chicks II, 773 that eagles throw out because they are weary of feeding them: When they have been thrown out by these, the related kind of the ossifrage takes them up and rears them with its own. And in ch. 7: Some think that the immussulus is the chick of the vulture, and the sanqualis that of the ossifrage. Likewise Massurius says that the sanqualis is the ossifrage. And in book 30, ch. 7: The ossifrage has a single intestine of wonderful nature, which digests everything it has swallowed. Ossifragus is rendered in Greek in one Glossary as ὀστοκόπος [ostokopos], ὀστοκλάστης [ostoklastēs], ὀστοκατεάκτης [ostokateaktēs]; in another, but wrongly, as ὀστοκόραξ [ostokorax]; in Dioscorides as φίνις [phinis].
