III, 177 Chapter VII. col. 395 ¶

Probatur, צבוע tseboa esse Serpentem varium, quem Hyaenam vocarunt Graeci et Aegyptii.

It is proved that צבוע tseboa is a spotted serpent, which the Greeks and the Egyptians called hyaena. ¶

Vallem Seboim, de qua mentio 1 Sam. XIII, 18., nomen habere vult Iunius ab urbe Benjamitica SEBOIM, Nehem. XI, 34. Sed, cum illa vallis Hebraice dicatur גי הצבעים, cum Articulo praefixo voci Seboim, constat, ibi Seboim nomen esse Appellativum. Proinde Hebraei omnes, R. Selomo, Kimchius, et Esaias in Notis in hunc locum, R. Mardochaeus in Concordantiis, Baal Aruch, Pomarius, et Aquinas in Lexicis suis voce SEBOIM serpentis genus intelligunt. Et praeiverat Ionathan in Chaldaea paraphrasi, ubi, pro valle Seboim, reperias חילת אפעיא, vallem viperarum (sic enim illi accipiunt). Quale est, quod in tractatu Talmudico Bava Kama cap. 1. fol. 16. a. אמר רב יוסף צבוע הוא אפה Dixit Rabbi Ioseph, TSEBOA est vipera. Ibi enim אפה idem est, quod אפעה, elisa Gutturali, ut observatum a Kimchio. Nolim tamen intelligi viperam, proprie dictam, sed maculosi serpentis genus: cujusmodi in Arabum scriptis non pauci describuntur. Sic in Avicenna Tom. 2. pag. 139. الحية الرقشا ذات الالوان المتخلفة Serpens raksa, qui variorum est colorum. In Damire, الارقم الحية التي فيها بياض وسواد كانه رقم اي نقش Arkamo, serpens, albo et nigro distinctus, tanquam Phrygium opus, seu interpunctum. In Giauhari, الحريش نوع من الحيات ارقط Alharis, varii serpentis species. Denique in Alcamus, السف الارقم من الحيات والتي تطير Assuph (vel assiph) serpens varius, et qui volat. Est et in hoc censu Graecorum cenchris, seu cenchrites, Arabice جاورسية giavversia, de quo Nicander Theriacῶn versu 463.

The valley of Seboim, mentioned in 1 Sam 13:18, Junius [=Franciscus Junius, of the Tremellius-Junius Latin Bible] would have take its name from the Benjamite city SEBOIM, Neh 11:34. But since that valley is called in Hebrew גי הצבעים, with the article prefixed to the word Seboim, it is clear that Seboim is there a common noun. Accordingly all the Hebrews, R. Selomo [=Rashi (R. Solomon ben Isaac)], Kimchi [=David Kimḥi (Radak), Sefer ha-Shorashim], and Esaias in their Notes on this passage, R. Mardochaeus in his Concordances, and Baal Aruch [=the ʿArukh of Nathan ben Jehiel of Rome] [=Nathan ben Jehiel of Rome, the ʿArukh], Pomarius [=David de Pomis, Ṣemaḥ David (Venice 1587)] and Aquinas [=Philippe d'Aquin, Dictionarium hebraeo-chaldaeo-talmudico-rabbinicum (Paris 1629)] in their Lexicons, understand by the word SEBOIM a kind of serpent. And Jonathan [=Targum Jonathan] had led the way in the Chaldee paraphrase, where in place of the valley of Seboim you find חילת אפעיא, the valley of vipers (for so they take it). Similar is the passage in the Talmudic tractate Bava Kama, ch. 1, fol. 16a: אמר רב יוסף צבוע הוא אפה, Rabbi Joseph said: TSEBOA is a viper. For there אפה is the same as אפעה, with the guttural elided, as Kimchi observed. Yet I would not have it understood as a viper in the proper sense, but as a kind of spotted serpent, of which not a few are described in the writings of the Arabs. Thus in Avicenna [=Ibn Sīnā, al-Qānūn fī l-ṭibb (the Canon)], vol. 2, p. 139: الحية الرقشا ذات الالوان المتخلفة [al-ḥayya al-raqshā dhāt al-alwān al-mutakhallifa], the serpent raksa, which is of varied colours. In Damir [=al-Damīrī, Ḥayāt al-ḥayawān]: الارقم الحية التي فيها بياض وسواد كانه رقم اي نقش [al-arqam al-ḥayya allatī fīhā bayāḍ wa-sawād ka-annahu raqm ay naqsh], Arkamo, a serpent marked with white and black, like Phrygian work, or embroidery. In Giauhari [=al-Jawharī, al-Ṣiḥāḥ]: الحريش نوع من الحيات ارقط [al-ḥarīsh nawʿ min al-ḥayyāt arqaṭ], Alharis, a species of spotted serpent. Finally in the Alcamus [=al-Qāmūs al-muḥīṭ, the Arabic dictionary of al-Fīrūzābādī]: السف الارقم من الحيات والتي تطير [al-saff al-arqam min al-ḥayyāt wa-allatī taṭīr], Assuph (or assiph), a spotted serpent, and one that flies. In this class too is the cenchris or cenchrites of the Greeks, in Arabic جاورسية [jāwarsiyya] giavversia, of which Nicander, Theriaca, line 463: ¶

Δήεις κεγχρίνου δολιχὸν τέρας, ὅν τε λέοντα
Αἴολον αὐδάξαντο, περίστικτον φολίδεσσι.

Δήεις κεγχρίνου δολιχὸν τέρας, ὅν τε λέοντα Αἴολον αὐδάξαντο, περίστικτον φολίδεσσι. [Dēeis kenchrinou dolichon teras, hon te leonta Aiolon audaxanto, peristikton pholidessi.] ¶

Reperies (in Lemno, Samothracia, etc.) cenchritem, monstrum oblongum, quem leonem varium appellant, squamis distinctum. Et Lucanus lib. 9. versu 716.

You will find (in Lemnos, Samothrace, etc.) the cenchrites, a long monster, which they call the spotted lion, marked with scales. And Lucan, book 9, line 716: ¶

Pluribus ille notis variatam tingitur alvum,
Quam parvis tinctus maculis Thebanus ophites. col. 396

His belly is dyed, varied with more marks than the Theban ophites is tinged with small spots. col. 396 ¶

Hujusmodi serpentem voce צבוע tseboa, significari, suadet primo, quod צבוע tseboa proprie tinctum, aut pictum sonat. Secundo, quod illum Hebraei varium esse asserunt. Kimchius enim in Notis sic nominari docet; לפי שעשוי בגוונים ובמיני הצבוענים, quia coloribus et picturis distinctus est. Et Pomarius, מין III, 178 נחשים ונקראו כן על השתנות הצבעונים, Species serpentum, qui sic vocantur propter colorum varietatem. Et multo ante Beresith Rabba, הצבוע הזה מטפה של לובן הוא ויש לו שס״ה מיני צבועים כמנין ימות השנה של חמה, Serpens, nomine tseboa, ex albi seminis guttula constat. Et tamen colores habet trecentos sexaginta quinque: quot nimirum sunt solaris anni dies. Ex qua insigni hyperbole hoc saltem elicimus, serpentes varios significari voce TSEBOIM. De eodem Baal Aruch in voce ברדלס bardales, ברדלס זו אפא וז׳ שנים הראשונים שהוא זכר נקרא צבוע וכשמתהפך לנקבה נקראת נפרזא ורעה היא יותר מן הזכר Bardales est vipera, quae primis septem annis, dum est mas, appellatur tseboa. Sed, cum mutatur in foeminam, vocatur niphreza, et est mare multo pejor. Bar- dales et niphreza deflexa videntur ex πάρδαλις et νέβρισσα. Huic serpenti utrumque nomen ab Hellenistis inditum propter varios colores. Variam enim esse πάρδαλιν, nemo nescit. Ita etiam νεβρὶς, id est, hinnuli pellis, aut cer- vi. Proinde cervi passim a poetis στικτοὶ vocantur, et κατάστικτοι, id est, varii et maculis distincti. Sic, in Electra Sophoclis, Agamemnon in Dianae luco interfecisse legitur στικτὸν κεράστην ἔλαφον, varium, cornutum cervum. Et cervorum genus describit Oppianus libro 2. Cynegeticῶν,

That a serpent of this kind is meant by the word צבוע tseboa is suggested, first, by the fact that צבוע tseboa properly means dyed or painted. Second, by the fact that the Hebrews assert that it is spotted. For Kimchi teaches in his Notes that it is so named לפי שעשוי בגוונים ובמיני הצבוענים, because it is marked with colours and patterns. And Pomarius: מין III, 178 נחשים ונקראו כן על השתנות הצבעונים, a species of serpents, which are so called on account of the variety of their colours. And much earlier Bereshith Rabba: הצבוע הזה מטפה של לובן הוא ויש לו שס״ה מיני צבועים כמנין ימות השנה של חמה, The serpent called tseboa comes from a little drop of white seed. And yet it has three hundred and sixty-five colours: as many, namely, as there are days in the solar year. From this remarkable hyperbole we at least gather that spotted serpents are meant by the word TSEBOIM. Of the same Baal Aruch, under the word ברדלס bardales: ברדלס זו אפא וז׳ שנים הראשונים שהוא זכר נקרא צבוע וכשמתהפך לנקבה נקראת נפרזא ורעה היא יותר מן הזכר, Bardales is a viper which for the first seven years, while it is male, is called tseboa. But when it changes into a female it is called niphreza, and is much worse than the male. Bardales and niphreza seem to be derived from πάρδαλις [pardalis] and νέβρισσα [nebrissa]. Both names were given to this serpent by the Hellenists on account of its varied colours. For no one is unaware that the πάρδαλιν [pardalin] is spotted. So too the νεβρὶς [nebris], that is, the skin of a fawn or of a deer. Accordingly deer are everywhere called by the poets στικτοὶ [stiktoi] and κατάστικτοι [katastiktoi], that is, spotted and marked with spots. Thus in the Electra of Sophocles we read that Agamemnon killed in the grove of Diana στικτὸν κεράστην ἔλαφον [stikton kerastēn elaphon], a spotted, horned deer. And Oppian describes a kind of deer in book 2 of the Cynegetica: ¶

Στικτὸν, ἀρίζηλον, ποταμηπόρον, ὑψικάρηνον,

Στικτὸν, ἀρίζηλον, ποταμηπόρον, ὑψικάρηνον, [Stikton, arizēlon, potamēporon, hypsikarēnon,] ¶

Varium, eximium, flumini-gradum, capite arduum. Et, apud Nonnum Dio- nysiacῶn lib. 4. Actaeone in cervum mutato, canes ejus

Spotted, conspicuous, river-crossing, high-headed. And in Nonnus, Dionysiaca, book 4, when Actaeon has been changed into a deer, his dogs ¶

Στικτὸν ἐθοινήσαντο νόθον δέμας ἄφρονι λύσσῃ,

Στικτὸν ἐθοινήσαντο νόθον δέμας ἄφρονι λύσσῃ, [Stikton ethoinēsanto nothon demas aphroni lyssē,] ¶

Varium adulterinum corpus insana rabie devorabant. Unde Pollux, Ἔστι δὲ τοῖς ἐλάφοις τὸ χρῶμα ἐπίξανθον, κατάστικτον λευκοῖς γράμμασι· πλείω δὲ αἱ θήλειαι τὰ στίγματα ἔχουσι, καὶ μάλιστα τῶν ἐλάφων οἱ νεβροί. Cervorum color subflavus est, albis lineis distinctus; foeminae vero plures habent maculas, et hinnuli inprimis. Itaque a vocibus νέβρος et νεβρὶς serpens tseboa propter maculas primo νέβρισσα (ut Hispaniae quoddam oppidum): deinde, corrupta voce, נפרזא nephriza, vel (ut hodie efferunt) nephreza est appellatus. Atque eodem recidunt tria haec ejusdem serpentis nomina צבוע tseboa, ברדלס bardales, seu πάρδαλις· et נפרזא nephriza, seu νέβρισσα. Illis enim omnibus hoc commune est, quod maculosum serpentem sonant. Sed nomini צבוע tseboa aliquid est peculiare, quod jam notandum. Proprie צבוע tseboa Hebraeis est hyaena, etiam ipsa a maculis ita dicta. Hyaenae enim

“They devoured the spotted, counterfeit body with insane frenzy.” Hence Pollux: Ἔστι δὲ τοῖς ἐλάφοις τὸ χρῶμα ἐπίξανθον, κατάστικτον λευκοῖς γράμμασι· πλείω δὲ αἱ θήλειαι τὰ στίγματα ἔχουσι, καὶ μάλιστα τῶν ἐλάφων οἱ νεβροί. [Esti de tois elaphois to chrōma epixanthon, katastikton leukois grammasi; pleiō de hai thēleiai ta stigmata echousi, kai malista tōn elaphōn hoi nebroi.] “The colour of deer is yellowish, marked with white lines; the females have more spots, and the fawns above all.” Thus from the words νέβρος [nebros] and νεβρὶς [nebris] the serpent tseboa, because of its spots, was first called νέβρισσα [nebrissa] (like a certain town of Spain); then, the word being corrupted, נפרזא nephriza, or (as they pronounce it today) nephreza. And so these three names of the same serpent come to the same thing: צבוע tseboa; ברדלס bardales, or πάρδαλις [pardalis]; and נפרזא nephriza, or νέβρισσα [nebrissa]. For all of them have this in common, that they mean “spotted serpent.” But the name צבוע tseboa has something peculiar to it, which must now be noted. Properly, צבוע tseboa among the Hebrews is the hyaena, itself also so called from its spots. For the hyaena’s ¶

— — — ἔῤῥαπται δέμας αἰνὸν
Κυανέαις ἑκάτερθεν ἐπίτριμα ταινίαισιν·

— — — ἔῤῥαπται δέμας αἰνὸν Κυανέαις ἑκάτερθεν ἐπίτριμα ταινίαισιν· [errhaptai demas ainon / Kyaneais hekaterthen epitrima tainiaisin] ¶

— — — cutis horrida corpore toto Coeruleis dense conspergitur undique vittis. III, 179 Ita Oppianus lib. 3. Cynegeticῶn versu 274. Qui proinde στικτὰς, varias paullo infra nominat. Atque, ut tseboa serpens sexum alternare creditur, ita etiam tseboa quadrupes, id est, hyaena. Quod cum multis probaverim in capite De hyaena a), hic sufficiet ejusdem Oppiani verba proferre,

— — — “grim hide over the whole body is thickly sprinkled on every side with dark-blue stripes.” III, 179 So Oppian, Cynegetica book 3, line 274. Accordingly, a little further on, he calls them στικτὰς [stiktas], spotted. And just as the serpent tseboa is believed to alternate its sex, so too is the quadruped tseboa, that is, the hyaena. Since I have proved this with many arguments in the chapter On the hyaena a), it will suffice here to cite the words of the same Oppian: ¶

Θαῦμα δὲ καὶ τὸ δ᾽ ἄκουσα περὶ στικτῆσιν ὑαίνης,
Ἄρσενα καὶ θήλειαν ἀμείβεσθαι λυκάβαντι·

Θαῦμα δὲ καὶ τὸ δ᾽ ἄκουσα περὶ στικτῆσιν ὑαίνης, Ἄρσενα καὶ θήλειαν ἀμείβεσθαι λυκάβαντι· [Thauma de kai to d’ akousa peri stiktēsin hyainēs, / Arsena kai thēleian ameibesthai lykabanti] ¶

Miraculum etiam hoc audivi de pictis hyaenis, marem et foeminam alternare quotannis. Merito igitur tseboa, id est, hyaena, vocatur serpens, qui utrumque hoc cum hyaena commune habet, pellem variam et sexus alternationem, ab col. 397 Hebraeis supra observatam. Et iisdem de causis hic idem serpens Graecis et Aegyptiis hyaena dictus. Gesnerus de quadrupedibus in Hyaena pag. 556. Aelianus et Orus in Hieroglyphicis hyaenam, non quadrupedem, sed serpentem, per vices marem et foeminam esse prodiderunt. Nempe in Aeliano legerat, Historiae lib. 1. cap. 25. Τὴν ὕαιναν ὄφιν τῆτες μὲν ἄῤῥενα εἰ θεάσαιο, τὴν αὐτὴν ἐς νέω-τα ὄψει θῆλυν δέ· Hyaenam serpentem hoc anno, si marem videris, illam ipsam anno altero foeminam videbis. Et in Horo Hieroglyphicῶn libri 2. cap. 65. Ὕαι-ναν ὄφιν ζωγραφοῦσιν· αὕτη γὰρ ὅτε μὲν ἄῤῥην γίνεται, ὅτε δὲ θήλεια· Hyaenam serpentem pingunt (ut hominem parum constantem designent): haec enim modo mas est, modo foemina. Ita habebant libri, quos Gesnerus consulerat. At in nostris editionibus vox ὄφιν utroque loco desideratur; quia illam editores tanquam superfluam induxerunt. Quod malo exemplo videtur et praeter rationem esse factum: cum, praeter Graecos et Aegyptios, Hebraei quoque צבעים tseboim, id est hyaenas, inter serpentes censeant, et illis eandem tribuant sexuum vicissitudinem. In Horo igitur et Aeliano vox ὄφις, delata perperam, est omnino restituenda: et Hebraeorum Lexicis addenda vox צבוע tseboa, quae et hyaenam quadrupedem, et hyaenam serpentem sonat. Quamvis pro fabuloso habeam, quod Hebraei, Graeci et Aegyptii vel hanc, vel illam sexum per vices mutare scribunt *).

“This marvel too I have heard about the spotted hyaenas: that male and female alternate every year.” Rightly, then, the serpent is called tseboa, that is, hyaena, since it has both these things in common with the hyaena: a spotted skin and the alternation of sex, observed above by the col. 397 Hebrews. And for the same reasons this same serpent was called hyaena by the Greeks and the Egyptians. Gesner [=Conrad Gesner, Historia animalium], on quadrupeds, under Hyaena, p. 556: “Aelian and Horus in the Hieroglyphics have reported that the hyaena, not the quadruped but the serpent, is by turns male and female.” He had evidently read in Aelian, Historia book 1, ch. 25: Τὴν ὕαιναν ὄφιν τῆτες μὲν ἄῤῥενα εἰ θεάσαιο, τὴν αὐτὴν ἐς νέωτα ὄψει θῆλυν δέ· [Tēn hyainan ophin tētes men arrhena ei theasaio, tēn autēn es neōta opsei thēlyn de] “If this year you see the hyaena serpent as a male, next year you will see the very same one as a female.” And in Horus, Hieroglyphica book 2, ch. 65: Ὕαιναν ὄφιν ζωγραφοῦσιν· αὕτη γὰρ ὅτε μὲν ἄῤῥην γίνεται, ὅτε δὲ θήλεια· [Hyainan ophin zōgraphousin; hautē gar hote men arrhēn ginetai, hote de thēleia] “They paint a hyaena serpent (to signify an inconstant man): for it is now male, now female.” So read the books that Gesner had consulted. But in our editions the word ὄφιν [ophin] is missing in both places, because the editors struck it out as superfluous. This seems to have been done on a bad precedent and without reason, since, besides the Greeks and Egyptians, the Hebrews too reckon צבעים tseboim, that is, hyaenas, among serpents, and ascribe to them the same alternation of sexes. In Horus and Aelian, therefore, the word ὄφις [ophis], wrongly deleted, must certainly be restored; and to the Hebrew Lexicons must be added the word צבוע tseboa, which means both the quadruped hyaena and the serpent hyaena. For my part, though, I regard as fable what the Hebrews, Greeks and Egyptians write, that either the one or the other changes its sex by turns *). ¶

For after relating that fable about the change of sex from Aelian and Horus, he adds: But more authors write this of the four-footed hyaena. And it is indeed uncertain whether there is any such kind of serpent, nor is it even likely that such a change of sex could be detected in it. Indeed, I cannot wonder enough that Bochart attributes so much to the futile inventions of the Rabbis that, on their authority alone, he bids the word צבוע enter the lexicons also as the name of a serpent.
