II, 463 ON THE ANIMALS OF SCRIPTURE BOOK FOUR ON THE OVIPAROUS QUADRUPEDS. col. 1043 ¶

CAP. I.

CHAPTER I. ¶

Probatur, צב tsab Levit. XI, 29. esse Crocodilum terrestrem, de quo ex Arabum monumentis multa afferuntur, nondum vulgata.

It is proved that צב tsab in Lev 11:29 is the land crocodile, concerning which much is brought forward from the records of the Arabs that has not yet been published. ¶

Post quadrupedes viviparas sequentur oviparae, quae et in natura paucae sunt, et in Scriptura pauciores. Harum sex species a Mose recensentur Levit. XI, 29. 30. nempe צב tsab, אנקה anaka, כח coach, לטאה letaa, חמט chomet, תנשמת thinsemeth. Septima videbitur addi posse, שממית semamith, ex Prov. XXX, 28. Sed, semamith ab anaka solo nomine differre, suo loco docebimus. Et octava, לויתן leviathan, qui palam est crocodilus, Iob. XL et XLI. Sed crocodilum maluimus in aquaticis describere; quia ἀμφίβιον est animal. Testudo col. 1044 denique hic locum habebit, si Seniores sequimur Os. XII, 12. Oviparae quadrupedes, quas ex Mose recensuimus, ad lacertorum species aliquo modo pertinent, et ita sunt inter se similes, ut eas certis notis ab invicem distinguere non sit cujusvis. Nobis certe fuit valde operosum; quia hic ab Hebraeis prorsus destituimur. Neque ex aliis Scripturae interpretibus multo plus fuit subsidii, qui in tenebris plerique palpant, cum ad has minutias deveniunt: sed longe plus affulsit lucis ex monumentis Arabum, in quorum solo hae pestes nascuntur et edu- col. 1045 cantur. Quod sequentia palam facient. In his singula verba explicare conabimur, illo ipso ordine, quo a Mose proponuntur.

After the viviparous quadrupeds follow the oviparous, which are few in nature and fewer still in Scripture. Moses lists six species of them in Lev 11:29–30., namely צב tsab, אנקה anaka, כח coach, לטאה letaa, חמט chomet, תנשמת thinsemeth. It will seem that a seventh can be added, שממית semamith, from Prov 30:28. But we shall show in its place that semamith differs from anaka in name only. And an eighth, לויתן leviathan, which is plainly the crocodile, Iob. XL et XLI. But we have preferred to describe the crocodile among the aquatic animals, because it is an ἀμφίβιον [amphibion] animal. Finally, the tortoise col. 1044 will have a place here, if we follow the Elders at Hos 12:12. The oviparous quadrupeds that we have listed from Moses belong in some way to the species of lizards, and are so similar to one another that it is not for just anyone to distinguish them from each other by definite marks. For us it was certainly very laborious, because here the Hebrews leave us entirely destitute. Nor was there much more help from the other interpreters of Scripture, most of whom grope in the dark when they come to these minutiae. But far more light has shone from the records of the Arabs, in whose soil these pests are born and reared. col. 1045 What follows will make this plain. In it we shall try to explain the individual words in the very order in which Moses sets them out. ¶

II, 464 Lev 11:29. For Jerome the Hebrew צב tsab is the crocodile; for the Greeks ὁ κροκόδειλος ὁ χερσαῖος [ho krokodeilos ho chersaios]. But among more recent interpreters some render it toad or bramble-toad [rubeta], others tortoise; Castalio [=Sebastian Castellio, Latin Bible (1551)], however, "kinds of shellfish." The first group, if I am not mistaken, is led by R. Selomo [=Rashi (R. Solomon ben Isaac)], a French Jew, who wrote about 550 years ago. For he was the first, so far as I know, to write that צב tsab is an animal similar to a frog, called פויוט. But one should read פרוט frot, the German name for the toad, then in use in France. To this I refer the fact that Kimchi [=David Kimḥi (Radak), Sefer ha-Shorashim] renders צב tsab in Spanish as גריישנט graissant. At that time, namely, graissant was for the Spaniards the bramble-toad, as graisset and croisset for the French, and Grasfrosch for the Germans, literally "grass frog." Following these, Pomarius [=David de Pomis, Ṣemaḥ David (Venice 1587)], Aquinas [=Philippe d'Aquin, Dictionarium hebraeo-chaldaeo-talmudico-rabbinicum (Paris 1629)], Munster, Pagninus [=Santes Pagnino, Latin Bible (1528) and Thesaurus linguae sanctae], Arias [=Benito Arias Montano, the interlinear Latin of the Antwerp Polyglot], and Robert Olivetan interpret צב tsab as toad, or bramble-toad, or frog, or שרץ שדומה לצפרדע, "a creeping thing similar to a frog." They thought, namely, that this animal was so called from swelling, because צבה means to swell; just as from bufo [toad] the vernacular word bouffi was formed, which means swollen. Add that in Illyrian zaba is frog, as if the word were derived from צב tsab. But Elias [=Elijah Levita (1469–1549), author of the Aramaic lexicon Meturgeman and of Tishbi], easily the most learned of the Jews in Germany, explains צב tsab in his language as שלטפרוט schildfrot, that is, tortoise, because the shells of tortoises seem to resemble the roofs of covered wagons, which in Hebrew are called צבים tsabbim. So slight a reason had such force that it drew to assent Junius [=Franciscus Junius, of the Tremellius-Junius Latin Bible], Tremellius [=Immanuel Tremellius, of the Tremellius-Junius Latin Bible], Schindler, and the English, Italian and Genevan translators. Buxtorf [=Johannes Buxtorf the Elder, Synagoga Judaica and Lexicon Chaldaicum Talmudicum et Rabbinicum], in his Lexicon, not quite knowing which side to join, renders צב tsab both as toad and as tortoise. ¶

In hac tantorum virorum haesitatione, addo etiam hallucinatione, quod illorum pace dixerim, erudito lectori spero me facturum rem pergratam, si veram et genuinam vocis significationem, non jam ex conjectura, sed certis et solidis rationibus adstruam. Id vero, vel me tacente, nullo negotio praestabit, quisquis ea perpendet, quae de hoc animali scribunt Arabes. Quibus non potest non esse notissimum, quia in Arabia frequentius est, quam ullibi terrarum. At illis quam notum est animal, tam nobis est ignotum. Proinde necesse habeo, haec accuratius persequi.

Amid this hesitation of such great men, I will add also this hallucination, if I may say so with their leave, I hope I shall do the learned reader a most welcome service if I establish the true and genuine meaning of the word, not now from conjecture, but by certain and solid reasons. Indeed, even if I said nothing, anyone who weighs what the Arabs write about this animal will accomplish this without any trouble. To them it cannot but be very well known, because it is more common in Arabia than anywhere else on earth. But as well known as the animal is to them, so unknown is it to us. Therefore I find it necessary to pursue these matters more carefully. ¶

To begin with the name: it should be known that, like the Hebrew צב, the Arabic ضب [ḍabb] is written with the same letters. But, as the dialects vary, the Arabic Tsade at the beginning of a word is pronounced as d or dh, because of the dot added to it. Thus this little word is pronounced ضب [ḍabb] dhab among the Arabs. The plural is ضباب [ḍibāb] dhibab, or أضب [aḍubb] adhab, and the feminine ضبة [ḍabba] dhabba. Hence ضبب [ḍabab] dhabab, and مضب [maḍabb] mudhab, and مضبب [muḍbib] mudhbib denote a place full of these animals; and مضبب [muḍabbib] mudhabbib their hunter. Further, ضب [ḍabb] dhab is defined by Damir [=al-Damīrī, Ḥayāt al-ḥayawān] as حيوان بري معروف من الحشرات يشبه الورل [ḥayawān barrī maʿrūf min al-ḥasharāt yushbih al-waral] a well-known wild animal among the reptiles, similar to the guaril, or greater stellio: just as Avicenna [=Ibn Sīnā, al-Qānūn fī l-ṭibb (the Canon)] writes that it differs from the guaril, وان كان [wa-in kāna] II, 465 يشبهه وكان قريب الاحوال والقوي منه [yushbihuhu wa-kāna qarīb al-aḥwāl wa-al-quwā minhu] although it is similar to it, and very close to it both in constitution and in powers. So he writes in the second book of the Canon, in the chapter ضب [ḍabb] dhab, or tsab, if you prefer. And in the chapter حردون [ḥardūn] chardaun, col. 1046 which was entirely omitted by the Latin translator: هو الضب وطبعه قريب من طبع الورل وهو اشبه الوزغ بما يتعزي به [huwa al-ḍabb wa-ṭabʿuhu qarīb min ṭabʿ al-waral wa-huwa ashbah al-wazagh bi-mā yataʿazzī bihi] Chardaun is the same as dhab: its nature comes close to the guaril, and its attributes are the same as those of the guezga. And in the chapter ورل [waral] guaril, هو غير الضب والضب لا يكون او قل ما يكون الا في البادية وراسه وبدنه وذنبه خالف من الورل وربما قريب في طبايعه [huwa ghayr al-ḍabb wa-al-ḍabb lā yakūn aw qalla mā yakūn illā fī al-bādiya wa-raʾsuhu wa-badanuhu wa-dhanabuhu khālif min al-waral wa-rubbamā qarīb fī ṭabāyiʿihi] The guaril is different from the dhab. For the dhab is not found, or only rarely, anywhere but in the desert. And it differs from the guaril in head, trunk and tail, although it is close to it in nature. And Abdollatif of Baghdad: الورل والضب والحربا وشحمة الارض والوزغ كلها متناسبة في الخلق [al-waral wa-al-ḍabb wa-al-ḥirbā wa-shaḥmat al-arḍ wa-al-wazagh kulluhā mutanāsiba fī al-khalq] The guaril, and the dhab, and the hirba, and the fat of the earth, and the guezga all share the same nature. These, namely, are so many species of lizards, of which we shall speak below. Therefore ضب [ḍabb] dhab, or צב tsab, which is the same, is neither a toad, nor a bramble-toad, nor a tortoise, nor a shelled animal, but belongs to the lizard kind. Now, so that it may be known more fully, we shall say what the Critics, Philosophers, Theologians, Magicians and Physicians of the Arabs have written about it, most of which has not yet been published. ¶

The Critics, then, teach that it is an animal πολυώνυμον [polyōnymon], with many names. For besides ضب [ḍabb] dhab, or צב tsab, the common name, it is also called حردون [ḥardūn] chardaun (the Chaldeans write חרדונא chara[?], the Syrians ܚܕܪܘܢܐ [חדרונא] chadrona, and the Samaritans [?] adrona, as will be shown below). It is likewise called برق [barq] bork, and علين [ʿlyn] alin, and هيقل [hayqal] haikal. And, if it is fat, سبحل [?] or سحبل [siḥbal] sichbal: if old, قرسم [qirsim] kirsem; if of bad disposition, ضغطار [ḍighṭār] dhiphtar: just as the female is commonly ضبة [ḍabba] dhabba; but, when fat, حيكانا [ḥaykānā] chaicana; when with young, مكون [makūn] machun, and ممكن [mumkin] mamchin. And حلم [ḥalama] halama properly means to take out that fat, just as عكد [ʿakida] achada, to be fat, is used of the dhab ضب [ḍabb] alone. Et Ibno-chalvia, يعيش سبع ماية فصاعدا Vivit annos septingentos, et amplius. Quod ab illis confictum puto, qui de magnis majora loquuntur. Sed et falsum omnino est, et ex chamaeleontis historia perperam huc translatum, quod ali halitu suo et frigido aëre vesci traditur. Est enim animal pene παμφάγον, quodque radicibus potissimum fructibusque vescitur. Hinc Alcamus, postquam خبلة chobla vocari scripsit vitis radices, et fructus spinosarum arborum, addit ضب خابل ياكلها dhab chabil est dhab, qui illis vescitur. Et foemina, a scorpione puncta, اكلت من حشيشة تسمي اذان الفار يزول عنها الم اللسع edit herbam, quam vocant auriculam muris, et remittitur puncturae dolor. Ita est in Alkazuinio. Sed et, auctore ipso Damire (ut pluribus infra dicetur), dhab ad vomitum redit ut canis, et proprios catulos vorat. Illum igitur solo aëre vesci, merum commentum est. Hoc commune habet cum jarboa, seu choerogryllio, quod in petrosis et arduis locis latibulum sibi deligit. Alkazuinius, من كيسه انه لا يتخذ البيت الا في موضع صلب ليلا ينهدر عليه في حوافر الدواب ولما علم انه نساء لا يتخذ البيت الي عند الاكمته او صخرة عظيمة او شجرة ليستدل بها علي بيته اذا عاب ويتباعد عن col. 1049 بيته E solertia ejus est, quod domum sibi non sumit, nisi in loco duro, ne jumentorum ungulis obtrita in se corruat. Sed et quia se novit esse valde obliviosum, eam in sublimi colle, aut in celsa rupe deligit, aut ad arborem, quam pro II, 468 indice habeat, cum ab illa abest, aut procul excedit. Damir etiam لا يتخذ حجره الا في كدية حجر خوفا من السيل والحافر ولذلك براثنه ناقصة كليلة لحفره بها في الاماكن الصلبة Neque latibulum alibi habet, quam in petris durissimis; quia ab inundationibus et fossoribus sibi timet. Ac proinde ungues illi detriti et obtusi sunt, ut qui lapidosa loca iis fodiat. Item, وفي طبعه النسيان وعدم الهداية وبه يضرب المثل في الحيرة ولذلك يحفر جحره عند الاكمة او صخرة ليلا يضل عنه اذا خرج لطلب والطعم Naturalis ipsis est oblivio, et ἀβλε-ψία. Itaque stupor ejus in proverbium cessit. Proinde lustrum suum in colle fodit, aut in rupe, ne ab eo aberret, cum exit, ut victum sibi quaerat. Proverbium, de quo Damir, ab eodem sic citatur in titulo De Proverbiis, اضل من الضب Qui a via magis aberrat, quam dhab. Quia ضب dhab pravum est animal, unde vocatur ضغطار dhiphtar, ut jam observatum, ecce aliud proverbium in usu apud Arabes, quod me docuit Alcamus, اطعم اخاك كشية الضب حشة على المواسات Cibavit fratrem tuum pinguedine τοῦ dhab, id est, ad malum patrandum eum incitavit! Partum quod attinet: اذا ارادت الضبة ان تخرج بيضها حفرت في الارض حفرة ورمت فيها البيض وطمته بالتراب وتعاهده كل يوم حتي يخرج وذلك في اربعين يوما Cum dhabba (id est, foemina τοῦ dhab) ova sua positura est, scrobem in terra defodit, in quam ova conjecta pulvere obtegit, et quotidie visitat, donec excludantur, id est, usque ad diem quadragesimum. Sic Damir, Alkazuinium ex parte sequutus, cujus verba sunt, فاذا اراد ان يبيض حفر مثل ادحي النعام ويرمي فيها ثمانين بيضة ويدفنها col. 1050 في التراب وبيضها مثل بيض الحمام ويدعها اربعين يوما ثم ياتي وحسول قد خرجت منها فيتعدون فياكل منها ما قدرت Cum in eo est, ut ovisivet, fodit tanquam struthionis nidum, in quem ova octoginta, columbarum ovis similia, conjicit et in pulvere sepelit. Ad quae, per dies quadraginta relicta, cum revertitur, catuli, inde exeuntes, cursitant. Ex quibus ille comedit quantumcunque potest. Nempe, ut ait Damir, يوصف العقوق لانه ياكل حسوله describitur crudelitate insignis; quia proprios catulos vorat. Unde Poeta, II, 469 اكلت بنيك كاكل الضب حتي تركت بنيك ليس لهم غذي

But the philosophers report a good many notable things about the nature of the ضب [ḍabb] dhab. That its penis is forked, as we said, Damir teaches in these words: للضب نكران وللانثي فرجان كما للورل وللحردون [li-l-ḍabb nakrān wa-li-l-unthā farjān kamā li-l-waral wa-li-l-ḥardūn] The male dhab has two penises, and its female two vulvas, like the guaril and the chardaun. And Alcamus: للنزك نكر الضب والورل ولهم نزكان [li-l-nazk nakr al-ḍabb wa-l-waral wa-lahum nazkān] Nazach is the penis of the dhab and of the guaril, which have a double one (the lexicographers, not understanding these words, twisted them to another sense). Accordingly the priest Abuchanipha writes that the penis of the ضب [ḍabb] dhab is كلسان الحية اصل واحد له فرعان [ka-lisān al-ḥayya aṣl wāḥid lahu farʿān] like the tongue of a serpent, which, though born from one root, is nevertheless forked. It has a remarkably knotty tail, whence the proverb in Damir, اعقد من ذنب الضب [aʿqad min dhanab al-ḍabb] Knottier than the tail of the dhab. For they say there are حد وعشرون عقدة [ḥd wa-ʿishrūn ʿuqda] 21 knots in it, whereas the scorpion's tail, which is itself very knotty, has at most seven knots [?] joints. It is added that the same animal [?] has continuous [teeth], which therefore never fall out. [?] يقال ان اسنانه قطعة واحدة [yuqāl anna asnānahu qiṭʿa wāḥida]: [?] its teeth cohere into one. Alcamus: لا اتيك سن الحسل اي ابدا لان سنه لا تسقط [lā ātīka sinn al-ḥisl ay abadan li-anna sinnahu lā tasquṭ] The tooth of the chisel (that is, of the young of the dhab) will not come to you: that is, it will never come to you, because its teeth do not fall out. Here is another similar proverb about the same animal in Damir! لا افعله حتي يرد الضب لان الضب لا يرد [lā afʿaluhu ḥattā yarida al-ḍabb li-anna al-ḍabb lā yarid] I will not do this until the dhab goes down to the water (that is, I will never do it), because the dhab never goes down to the water. For it does not drink at all, and so it rarely urinates. The same: الضب لا يشرب الما [al-ḍabb lā yashrab al-mā] The dhab abstains entirely from drinking water. And a little later: يقال انه يبول في كل اربعين يوما مرة [yuqāl annahu yabūl fī kull arbaʿīn yawman marra] It is said to urinate only once every forty days, or قطرة [qaṭra] one little col. 1048 drop, as another copy has it. On its eyes, in the same place: الضب يخرج من II, 467 حجره كليل البصر فيجلوه بالتحدق في الشمس [al-ḍabb yakhruj min juḥrihi kalīl al-baṣar fa-yajlūhu bi-l-taḥadduq fī al-shams] The dhab comes out of its hiding place with rather dim sight; but when its pupil has been cleansed against the sun, it sees more clearly. Epiphanius, in the Sampsaeans, asserts that the σαῦραν ἡλιακὴν [sauran hēliakēn], the solar lizard, recovers its sight in the same way when it begins to grow dim: Ἐκείνη, he says, ἀμβλυωπήσασα χρόνῳ ἀναβλέπει, διὰ ἡλιακῆς στροφάλιγγος ἐν φωλεῷ πρὸς ἀνατολὴν προσέχοντι ἑαυτὴν προσθλίψασα, καὶ νηστεύσασα πρὸς ἀνατολὴν κατανοοῦσα· [Ekeinē amblyōpēsasa chronō anablepei, dia hēliakēs strophalingos en phōleō pros anatolēn prosechonti heautēn prosthlipsasa, kai nēsteusasa pros anatolēn katanoousa] When it has dulled sight, it recovers it some time later by turning toward the sun, pressing itself into a den facing the east, and, fasting, looking toward the East. Again Damir on the dhab: ويتغذي بالنسيم ويعيش بالبره الهوا وذلك عند الهرم وفنا الرطوبات ونقص الحرارت [wa-yataghadhdhā bi-l-nasīm wa-yaʿīsh bi-l-barh al-hawā wa-dhālika ʿind al-haram wa-fanā al-ruṭūbāt wa-naqṣ al-ḥarārāt] And it is nourished by breath and lives on cold air, and this until decrepit old age, and until the (radical) moisture is consumed and the heat fails. And below: وهو طويل العمر ومن هذه الجهة يناسب الحيات والافاعي [wa-huwa ṭawīl al-ʿumr wa-min hādhihi al-jiha yunāsib al-ḥayyāt wa-l-afāʿī] It is a long-lived animal, which it has in common with serpents and vipers. And Ibno-chalvia: يعيش سبع ماية فصاعدا [yaʿīsh sabʿ māya fa-ṣāʿidan] It lives seven hundred years and more. This I think was invented by those who say greater things about great matters. But it is also altogether false, and wrongly transferred here from the account of the chameleon, that it is said to be fed by its own breath and to live on cold air. For it is an almost παμφάγον [pamphagon] animal, feeding chiefly on roots and fruits. Hence Alcamus, after writing that خبلة [khabla] chobla is the name for the roots of the vine and the fruits of thorny trees, adds: ضب خابل ياكلها [ḍabb khābil yaʾkuluhā] dhab chabil is a dhab that feeds on them. And the female, when stung by a scorpion, اكلت من حشيشة تسمي اذان الفار يزول عنها الم اللسع [akalat min ḥashīsha tusammā ādhān al-fār yazūl ʿanhā alam al-lasʿ] eats a herb called mouse-ear, and the pain of the sting abates. So it is in Alkazuinius [=al-Qazwīnī, ʿAjāʾib al-makhlūqāt]. Moreover, on the authority of Damir himself (as will be said more fully below), the dhab returns to its vomit like a dog, and devours its own young. That it lives on air alone is therefore a mere fiction. It has this in common with the jarboa, or choerogryllius, that it chooses its hiding place in rocky and steep places. Alkazuinius: من كيسه انه لا يتخذ البيت الا في موضع صلب ليلا ينهدر عليه في حوافر الدواب ولما علم انه نساء لا يتخذ البيت الي عند الاكمته او صخرة عظيمة او شجرة ليستدل بها علي بيته اذا عاب ويتباعد عن col. 1049 بيته [min kaysihi annahu lā yattakhidh al-bayt illā fī mawḍiʿ ṣulb laylā yanhadir ʿalayhi fī ḥawāfir al-dawābb wa-lammā ʿalima annahu nassāʾ lā yattakhidh al-bayt ilā ʿind al-akamatih aw ṣakhra ʿaẓīma aw shajara li-yastadill bihā ʿalā baytihi idhā ʿāb wa-yatabāʿad ʿan baytihi] It is part of its shrewdness that it does not take a home except in hard ground, lest, trampled by the hooves of beasts of burden, it collapse upon it. And since it knows itself to be very forgetful, it chooses it on a high hill or a lofty rock, or by a tree, which it may have as II, 468 a marker when it is away from it or goes far off. Damir also: لا يتخذ حجره الا في كدية حجر خوفا من السيل والحافر ولذلك براثنه ناقصة كليلة لحفره بها في الاماكن الصلبة [lā yattakhidh juḥrahu illā fī kudyat ḥajar khawfan min al-sayl wa-l-ḥāfir wa-li-dhālika barāthinuhu nāqiṣa kalīla li-ḥafrihi bihā fī al-amākin al-ṣulba] Nor does it have a hiding place anywhere but in the hardest rocks, because it fears floods and diggers. And accordingly its claws are worn and blunt, since it digs stony places with them. Likewise: وفي طبعه النسيان وعدم الهداية وبه يضرب المثل في الحيرة ولذلك يحفر جحره عند الاكمة او صخرة ليلا يضل عنه اذا خرج لطلب والطعم [wa-fī ṭabʿihi al-nisyān wa-ʿadam al-hidāya wa-bihi yuḍrab al-mathal fī al-ḥayra wa-li-dhālika yaḥfir juḥrahu ʿind al-akama aw ṣakhra laylā yaḍill ʿanhu idhā kharaja li-ṭalab wa-l-ṭuʿm] Forgetfulness and ἀβλεψία [ablepsia] are natural to them. So its bewilderment has become proverbial. Accordingly it digs its den on a hill or in a rock, so as not to stray from it when it goes out to seek its food. The proverb Damir mentions is cited by him thus under the heading On Proverbs: اضل من الضب [aḍall min al-ḍabb] More apt to stray from the way than the dhab. Because the ضب [ḍabb] dhab is a wicked animal, whence it is called ضغطار [ḍghṭār] dhiphtar, as already observed, here is another proverb in use among the Arabs, which Alcamus taught me: اطعم اخاك كشية الضب حشة على المواسات [aṭʿim akhāka kushyat al-ḍabb ḥashsha ʿalā al-muwāsāt] He fed your brother the fat of the dhab, that is, he incited him to commit evil! As for breeding: اذا ارادت الضبة ان تخرج بيضها حفرت في الارض حفرة ورمت فيها البيض وطمته بالتراب وتعاهده كل يوم حتي يخرج وذلك في اربعين يوما [idhā arādat al-ḍabba an tukhrija bayḍahā ḥafarat fī al-arḍ ḥufra wa-ramat fīhā al-bayḍ wa-ṭammathu bi-l-turāb wa-taʿāhadathu kull yawm ḥattā yakhruj wa-dhālika fī arbaʿīn yawman] When the dhabba (that is, the female of the dhab) is about to lay her eggs, she digs a pit in the ground, into which she throws the eggs and covers them with dust, and visits them daily until they hatch, that is, until the fortieth day. So Damir, following Alkazuinius in part, whose words are: فاذا اراد ان يبيض حفر مثل ادحي النعام ويرمي فيها ثمانين بيضة ويدفنها col. 1050 في التراب وبيضها مثل بيض الحمام ويدعها اربعين يوما ثم ياتي وحسول قد خرجت منها فيتعدون فياكل منها ما قدرت [fa-idhā arāda an yabīḍa ḥafara mithl udḥī al-naʿām wa-yarmī fīhā thamānīn bayḍa wa-yadfinuhā fī al-turāb wa-bayḍuhā mithl bayḍ al-ḥamām wa-yadaʿuhā arbaʿīn yawman thumma yaʾtī wa-ḥusūl qad kharajat minhā fa-yataʿaddawn fa-yaʾkul minhā mā qadarat] When it is about to lay, it digs something like an ostrich's nest, into which it throws eighty eggs, like pigeons' eggs, and buries them in the dust. When it returns to them after leaving them forty days, the young, coming out of them, run about. Of these it eats as many as it can. Indeed, as Damir says, يوصف العقوق لانه ياكل حسوله [yūṣaf al-ʿuqūq li-annahu yaʾkul ḥusūlahu] it is described as notable for cruelty, because it devours its own young. Hence the poet: II, 469 اكلت بنيك كاكل الضب حتي تركت بنيك ليس لهم غذي [akalta banīka ka-akl al-ḍabb ḥattā tarakta banīka laysa lahum ghidhā] ¶

Cur timerem paupertatem, cum Deus provideat mihi? Providet is creaturis in angustia et egestate. Victum largitur creaturis omnibus, etiam τῷ dhab in deser- to et piscibus in mari. Etiam observarunt Arabes, quibus animalibus dhab συμ-παθεῖ et ἀντιπαθεῖ. Damir, بينه وبين العقارب مودة فلذلك يويها في حجره لتلسع المتحرش به اذا ادخل يده لاخذه Inter eum et scorpiones mutuus amor intercedit. Proinde illos in latibulo suo recipit, ut venatorem pungant, cum manum suam immittit, ut eam capiat. Idem in capite فالية الافاعي quasi Viperarum scrutatorem dixeris, sic appellari asserit ضرب من الخنافس رقط تالف العقارب في col. 1051 حجرة الضب لانها اذا اخرجت يعلم ان الضب خارج لا محالة واذا زويت في الحجر علم ان وراها العقارب والحيات Speciem scarabaeorum varii coloris, quae cum scorpionibus junctim habitat in latibulis τοῦ dhab. Nempe hoc animalculum, cum exit e latibulo, signi- ficat etiam τὸν dhab sine dolo exiisse. At illud repetens significat, post eum la- tere serpentes et scorpiones. Sed τῷ dhab maxime infensus est الورل alvvarlo, seu stellio major, quem guaril vocant barbari scriptores. Abdollatif Bagdaden- sis, ubi de guaril: بينه وبين الضب عداوة فيغلب الورل II, 470 الضب ويقتله لكنه لا ياكله كما يفعل بالحية وهو لا يتخذ بيتا لنفشه ولا يحفر حجر بل يخرج الضب من حجره صارعا يستولي عليه, Inter τὸν guaril et τὸν dhab gravis est inimicitia. Sed guaril illum vincit et interimit. Non edit tamen, quomodo serpentes solet: sed, cum domum sibi non exstruat, nec latibulum fodiat, e lustro suo τὸν dhab luctando ejicit et illi praevalet. Nam حرش charrasa verbum est venari, non in genere, sed peculiariter venari τὸν dhab, ut jam ante b) monuimus. Why should I fear poverty, when God provides for me? He provides for His creatures in hardship and want. He bestows sustenance on all creatures, even on the dhab in the desert and on the fish in the sea. The Arabs have also observed with which animals the dhab has sympathy and antipathy. Damir: بينه وبين العقارب مودة فلذلك يويها في حجره لتلسع المتحرش به اذا ادخل يده لاخذه [baynahu wa-bayna al-ʿaqārib mawadda fa-li-dhālika yuwīhā fī juḥrihi li-talsaʿa al-mutaḥarrish bihi idhā adkhala yadahu li-akhdhihi] There is mutual love between it and scorpions. It therefore receives them into its hiding place, so that they may sting the hunter when he puts his hand in to catch it. The same author, in the chapter on فالية الافاعي [fāliyat al-afāʿī], as if you were to say searcher of vipers, asserts that this name is given to ضرب من الخنافس رقط تالف العقارب في col. 1051 حجرة الضب لانها اذا اخرجت يعلم ان الضب خارج لا محالة واذا زويت في الحجر علم ان وراها العقارب والحيات [ḍarb min al-khanāfis ruqṭ taʾlafu al-ʿaqārib fī ḥujrat al-ḍabb li-annahā idhā kharajat yuʿlamu anna al-ḍabba khārij lā maḥāla wa-idhā zuwiyat fī al-juḥr ʿulima anna warāhā al-ʿaqārib wa-l-ḥayyāt] a species of beetle of varied colour, which lives together with scorpions in the hiding places of the dhab. That is, when this little creature comes out of the hiding place, it signifies that the dhab has certainly gone out too. But when it goes back in, it signifies that serpents and scorpions lurk behind it. But the dhab is most hostile to الورل [al-waral] alvvarlo, or the greater stellio, which barbarous writers call guaril. Abdollatif of Baghdad, on the guaril: بينه وبين الضب عداوة فيغلب الورل II, 470 الضب ويقتله لكنه لا ياكله كما يفعل بالحية وهو لا يتخذ بيتا لنفشه ولا يحفر حجر بل يخرج الضب من حجره صارعا يستولي عليه [baynahu wa-bayna al-ḍabb ʿadāwa fa-yaghlibu al-waral al-ḍabba wa-yaqtuluhu lākinnahu lā yaʾkuluhu kamā yafʿalu bi-l-ḥayya wa-huwa lā yattakhidhu baytan li-nafshihi wa-lā yaḥfiru juḥr bal yukhriju al-ḍabba min juḥrihi ṣāriʿan yastawlī ʿalayhi], Between the guaril and the dhab there is grave enmity. The guaril overcomes it and kills it. Yet it does not eat it, as it usually does serpents; but, since it builds no home for itself nor digs a hiding place, it drives the dhab out of its lair by fighting and prevails over it. It also has a second enemy, a black serpent, which in Arabic is called اسود سالخ [aswad sālikh] asvvad-salich. Alcamus: الحراش الاسود السالخ لانه يحرش الضباب [al-ḥarrāsh al-aswad al-sālikh li-annahu yaḥrushu al-ḍibāb]. The sense is that this serpent is called by another name charras, that is, hunter, because it hunts the dhab. For حرش [ḥarasha] charrasa is a verb meaning to hunt, not in general, but specifically to hunt the dhab, as we noted earlier b). To these let a third enemy be added, ظربان [ẓarbān] dhorban, that is, the putorius [polecat], as they call it, a most fetid animal of the weasel kind, by which it is vanquished not by strength but by blasts from its hind parts turned toward its burrow. الظربان يقصد حجر الضب وفيه حسوله وبيضه فياتي اضيق موضع فيه فيسده بذنبه فيحول دبره ويفسوا ثلاث فسوات حتي يغشي علي الضب فياكله ويقيم في حجره حتي ياتي علي اخر حسولة [al-ẓarbān yaqṣidu juḥr al-ḍabb wa-fīhi ḥusūluhu wa-bayḍuhu fa-yaʾtī aḍyaqa mawḍiʿ fīhi fa-yasudduhu bi-dhanabihi fa-yuḥawwilu duburahu wa-yafsū thalāth faswāt ḥattā yughshā ʿalā al-ḍabb fa-yaʾkuluhu wa-yuqīmu fī juḥrihi ḥattā yaʾtī ʿalā ākhir ḥusūlih] The dhorban (or putorius) approaches the burrow of the dhab when it has its young and its eggs there, and occupies its narrowest spot, which it blocks with its tail; and, turning its rear toward it, it emits three blasts of wind, until the dhab falls into a swoon. Having devoured it, it stays in the burrow until it has finished off its young, down to the last one col. 1052. So much from the philosophers about the species of lizard with which we are dealing. ¶

Theologis quoque hoc idem animal magnam suggessit disputandi materiam. Quaeritur enim, quorsum concedatur ejus esus, cum sit e numero الحشرات reptilium, quorum esus est prohibitus. Et nonnulli regerunt, hoc solum excipi. Ita enim habet الوسيط id est, liber, cui titulus Mediator, in quo hae quaestiones plene deciduntur, لا يوكل من الحشرات الا الضب Nullum reptile comedatur praeter τὸν dhab. Ab aliis τῷ dhab junguntur اليربوع والقنفد jarbuo et kunphud, id est, choerogryllus et hericius, quae et ipsa reptilia sunt, et tamen ad esum conceduntur. Alii rem in medio relinquunt, postquam in utramque partem multa disputaverunt, quamvis in II, 471 Arabia hunc cibum agnoscant esse in frequenti usu. Iidem pseudo-Theologi de hoc animali magnas congerunt ineptias, quarum unam hic referre sit satis, imo plus satis. Quidam juraverat in Mahomethem, se haud unquam crediturum, nisi prior crederet هذا الضب iste dhab, qui in ore speluncae suae forte fortuna apparuit. Tum is, e spelunca exiens, ad Prophetam accessit, et dixit ipsi بلسان طلق فصيح عربي مبين يفهمه القوم جميعا لبيك وسعديك يا رسول رب العالمين lingua diserta et perspicua, et sermone Arabico manifesto, quemque omnes intellexerunt: Tibi addictus sum, et beatus sis, O Apostole Domini saeculorum. Et cum propheta dixisset, من تعبد Quem colis? Respondit dhab, Eum colo, cujus thronus est in coelis, et in terra dominatio ejus, et in mari semita ejus, et in inferis indignatio et supplicium ejus. Tum illi dixit Propheta, Quis sum ego? Et dhab regessit, Tu es Apostolus Domini saeculorum, et sigillum Prophetarum. Bene sit illi, qui in te credet et tibi obsequetur. Et male pereat, quisquis a te recedet, aut te falsitatis arguet.

This same animal has also given the theologians ample matter for dispute. For it is asked why it is permitted to eat it, since it is one of الحشرات [al-ḥasharāt], the reptiles, which are forbidden as food. Some reply that this one alone is excepted. For so says الوسيط [al-Wasīṭ], that is, the book entitled The Mediator, in which these questions are fully settled: لا يوكل من الحشرات الا الضب [lā yūkal min al-ḥasharāt illā al-ḍabb] No reptile may be eaten except τὸν [ton] dhab. Others join to the dhab اليربوع والقنفد [al-yarbūʿ wa-l-qunfud] jarbuo and kunphud, that is, the choerogryllus and the hedgehog, which are themselves reptiles too and yet are permitted as food. Others leave the matter undecided, after arguing at length on both sides, although they admit that in II, 471 Arabia this food is in frequent use. These same pseudo-theologians heap up great absurdities about this animal, of which it will be enough, indeed more than enough, to relate one here. A certain man had sworn concerning Muhammad that he would never believe unless هذا الضب [hādhā al-ḍabb] this dhab, which by chance had appeared at the mouth of its burrow, believed first. Then the dhab came out of the burrow, approached the Prophet, and said to him بلسان طلق فصيح عربي مبين يفهمه القوم جميعا لبيك وسعديك يا رسول رب العالمين [bi-lisān ṭalq faṣīḥ ʿarabī mubīn yafhamuhu al-qawm jamīʿan labbayka wa-saʿdayka yā rasūl rabb al-ʿālamīn] in eloquent and clear speech, in plain Arabic language, which all understood: I am devoted to you, and may you be blessed, O Apostle of the Lord of the worlds. And when the Prophet had said من تعبد [man taʿbud] Whom do you worship?, the dhab answered: I worship Him whose throne is in heaven, and His dominion on earth, and His path in the sea, and His wrath and punishment in the underworld. Then the Prophet said to it: Who am I? And the dhab replied: You are the Apostle of the Lord of the worlds, and the seal of the Prophets. Well be it for him who believes in you and obeys you; and may whoever turns away from you or accuses you of falsehood perish miserably. ¶

Magi quoque suas partes hic habent. Ad eos enim refero superstitiosa illa, quae in Damire et Alkazuinio lego. Si e medio pedum hominis ضب dhab exeat, non poterit is, cui id accidit, cum ulla muliere coire. Testiculos ejus si quis deferat, illum servi sui maxime amabunt. Equum, qui talum τοῦ dhab fronti alligatum habuerit, non praeveniet ullus alius in cursus certamine. Quisquis ensi قرابا vaginam. aut thecam potius ex illius pelle fecerit, strenuus erit. Alii solum نصاب السيف capulum ensis hac pelle involvi malunt, quasi id multum referat. In vanissimis scriptoribus plura reperias hujus farinae, quae referre pudet ac piget.

The magicians also play their part here. For to them I attribute those superstitions which I read in Damir and Alkazuini. If a ضب [ḍabb] dhab comes out from between a man's feet, the man to whom this happens will be unable to have intercourse with any woman. If anyone carries its testicles, his slaves will love him greatly. A horse that has the ankle-bone of the dhab tied to its forehead will not be outrun by any other in a race. Whoever makes for his sword a قرابا [qirāban] scabbard, or rather a sheath, from its skin will be valiant. Others prefer that only the نصاب السيف [niṣāb al-sayf] hilt of the sword be wrapped in this skin, as though that mattered much. In the most frivolous writers you may find more of this sort, which one is ashamed and loath to relate. ¶

Albugini item, et oculorum suffosionibus. Et in capite دم Dam, seu De sanguine animalium, دم الورل والحردون يقوي البصر Sanguis guaril et chardaun roborat visum. The physicians have less, but more reliable. Avicenna, in the chapter ضب [ḍabb] dhab: يطلي بعره علي الكلف والنمش فينفع ۞ زبله نافع لبياض العين ونزول الما ۞ [yuṭlī baʿruhu ʿalā al-kalaf wa-l-namash fa-yanfaʿ ۞ zibluhu nāfiʿ li-bayāḍ al-ʿayn wa-nuzūl al-mā ۞] Its dung, smeared on, helps freckles and col. 1053 spots of the face; likewise white specks of the eye and cataracts. And in the chapter دم [dam] Dam, or On the blood of animals: دم الورل والحردون يقوي البصر [dam al-waral wa-l-ḥardūn yuqawwī al-baṣar] The blood of the guaril and the chardaun strengthens the sight. II, 472 وكذلك زبل الحردون والورل ويحسن اللون [wa-ka-dhālika zibl al-ḥardūn wa-l-waral wa-yuḥassin al-lawn] The dung of the chardaun and the guaril does the same, and makes a good complexion. And below: بعر الضب يجلو الكلف مجرب [baʿr al-ḍabb yajlū al-kalaf mujarrab] The dung of the dhab cleanses spots from the face: this has been tested. Likewise: زبل الورل والضب والتمساح لبياض العين [zibl al-waral wa-l-ḍabb wa-l-timsāḥ li-bayāḍ al-ʿayn] The dung of the guaril, the dhab and the crocodile for white specks of the eyes; supply is to be applied. And in the chapter بعر الحيوان [baʿr al-ḥayawān], that is, On the dung of animals: بعر الضب ينفع من البرص والكلف بجلايه [baʿr al-ḍabb yanfaʿ min al-baraṣ wa-l-kalaf bi-jalāyih] The dung of the dhab by its cleansing power heals elephantiasis and spots on the face. And shortly after: بعر الضب يجلو بياض العين [baʿr al-ḍabb yajlū bayāḍ al-ʿayn] The dung of the dhab cleanses white specks of the eyes. So too Damir: بعرة ينفع من البرص والكلف طلا ومن البياض ونزول الما في العين اكتحالا [baʿra yanfaʿ min al-baraṣ wa-l-kalaf ṭilā wa-min al-bayāḍ wa-nuzūl al-mā fī al-ʿayn iktiḥālan] Its dung, smeared on, heals leprosy and spots of the face; and eye-salves made from it remove white specks and the flux of humours into the eyes. This is taken from Alkazuini, where it is found in almost the same words. You may also read similar things in Abenbitar [=Ibn al-Bayṭār], and in other anonymous writers that I have R1. ¶

II, 473 Iam id animal, quod nobis descripserunt Arabes sub nominibus ضب dhab et حردون chardaun, id ipsum esse, quod צב tsab Mosis, tam multa docent, ut de II, 474 eo dubitare sit nefas. Primo enim id adstruit similitudo nominis Hebraici cum Arabico. Utut enim sono differant Hebraicum צב tsab et Arabicum ضب dhab; tamen utrobique litera initialis est Tsade, quae Arabibus aliter sonat propter punctum adscriptum. Qua mutatione nihil frequentius. Sic צאן tson Hebraeis ovis, Arabibus est ضان dhan. Hyaena illis צבוע tsebua: his ضبع dhebu. Rana illis צפרדע tsephardea: his ضفدع dhephda. Costa illis צלע tsela: his ضلع dhila. Diadema, aut plexus crinium, illis צפירה tsephira: his ضفيرة col. 1054 dhephira. Stercus illis צפיע tsephia: his ضفع dhepha. Angustia illis צוק tsuk: his ضيق dhik. Ridere illis צחק tsachak: his ضحق dhachika. 2. Secundo, צב tsab id esse, quod diximus, planum facit series homogeneorum animalium in eodem contextu orationis. Nam sequente versu lacertae mentio fit, et stellionis, et chamaeleontis, et salamandrae, etc. ut suo loco docebitur. Et par fuit ratio, cur ea lacertae species ut immunda rejiceretur. Neque illa omitti debuit, cum in Arabia nascatur, quae Iudaeae est contermina, et sit edulis apud Arabes. 3. Huc accedit veterum auctoritas, quorum assero nullum esse, qui aliter sumserit. Certe quatuor Arabes, quos consulere licuit, editi duo, et manu-scripti II, 475 duo, cum in aliis inter se multum differant, tamen hic constanter ha-bent الضب aldhab, et Onkelos צבא tsaba, quae est ipsa vox Hebraea, sola terminatione ad Chaldaismum inflexa. In Ionathane pro צב tsab legas חרדונא chardona; in Syro ܚܕܪܘܢܐ chadrona; et in Samaritano [?] adrona: quae tria eodem recidunt. Significatur scilicet Arabum حردون chardaun, quod idem esse cum ضب dhab supra c) asseruit Avicenna. Alii similem esse volunt, alii ejus marem. Damir, الحردون دويبة شبيه بالضب وقيل هو ذكر الضب لان له ذكرين مثله Alchardaun (vel al-chirdaun potius, jubet enim ita scribere) animalculum, simile τῷ dhab: quin mas ejus esse dicitur, quia ad instar illius duo habet veretra. Alcamus, الحردون لذكر الضب ودويبة احري Alchardaun de mare τοῦ dhab (dicitur). Est et aliud (huic simile) animalculum (2).

II, 473 Now, that the animal which the Arabs have described to us under the names ضب [ḍabb] dhab and حردون [ḥardūn] chardaun is the very same as the צב tsab of Moses is shown by so many considerations that to doubt it II, 474 would be wrong. First, it is supported by the similarity of the Hebrew name to the Arabic. For although the Hebrew צב tsab and the Arabic ضب [ḍabb] dhab differ in sound, in both the initial letter is Tsade, which among the Arabs sounds differently because of the dot added to it. Nothing is more frequent than this change. Thus sheep, צאן tson to the Hebrews, is ضان [ḍān] dhan to the Arabs. Hyena is צבוע tsebua to the former, ضبع [ḍabuʿ] dhebu to the latter. Frog is צפרדע tsephardea to the former, ضفدع [ḍifdaʿ] dhephda to the latter. Rib is צלע tsela to the former, ضلع [ḍilʿ] dhila to the latter. Diadem, or braid of hair, is צפירה tsephira to the former, ضفيرة [ḍafīra] col. 1054 dhephira to the latter. Dung is צפיע tsephia to the former, ضفع [ḍfʿ] dhepha to the latter. Distress is צוק tsuk to the former, ضيق [ḍīq] dhik to the latter. To laugh is צחק tsachak to the former, ضحق [ḍḥq] dhachika to the latter. 2. Secondly, that צב tsab is what we have said is made plain by the series of related animals in the same context. For in the following verse mention is made of the lizard, the stellio, the chameleon, the salamander, etc., as will be shown in its place. And there was equal reason why this species of lizard should be rejected as unclean. Nor ought it to have been omitted, since it is native to Arabia, which borders on Judaea, and is eaten among the Arabs. 3. To this is added the authority of the ancients, none of whom, I assert, took it otherwise. Certainly the four Arabic versions I was able to consult, two printed and two in manuscript, II, 475 though they differ greatly among themselves elsewhere, here consistently have الضب [al-ḍabb] aldhab; and Onkelos [=Targum Onkelos] has צבא tsaba, which is the Hebrew word itself, with only its ending adapted to Chaldee usage. In Jonathan [=Targum Jonathan], for צב tsab you read חרדונא chardona; in the Syriac ܚܕܪܘܢܐ [חדרונא] chadrona; and in the Samaritan [?] adrona: all three come to the same thing. What is meant, namely, is the حردون [ḥardūn] chardaun of the Arabs, which Avicenna asserted above c) to be the same as the ضب [ḍabb] dhab. Some hold it to be similar to it, others its male. Damir: الحردون دويبة شبيه بالضب وقيل هو ذكر الضب لان له ذكرين مثله [al-ḥardūn duwaybba shabīh bi-l-ḍabb wa-qīla huwa dhakar al-ḍabb li-anna lahu dhakarayn mithlahu] Alchardaun (or rather alchirdaun, for he directs it to be written so) is a small animal, similar to τῷ [tō] dhab: indeed it is said to be its male, because like it it has two penises. Alcamus: الحردون لذكر الضب ودويبة احري [al-ḥardūn li-dhakar al-ḍabb wa-duwaybba aḥrī] Alchardaun is said of the male τοῦ [tou] dhab. There is also another (similar) small animal R2. ¶

Arabibus et Chaldaeis addo veteres Hebraeos, qui צב tsab, vel חרדון chardon, aperte ponunt inter lacertorum species. Quod planum facient duo loca, quae proferam, a multis jam observata, nec tamen explicata hactenus. Prior est in tractatu Talmudico Cholin cap. 9. fol. 127. a. ubi sub nomine צב tsab, in Le-ge prohibiti, Hebraei volunt similes species contineri; quia additum est juxta speciem suam. Sic enim habent verba Legis: Immunda vobis erunt inter reptilia, quae repunt super terram, talpa et mus, והצב למינהו, et tsab, juxta speciem II, 476 suam. Nempe inquiunt: להביא העריד ובן־נפילים וסלמנדרא, ut includantur arod et ben-nephilim, et salamandra, tanquam vicinae species τῷ dhab. Et recentiores quidem ignorant, quid hic sint ערוד arod et בן־נפילים ben-nephilim. Sed salamandram saltem notum est in lacertorum esse genere. Itaque צב tsab necesse est ut referatur ad idem genus. Res erit manifestior, si doceamus, quid hic sint ערוד arod et בן־נפילים ben-nephilim: et prius quidem alibi onagrum, posterius filium gigantum significat. Quae videntur esse extra rem. Itaque hic haerent interpretes, neque Talmudis Glossatores, aut Lexicographi quidquam afferunt, quod veri speciem habeat. Sed prior vox est corrupta, et pro ערוד arod, vel iruad scribendum עסוד isvvad. Id lacerti genus est album, in arenis degens, cum quo puellarum digitos conferunt poetae Arabes. Nonnulli عذفوط odphut, et عضرفوط adhraphut vocant. Alcamus, العذفوط دويبة col. 1055 بيضا ناعمة يشبة بها اصابع الجواري Alodphut, animalculum album, amoenum, quocum puellarum digiti conferuntur. Et alibi, العضرفوط العذفوط او ذكر العظا Aladhraphut, idem, quod alodphut, aut lacertae mas. Et rursus, العسود للعضرفوط من العظاء Aliswad, de adraphut lacerti genere dicitur. Item, العسودة دويبة بضا يشبه بها بنان العذاري وتكني بنة النقا Aliswada, animalculum album, cui comparantur extremae pulpae digitorum in virginibus, cognominatur Binth-onnakai, id est, arenae filia. Itaque cum inter lacertorum species ערוד censetur a Talmudicis, levi mutatione, pro ערוד legendum עסוד, ut significetur illa lacerta, quam descripsimus. Parum usitatum עסוד, nec fortasse aliunde notum, imperiti scriptores in ערוד mutaverunt, quod plane aliud, nempe onagrum sonat. Superest, ut dicamus, quod animal sit בן־נפילים ben-nephilim, istis homogeneum. To the Arabs and Chaldeans I add the ancient Hebrews, who openly place צב tsab, or חרדון chardon, among the species of lizards. This will be made plain by two passages which I shall cite; many have already noticed them, but they have not yet been explained. The first is in the Talmudic tractate Cholin, ch. 9, fol. 127a, where the Hebrews hold that similar species are included under the name צב tsab, forbidden in the Law, because the words after its kind are added. For the words of the Law run thus: These shall be unclean to you among the creeping things that creep upon the earth: the mole and the mouse, והצב למינהו, and the tsab after its II, 476 kind. They say: להביא העריד ובן־נפילים וסלמנדרא, to include the arod and the ben-nephilim and the salamander, as species close to the dhab. Now the more recent writers do not know what ערוד arod and בן־נפילים ben-nephilim are here. But it is at least known that the salamander belongs to the lizard kind. Therefore צב tsab must necessarily be assigned to the same kind. The matter will be clearer if we show what ערוד arod and בן־נפילים ben-nephilim are here. Elsewhere the former means wild ass, the latter son of giants. These seem beside the point. Here, therefore, the interpreters are at a loss, and neither the Glossators of the Talmud nor the Lexicographers offer anything with the appearance of truth. But the first word is corrupt, and instead of ערוד arod, or iruad, one should write עסוד isvvad. This is a white kind of lizard, living in the sands, to which the Arab poets compare the fingers of girls. Some call it عذفوط [ʿadhfūṭ] odphut, and عضرفوط [ʿaḍrafūṭ] adhraphut. Alcamus: العذفوط دويبة col. 1055 بيضا ناعمة يشبة بها اصابع الجواري [al-ʿadhfūṭ duwaybba bayḍā nāʿima yushbih bihā aṣābiʿ al-jawārī] Alodphut, a small animal, white and pleasing, to which the fingers of girls are compared. And elsewhere: العضرفوط العذفوط او ذكر العظا [al-ʿaḍrafūṭ al-ʿadhfūṭ aw dhakar al-ʿaẓā] Aladhraphut, the same as alodphut, or the male lizard. And again: العسود للعضرفوط من العظاء [al-ʿaswad li-l-ʿaḍrafūṭ min al-ʿaẓāʾ] Aliswad is said of the adraphut, a kind of lizard. Likewise: العسودة دويبة بضا يشبه بها بنان العذاري وتكني بنة النقا [al-ʿaswada duwaybba baḍā yushbah bihā banān al-ʿadhārī wa-tukannā binat al-naqā] Aliswada, a small white animal, to which the fingertips of virgins are compared; it is surnamed Binth-onnakai, that is, daughter of the sand. Therefore, since ערוד is reckoned by the Talmudists among the species of lizards, by a slight change we should read עסוד instead of ערוד, so that the lizard we have described is meant. Unskilled scribes changed עסוד, a little-used word and perhaps not known from elsewhere, into ערוד, which means something quite different, namely wild ass. It remains for us to say what animal the בן־נפילים ben-nephilim is, of the same kind as these. A man outstanding in these studies, who today has no equal, rightly infers that this animal is aquatic, because we read in Semoth rabba, sect. 15: וזיע את הימים והראהו את בן־הנפילים שנאמר קול יי על המים He (God) moved the seas and showed him (Moses) the Ben-nephilim, as it is said Ps 29:3: „The voice of the Lord is upon the waters.“ But the same man errs very gravely when, from the affinity of the words and the similarity of the animals (as he asserts), he infers that בן־נפילים ben-nephilim is the nauplius, or nautilus. For even if we grant that nephilim alludes in some way to nauplius, in the things themselves there is no similarity. For the nauplius, or nautilus, has nothing in common with the tsab, whether this word signifies, as he holds, a toad or a tortoise, or some species of lizard, which with me is most firmly established. For lizards, toads and tortoises differ in their whole kind from the nauplius or nautilus fish. Nor is it at all likely that the Hebrews derived their נפילים nephilim from the name nauplius, which is little used, II, 477 and occurs nowhere, so far as I remember, except in one passage of Pliny. Nor, conversely, was nauplius made from נפילים nephilim. For it is evident that this name was taken from the thing itself, and that the nautilus is called nauplius because it sails as in a ship, from the noun ναῦς [naus] and the verb πλεῖν [plein]. What, then, is the בן־נפילים ben-nephilim? It seems altogether to be the Egyptian skink, of which Avicenna says in book 2, in the chapter اسقنقور [isqanqūr] Iscancur, as the Arabs call the skink: هو ورل الماي يصاد من نيل مصر ويقولون انه من نسل التمساح اذا وضعه خارج الما ونشا خارجا [huwa waral al-māy yuṣād min Nīl Miṣr wa-yaqūlūn innahu min nasl al-timsāḥ idhā waḍaʿahu khārij al-mā wa-nashaʾa khārijan] It is the aquatic guaril, which is caught in Egypt on the bank of the Nile. They say that it is born from the seed of the crocodile when it sheds it outside the water, and that it grows up outside the water R3. And again in the chapter سقنقور [saqanqūr] Scancur: ورل نيلي يصاد بمصر ويزعمون انه من نتاج التمساح في البر [waral nīlī yuṣād bi-Miṣr wa-yazʿumūn annahu min nitāj al-timsāḥ fī al-barr] It is the Nile guaril, which they catch in Egypt, and col. 1056 they say it is born on land from the seed of the crocodile. And Alkazuinius, after citing Avicenna: وقال غيره انه من نسل التمساح اذا وضعه خارج الما فما قصد الما صار تمساحا وما قصد البر صار سقنقورا [wa-qāla ghayruhu innahu min nasl al-timsāḥ idhā waḍaʿahu khārij al-mā fa-mā qaṣada al-mā ṣāra timsāḥan wa-mā qaṣada al-barr ṣāra saqanqūran] Others, he says, have said that it comes from the seed of the crocodile when it has cast it out of the waters; and that whatever reaches the waters becomes a crocodile, and whatever stays on land a skink. It is caught only in winter; for, driven by the force of the cold, it comes out onto land, and is caught there. But the Egyptian skink is forbidden, because it is born from the crocodile; it is therefore forbidden like its root. Of the many things the Arabs R5 say about the skink, we have excerpted these few, because they serve our purpose. For three things are inferred from them. First, that the skink is contained under the same kind as the dhab and the guaril, since it is similar to them in appearance. Second, that it is amphibious, and lives mostly in the waters of the Nile, and so also in the sea, so that God can rightly be said to have moved את הימים, the seas, in order to show the skink to Moses. Though there is an error in that word, and instead of הימים, that is, seas, one should read המים, waters, with one small letter fewer, as what follows shows: As it is said, „The voice of the Lord is upon the waters“. Third, since the skink is believed (how truly, we do not now ask) to be born from the seed of the crocodile, as the Arabs consistently write, it is clear why it was called by the Hebrews בן-נפילים, that is, son of giants. For by the name of giants the Orientals denote not only men but also any beasts of greater bulk. Thus an Arab writer in Damir: في جرادة خلق عشرة من جبابرة من الحيوان [fī jarāda khalq ʿashara min jabābira min al-ḥayawān] In the locust is the nature of ten giants among animals; so it reads word for word, that is, of ten larger animals. For, as follows, the locust II, 479 has the face of a horse, the eyes of an elephant, the neck of a bull, the horns of a stag, etc. On the same pattern the skink is called by the Hebrews son of giants, that is, of crocodiles, compared with which it is nothing but a very tiny animal. These things being thus explained, the truth will shine out more and more if the whole passage is read, of which we have cited only a part. It runs thus: God stirred the waters, to show him (Moses) the son of giants, as it is said Ps 29:3: „The voice of the Lord is upon the waters“. He stirred the earth, to show him the צב tsab, as it is written Ps 29:8: „The voice of the Lord makes the desert bring forth“. He stirred fire, to show him the salamander, as it is said Ps 29:7: „The voice of the Lord cuts out flames of fire“. Thus from three elements are drawn three animals of different species but of the same kind: the son of giants, or of crocodiles, the skink, from the waters, in which it mostly lives; the צב tsab from the earth, to which it is so attached, as if to the soil, that it never goes down to the waters, as we proved above d); and finally the salamander from fire, in which many wrongly suppose it is born and on which it feeds. ¶

For the toad, or the tortoise, has nothing in common with serpents. Such different animals cannot copulate, nor, if they did mate, could some third thing be born from them. Therefore, however inept the masters of the Hebrews may be, far be it from me to think that they reached such a degree of folly as to invent these monsters. These three words, then, חרדון chardon, חכינא chacina, and חברבר chabarber, certainly designate three animals which, though different in species, nevertheless agree in genus. Nor is it so very difficult to show what they are. For we have already learned that חרדון chardon is the same as צב tsab, or ضب [ḍabb] dhab, a kind of lizard about which the Arabs have taught us so much. And for חכינא chacina we must most certainly read חבינא chabina, with Beth and Caph interchanged; these letters are very similar and are not rarely confused through the carelessness of copyists. How potent its venom is, namely in warmer places, these words of Pliny, book 29, chapter 4, may show: Among all venomous creatures the salamander's wickedness is the greatest. Hujus interpretationis ratio non potest plene percipi, nisi rem altius repetamus in rudiorum col. 1060 II, 482 gratiam, qui crocodili nomine nihil aliud intelligunt, quam Aegyptiam belluam magnae molis. At Ionibus, qui primi mortalium hanc vocem usurparunt, crocodilus erat lacertae genus in sepibus et maceriis commorari solitae. It remains for us to say why in the Greek version ὁ κροκόδειλος ὁ χερσαῖος [ho krokodeilos ho chersaios] is read for צב tsab, and in the Vulgate crocodilus [crocodile], without an epithet. The reason for this rendering cannot be fully grasped unless we go further back, for the sake of the less learned, col. 1060 II, 482 who understand by the name crocodile nothing other than the Egyptian beast of great bulk. But for the Ionians, the first of mortals to use this word, the crocodile was a kind of lizard that usually lives in hedges and dry-stone walls. Ridiculous, therefore, is the invention of the grammarians, who dream that the crocodile is so called either from its saffron colour, or because it fears saffron, or from τὰς κρόκας [tas krokas], that is, the shores. For there was no other reason for the name than its likeness in form to the Ionian crocodiles, that is, the lizards of the hedges. Herodotus, book 2: Κροκοδείλους δὲ Ἴωνες ὠνόμασαν, εἰκάζοντες αὐτῶν τὰ εἴδεα τοῖσι παρὰ σφίσι γινομένοισι κροκοδείλοισι, τοῖσιν ἐν τῇσιν αἱμασίῃσι [Krokodeilous de Iōnes ōnomasan, eikazontes autōn ta eidea toisi para sphisi ginomenoisi krokodeiloisi, toisin en tēisin haimasiēisi]. Nor did the old grammarian, whoever he is, from whom the Etymologicum and Phavorinus copied these words, have anything else in mind: Ὁ δὲ κροκόδειλος ζωΰφιόν ἐστι μικρόν [Ho de krokodeilos zōyphion esti mikron] (unless, with Eustathius, we read κροκύδειλος [krokydeilos] for κροκόδειλος [krokodeilos]). And this may have been taken from the account of the spring Zareta, or Azaritia, near Chalcedon, in which the ancients described small crocodiles, that is, water lizards. Antigonus, Mirabilia, chapter 162: Εὔδοξον δ’ ἱστορεῖν τὴν μὲν ἐν Καρχηδόνι [Eudoxon d’ historein tēn men en Karchēdoni] (read Χαλκηδόνι [Chalkēdoni]) κρήνην κροκοδείλους ἐν αὐτῇ μικροὺς (ἔχουσαν) ὁμοίους τοῖς ἐν Αἰγύπτῳ [krēnēn krokodeilous en autēi mikrous (echousan) homoious tois en Aigyptōi]. This was the use of the word crocodile among the most ancient Greeks. But the same Greeks, having transferred the name to the beasts of the Nile, gradually took it away from lizards. Except that they did not cease to call by this name certain foreign species of that kind which seemed remarkable above the rest in use, shape or size, or to come closer to the form of the larger crocodile. Yet the epithet land was generally added, to distinguish them from the river crocodile. Thus Aristotle, History, book 2, chapter 17, on the belly of animals, says that the belly is of the same structure τοῖς κροκοδείλοις ἀμφοῖν [tois krokodeilois amphoin], in both crocodiles, that is, the land as well as the aquatic. And in book 5, chapter 33: Τίκτουσι δὲ καὶ σαῦροι, καὶ κροκόδειλοι οἱ II, 483 χερσαῖοι, καὶ οἱ ποταμιοὶ, εἰς τὴν γῆν [Tiktousi de kai sauroi, kai krokodeiloi hoi chersaioi, kai hoi potamioi, eis tēn gēn]. Apollonius, Marvels, chapter 39, from Aristotle's Extracts τῶν ἀνατομῶν [tōn anatomōn]: Ὄφις ὤφθη ἐν Πάφῳ πόδας ἔχων δύο, ὁμοίους χερσαίῳ κροκοδείλῳ [Ophis ōphthē en Paphō podas echōn dyo, homoious chersaiō krokodeilō]. Aelian, History, book 1, chapter 58: Καὶ αἱ σαῦραι δὲ ἐπιβουλεύουσι ταῖς μελίτταις, καὶ οἱ κροκόδειλοι οἱ χερσαῖοι [Kai hai saurai de epibouleuousi tais melittais, kai hoi krokodeiloi hoi chersaioi]. And in book 16, chapter 6, on the Indian phattages: Ἐν Ἰνδοῖς γίνεται ζῶον κροκοδείλῳ χερσαίῳ παραπλήσιον ἰδεῖν [En Indois ginetai zōon krokodeilō chersaiō paraplēsion idein], etc. The Scholiast on Nicander, on the salamander: Ἔοικε δὲ τῷ χερσαίῳ κροκοδείλῳ [Eoike de tō chersaiō krokodeilō]. Meanwhile it is not sufficiently established which species of lizards, exactly, are given the name land crocodile. In Herodotus the same are said to be κροκόδειλοι, ὅσον τε τριπήχεες χερσαῖοι, τῇσι σαυρῇσι ἐμφερέστατοι [krokodeiloi, hoson te tripēchees chersaioi, tēsi saurēsi empherestatoi], crocodiles about three cubits long, living on land, very like lizards. Strabo calls col. 1061 them σαύρας, διπήχεις [sauras, dipēcheis]. And Aristotle describes similar ones in Arabia, if these words of Aelian, book 16, chapter 41, are true: Σαύρους δὲ Ἀριστοτέλης ἐν τῇ τῶν Ἀράβων γῇ τίκτεσθαί φησι, καὶ δύο πήχεις ἔχειν αὐτούς [Saurous de Aristotelēs en tē tōn Arabōn gē tiktesthai phēsi, kai dyo pēcheis echein autous]. But in the passage of Aristotle from which Aelian drew this, they are said only to be πηχυαῖοι [pēchyaioi], a cubit long, nowhere διπήχεις [dipēcheis], two cubits long. The words are in the History, book 8, chapter 28: Ἐν δὲ τῇ Ἀραβίᾳ σαῦροι μείζους πηχυαίων [En de tē Arabia sauroi meizous pēchyaiōn]. But Pliny rebukes those who hold this view and assigns to the skink definite marks by which it may be distinguished from the land crocodile, though how truly, I do not quite know: Of the same likeness, he says, is the skink, which some have said is a land crocodile, only with a whiter and thinner skin. Its chief difference from the crocodile, however, is recognized in that the set of its scales is turned toward the head. Others imagine the land crocodile to be a huge beast, almost equal to the river crocodile. See Bellonius, and Cardano, book 9, On Subtlety. ¶

But here let everyone believe what he will. I, however, am fully persuaded that the land crocodile among most of the ancients was neither the skink nor a huge beast, but that very animal which, as we have said, is called צב tsab by the Hebrews, ܚܪܕܘܢܐ [חרדונא] chardona by the Chaldeans and Syrians, and ضب [ḍabb] dhab and حردون [ḥardūn] chardaun by the Arabs II, 484. How it differs from the skink, these same writers teach us abundantly. That the matter is so will at once be noticed by anyone who compares the nature and powers of the two. 1. The blood of the dhab strengthens the sight. So in Pliny, book 28, chapter 8: the blood of the land crocodile, applied as an ointment, gives clearness of sight and remedies scars of the eyes. Hence Galen, On Simples, book 10, chapter 6: Εἰ δὲ τὸ τοῦ χερσαίου κροκοδείλου (αἷμα) ποιητικὸν ὀξυδερκίας ἐστὶν, οὐ δε πειράζειν ἔμελλον, ἔχων ὀξυδερκῆ φάρμακα δόκιμα· [Ei de to tou chersaiou krokodeilou (haima) poiētikon oxyderkias estin, ou de peirazein emellon, echōn oxyderkē pharmaka dokima] Whether the blood of the land crocodile sharpens the sight I had no need to test, since I had other proven drugs for that purpose. 2. But the ضب [ḍabb] dhab has more potency in its dung, by which white spots and cataracts are removed from the eyes. See this very thing in Pliny concerning crocodilea, that is, the dung of the land crocodile! It lives only on land and among the most fragrant flowers. For this reason its intestines are diligently sought out, stuffed with a pleasant scent. They call it crocodilea; it is most useful for defects of the eyes, applied with leek juice, and against cataracts or dimness. Alexander of Tralles too, book 2, chapter 5, wants the dung of the land crocodile mixed into an eye-salve for scars of the eyes and for wiping away λευκώματα [leukōmata]. 3. The dung of the ضب [ḍabb] dhab gives brightness to the face, removing spots, warts, scurf and freckles. The same is the power of crocodilea; Pliny: Smeared on with Cyprus oil, it also removes blemishes growing on the face; with water, all diseases whose nature is to spread over the face, and it restores brightness. It removes freckles and pimples and all spots. But Dioscorides states that skinks are found in Mauretania, Egypt, India and Arabia; Solinus says they are very numerous around the Nile. 7. Nor are the same powers attributed to the skink and the land crocodile: of the latter no one, as far as I know, has written what all report of the skink, that its use rouses torpor of the nerves and extinguishes the force of poison. So that from this too it appears that they are different animals. ¶

I shall add here some names of the land crocodile that are less commonly known. One is from Hesychius: Κίκερος· ὁ χερσαῖος κροκόδειλος [Kikeros; ho chersaios krokodeilos]. But I would think this fits the skink rather. For κίκερος [kikeros] seems to be a name shortened from the Egyptian and Arabic سقنقور [saqanqūr] skankur, or skinkur, in Avicenna and others. Yet κίκερος [kikeros] also echoes the Coptic name ΧΑΡΟΥΚῚ, by which الضب [al-ḍabb], or the land crocodile, is called in the Lexicon. What if it was called κίκερος [kikeros] from its smallness, because it is far smaller than the other? For in Hesychius κύκερος [kykeros] is rendered τὸ ἐλάχιστον [to elachiston]. And the Hebrew רקיק rekik has the same meaning, if we believe Pomis and Boderianus [=Guy Le Fèvre de la Boderie], who explain it as קטן, small, as if from the Aruch [=the ʿArukh of Nathan ben Jehiel of Rome], where, however, I do not find it. Accordingly רקיק rekic seems to be wrongly written for דקיק dekik, Res for Daleth. Two barbarous names of the same little animal are found in the Arabic Pandectarius: Dhab (he says) is a certain animal like a large lizard, big-bellied; and in Latin it is called ballecola. It is a species of Arabian stellio. In Spanish it is called botrax. What ballecola is, I do not know. I think the word is corrupt, since elsewhere it is written balleccara. But botrax appears in Isidore among the species of lizard. There are several kinds of lizards, he says, such as the botrax, the salamander, the saura, the stellio. The botrax is so called because it has the appearance of a frog. Perhaps it is a skink-like frog. Hence the borax stone (read botrax), crapaudine, in the Pandectarius. ¶
