III, 149 col. 375 Chapter 3. ¶

Probatur, כפיר chephir, et שחל sachal, non solum lconum, ut vulgo, sed et serpentum esse nomina.

It is proved that כפיר chephir and שחל sachal are names not only of lions, as is commonly held, but also of serpents. ¶

Leonis nomen nonnnullis animalibus commune est, a leone diversissimis. Sic col. 376 Diphilus in Athenaeo, Plinius et Aelianus λέοντα θαλάττιον, leonem marinum describunt inter astacorum genera. Lacertae vel stellionis species est ὁ χαμαιλέων, id est, terrestris leo; qui idem Arabice vocatur a) ليث عفرين leo Iphrina, ab Arabiae loco, in quo frequens est. Quale, quod ليث, leo, in Alcamus est ضرب من العناكب, araneae species. Et in serpentum genere cenchris, seu cenchrine (1), a nonnullis vocatur λέων αἴολος, leo varius. Nicander in Theriacis versu 463.

The name of the lion is shared by several animals very different from the lion. Thus col. 376 Diphilus in Athenaeus, Pliny and Aelian describe a λέοντα θαλάττιον [leonta thalattion], a sea lion, among the kinds of lobster. A species of lizard or gecko is ὁ χαμαιλέων [ho chamaileōn], that is, the ground lion; in Arabic the same animal is called a) ليث عفرين [layth ʿifrīn], the lion of Iphrin, from a place in Arabia where it is common. Similar is the fact that ليث [layth], lion, in Alcamus [=al-Qāmūs al-muḥīṭ, the Arabic dictionary of al-Fīrūzābādī] is ضرب من العناكب [ḍarb min al-ʿanākib], a species of spider. And in the serpent kind the cenchris or cenchrines R1 is called by some λέων αἴολος [leōn aiolos], the variegated lion. Nicander in the Theriaca, line 463: ¶

Δήεις κεγχρίνου δολιχὸν τέρας, ὅν τε λέοντα
Αἴολον αὐδάξαντο, περίστικτον φολίδεσσι·

Δήεις κεγχρίνου δολιχὸν τέρας, ὅν τε λέοντα Αἴολον αὐδάξαντο, περίστικτον φολίδεσσι· [Dēeis kenchrinou dolichon teras, hon te leonta / Aiolon audaxanto, peristikton pholidessi] ¶

Invenies cenchrinis longum monstrum, quem et leonem varium vocarunt, squamis maculosum. Ad quae verba Scholiastes, Ἔστι δὲ οὗτος μακρὸς κατάστικτος, ὃν καὶ λέοντά φησι καλεῖν· ἢ διὰ τὸ πολύστικτον τῶν φολίδων· ἢ διὰ δὲ γενναῖον λέοντα ἐκάλουν αὐτόν· ἤτοι διὰ τὸ ὀρθὴν τὴν οὐρὰν βαστάζοντα διεγείρειν ἑαυ-τὸν εἰς μάχην· ἢ διὰ τὸ δάκνειν, καὶ ῥοφᾷν τὸ αἷμα τοῦ ἀνθρώπου, ἅτινα δὲ καὶ λέων ποιεῖ. In quibus verbis cur dicatur leo vocari διὰ τὸ πολύστικτον τῶν φολίδων, propter squamas maculosas, cum in leone nihil tale est. Sed scripserat Scholiastes, ὃν καὶ λέοντα αἰολόν φησι καλεῖν. Itaque mens ejus haec est, leonem quidem vocari propter generosum animum; aut quia erecta cauda ad pugnam se incitat; aut quia leonum more mordet, et humanum sanguinem exsorbet: varium autem appellari propter maculas, squamis adspersas. Utut sit, hinc patet, Graecos quoque leonum nomen serpentibus adhibuisse: ne cui novum videatur, quod Hebraea leonum nomina כפיר chephir, et שחל sachal, etiam ad serpentes pertinent. Inde est, quod, cum כפיר chephir proprie leunculum sonet, tamen a Senioribus nonnunquam pro dracone sumitur. Sic Iob. IV, 10. Hebraea ושני כפירים נתעו, Et dentes chephirim (seu leunculorum) avulsi sunt, reddunt mira libertate, γαυρίαμα δὲ δρακόντων ἐσβέσθη, draconum autem gloriatio extincta est. Et Iob. XXXVIII, 39. pro Hebraeis וחית כפירים תמלא, An animam leunculorum III, 150 implebis? iidem habent, Ψυχὰς δὲ δρακόντων ἐμπλήσεις; Animas autem draconum an explebis? Nempe כפיר chephir apud Hebraeos non solum le- unculum, sed et dracunculum sonabat (2). Quomodo Arabicum شبل sabl, quod Hebraeo כפיר chephir plane respondet, et pro leunculo fere sumitur. Tamen etiam de catulo draconis usurpari, docent haec Arabici paraphrastis Thren. IV, 3. والتنانين خلعوا ثديهم اشبالهم ارضعوا Et draco- nes mammas eduxerunt, catulos suos lactarunt. Dracones nempe marini, id est, cete, ut Hebraice תנין, et Graece δράκοντες hoc ipso loco.

You will find the long monster of the cenchrines, which they have also called the variegated lion, spotted with scales. On these words the Scholiast says: Ἔστι δὲ οὗτος μακρὸς κατάστικτος, ὃν καὶ λέοντά φησι καλεῖν· ἢ διὰ τὸ πολύστικτον τῶν φολίδων· ἢ διὰ δὲ γενναῖον λέοντα ἐκάλουν αὐτόν· ἤτοι διὰ τὸ ὀρθὴν τὴν οὐρὰν βαστάζοντα διεγείρειν ἑαυτὸν εἰς μάχην· ἢ διὰ τὸ δάκνειν, καὶ ῥοφᾷν τὸ αἷμα τοῦ ἀνθρώπου, ἅτινα δὲ καὶ λέων ποιεῖ [Esti de houtos makros katastiktos, hon kai leonta phēsi kalein; ē dia to polystikton tōn pholidōn; ē dia de gennaion leonta ekaloun auton; ētoi dia to orthēn tēn ouran bastazonta diegeirein heauton eis machēn; ē dia to daknein, kai rhophan to haima tou anthrōpou, hatina de kai leōn poiei]. In these words, why should it be said that it is called lion διὰ τὸ πολύστικτον τῶν φολίδων [dia to polystikton tōn pholidōn], on account of its spotted scales, when there is nothing of the kind in the lion? But the Scholiast had written ὃν καὶ λέοντα αἰολόν φησι καλεῖν [hon kai leonta aiolon phēsi kalein]. His meaning, then, is this: it is called lion because of its noble spirit; or because it rouses itself to fight with its tail erect; or because it bites in the manner of lions and sucks out human blood; but it is called variegated because of the spots sprinkled over its scales. However that may be, it is clear from this that the Greeks too applied the name of lions to serpents, so that no one should find it strange that the Hebrew names of lions, כפיר chephir and שחל sachal, also apply to serpents. Hence, although כפיר chephir properly means young lion, it is nevertheless sometimes taken by the Elders for a dragon. Thus in Iob. IV, 10 they render the Hebrew ושני כפירים נתעו, And the teeth of the chephirim (or young lions) are torn out, with remarkable freedom as γαυρίαμα δὲ δρακόντων ἐσβέσθη [gauriama de drakontōn esbesthē], but the boasting of the dragons is extinguished. And in Job 38:39, for the Hebrew וחית כפירים תמלא, Will you fill the appetite of the young lions? III, 150 they have Ψυχὰς δὲ δρακόντων ἐμπλήσεις; [Psychas de drakontōn emplēseis;] Will you satisfy the appetites of the dragons? Evidently כפיר chephir among the Hebrews meant not only young lion but also young dragon R2. So too the Arabic شبل [shibl] sabl, which corresponds exactly to the Hebrew כפיר chephir, is generally taken for a young lion. Yet that it is also used of the young of a dragon is shown by these words of the Arabic paraphrast on Thren. IV, 3.: والتنانين خلعوا ثديهم اشبالهم ارضعوا [wa-l-tanānīn khalaʿū thudīyahum ashbālahum arḍaʿū] And the dragons drew out their breasts, they suckled their young. Sea dragons, that is, whales, as is the Hebrew תנין and the Greek δράκοντες [drakontes] in this very passage. ¶

Ita vox שחל sachal, cum alibi leonem sonet, tamen Psal. XCI, 13. pro aspi- de, aut alio serpente, sumunt vetustissimi interpretes. Ubi Hebraea verba על שחל ופתן תדרך, Seniores exponunt, Ἐπ᾽ ἀσπίδα καὶ βασιλίσκον ἐπιβήσῃ. Et Vulgatus, Super aspidem et basiliscum ambulabis. Et Hieronymus, Super aspi- col. 377 dem et basiliscum calcabis. Et Apollinaris,

Likewise the word שחל sachal, although elsewhere it means lion, is nevertheless taken by the most ancient translators in Ps 91:13 for the asp or some other serpent. There the Elders render the Hebrew words על שחל ופתן תדרך as Ἐπ᾽ ἀσπίδα καὶ βασιλίσκον ἐπιβήσῃ [Ep᾽ aspida kai basiliskon epibēsē]. And the Vulgate translator: You shall walk upon the asp and the basilisk. And Jerome: You shall tread upon the asp col. 377 and the basilisk. And Apollinaris: ¶

Ἀσπίδα θαρσαλέως ἐπιβήσῃ, καὶ βασιλίσκον.

Ἀσπίδα θαρσαλέως ἐπιβήσῃ, καὶ βασιλίσκον. [Aspida tharsaleōs epibēsē, kai basiliskon.] ¶

Et Syrus, ܥܠ ܓܪܣܐ ܘܥܠ ܚܪܡܢܐ ܬܕܘܫ Et Arabs, تطا علي الافعي والحية التنين Et Aethiops, ላዕለ፡ ተኵላ፡ ወከይሲ፡ ትጼዐን፡ Omnes eodem sensu. Neque enim eorum ullus leonem hic intelligit voce שחל, sed serpentis aliquod genus. Quamvis de specie inter illos non conveniat. Nam Arabi שחל est الافعي, vipera, Syro ܓܪܣܐ, amphisbaena, Aethiopi ተኵላ፡ serpentis genus, a lupino ingenio sic appellati. Et toto illo versu על שחל ופתן תדרך תרמס כפיר ותנין, Davidem verisimile est solum egisse de serpentibus, adeoque vocibus כפיר et שחל tam angues intelligi, quam vocibus פתן et תנין; quarum illa aspidem, haec draconem significat. Sic enim omnia melius cohaerebunt; quam si leones cum serpentibus permisceas: ut novi interpretes, qui fere hic habent: Super leonem et basiliscum ambulabis, conculcabis leunculum et dra- conem. In quibus verbis praeter quam ferae junguntur plane diversae naturae, nemo facile dixerit, quid sit ambulare super leonem, aut calcare leunculum. Neque enim quisquam est, qui leonem ambulando calcet, quomodo solemus aspides et anguium reliqua genera (3).

And the Syriac: ܥܠ ܓܪܣܐ ܘܥܠ ܚܪܡܢܐ ܬܕܘܫ [על גרסא ועל חרמנא תדוש]. And the Arabic: تطا علي الافعي والحية التنين [taṭā ʿalī al-afʿī wa-l-ḥayya al-tinnīn]. And the Ethiopic: ላዕለ፡ ተኵላ፡ ወከይሲ፡ ትጼዐን፡ [lāʿəla takʷəlā wakayəsi təṣēʿan] All in the same sense. For none of them understands a lion here by the word שחל, but some kind of serpent, although they do not agree on the species. For to the Arabic translator שחל is الافعي [al-afʿī], the viper; to the Syriac ܓܪܣܐ [גרסא], the amphisbaena; to the Ethiopic ተኵላ፡ [takʷəlā], a kind of serpent so called from its wolf-like nature. And in that whole verse, על שחל ופתן תדרך תרמס כפיר ותנין, it is probable that David dealt only with serpents, and therefore that by the words כפיר and שחל snakes are meant just as much as by the words פתן and תנין, of which the former signifies the asp, the latter the dragon. For thus everything will cohere better than if you mix lions with serpents, as the modern translators do, who here generally have: You shall walk upon the lion and the basilisk, you shall trample the young lion and the dragon. In these words, besides the fact that beasts of entirely different nature are joined together, no one could easily say what it means to walk upon a lion or to tread on a young lion. For there is no one who treads on a lion while walking, as we commonly do on asps and the other kinds of snakes R3. ¶

III, 151 Iam, si quaeras, quod anguis genus significetur nomine שחל sachal, respondebo, ut in leonibus שחל sachal dicitur, qui niger est, ut suo loco b) docuimus, ita etiam nigrum serpentem eadem voce designari. Cujusmodi Nicander in Theriacis multos describit: ut cum scribit de aspide versu 172.

III, 151 Now, if you ask what kind of snake is signified by the name שחל sachal, I will answer: just as among lions the one called שחל sachal is the one that is black, as we showed in its place b), so also a black serpent is designated by the same word. The colour on its rough back sometimes appears quince-yellow and mottled, sometimes ash-grey, often also sooty, blackened under the soil of the Ethiopians, like the mud that the Nile, when full, pours into the sea through its many mouths and drives into the deep. And rough tufa, and chalk eaten out by black chelydri. — — — — — III, 152 Aelian too has black asps and black haemorrhoi, Historia book 6, ch. 38 and book 15, ch. 13. Aëtius describes the ammodytes, marked with black spots; the chelidonian asp, black on the back like swallows and white on the belly; the dipsas with a black tail; and dragons, some black, some red, some ash-grey. Pausanias in the Boeotica says: Ἐν Αἰθιοπίᾳ μέλανας τὰς ἀσπίδας οὐ μεῖον ἢ καὶ τοὺς ἀνθρώπους ἡ γῆ τρέφει [En Aithiopia melanas tas aspidas ou meion ē kai tous anthrōpous hē gē trephei]; In Ethiopia the earth nourishes black asps no less than black men. Eae nimirum aspides nigerrimae sunt, quod praeter Nicandrum docent Pausanias, Aelianus, Aëtius, atque Arabum nonnulli. Quo dato, veteres שחל sachal recte aspidem interpretantur. De nigro serpente multa fabulatur Damir, quae piget hic referre. In his Iesum, Mariae filium, lignatori praedixisse, fore, ut illa ipsa die in summum vitae discrimen incideret: qui, cum lignatum ivisset, et rediisset sub vesperam cum fasce ligni, cumque jussu Christi solvisset, reperit in illo حية سودا, serpentem nigrum. Tum, Iesu rogante, quid fecisset, et illo negante, se quidquam fecisse, nisi quod transeunti mendico sui panis partem dederat, regessit Iesus, بهذا دفع عنك Propterea Deus a te malum avertit. Alii de col. 379 Saleh, propheta, idem narrant, et dicunt, serpentem اسود سالخ asuad salich crassum fuisse مثل الجذع instar trunci palmae.

that is, many of which are black in colour. Yet one serpent is black above the rest, and for that very reason it is called in Arabic اسود [aswad] asuad, that is, black. Alcamus: الاسود الحية العظيمة [al-aswad al-ḥayya al-ʿaẓīma] Alasuad (the black one), a great serpent R4. Some add سالخ [sālikh] salich as an epithet, as if you were to say one coming out III, 153, which Damir [=al-Damīrī, Ḥayāt al-ḥayawān] explains thus: الاسود السالخ نوع من الحيات شديد السواد سمي بذلك لانه يسلخ جلده كل عام [al-aswad al-sālikh nawʿ min al-ḥayyāt shadīd al-sawād summiya bi-dhālika li-annahu yaslukhu jildahu kull ʿām] Alasuad assalich, a kind of serpent of the most intense blackness, which is so called because it sheds its skin every year. Another copy has نوع من الافعوان [nawʿ min al-afʿuwān] a kind of viper. I understand black asps, which many Arabs include under the kind of vipers. These asps are indeed very black, as besides Nicander is taught by Pausanias, Aelian, Aëtius, and some of the Arabs. Granted this, the ancients rightly interpret שחל sachal as asp. Damir tells many fables about the black serpent, which I am loath to relate here. Among them: that Jesus, son of Mary, foretold to a woodcutter that on that very day he would fall into the gravest danger to his life. When he had gone to cut wood and returned towards evening with a bundle of wood, and at Christ's command had untied it, he found in it حية سودا [ḥayya sūdā] a black serpent. Then, when Jesus asked what he had done, and he denied having done anything except that he had given part of his bread to a passing beggar, Jesus replied: بهذا دفع عنك [bi-hādhā dafaʿa ʿanka] For this reason God has turned evil away from you. Others tell the same of col. 379 Saleh, the prophet, and say that the serpent اسود سالخ [aswad sālikh] asuad salich was as thick مثل الجذع [mithl al-jidhʿ] as the trunk of a palm. ¶
