III, 125 col. 357 Chapter I. ¶

Probatur, אפעה ephe esse Viperam.

It is proved that אפעה ephe is the viper. ¶

Inter species serpentum aut vere sunt, aut putantur esse: 1. אפעה ephe. 2. כפיר chephir. 3. עכשוב acsoub. 4. פתן pethen. 5. צבע tseboa. 6. צמאון tsimmaon. 7. צפע tsepha, vel צפעני tsiphoni. 8. קפוז kippoz. 9. שחל sachal. 10. שפיפון sephiphon. 11. שרף saraph. 12. תנין thannin, qui et לויתן leviathan. Itaque res postulat, ut de singulis dicamus, et has species conemur inter se distinguere, quamvis res sit difficillima; quia in Scriptura vix quidquam de una specie dicitur, quod non sit omnibus commune. Neque interpretes sibi constant. Dicam tamen, quid videatur esse vero proximum. Et in hoc capite de אפעה ephe agetur, quae prima est in ordine alphabetico.

Among the species of serpents, either truly or by repute, are: 1. אפעה ephe. 2. כפיר chephir. 3. עכשוב acsoub. 4. פתן pethen. 5. צבע tseboa. 6. צמאון tsimmaon. 7. צפע tsepha, or צפעני tsiphoni. 8. קפוז kippoz. 9. שחל sachal. 10. שפיפון sephiphon. 11. שרף saraph. 12. תנין thannin, which is also לויתן leviathan. The matter therefore requires that we speak of each one and try to distinguish these species from one another, although this is a very difficult thing, since in Scripture hardly anything is said of one species that is not common to all. Nor are the translators consistent. Still, I shall say what seems closest to the truth. In this chapter we shall deal with אפעה ephe, which is first in alphabetical order. ¶

In Historia Templi secundi pag. 135. de Cleopatra dicitur, וקראה להביאו col. 358 לה אפעה והכנסתו תחת דדיה וישך אותה ותמת, Et jussit, ut sibi afferretur אפעה ephe, a qua, uberibus admota, morsu interempta est. Ephe, id est, aspis. Cleopatram enim ab aspide fuisse demorsam, statuit vulgus scriptorum. Strabo libro decimo septimo, Ἑαυτὴν ἐν τῇ φρουρᾷ διεχειρίσατο λάθρα δήγματι ἀσπίδος· Se ipsam in custodia clam interfecit morsu aspidis. Vellejus Paterculus libro secundo, Cleopatra, frustratis custodibus, illata aspide, morsu sane ejus, expers muliebris metus, spiritum reddidit. Suetonius in Augusto, Periisse morsu aspidis putabatur. Plutarchus in Antonio, Λέγεται δὲ τὴν ἀσπίδα κομισθῆ-ναι σὺν τοῖς σύκοις, etc. καὶ τὸν βραχίονα παρασχεῖν τῷ δήγματι γυμνώσασαν· III, 126 Aspidem ajunt cum ficubus fuisse allatam, etc. et illam nudatum brachium ad morsum exhibuisse. Item, Οἱ δὲ τηρεῖσθαι μὲν ἐν ὑδρίᾳ τὴν ἀσπίδα καθειργ-μένην φάσκουσιν. Alii autem in urna clausam aspidem servatam fuisse dicunt. col. 359 Et infra, Οἷς ἔοικε πιστεύσας καὶ ὁ Καῖσαρ· ἐν γὰρ τῷ θριάμβῳ τῆς Κλεοπά-τρας αὐτῆς εἴδωλον ἐκομίζετο, καὶ τῆς ἀσπίδος ἐμπεφυκυίας. Quibus credidisse videtur Caesar: siquidem in triumpho Cleopatrae imago ferebatur, et aspides ei affixae. Aelianus Historiae libri noni capite undecimo, Τὸ τῆς ἀσπίδος δῆγμα πρᾳότατον εἶναι ἤλεγξε Κλεόπατρα· Lenissimum esse aspidis morsum, argumentata est Cleopatra. Et capite sexagesimo primo, postquam morsum aspidis vix deprehendi dixit, quia venenum intus latet, neque in superficiem diffunditur, Ἔνθεν τοι καὶ τῆς Κλεοπάτρας ὁ θάνατος τοῖς ἀμφὶ τὸν Σεβα-στὸν οὐ πάνυ τι ῥᾳδίως ἐγνώσθη· Unde est, quod Cleopatrae mors ab iis, qui circa Augustum erant, non facile cognita est. Dio in Augusto, Ἀπέθανεν, ἀσπί-δος αὐτῇ ἐν ὑδρίᾳ εἰσκομισθείσης. Mortua est, aspide ad se in urna allata. Galenus libri primi Ad Pisonem de Theriaca capite octavo, postquam egit de speciebus aspidum; Τούτων γοῦν φασι τῶν θηρίων τινὶ τὴν βασιλίδα Κλεοπάτραν, βουληθεῖσαν λαθεῖν τοὺς φυλάττοντας, ταχέως τε, καὶ ἀνυπόπτως ἀποθα-νεῖν· Harum igitur ferarum una reginam Cleopatram ajunt, cum vellet latere custodes, statim, et citra suspicionem mortem sibi conscivisse. Tertullianus Ad Martyres capite quarto, Bestias foemina libens appetiit, et utique horridiores aspides serpentes tauro, vel urso, quas Cleopatra immisit sibi, ne in manus inimici perveniret. Sextus Aurelius Victor De viris illustribus in Cleopatra, cum se illi (An-tonio) inferias ferre simularet in Mausoleo ejus, admotis aspidibus periit. Eutropius lib. 7. Cleopatra sibi aspidem admisit, et veneno ejus extincta est. Solinus, Plures diversaeque aspidum species, verum dispares effectus ad nocendum. Dipsas siti interficit. Hypnale, quod somno necat, teste etiam Cleopatra, emitur ad mortem. Isidorus Originum libri duodecimi capite quarto, Hypnalis, genus aspidis dictae, quod somno necat. Hanc sibi Cleopatra apposuit, atque ita morte, quasi somno, soluta est. Omnino igitur scriptori Hebraeo אפעה ephe, quae Cleopatram morsu peremit, est aspis. Quomodo apud Eutychium Alexandrinum illustres illas ancillas jussit eadem Cleopatra ان ياتياها بالافعا التي تسي بالاسغيدة ut sibi afferrent ephe, quam vocant aspidem. Sic enim legendum est pro corrupta voce بالاسيليدة. Ita Iob. XX, 16. לשון אפעה, lingua ephe in Syro est ܠܫܢܐ ܕܐܣܦܣ, lingua aspidis. Et Es. XXX, 6. idem pro אפעה ephe, rursus habet ܐܣܦܣ, aspidem, et Graeci ἀσπίδες.

In the History of the Second Temple, p. 135, it is said of Cleopatra: וקראה להביאו col. 358 לה אפעה והכנסתו תחת דדיה וישך אותה ותמת, And she ordered that אפעה ephe be brought to her; applied to her breasts, it killed her with its bite. Ephe, that is, the asp. For the common run of writers holds that Cleopatra was bitten by an asp. Strabo, book 17: Ἑαυτὴν ἐν τῇ φρουρᾷ διεχειρίσατο λάθρα δήγματι ἀσπίδος [Heautēn en tē phroura diecheirisato lathra dēgmati aspidos]; She secretly killed herself in custody by the bite of an asp. Velleius Paterculus, book 2: Cleopatra, having eluded her guards, had an asp brought in and, free of a woman's fear, gave up her breath at its bite. Suetonius in Augustus: She was thought to have died from the bite of an asp. Plutarch in Antony: Λέγεται δὲ τὴν ἀσπίδα κομισθῆναι σὺν τοῖς σύκοις [Legetai de tēn aspida komisthēnai syn tois sykois], etc. καὶ τὸν βραχίονα παρασχεῖν τῷ δήγματι γυμνώσασαν [kai ton brachiona paraschein tō dēgmati gymnōsasan]; III, 126 They say that the asp was brought in with the figs, etc., and that she bared her arm and offered it to the bite. Again: Οἱ δὲ τηρεῖσθαι μὲν ἐν ὑδρίᾳ τὴν ἀσπίδα καθειργμένην φάσκουσιν [Hoi de tēreisthai men en hydria tēn aspida katheirgmenēn phaskousin]. Others say that the asp was kept shut up in a jar. col. 359 And below: Οἷς ἔοικε πιστεύσας καὶ ὁ Καῖσαρ· ἐν γὰρ τῷ θριάμβῳ τῆς Κλεοπάτρας αὐτῆς εἴδωλον ἐκομίζετο, καὶ τῆς ἀσπίδος ἐμπεφυκυίας [Hois eoike pisteusas kai ho Kaisar; en gar tō thriambō tēs Kleopatras autēs eidōlon ekomizeto, kai tēs aspidos empephykuias]. Caesar seems to have believed them, since in the triumph an image of Cleopatra was carried, with asps fastened to it. Aelian, History, book 9, chapter 11: Τὸ τῆς ἀσπίδος δῆγμα πρᾳότατον εἶναι ἤλεγξε Κλεόπατρα [To tēs aspidos dēgma praotaton einai ēlenxe Kleopatra]; Cleopatra proved that the bite of the asp is the gentlest. And in chapter 61, after saying that the bite of the asp is scarcely detectable, because the poison lies hidden inside and is not spread over the surface: Ἔνθεν τοι καὶ τῆς Κλεοπάτρας ὁ θάνατος τοῖς ἀμφὶ τὸν Σεβαστὸν οὐ πάνυ τι ῥᾳδίως ἐγνώσθη [Enthen toi kai tēs Kleopatras ho thanatos tois amphi ton Sebaston ou pany ti rhadiōs egnōsthē]; Hence it is that the death of Cleopatra was not easily recognized by those around Augustus. Dio in Augustus: Ἀπέθανεν, ἀσπίδος αὐτῇ ἐν ὑδρίᾳ εἰσκομισθείσης [Apethanen, aspidos autē en hydria eiskomistheisēs]. She died, an asp having been brought to her in a jar. Galen, To Piso on Theriac, book 1, chapter 8, after treating the species of asps: Τούτων γοῦν φασι τῶν θηρίων τινὶ τὴν βασιλίδα Κλεοπάτραν, βουληθεῖσαν λαθεῖν τοὺς φυλάττοντας, ταχέως τε, καὶ ἀνυπόπτως ἀποθανεῖν [Toutōn goun phasi tōn thēriōn tini tēn basilida Kleopatran, boulētheisan lathein tous phylattontas, tacheōs te, kai anypoptōs apothanein]; They say, then, that by one of these beasts Queen Cleopatra, wishing to escape the notice of her guards, brought death on herself quickly and without suspicion. Tertullian, To the Martyrs, chapter 4: A woman willingly sought out beasts, and indeed asp serpents more horrid than a bull or a bear, which Cleopatra set upon herself so as not to fall into the hands of her enemy. Sextus Aurelius Victor, On Illustrious Men, under Cleopatra: When she pretended to bring funeral offerings to him (Antony) in his Mausoleum, she died by applying asps. Eutropius, book 7: Cleopatra let an asp at herself and was killed by its poison. Solinus: There are many different species of asps, but unequal in their effects for harm. The dipsas kills by thirst. The hypnale, because it kills by sleep, is bought for death, as Cleopatra also testifies. Isidore, Etymologies, book 12, chapter 4: The hypnalis, a kind of asp so called because it kills by sleep. This Cleopatra applied to herself, and so was released by death as if by sleep. Clearly, then, for the Hebrew writer אפעה ephe, which killed Cleopatra by its bite, is the asp. Likewise in Eutychius of Alexandria the same Cleopatra ordered those famous maidservants ان ياتياها بالافعا التي تسي بالاسغيدة [an yaʾtiyāhā bi-l-afʿā allatī tusī bi-l-asghīda] to bring her the ephe which they call the asp. For so one must read instead of the corrupt word بالاسيليدة [bi-l-asīlīda]. So in Job 20:16. לשון אפעה, the tongue of the ephe, is in the Syriac ܠܫܢܐ ܕܐܣܦܣ [לשנא דאספס], the tongue of the asp. And in Isa 30:6 the same version again has ܐܣܦܣ [אספס], asp, for אפעה ephe, and the Greeks ἀσπίδες [aspides]. ¶

Sed alii βασιλίσκον, aut regulum reddunt, ut Hieronymus, et illi ipsi Graeci Es. LIX, 5. Alii ἔχιδναν, viperam, ut Aquila, et idem Hieronymus in III, 127 Iobo, et Es. XXX, 6. Chaldaeus modo תנינא, draconem, modo חיון, serpentem, modo חרמנין מפרחין, volucres angues. Et Syrus alicubi ܚܘܝܘܢܐ anguiculum, et Arabs الحية الجردا serpentem glabrum, qui et alio nomine dicitur الاقرع calvus. Unde Alcamus, الاقرع من الحيات متعط شعر راسه لكثرة سمه Calvus in serpentibus, is, qui nudatus est pilis capitis propter copiam veneni ejus. Ita, ut e vetustis Scripturae versionibus de hac voce vix quidquam certi possit elici.

But others render it βασιλίσκον [basiliskon], or regulus, as Jerome does, and those same Greeks at Isa 59:5. Others render it ἔχιδναν [echidnan], viper, as Aquila does, and the same Jerome in III, 127 Job and at Isa 30:6. The Chaldee sometimes has תנינא, dragon, sometimes חיון, serpent, sometimes חרמנין מפרחין, flying snakes. And the Syriac in one place has ܚܘܝܘܢܐ [חויונא], little snake, and the Arabic الحية الجردا [al-ḥayya al-jardā] smooth serpent, which is also called by another name, الاقرع [al-aqraʿ] bald. Hence Alcamus [=al-Qāmūs al-muḥīṭ, the Arabic dictionary of al-Fīrūzābādī]: الاقرع من الحيات متعط شعر راسه لكثرة سمه [al-aqraʿ min al-ḥayyāt mutamaʿʿiṭ shaʿr raʾsihi li-kathrat summihi] The bald one among serpents is the one that is stripped of the hair of its head because of the abundance of its poison. Thus hardly anything certain about this word can be drawn from the ancient versions of Scripture. ¶

Hebraeorum magistris eadem hic haesitatio. אפעה ephe a Selomone in Iobum redditur serpens adurens, id est, prester: a Mardochaeo et Pomario, serpens, qui solo aspectu inficit, id est, regulus, seu basiliscus. In qua fuisse sententia col. 360 Kimchium, collegerit aliquis ex eo, quod in Es. XXX. אפעה ephe, et צפעוני tsiphoni, facit esse synonyma. Videtur tamen potius cenchritem significare, cum idem alibi describit אפעה ephe, serpentem variae pellis et maculosae, ex sententia R. Meir, quae nititur auctoritate Chaldaicae paraphrasis, ubi אפעיא sunt הצבועים, id est, ut quidem volunt, picti serpentes et maculosi, 1 Sam. XIII, 18. Taceo nugas Talmudicorum, in arbore siliqua, anno quoque septuagesimo, hujus speciei duos generari serpentes gemini sexus, marem appellari צבוע, et foeminam נפרוא, et quasi certo consilio naturam hic subsistere, ne scilicet nimio mortalitatis damno haec pestis ingravescat.

Among the masters of the Hebrews there is the same hesitation here. אפעה ephe is rendered by Solomon on Job as burning serpent, that is, prester; by Mordecai and Pomarius [=David de Pomis, Ṣemaḥ David (Venice 1587)] as the serpent which infects by its look alone, that is, the regulus or basilisk. That col. 360 Kimchi [=David Kimḥi (Radak), Sefer ha-Shorashim] was of this opinion one might gather from the fact that on Es. XXX he makes אפעה ephe and צפעוני tsiphoni synonyms. Yet he seems rather to mean the cenchrites, since elsewhere the same author describes אפעה ephe as a serpent with a variegated and spotted skin, following the opinion of R. Meir, which rests on the authority of the Chaldee paraphrase, where אפעיא stand for הצבועים, that is, as some would have it, painted and spotted serpents, 1 Sam 13:18. I pass over the trifles of the Talmudists: that in the carob tree, every seventieth year, two serpents of this species are generated, one of each sex, the male being called צבוע and the female נפרוא, and that nature stops here as if by deliberate design, so that this plague may not grow worse through excessive loss of life. ¶

In hac interpretum turba non dubito, quin Aquilae laurea deferenda sit, cui אפעה ephe est vipera. Quod merito sequuntur multi recentiorum: cum hodieque apud Arabes الافعي alephe sonare viperam res sit compertissima, non solum ex Novi Testamenti Arabica paraphrasi, in qua الافعي alephe, id est, vipera, Pauli manui se adalligat, Act. XXVIII, 3. et Pharisaei passim a) vocantur اولاد الافاعي progenies alaphai, id est, viperarum, sed etiam ex Medicorum libris. Sic Avicenna libro secundo Canonis ابوجلسا seu شنجار anchusam, et اناجلس anagallidem, et لوف dracontium, et كرسنة ervum, et قسط costum, et حسك tribulum, et كرم vitem, et فاشرا bryoniam, et بول الانسان urinam hominis praescribit contra morsum الافاعي alaphai, id est, viperarum. Omnia enim haec ex Dioscoride sumta sunt, qui iisdem remediis consulit ἐχιοδήκτοις. Idem Avicenna tradit b), adipem III, 128 الافعي alephe remedium esse efficax contra oculorum hebetudines, et ejusdem carnes adhiberi jubet ad curam leprae c). Quàs ipsas vires viperae carni atque adipi tribuunt Dioscorides, Galenus, Paulus et Aëtius. Denique vocem افعي ephe pro vipera usurpari, constabit luce clarius Graecorum scripta conferenti cum Avicennae tractatibus De lepra, De theriaca, et De morsu الافاعي alaphai (1).

In this crowd of interpreters I do not doubt that the laurel must go to Aquila, for whom אפעה ephe is the viper. Many of the more recent scholars rightly follow him, since it is a well-established fact that even today among the Arabs الافعي [al-afʿī] alephe means viper. This is clear not only from the Arabic paraphrase of the New Testament, in which الافعي [al-afʿī] alephe, that is, the viper, fastens itself on Paul's hand, Acts 28:3, and the Pharisees are repeatedly a) called اولاد الافاعي [awlād al-afāʿī] offspring of alaphai, that is, of vipers, but also from the books of the physicians. Thus Avicenna [=Ibn Sīnā, al-Qānūn fī l-ṭibb (the Canon)], in book 2 of the Canon, prescribes ابوجلسا [abūjalsā] or شنجار [shinjār] anchusa, and اناجلس [anājalis] anagallis, and لوف [lūf] dracontium, and كرسنة [kirsanna] bitter vetch, and قسط [qusṭ] costus, and حسك [ḥasak] caltrop, and كرم [karm] the vine, and فاشرا [fāshirā] bryony, and بول الانسان [bawl al-insān] human urine against the bite of الافاعي [al-afāʿī] alaphai, that is, of vipers. For all these are taken from Dioscorides, who recommends the same remedies for ἐχιοδήκτοις [echiodēktois]. The same Avicenna reports b) that the fat of III, 128 الافعي [al-afʿī] alephe is an effective remedy against dimness of the eyes, and he orders its flesh to be used for the treatment of leprosy c). These very powers are attributed to the flesh and fat of the viper by Dioscorides, Galen, Paul and Aëtius. Finally, that the word افعي [afʿī] ephe is used for the viper will be clearer than daylight to anyone who compares the writings of the Greeks with Avicenna's treatises On leprosy, On theriac, and On the bite of الافاعي [al-afāʿī] alaphai R1. ¶

Praecipue notanda sunt, quae ad descriptionem pertinent: ut, cum dicit Avicenna libro quinto pag. 179. in capite, cui titulus صنعة اقراص الافاعي Ars trochiscorum alaphai, id est, e viperis, الافاعي هي الحياة المفرطحة الرووس المستعرضتها خصوصا عند قرب الرقبة الدقاق رقابها جدا البتر اذنابها الفحاحة الكشاشة وليس يصلح لهذا الاقراص كل الافاعي بل الشقر ومن الشقر الاناث وعلامتها ان الذكر col. 361 في كل شدق ناب واحد وللاناث اكثر من ناب واحد Alaphai (viperae) sunt serpentes plani capitis, et lati maxime circa collum, colli valde tenuis, caudae mutilae, clari sibili, et sonori incessus. Non tamen ad hos trochiscos bonae sunt omnes الافاعي alaphai (viperae) sed rubrae, et e rubris foeminae: quarum nota est, quod, cum mari ab unaquaque maxillae parte dens caninus sit unus, foemina plures habeat (2). Hinc idem lib. 4. in capite في III, 129 لسع الافاعي De morsu alaphai (viperarum) Arab. pag. 137. Lat. Fen. 6. tract. 3. cap. 32. لسع الانثي تعرف بوجود مغارز لاكثر من نابين في الجهة التي عض بها Foeminae morsus ex eo cognoscitur, quod plures duobus caninis dentibus infixi reperiantur in ea parte, quam morsu attigit. Quae ad viperae descriptionem pertinere, nemo non videt, de qua sic Aelianus lib. 10. Hist. cap. 9. Νίκανδρος δέ φησιν, ἐκ μὲν τοῦ δήγμα-τος, ὅπερ οὖν ὁ ἔχις ἐμφύει, δύο ὀδόντων ἴχνη φαίνεσθαι· πλέον δὲ, εἰ δάκοι ἡ ἔχιδνα. Nicander ait, ex morsu, quem infligit echis (id est, vipera mas) duo dentium vestigia apparere; sed plurimum, cum mordet echidna, id est, vipera foemina: quod de caninis intelligendum. Ita enim Nicander in Theriacis versu 231.

Particular note should be taken of what pertains to the description. Haec et alia plus satis docent, Hebraeorum אפעה et Arabum افعى esse viperam. Neque obscura est ratio nominis. Nam Hebraice פעה est clamare contenta voce: inde igitur dicta est אפעה, vipera. Cui الفحيحة, id est, clarum sibilum, Avicenna supra d) tribuit. Et eandem Itali circulatores, Massario teste apud Gesnerum, suffili appellant ab horrendo sibilo. Quia, cum divexatur, tales edit sibilos, ut ad ictum unius lapidis audiatur. Porro in partu potissimum eos intendi verisimile est; quia tum acerbissimo dolore conflictatur. At-que id est proprie פעה. Neque enim de clamore quovis verbum פעה usurpatur, col. 364 sed de clamore in partu. Sic Es. XLII. 14. כיולדה אפעה, ut parturiens clamabo (4). Inde obstetricem Aegyptiam Exod. I, 15. פועה, vocitatam Hebraei volunt, quod tali mulierum parturientium ejulatu soleret inclamari. Proinde אפעה ephe proprie id serpentis genus significat, cui dolores partus extorquent vehementiorem sibilum (5). Quod viperae maxime convenit. Non, quod catuli ejus exeant erosa matris ab alvo; ut a veteribus creditum. Id enim ut fabulosum merito rejecit Theophrastus, et post eum Aelianus Historiae lib. 15. cap. 16. Sed neque id certum, quod illi statuunt, ejus uterum propter angustias in ipso partu disrumpi. Certe Philosophus uterum in partu rumpi non dicit, sed membranas, quibus catuli involvuntur. Verba sunt Historiae lib. 5. capite 34. Τίκτει δὲ III, 133 μικρὰ ἐχίδια ἐν ὑμέσιν, οἳ περιῤῥήγνυνται τριταῖοι· Parvas viperas in membranis parit, quae tertia die rumpuntur. At commento dedit occasionem, quod sequitur, Ἐνίοτε δὲ καὶ ἔσωθεν διαφαγόντα αὐτὰ ἐξέρχεται· Quandoque etiam, dum intus sunt, exedentes (non matris uterum, sed membranas, quas dixerat) per se exeunt. Viperae tamen ideo partus est difficilior, quia vivos parit catulos, cum reliquae serpentes solum ova excludant. Unde est, quod vipera dicitur, aut quasi vi pariat, aut potius quia vivipara. Ex his jam abunde constat, אפעה ephe esse viperam. Itaque haec versio convenit satis tribus Scripturae locis, quibus occurrit illa vox; quod patebit ea percurrenti.

These and other things show more than enough that the אפעה of the Hebrews and the افعى [afʿā] of the Arabs is the viper. Nor is the reason for the name obscure. For in Hebrew פעה means to cry out with a strained voice: hence, therefore, the viper is called אפעה. Avicenna above d) attributes to it الفحيحة [al-faḥīḥa], that is, a clear hiss. And the Italian mountebanks, on the testimony of Massario in Gesner [=Conrad Gesner, Historia animalium], call it suffili from its horrible hissing, because, when it is harassed, it gives out such hisses that they are heard a stone's throw away. Further, it is likely that these hisses are strained most of all in giving birth, because it is then afflicted with the sharpest pain. And this is properly פעה. For the verb פעה is not used of just any cry, col. 364 but of the cry in childbirth. Thus Isa 42:14. כיולדה אפעה, like a woman in labour I will cry out R4. Hence the Hebrews hold that the Egyptian midwife in Exod 1:15 was called פועה, because she was customarily called upon with such wailing of women in labour. Accordingly אפעה ephe properly signifies that kind of serpent from which the pains of birth wring a more violent hiss R5. This fits the viper above all. Not because its young come out by gnawing through their mother's belly, as the ancients believed. For Theophrastus rightly rejected this as fabulous, and after him Aelian, History book 15, ch. 16. But neither is it certain, as they maintain, that its womb is burst during birth itself because of its narrowness. Certainly the Philosopher does not say that the womb bursts in birth, but the membranes in which the young are wrapped. His words are in the History, book 5, chapter 34: Τίκτει δὲ III, 133 μικρὰ ἐχίδια ἐν ὑμέσιν, οἳ περιῤῥήγνυνται τριταῖοι [Tiktei de mikra echidia en hymesin, hoi perirrhēgnyntai tritaioi]· It gives birth to small vipers in membranes, which break on the third day. But what follows gave occasion to the fiction: Ἐνίοτε δὲ καὶ ἔσωθεν διαφαγόντα αὐτὰ ἐξέρχεται [Eniote de kai esōthen diaphagonta auta exerchetai]· Sometimes also, while they are inside, they gnaw through (not the mother's womb, but the membranes he had mentioned) and come out by themselves. Yet the viper's birth is more difficult for this reason, that it bears live young, whereas the other serpents only hatch eggs. Hence it is called vipera [viper], either as if vi pariat [it gives birth by force], or rather because it is vivipara [viviparous]. From this it is now abundantly clear that אפעה ephe is the viper. And so this rendering fits well enough the three passages of Scripture in which that word occurs, as will be evident to anyone who runs through them. ¶

Porro, quia viperae in secundo illo serpentium ordine notissimae sunt, hinc factum, ut viperarum nomen commune esset omnibus ejusdem ordinis serpentibus. Hebraeum igitur אפעה ephe, etsi proprie viperam sonet, tamen, ut Arabicum أفعى, potuit patere latius, atque inde fieri, quod interpretes hanc vocem in tam diversa significata distrahunt. Neque soli Hebraei, aut Arabes viperae nomine sic abusi sunt: sic enim in Aëtio dipsas definitur viperae species, quae in maritimis magis locis reperitur. Et in Antipatro Sidonio Anthologiae lib. 3. cap. 16.

Although it is by no means necessary to understand the viper precisely in that place. For even though אפעה ephe properly means the viper, as what we have taught so far amply proves, it is nevertheless certain that among the Arabs the word افعي [afʿī] ephe extends much more widely. This is proved by the following words of Avicenna, Canon book 4, Fen 4, tract. 3, ch. 3, which is On the Cure of Leprosy: ولا يجب ان تكون الافعي سبخية او ريغية ولا رقية ولا شطية فانها في الاكثر قليلة المنفعة ولكثير منها غاياة التعطيش والاتلاف به [wa-lā yajibu an takūna al-afʿī sabakhiyya aw rīghiyya wa-lā raqqiyya wa-lā shaṭṭiyya fa-innahā fī al-akthar qalīlat al-manfaʿa wa-li-kathīr minhā ghāyāt al-taʿṭīsh wa-al-itlāf bihi] col. 366 بل تختار الجبلية لاسيما البيض [bal tukhtār al-jabaliyya lā siyyamā al-bīḍ] The alephe (viper) should not be from salty places, or muddy ones, or riverbanks, or seashores; for such a one is, for the most part, of little efficacy. Above all, among these the one that excites a deadly thirst (the dipsas) is fatal. Let a mountain one therefore be chosen, especially a white one, etc. And in book 5, in the chapter On Troches made from Viper's Flesh: وليس يصلح لهذه الاقراص كل الافاعي بل شقر [wa-laysa yaṣluḥu li-hādhihi al-aqrāṣ kull al-afāʿī bal shuqr] Not all alaphai are suitable for these troches, but only the yellow ones, etc. And a little later: ويجب ان تجتنب الضارنة والرقم والرقش الضارية الي البياض ولا تصطاد من السباخ وشطوط الاودية الانهار والبحار ولا المشجرة فان فيها البلوطية الخبيثة المعطشة [wa-yajibu an tujtanaba al-ḍārina wa-al-ruqm wa-al-ruqsh al-ḍāriya ilā al-bayāḍ wa-lā tuṣṭād min al-sibākh wa-shuṭūṭ al-awdiya al-anhār wa-al-biḥār wa-lā al-mushajjara fa-inna fīhā al-ballūṭiyya al-khabītha al-muʿṭisha] And the horned ones (vipers) must be rejected, and the variegated or two-coloured ones tending toward white. Nor should they be sought from salty places, or the banks of torrents and rivers, and the shores of seas; nor from trees, because in them are the dryini, foul and thirst-exciting. And in the chapter On the Species of Serpents, which is book 4, Fen 6, tract. 3, ch. 21: والطبقة الثانية من الافاعي ونحوها ايضا مختلفة منها الافاعي الاصلية ومنها الافاعي البدوطية ومنها III, 136 المعطشة وساير ما نذكر [wa-al-ṭabaqa al-thāniya min al-afāʿī wa-naḥwihā ayḍan mukhtalifa minhā al-afāʿī al-aṣliyya wa-minhā al-afāʿī al-badūṭiyya wa-minhā al-muʿṭisha wa-sāyir mā nadhkuru] The second order is that of the alaphai (or vipers) and similar creatures, whose species are diverse. For among them are alaphai ῥιζίαι [rhiziai], that is, those living in the roots of trees; likewise alaphai dryinae, and thirst-exciting ones, and the rest, which we shall mention. That is, according to the teaching of the Arabs there are three orders of serpents. The first contains those whose bite rejects all medicine and kills within three hours. The second, those that are treated by vomiting and by eating salted foods, and that bring death only on the third day after the bite. The third, those whose bite is not usually lethal, and is treated only by external remedies and by binding the affected part, so that the poison does not creep further. Further, since vipers are the best known in that second order of serpents, it came about that the name of vipers was common to all serpents of the same order. Therefore the Hebrew אפעה ephe, although it properly means the viper, could nevertheless, like the Arabic أفعى [afʿā], extend more widely; and hence it happens that interpreters pull this word apart into such diverse meanings. Nor were the Hebrews or the Arabs alone in so stretching the name of the viper: for in Aëtius the dipsas is defined as a species of viper that is found more in coastal places. And in Antipater of Sidon, Anthology book 3, ch. 16: ¶

— — — — διψὰς ἔχιδνα
Σαρκὶ τὸν ἐκ γενύων πικρὸν ἐνεῖσα χόλον, col. 367

— — — — διψὰς ἔχιδνα Σαρκὶ τὸν ἐκ γενύων πικρὸν ἐνεῖσα χόλον, [— — — — dipsas echidna Sarki ton ek genyōn pikron eneisa cholon,] col. 367 ¶

dipsas vipera carni ex malis acerbum immittens virus. Et Nazianzenus Iamb. 22.

the dipsas viper, injecting into the flesh the bitter poison from its jaws. And Nazianzen, Iamb. 22: ¶

Διψὰς τίς ἐστι τῶν ἐχιδναίων γενῶν,

Διψὰς τίς ἐστι τῶν ἐχιδναίων γενῶν, [Dipsas tis esti tōn echidnaiōn genōn,] ¶

Dipsas quaedam est ex viperarum genere. Galenus etiam Simplicium lib. 11. cap. 1. viperarum meminit διψάδων cognomine, a quibus τοὺς δηχθέντας φασὶν οὐκ ἐμπίπλασθαι πίνοντας, ἀλλὰ διαῤῥήγνυσθαι πρότερον, ἢ παύσασθαι διψῶν-τας, demorsos ajunt potu nunquam satiari, sed crepare medios antequam sedetur sitis. Apparet, dipsades describi, quae habentur pro viperis propter formae similitudinem. Nam, ut asserit Lucianus in Dipsadibus, ἡ διψὰς ἐστιν ὄφις οὐ πάνυ μέγας, ἐχίδνῃ ὅμοιος, dipsas est serpens non valde magnus, viperae similis. Et Nicander Theriacῶn versu 266.

There is a certain dipsas of the viper kind. Galen too, On Simples book 11, ch. 1, mentions vipers with the surname διψάδων [dipsadōn], by which τοὺς δηχθέντας φασὶν οὐκ ἐμπίπλασθαι πίνοντας, ἀλλὰ διαῤῥήγνυσθαι πρότερον, ἢ παύσασθαι διψῶντας [tous dēchthentas phasin ouk empiplasthai pinontas, alla diarrhēgnysthai proteron, ē pausasthai dipsōntas], they say those bitten are never sated by drinking, but burst in the middle before their thirst is allayed. It is evident that dipsades are described, which are taken for vipers because of the similarity of form. For, as Lucian asserts in the Dipsads, ἡ διψὰς ἐστιν ὄφις οὐ πάνυ μέγας, ἐχίδνῃ ὅμοιος [hē dipsas estin ophis ou pany megas, echidnē homoios], the dipsas is a serpent not very large, similar to a viper. And Nicander, Theriaca, verse 266: ¶

Ναὶ μὴν δίψαδος εἶδος ὁμώσεται αἰὲν ἐχίδνῃ,

Ναὶ μὴν δίψαδος εἶδος ὁμώσεται αἰὲν ἐχίδνῃ, [Nai mēn dipsados eidos homōsetai aien echidnē,] ¶

Sane dipsadis species viperinam semper refert. Cerasten quoque, scribit Gesnerus, a multis censeri inter viperae species, eadem scilicet de causa. Illum quippe describit idem Nicander, versu 259.

Truly the form of the dipsas always resembles the viper's. The cerastes too, Gesner writes, is reckoned by many among the species of viper, evidently for the same reason. For the same Nicander describes it, verse 259: ¶

Ἠΰτ᾽ ἔχιν· τῷ γάρ τε δομὴν ἰνδάλλεται ἴσην,

Ἠΰτ᾽ ἔχιν· τῷ γάρ τε δομὴν ἰνδάλλεται ἴσην, [Ēyt᾽ echin; tō gar te domēn indalletai isēn,] ¶

Tanquam viperam; illi enim corpus est simile. Et in Suida voce Ἔχις, Λέγεται καὶ αἱμόῤῥους γένος ἔχεως· Dicitur etiam haemorrhous genus esse viperae. Quod III, 137 sumptum ex Aeliano libri 15. cap. 13. Sic hydra, ab Hercule devicta, a nonnullis ἔχιδνα, id est vipera, nominatur. Unde Hercules, Acheloo in serpentem mutato, lib. 9. Metamorphoseωs Ovidii,

Like a viper; for its body appears similar to it. And in the Suda, under the word Ἔχις [Echis]: Λέγεται καὶ αἱμόῤῥους γένος ἔχεως [Legetai kai haimorrhous genos echeōs]; The haemorrhous is also said to be a kind of viper. This is III, 137 taken from Aelian, book 15, ch. 13. Thus the hydra overcome by Hercules is called by some ἔχιδνα [echidna], that is, viper. Hence Hercules, when Achelous had been changed into a serpent, in Ovid's Metamorphoses book 9: ¶

Pars quota Lernaeae serpens eris unus echidnae?

What fraction of the Lernaean viper will you, a single serpent, be? ¶

Ita sagittam, qua Nessum occidit Hercules, hydrae Lernaeae veneno tinctam fuisse, omnes scribunt: tamen de eadem sic Diodorus libro 3. Τῆς ἀκίδος τὸν ἐκ τῆς ἐχίδνης ἰὸν ἀνειληφυίας· Cum sagitta echidnae (seu viperae) veneno imbuta fuisset. Iuxta Homerum Iliad. β΄. versu 723. Graeci Philoctetem in Lemno, insula, reliquerunt

So all write that the arrow with which Hercules killed Nessus was dipped in the poison of the Lernaean hydra; yet Diodorus, book 3, speaks of the same thus: Τῆς ἀκίδος τὸν ἐκ τῆς ἐχίδνης ἰὸν ἀνειληφυίας [Tēs akidos ton ek tēs echidnēs ion aneilēphuias]; Since the arrow had been imbued with the poison of the echidna (or viper). According to Homer, Iliad 2, verse 723, the Greeks left Philoctetes on the island of Lemnos ¶

Ἕλκεϊ μοχθίζοντα κακῷ ὀλοόφρονος ὕδρου,

Ἕλκεϊ μοχθίζοντα κακῷ ὀλοόφρονος ὕδρου, [Helkei mochthizonta kakō oloophronos hydrou,] ¶

Malo ulcere laborantem perniciosi hydri: at in Philoctete Sophoclis Graeci eundem exposuerunt

Suffering from the evil ulcer of the deadly hydrus: but in the Philoctetes of Sophocles the Greeks expressed the same thing as ¶

Πληγέντ᾽ ἐχίδνης ἀγρίῳ χαράγματι,

Πληγέντ᾽ ἐχίδνης ἀγρίῳ χαράγματι, [Plēgent᾽ echidnēs agriō charagmati,] ¶

percussum viperae acerbo vulnere. Sed et Epiphanius in Panario serpentum fere omne genus sub viperae genere complectitur. Sic Cerinthianorum haeresim confert τῇ καλουμένῃ σηπεδόνι ἐχίδνῃ. Et Secundianorum haeresin evangelii calceo conculcari dicit, ὡς μύαγρον ταύτην τὴν ἔχιδναν τὴν πολλαῖς ἑτέραις ἐχίδναις ἐοικυῖαν. Idem in Marcosiis, Διψάδα γάρ τινα ἔχιδνάν φασιν οἱ ἱστορήσαντες, etc. Et in Colorbasiis, Ὡς ἀπὸ ἀμφισβαίνης τῆς δικεφάλου ἐχίδνης. Et in Cataphrygastis, Τῇ αἱμοῤῥοίᾳ ἐχίδνῃ ἐοικυῖα. Et in Origenianis, Ὥσπερ τὴν δεινὴν σκυτάλην, τὴν ἔχιδναν καλουμένην. Et in Paulo Samosateno, Δρυΐνας τις ἔχις οὕτω καλουμένη. Unde illum apparet pro viperis habuisse sepedonem, et myagrum, et dipsadem, et amphisbaenam, et haemorrhoum, et scytalen, et dryinum, et quid non?

struck by the cruel wound of a viper. And Epiphanius too, in the Panarion, includes almost every kind of serpent under the genus of viper. Thus he compares the heresy of the Cerinthians τῇ καλουμένῃ σηπεδόνι ἐχίδνῃ [tē kaloumenē sēpedoni echidnē]. And he says that the heresy of the Secundians is trampled under the shoe of the gospel, ὡς μύαγρον ταύτην τὴν ἔχιδναν τὴν πολλαῖς ἑτέραις ἐχίδναις ἐοικυῖαν [hōs myagron tautēn tēn echidnan tēn pollais heterais echidnais eoikuian]. Likewise on the Marcosians: Διψάδα γάρ τινα ἔχιδνάν φασιν οἱ ἱστορήσαντες [Dipsada gar tina echidnan phasin hoi historēsantes], etc. And on the Colorbasians: Ὡς ἀπὸ ἀμφισβαίνης τῆς δικεφάλου ἐχίδνης [Hōs apo amphisbainēs tēs dikephalou echidnēs]. And on the Cataphrygians: Τῇ αἱμοῤῥοίᾳ ἐχίδνῃ ἐοικυῖα [Tē haimorrhoia echidnē eoikuia]. And on the Origenists: Ὥσπερ τὴν δεινὴν σκυτάλην, τὴν ἔχιδναν καλουμένην [Hōsper tēn deinēn skytalēn, tēn echidnan kaloumenēn]. And on Paul of Samosata: Δρυΐνας τις ἔχις οὕτω καλουμένη [Dryinas tis echis houtō kaloumenē]. From this it is evident that he regarded as vipers the sepedon, the myagrus, the dipsas, the amphisbaena, the haemorrhous, the scytale, the dryinus, and what not? ¶
