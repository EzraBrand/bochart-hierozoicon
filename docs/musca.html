De Musca.

On the Fly. ¶

Vel in ipsa Scriptura, vel in vetustis Scripturae versionibus, septem leguntur muscarum species. I. Musca, proprie dicta, Hebraice זבוב zebub, Graece μυῖα, Eccles. X, 1. Muscae mortuae faciunt, ut foetorem eructet unguentum pigmentarii. Esa. VII, 18. Sibilabit Dominus ad muscam, quae est in extremo fluminum Aegypti. II. Apis, Hebraeis דברה debora; Graecis μέλιττα, Deut. I, 44. Persequuti sunt vos, ut faciunt apes. Iud. XIV, 8. Et, ecce! examen apum in corpore leonis. Psalm. CXVIII, 12. Circumdederunt me ut apes. Esa. VII, 18. Sibilabit Dominus ad muscam, etc. et ad apem, quae est in terra Assyriae. III. Vespa, vel crabro, Hebraeis צרעה tsira: Graecis σφὴξ, vel σφηκία, ut habent Graeci interpretes Exod. XXIII, 28. Mittam crabronem ante te, qui expellet Hevaeum, etc. Deut. VII, 20. Etiam crabronem immittet Dominus Deus tuus in eos. Ios. XXIV, 12. Misi ante vos crabronem, qui expulit eos a facie vestra. IV. Οἶστρος, oestrum, seu asilus: ad cujus nomen alludentes Graeci dicunt Ezech. II, 6. Διότι παροιστρήσουσιν, Quia, ut oestro perciti, insanient, et Os. IV, 16. Ὡς δάμαλις παροιστρῶσα παρεῖστρησεν Ἰσραήλ· Ut juvenca, oestro percussa, insanivit Israel. V. Κυνόμυια, seu canina musca, in Graeca versione saepe nominatur. Sic Exod. VIII, 21. Ἰδοὺ, ἐγὼ ἐξαποστέλλω ἐπὶ σὲ κυνόμυιαν. Et passim reliquo capite. Et Psal. LXXVII, 45. Ἐξαπέ- col. 497 στειλεν εἰς αὐτοὺς κυνόμυιαν. Et Psal. CIV, 31. Εἶπε, καὶ ἦλθε κυνόμυια. Sed Hieronymus in fine Epistolae ad Suniam et Fretellam, legi vult κοινόμυιαν. Pro quo Aquila παμμυῖαν habet, quia Hebraeum ערב arob a mixtura putatur sumptum. Itaque sunt etiam in Hebraeis, qui sic interpretantur. Sed de his infra suo loco. VI. Ad muscarum species potest referri κώνωψ, culex, de quo Matth. XXIII, 24. Οἱ διυλίζοντες τὸν κώνωπα, τὴν δὲ κάμηλον καταπίνοντες, Excolantes culicem, camelum autem deglutientes. VII. Ut et σκνίπες (vel, ut alii legunt, σκνίφες) Exod. VIII, 16. Ἔσονται σκνίπες ἔν τε τοῖς ἀνθρώποις, καὶ ἐν τοῖς τετράποσι καὶ ἐν πάσῃ γῇ Αἰγύπτου. Et Psal. CIV, 31. Καὶ σκνίπες ἐν πᾶσι τοῖς ὁρίοις αὐτῶν. Vulgatus quoque interpres et Hieronymus, sciniphes. Sed Hebraeum כנים Iosephus et Hebraei recentiores pediculos verius reddunt. Iam dicemus de musca, proprie sic vocata.

Either in Scripture itself or in the ancient versions of Scripture, seven kinds of fly are found. I. The fly properly so called, in Hebrew זבוב zebub, in Greek μυῖα [myia], Eccl 10:1: Dead flies make the perfumer's ointment give off a stench. Isa 7:18: The Lord will hiss for the fly that is in the uttermost part of the rivers of Egypt. II. The bee, in Hebrew דברה debora, in Greek μέλιττα [melitta], Deut 1:44: They chased you, as bees do. Iud. XIV, 8: And behold, a swarm of bees in the body of the lion. Ps 118:12: They surrounded me like bees. Isa 7:18: The Lord will hiss for the fly, etc. and for the bee that is in the land of Assyria. III. The wasp or hornet, in Hebrew צרעה tsira, in Greek σφὴξ [sphēx] or σφηκία [sphēkia], as the Greek translators have it, Exod 23:28: I will send the hornet before you, which will drive out the Hivite, etc. Deut 7:20: Moreover the Lord your God will send the hornet among them. Josh 24:12: I sent the hornet before you, which drove them out from before you. IV. Οἶστρος [oistros], the oestrum or asilus [gadfly]. Alluding to its name, the Greeks say at Ezek 2:6 Διότι παροιστρήσουσιν [Dioti paroistrēsousin], For, as if stung by the gadfly, they will rage, and at Hos 4:16 Ὡς δάμαλις παροιστρῶσα παρεῖστρησεν Ἰσραήλ [Hōs damalis paroistrōsa pareistrēsen Israēl]: Like a heifer struck by the gadfly, Israel has raged. V. The κυνόμυια [kynomyia], or dog-fly, is often named in the Greek version. Thus Exod 8:21 Ἰδοὺ, ἐγὼ ἐξαποστέλλω ἐπὶ σὲ κυνόμυιαν [Idou, egō exapostellō epi se kynomyian], and throughout the rest of the chapter; and Ps 77:45 Ἐξαπέ- col. 497 στειλεν εἰς αὐτοὺς κυνόμυιαν [Exapesteilen eis autous kynomyian]; and Ps 104:31 Εἶπε, καὶ ἦλθε κυνόμυια [Eipe, kai ēlthe kynomyia]. But Jerome, at the end of his Letter to Sunnia and Fretela, wants κοινόμυιαν [koinomyian] to be read. For this Aquila has παμμυῖαν [pammyian], because the Hebrew ערב arob is thought to be taken from mixture. Accordingly there are also some among the Hebrews who interpret it so. But of these below, in their proper place. VI. To the kinds of fly may be referred κώνωψ [kōnōps], the gnat, of which Matt 23:24 Οἱ διυλίζοντες τὸν κώνωπα, τὴν δὲ κάμηλον καταπίνοντες [Hoi diylizontes ton kōnōpa, tēn de kamēlon katapinontes], Straining out the gnat, but swallowing the camel. VII. As also σκνίπες [sknipes] (or, as others read, σκνίφες [skniphes]), Exod 8:16 Ἔσονται σκνίπες ἔν τε τοῖς ἀνθρώποις, καὶ ἐν τοῖς τετράποσι καὶ ἐν πάσῃ γῇ Αἰγύπτου [Esontai sknipes en te tois anthrōpois, kai en tois tetraposi kai en pasē gē Aigyptou]. And Ps 104:31 Καὶ σκνίπες ἐν πᾶσι τοῖς ὁρίοις αὐτῶν [Kai sknipes en pasi tois horiois autōn]. The Vulgate translator too and Jerome have sciniphes. But Josephus and the more recent Hebrews more correctly render the Hebrew כנים as lice. Now we shall speak of the fly properly so called. ¶

Musca, ut Hebraice זבוב zebub, sic Chaldaice דבובא debuba; Syriace ܕܒܒܐ debaba; et Arabice ذباب dsebab, dicitur levi mutatione. Nominis originem, Hebraeis et Syris ignotam, aperiunt Arabes, apud quos ذب dsabba, incertis sedibus divagari, nec in uno loco consistere significat. Quod muscae III, 342 convenire, nemo est, qui nesciat. Hinc Lucianus in Muscae encomio, Νεοττιὰν δὲ, ἢ καλίαν, οὐχ᾽ ἑνὶ τόπῳ κατεστήσατο, ἀλλὰ πλάνητα τὴν πτῆσιν κατὰ τοὺς Σκύθας ἐπανῃρημένη, ὅπου ἂν τύχῃ ὑπὸ τῆς νυκτὸς καταληφθεῖσα, ἐκεῖ καὶ ἑστίαν καὶ εὐνὴν ποιεῖται· Nidum autem, aut nidulum, non uno in loco constituit, sed, Scytharum in morem, vago volatu sublata, ubicunque a nocte fuerit deprehensa, ibi domum et cubile facit. Eodem pertinet, quod, qui maxime vagus est et inconstans, Arabico proverbio dicitur esse اطيَش من ذباب Musca in- constantior (1).

The fly, as in Hebrew זבוב zebub, so in Chaldee is called דבובא debuba, in Syriac ܕܒܒܐ [דבבא] debaba, and in Arabic ذباب [dhubāb] dsebab, with slight change. The origin of the name, unknown to the Hebrews and Syrians, is disclosed by the Arabs, among whom ذب [dhabba] dsabba means to wander about with no fixed abode and not to stay in one place. That this suits the fly, there is no one who does not know. III, 342 Hence Lucian in the Encomium of the Fly: Νεοττιὰν δὲ, ἢ καλίαν, οὐχ᾽ ἑνὶ τόπῳ κατεστήσατο, ἀλλὰ πλάνητα τὴν πτῆσιν κατὰ τοὺς Σκύθας ἐπανῃρημένη, ὅπου ἂν τύχῃ ὑπὸ τῆς νυκτὸς καταληφθεῖσα, ἐκεῖ καὶ ἑστίαν καὶ εὐνὴν ποιεῖται [Neottian de, ē kalian, ouch' heni topō katestēsato, alla planēta tēn ptēsin kata tous Skythas epanērēmenē, hopou an tychē hypo tēs nyktos katalēphtheisa, ekei kai hestian kai eunēn poieitai]: She does not establish a nest or a little nest in one place, but, borne up in wandering flight after the manner of the Scythians, wherever she happens to be overtaken by night, there she makes her home and her bed. To the same point belongs the fact that one who is extremely fickle and inconstant is said in an Arabic proverb to be اطيَش من ذباب [aṭyash min dhubāb], more inconstant than a fly R1. ¶

III, 343 Muscarum esum concedunt nonnulli Arabum; quia saepe ex rebus concessis generantur: puta ex faba, quam corruptam ajunt Arabes in muscas ita mutari, ut nihil supersit praeter corticem. Sed Hebraei muscas vetabant, freti lege, quae habetur Deut. XIV, 19. Omne reptile volucre immundum est: non comedetur. Quam Ionathan sic παραφράζει· וכל דיבבי וזיבורי וזיזי דטלופחי ופולי דפרשי מן אוכלא ופרחין הי כעופא מסאבין אינון לכון לא יתאכלון, omnes muscae, et vespae (seu apes) et vermes lentium et fabarum, qui, a cibo separati, avis instar evolant, vobis erunt immundi, non comedentur. Idem concludunt ex his Levit. XI, 20. Omne reptile, volucre, et quadrupes, abominatio erit vobis. Ubi Ionathan, וכל ריחשא דעופא דמהלך על ארבע זני דיבבי וזני אורעי וזני זובורי שיקצא הוא לכון ברם דיבשא דזיבורי יתאכל, Et omne reptile, volucre quadrupes, species muscarum, et species crabronum, et species apum, abominatio erunt vobis: tamen mel apum comedetur. Et ibidem R. Selomo, הם הדקים הנמוכים הרחושים על הארץ כגון זבובין וצרעין ויתושין וחגבין, Haec minuta sunt animalcula, quae super terram repunt, ut muscae, et crabrones, et vespae, et locustae. Si muscas, crabrones, vespas et apes objeceris in censu reptilium quadrupedum ab Hebraeis perperam poni, cum muscarum genus non sit τετρά-πους, sed ἑξάπους· duos pedes anteriores respondebo non haberi pro pedibus; quia manuum vicem potius gerunt. Neque enim iis incedunt, quomodo quatuor reliquis, sed oculos, pulvere obsitos, abstergunt, cibos sumunt, et, quidquid tollendum est, evehunt. Lucianus de musca, Ἑξάπους δὲ οὖσα, τοῖς μὲν τέσσαρσι βαδίζει μόνοις· τοῖς δὲ προσθίοις δυσὶ, καὶ ὅσα χερσὶ χρῆται. Ἴδοις ἂν οὖν αὐτὴν ἐπὶ τεττάρων βεβηκυῖαν, ἔχουσάν τι ἐν ταῖν χεροῖν μετέωρον ἐδώ-διμον ἀνθρωπίνως πάνυ, καὶ καθ' ἡμᾶς. Cum sit sexipes, quatuor tantum pe- col. 498 dibus incedit; aliisque duobus anterioribus utitur tanquam manibus: ipsam igitur pedibus quatuor gradientem videas, et edule aliquid manibus in sublime tollentem, humano prorsus nostroque more. Sed et Philosophus libri quarti De partibus capite sexto, Τοὺς δὲ πόδας τοὺς προσθίους μείζους ἔνια τούτων ἔχει, ὅπως διὰ τὸ σκληρόφθαλμα εἶναι, ἐπειδὴ οὐκ ἀκριβῶς τὴν ὄψιν ἔχει, τὰ προσπίπτοντα τοῖς προσθίον ἀποκαθαίρωσι σκέλεσιν· ὅπερ καὶ φαίνονται ποιοῦσαι αἵ τε μυῖαι, καὶ τὰ μελιττώδη τῶν ζώων· ἀεὶ γὰρ χαρακίζουσι τοῖς προσθίοις σκέλεσι. Pedes priores nonnulla (animalia) longiores habent, ut, quoniam propter oculorum duritiem non exquisite cernunt, prioribus illis pedibus id abstergant, quod in III, 344 oculos incidit. Quod et muscas facere videas, et mellifica quaecunque sunt animalcula. Semper enim prioribus pedibus (tanquam palis) quidvis in sublime tollunt. Ita reddendum. Nam χαρακίζειν hic est μετεωρίζειν, ut est in Hesychio; non vallare et protegere, ut voluit Gaza. Plinius libri undecimi capite quadragesimo octavo, Insectorum pedes primi longiores, duros habentibus oculos, ut subinde pedibus eos tergeant, ceu notamus in muscis. Damir, والذباب ليس له اجفان لصغر احداقه ومن شان الاجفان ان تصقل مراة الحدقه من الغبار فجعل الله له يدين يصقل بها مراة احداقه فلهذا تري الذباب ابدا يمسح بيديه عينيه Muscae non habent palpebras propter pupillae parvitatem: sed, cum palpebrarum proprium sit, ut a speculo pupillae pulverem abstergant, Deus illis dedit manus, quibus id praestent. Illas igitur subinde videas oculos his manibus detergentes.

III, 343 Some of the Arabs permit the eating of flies, because they are often generated from permitted things: for instance from the bean, which the Arabs say, when corrupted, is so changed into flies that nothing remains but the husk. But the Hebrews forbade flies, relying on the law found in Deut 14:19. Every creeping thing that flies is unclean: it shall not be eaten. Which Jonathan [=Targum Jonathan] paraphrases [paraphrazei] thus: וכל דיבבי וזיבורי וזיזי דטלופחי ופולי דפרשי מן אוכלא ופרחין הי כעופא מסאבין אינון לכון לא יתאכלון, all flies, and wasps (or bees) and the worms of lentils and beans which, separated from the food, fly out like a bird, shall be unclean to you; they shall not be eaten. They conclude the same from these words, Lev 11:20. Every creeping thing that flies and goes on four feet shall be an abomination to you. Where Jonathan has: וכל ריחשא דעופא דמהלך על ארבע זני דיבבי וזני אורעי וזני זובורי שיקצא הוא לכון ברם דיבשא דזיבורי יתאכל, And every creeping thing that flies, four-footed, the species of flies, and the species of hornets, and the species of bees, shall be an abomination to you: yet the honey of bees shall be eaten. And in the same place R. Selomo [=Rashi (R. Solomon ben Isaac)]: הם הדקים הנמוכים הרחושים על הארץ כגון זבובין וצרעין ויתושין וחגבין, These are the minute little animals which creep upon the earth, such as flies, and hornets, and wasps, and locusts. If you object that flies, hornets, wasps and bees are wrongly placed by the Hebrews in the class of four-footed creeping things, since the fly kind is not τετράπους [tetrapous] but ἑξάπους [hexapous], I shall reply that the two front feet are not counted as feet, because they rather serve as hands. For they do not walk on them as on the other four; rather with them they wipe their eyes when covered with dust, take their food, and carry off whatever has to be lifted. Lucian on the fly: Ἑξάπους δὲ οὖσα, τοῖς μὲν τέσσαρσι βαδίζει μόνοις· τοῖς δὲ προσθίοις δυσὶ, καὶ ὅσα χερσὶ χρῆται. Ἴδοις ἂν οὖν αὐτὴν ἐπὶ τεττάρων βεβηκυῖαν, ἔχουσάν τι ἐν ταῖν χεροῖν μετέωρον ἐδώδιμον ἀνθρωπίνως πάνυ, καὶ καθ' ἡμᾶς [Hexapous de ousa, tois men tessarsi badizei monois; tois de prosthiois dysi, kai hosa chersi chrētai. Idois an oun autēn epi tettarōn bebēkuian, echousan ti en tain cheroin meteōron edōdimon anthrōpinōs pany, kai kath' hēmas]. Though it is six-footed, it walks on four feet only, and uses the other two in front as hands: so you may see it stepping on four feet and lifting up something edible in its hands, col. 498 entirely in human fashion, as we do. And the Philosopher too, book 4 On the Parts, chapter 6: Τοὺς δὲ πόδας τοὺς προσθίους μείζους ἔνια τούτων ἔχει, ὅπως διὰ τὸ σκληρόφθαλμα εἶναι, ἐπειδὴ οὐκ ἀκριβῶς τὴν ὄψιν ἔχει, τὰ προσπίπτοντα τοῖς προσθίον ἀποκαθαίρωσι σκέλεσιν· ὅπερ καὶ φαίνονται ποιοῦσαι αἵ τε μυῖαι, καὶ τὰ μελιττώδη τῶν ζώων· ἀεὶ γὰρ χαρακίζουσι τοῖς προσθίοις σκέλεσι [Tous de podas tous prosthious meizous enia toutōn echei, hopōs dia to sklērophthalma einai, epeidē ouk akribōs tēn opsin echei, ta prospiptonta tois prosthion apokathairōsi skelesin; hoper kai phainontai poiousai hai te myiai, kai ta melittōdē tōn zōōn; aei gar charakizousi tois prosthiois skelesi]. Some (animals) have longer front feet so that, since because of the hardness of their eyes they do not see precisely, they may wipe off with those front feet whatever III, 344 falls into their eyes. This you may see flies doing too, and all the honey-making little animals. For they always lift up (as on stakes) anything with their front feet. So it should be rendered. For χαρακίζειν [charakizein] here is μετεωρίζειν [meteōrizein], as it is in Hesychius; not vallare [to fence with stakes] and protegere [to protect], as Gaza would have it. Pliny, book 11, chapter 48: In insects that have hard eyes the front feet are longer, so that they may wipe them with their feet from time to time, as we observe in flies. Damir [=al-Damīrī, Ḥayāt al-ḥayawān]: والذباب ليس له اجفان لصغر احداقه ومن شان الاجفان ان تصقل مراة الحدقه من الغبار فجعل الله له يدين يصقل بها مراة احداقه فلهذا تري الذباب ابدا يمسح بيديه عينيه [wa-l-dhubāb laysa lahu ajfān li-ṣighar aḥdāqihi wa-min shaʾn al-ajfān an taṣqul mirāt al-ḥadaqa min al-ghubār fa-jaʿala Allāh lahu yadayn yaṣqul bihā mirāt aḥdāqihi fa-li-hādhā tarī al-dhubāb abadan yamsaḥ bi-yadayhi ʿaynayhi] Flies have no eyelids because of the smallness of the pupil; but since it is the property of eyelids to wipe dust from the mirror of the pupil, God gave them hands with which to do this. So you may see them constantly wiping their eyes with these hands. ¶

Nec sine causa, reptilium, atque in his muscarum, esus Iudaeis prohibitus est: cum musca sit foedum ac foetidum animal, quodque ex immunditia nascitur, atque in immundis libenter haeret. Et, sive ad hominum mensas, aut ad Deorum aras accedat, dapes impudenter diripit, contactuque omnia foedat. Quid, quod pusilla bestia, ad myropolium volans, et in narthecio casu mortua, unguentum, in eo reconditum, totum inficit? Unde illud Solomonis Eccles. X, 1. Muscarum mortuarum una facit, ut foetorem eructet unguentum pigmentarii. Sic parum stultitiae foetere facit hominem, sapientia et gloria eximium. Quomodo dicit Paulus, parum fermenti totam massam corrumpere, 1 Cor. V, 6. Nam multo facilius corrumpitur, quod bonum est, quam emendatur, quod malum. Proinde notat Aggaeus capitis secundi versu duodecimo, et decimo tertio, rei secrae contactu rem immundam non consecrari; sed, contra, sacram pollui contactu rei immundae (2).

Nor was it without reason that the eating of creeping things, and among them of flies, was forbidden to the Jews: for the fly is a foul and stinking animal, born from uncleanness and gladly clinging to unclean things. And whether it comes to the tables of men or to the altars of the gods, it shamelessly plunders the feast and defiles everything by its touch. What of the fact that the tiny beast, flying into a perfumer's shop and dying by chance in an ointment box, taints all the ointment stored in it? Hence that saying of Solomon, Eccl 10:1: One of the dead flies makes the ointment of the perfumer send forth a stench. So a little folly makes a man eminent in wisdom and honour stink. Just as Paul says that a little leaven corrupts the whole lump, 1 Cor 5:6. For what is good is corrupted much more easily than what is bad is amended. Accordingly Haggai, chapter 2, verses 12 and 13, notes that an unclean thing is not consecrated by the touch of a holy thing; but, on the contrary, a holy thing is polluted by the touch of an unclean thing R2. ¶

III, 345 Quaeritur, cur cum muscis conferantur Aegyptii in his Es. VII, 18. Sibilabit Dominus ad muscam, quae est in extremo fluminum Aegypti: et varii varia afferunt. Hieronymus, Muscas autem Aegyptios vocat propter sordes idololatriae et imbellem populum. Basilius respici putat τὸ εὐτελὲς τῶν ἀντικειμένων III, 346 δυνάμεων· μυῖα γάρ ἐστι ζῶον ἀπὸ κόπρου τὴν γένεσιν ἔχον, ὀχληρὸν τοῖς ἀν-θρώποις γένος, καὶ μυσαρὸν ὂν, καὶ ἡ ζωὴ ὀχληρὰ, καὶ ὁ θάνατος βδελυ-κτὸς, vilitatem copiarum, quae Israelitis oppositae sunt: musca enim ex stercore generatur, et genus est hominibus infestum, et abominandum, cujus vita molesta est, et mors execrabilis. At Cyrillus Aegyptios muscis comparari asserit διά τοι τὸ πλῆθος, καὶ τὸ μὴ λίαν εὔηχες τῆς φωνῆς, propter multitudinem et sermonem absonum atque inconditum. Quasi non tam sit sermo; quam muscarum susurrus. From the fly Beel-zebub, the god of the Ekronites, takes his name, 2 Reg. I, 2. The LXX render μυῖαν Θεὸν Ἀκκαρὼν [myian Theon Akkarōn], the fly, the god of Ekron. Many of the ancients follow this. Thus Josephus, book 9, chapter 1, relates that Ahaziah sent πρὸς τὸν Ἀκκάρων Θεὸν μυῖαν, τοῦτο γὰρ ἦν ὄνομα τῷ θεῷ [pros ton Akkarōn Theon myian, touto gar ēn onoma tō theō], to the god of Ekron, the fly, for that was the name of the god. And in Suidas, under the word Ἠλίας [Ēlias], an ancient writer says of the same Ahaziah: Ἐχρήσατο μυίᾳ τῶν ἐν Ἀκκάρων εἰδώλῳ [Echrēsato myia tōn en Akkarōn eidōlō], He consulted the fly, the idol of the Ekronites. Alluding to this, Nazianzen in his fourth Oration says: III, 347 Οὐδὲ ζητήσουσι μυῖαν Θεὸν Ἀκκαρὼν, ἢ εἴ τι ταύτης ἐστὶ γελοιότερον [Oude zētēsousi myian Theon Akkarōn, ē ei ti tautēs esti geloioteron]. Nor will they any longer seek the fly, the god of Ekron, or any other deity more ridiculous than that. The Greek Fathers, that is, think that this god was worshipped under the image of a fly. Theodoret on this passage: Τί μυσαρώτερον μυίας; ἀλλ᾽ ὅμως τὴν εἰκόνα ταύτης Θεὸν ἀνηγόρευσαν [Ti mysarōteron myias? all᾽ homōs tēn eikona tautēs Theon anēgoreusan]. What is more loathsome than a fly? Yet they declared its image to be a god. And a little later: Ἣν ζῶσαν ταῖς ῥιπίσιν ἐλαύνουσι, ταύτης τὸν τύπον Θεὸν προσηγόρευσαν [Hēn zōsan tais rhipisin elaunousi, tautēs ton typon Theon prosēgoreusan]· The creature which, when alive, they drive away with fly-whisks, its figure they called a god. Procopius too ascribes to this same god the image of a fly. Yet he doubts whether it was the name of a demon, or of an old woman raised to the gods. This fancy is refuted by the Hebrew name בעל־זבוב, Beel-zebub, which is compounded of masculine words. I prefer therefore to follow the Latins, for whom Beel-zebub is not, in the nominative, the god fly, but, with a genitive, the god or lord of the fly. And just as Apollo at Athens was called PARNOPIUS, and Hercules on Mount Oeta CORNOPION, because of locusts driven away; and by the Erythraeans Ἰποκτόνος [Ipoktonos], because of the ἶπες [ipes] he killed, that is, the small worms by which vines are infested: so whatever god was believed to drive away flies, as in Greek μυίαγρος [myiagros], μυιώδης [myiōdēs] and ἀπόμυιος [apomyios], will in Hebrew have been called BEEL-ZEBUB, that col. 501 is, lord of flies. Such he was believed to have shown himself, by removing and driving them away from his sacrifices. I therefore think this was the true name of that idol. Nor do I agree with a great man R6, who thinks that he was called by his worshippers בעל זבחים Beel-zebachim, that is, god of sacrifices, which the Israelites in contempt twisted into בעלזבוב Beel-zebub, that is, lord of the fly.
