CHAPTER I. ¶

Aquila, rex avium; ejus nomen, rostrum, ungues, pluma, calvitium, renovatio, color.

The eagle, king of the birds; its name, beak, talons, plumage, baldness, renewal, colour. ¶

Immundas aves in hoc libro eodem ordine proponemus, quo Moses Levit. XI, 13. et Deut. XIV, 2. Prima omnium est aquila. Quo pertinet, quod hic habet Baal Hatturim, פתח בנשר שהוא מלך בעופות, Incepit ab aquila, quia est rex avium. In earum scilicet avium censu, quarum esum Iudaeis interdixit Deus, vult aquilam merito ducere ordinem, cum in reliquas aves regnum ei dederit consensus gentium, propter eximias dotes, quibus supra illas eminet. De Graecis res manifesta est. Aquilam enim vocat Pindarus Olymp. 13.

In this book we shall set out the unclean birds in the same order as Moses in Lev 11:13 and Deut 14:2. First of all is the eagle. To this pertains what Baal Hatturim [=Jacob ben Asher (d. 1343), commentary on the Torah] has here: פתח בנשר שהוא מלך בעופות, He began with the eagle, because it is king of the birds. That is, in the list of those birds whose eating God forbade the Jews, he holds that the eagle rightly leads the line, since the consensus of nations has given it kingship over the other birds because of the outstanding gifts by which it surpasses them. Unde etiam apud Arabes manaverat aquilae cultus. Talmudici in libro De Idololatria cap. 1. fol. 11. b. בתי ע״ז קבועין אלו הן בית בל בבבל בית נבו בכורסי תרעתא שבמפג צריפא שבאשקלון נשרא שבערביא, Templa idololatrica II, 741 fixa haec sunt, templum Beli Babylone, templum Nebo Borsippae, Atergatis Hierapoli, Uraniae Veneris Ascalone, et Aquilae in Arabia. Fixa templa vocantur, quorum idola coluntur omnibus anni diebus, ut explicatur in Glossa. Quomodo Bel Babylone cultus est, et Nebo Borsippae (nam pro בכורסי lege בבורסיף). Et Hierapoli Derceto, vel Atergatis, quam Hierapoli fuisse cultam, multis docet Lucianus in libro De Syria Dea. Derceto, vel Atergatis, Hebraice est תרעתא Tirgata. Et Hierapolis ממבג Mambeg, ut Arabice منبج Manbeg. In Plinio quoque olim Bambyca et Mambeg, quod mutatum in Magog. Itaque in Talmude pro שבמפג legendum שבממבג. Uraniam porro Venerem Ascalone fuisse in summo cultu, ex Herodoto scimus. Proinde haec videtur esse צריפא שבאשקלון, Tseripha, quae Ascalone est. Quamvis, cur vocetur צריפא Tseripha, non satis constet. Nisi a conflando dictam putes. Hebraeis enim צרף tsaraph est conflare. Unde Talmudicis בית נצרפי, domus conflatilium, id est, idolorum templum. Aut a libidine, cui praeest. Nam Arabice صرف tsarapha est ardere libidine, et صارفة tsaripha dicitur canis foemina, cum catulit et ardet libidine in marem. Superest de Arabum aquila dicamus. Fuit id idolum Dulkelaitarum in Homeritis, populo Arabiae felicis notissimo. Alcamus, النسر الصنم كان لذي الكلا بارض حمير Aquila fuit idolum Dulkelae in terra Humeir, id est, Homeritarum. That the Egyptians too held the same view is shown by these words of Diodorus in book 1: Τὸν δ᾽ ἀετὸν Θηβαῖοι τιμῶσι, διὰ τὸ βασιλικὸν εἶναι δοκεῖν τοῦτο ζῶον, καὶ τοῦ Διὸς ἄξιον [Ton d᾽ aeton Thēbaioi timōsi, dia to basilikon einai dokein touto zōon, kai tou Dios axion]· The Thebans worship the eagle, because they think this animal is royal and worthy of Jupiter. From there the cult of the eagle had also spread to the Arabs. The Talmudists, in the treatise On Idolatry, ch. 1, fol. 11b: בתי ע״ז קבועין אלו הן בית בל בבבל בית נבו בכורסי תרעתא שבמפג צריפא שבאשקלון נשרא שבערביא, These are the fixed idolatrous II, 741 temples: the temple of Bel at Babylon, the temple of Nebo at Borsippa, Atergatis at Hierapolis, Venus Urania at Ascalon, and the Eagle in Arabia. Those temples are called fixed whose idols are worshipped on every day of the year, as the Gloss explains. Thus Bel was worshipped at Babylon and Nebo at Borsippa (for instead of בכורסי read בבורסיף). And at Hierapolis Derceto, or Atergatis, whose worship at Hierapolis Lucian describes at length in his book On the Syrian Goddess. Derceto, or Atergatis, is in Hebrew תרעתא Tirgata. And Hierapolis is ממבג Mambeg, as in Arabic منبج [Manbij] Manbeg. In Pliny too the text once read Bambyca and Mambeg, which was altered into Magog. Accordingly in the Talmud one should read שבממבג instead of שבמפג. Further, we know from Herodotus that Venus Urania was held in the highest worship at Ascalon. Hence she seems to be the צריפא שבאשקלון, Tseripha, which is at Ascalon. Why she is called צריפא Tseripha, however, is not quite clear. Unless you think she was named from casting [metal]; for among the Hebrews צרף tsaraph means to cast. Hence the Talmudists' בית נצרפי, house of molten images, that is, a temple of idols. Or from lust, over which she presides. For in Arabic صرف [ṣarafa] tsarapha means to burn with lust, and صارفة [ṣārifa] tsaripha is said of a female dog when she is in heat and burns with lust for the male. It remains for us to speak of the eagle of the Arabs. It was the idol of the Dulkelaites among the Homerites, a very well-known people of Arabia Felix. Alcamus [=al-Qāmūs al-muḥīṭ, the Arabic dictionary of al-Fīrūzābādī, which Bochart cites like an author]: النسر الصنم كان لذي الكلا بارض حمير [al-nasr al-ṣanam kāna li-Dhī al-Kalāʿ bi-arḍ Ḥimyar] The Eagle was the idol of Dulkela in the land of Humeir, that is, of the Homerites. Evidently among the Arabs too this opinion had prevailed, that the eagle is the chief of the birds. So Muaphius, in the book entitled نفحات الازهار [Nafaḥāt al-azhār] Fragrances of Flowers, attributes this to the impure Muhammad: هبط علي جبرييل فقال يا محمد ان لكل شي سيد فسيد البشر ادم وسيد ولد ادم انت وسيد الروم صحيب وسيد فارس سليمان وسيد col. 164 الحبش بلال وسيد الشجر السدر وسيد الطير النسر [habaṭa ʿalayya Jabrīl fa-qāla yā Muḥammad inna li-kull shayʾ sayyid fa-sayyid al-bashar Ādam wa-sayyid walad Ādam anta wa-sayyid al-Rūm Ṣuhayb wa-sayyid Fāris Salmān wa-sayyid al-Ḥabash Bilāl wa-sayyid al-shajar al-sidr wa-sayyid al-ṭayr al-nasr] Gabriel came down to me and said: O Muhammad, every thing has its lord. The lord of men was Adam; but you are the lord of the sons of Adam. And the lord of the Greeks is Sahib, and of the Persians Soliman, and of the Abyssinians Bilal. And of trees the connarus, a species of lotus, and of birds the eagle, etc. Now, if one wishes to hear the Hebrews: Josephus, On the Jewish War, book 3, chapter 5, writes that the Romans had the eagle as the standard in the first cohort of the legion because the ἀετὸς [aetos] is βασιλεύς τε οἰωνῶν ἁπάντων, καὶ ἀλκιμώτατος [basileus te oiōnōn hapantōn, kai alkimōtatos], king of all birds, and the strongest. And Kimchi [=David Kimḥi (Radak), Sefer ha-Shorashim] on Ezek 17:3: הנשר הוא משל לנבוכדנצר מלך בבל כי הנשר מלך העופות וכן נבוכדנצר היה מלך על כל מלכי הארץ, The eagle there is a figure of Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, because, as the eagle is king of the birds, so II, 742 Nebuchadnezzar was king over all the kings of the earth. The figure of the eagle upon its throne, and the council of birds sitting around it. That is, in the council of the birds the eagle is depicted, distinguished by a crown and raised high on a throne, and the other birds stand by it. Nec iis assentior, qui a defluvio pennarum dictam asserunt, quia נשר Rabbinis est defluere. Aut a visu; quia נשר neser videtur alludere ad verbum שור sur, quod videre est. Whence the eagle, in Hebrew נשר neser, gets its name has so far escaped the masters. For absurd is the fancy of Rabbi Bechai on Gen. II. that it derives from the Chaldee נשר nesar, which means to fall down, because, after it has risen in flight to the element of fire, as they indeed would have it, it then falls headlong into the sea. Nor do I agree with those who assert that it is named from the shedding of its feathers, because נשר among the Rabbis means to fall off; or from sight, because נשר neser seems to allude to the verb שור sur, which means to see. Rather, I most approve of the Arabs, for whom نسر [nasara] nasara means to pluck with the beak, and منسر [minsar] minsar means beak, and properly that of a bird of prey. From this, then, they hold that the eagle is called نسر [nasr] nesr, لانه ينسر الشي ويقتلعه [li-annahu yansiru al-shay wa-yaqtaliʿuhu] because it tears and plucks a thing with its beak. So Giauhari [=al-Jawharī, al-Ṣiḥāḥ], Damir [=al-Damīrī, Ḥayāt al-ḥayawān] and Alcamus. But many of the ancients also believed that in Ezek 17:3 7. the eagle is called מלא הנוצה and רב הנוצה on account of its talons. For this is rendered by the Greeks ὁ ἀετὸς πλήρης ὀνύχων [ho aetos plērēs onychōn], the eagle full of talons, and πολὺς ὄνυξι [polys onyxi], great in talons, or having many talons. The Arabic follows the same. Nor does the Syriac differ much in verse 3, ܡܠܝܢ ܛܦܪ̈ܘܗܝ [מלין טפרוהי], its talons are full, and in verse 7, ܣܓܝܐܢ ܛܦܪܘܗܝ [סגיאן טפרוהי] its talons are many. For נצה means to fly, whence נצו they flew, or flew away, Lam 4:15, and נוצה feather or plumage, which lifts birds up to flight. This suits this passage better. For I, at least, cannot understand what an eagle full of talons, or with many talons, would be, since all eagles have the same number of talons. Among the kinds of eagles, many take their name from their colour. R6 Rosenmüller, II, 749 This very eagle, which he had carefully observed, was described most accurately by Aldrovandi [=Ulisse Aldrovandi, Ornithologia (1599–1603)], Ornithol. vol. 1, book 2, p. 112. There, among other things, he says: "The colour of the wings and tail was dusky, the more so the larger the feathers. That of the other, smaller feathers of the whole body was a rusty or chestnut brown," with white spots scattered here and there, few on the back, more on the belly, the roots everywhere white. "The flight feathers of the wings were six on each side, two spans and a palm long, with very firm and hard quills." You see that Bochart, with every right, refers Ezekiel's words to the ἀστερίας [asterias], which is also the χρυσάετος [chrysaetos], the largest of the eagles.
