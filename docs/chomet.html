II, 500 col. 1075 CAP. V.

II, 500 col. 1075 Chapter 5. ¶

Probatur, חומט chomet esse lacertam, in arena degentem, quae Arabibus حلكا chulaca dicitur.

It is proved that חומט chomet is a lizard living in the sand, which the Arabs call حلكا [ḥlkā] chulaca. ¶

Hbraeis recentioribus, Selomoni, Kimchio, Pomario et Aquinati חומט chomet est limax. Neque aliud volunt Hispani illi, qui in Kimchio vernacule כרבר reddunt. Legendum enim כר״כול caracol, quomodo limacem illi appellant. Id sequuntur Arias, Munsterus, Petrus Robertus, Genevenses et Angli, qui hic quoque limacem habent. Tamen aliter veteres. Graecis enim et Hieronymo חומט chomet est σαῦρα, lacerta. Et Arabibus editis حربا chirba, chamaeleon: manu-scriptis سام ابرص sammabras, stellio. Neque aliud hic Syrus ܚܘܠܡܛܐ chaulmata juxta Gabrielem Sionitam, cui malo credere, quam Baptistae Ferrario, qui reddit: avem, dorsum habentem, nigris et purpureis pennis distinctum; plane ἀπροσδιονύσως. Neque enim de avibus hic agitur, sed de terrae reptilibus. Itaque, ut superiora, ita etiam חומט chomet videtur esse in lacertorum specie. Neque latet origo vocis. Talmudicis enim חומטון chometon est arena. Hinc in tractatu De Sabbatho cap. 5. fol. 54. a. (ubi Talmudici disputant, quorsum 1 Reg. IX, 13. Hiramus, rex Tyriorum, ארץ כבול, terram chabul appellaverit tractum illum, quo donaverat eum Solomon) dicit R. Nach- col. 1076 man, Isaaci filius, ארץ חומטין היתה אמאי קרו לה כבול דמשתרגה בה כרעא עד כבלא Terra erat chometon, unde est, quod vocarunt eam terram chabul quia in ea crus usque ad talum immergebatur. Et Kimchius, hunc locum citans in Lexico Radice כבל, sed verbis paullum immutatis, חומטון chometon explicat חול chol, id est, arenam. Ita, ut illi terra chometon sit terra arenae, vel arenosa. Baal Aruch itidem totum locum ita exponit: פי׳ שרגליו של אדם משתקעות בה מחמת החול כאילו הן כבולין לכן אינה עושה פירות Significat pedes hominis in ea (terra) fuisse immersos propter arenae (copiam) ac si fuissent II, 501 compediti, ac proinde illam fuisse infrugiferam. In Glossa Talmudis terra chome- ton est terra מלחה ומתבקעת salsuginosa et diffissa. Et ejusdem tractatus cap. 2. fol. 31. a. חומטון Glossatori est ארץ מלחה ומשמרת את הפירות מהתליע, ter- ra salsuginosa, quae fructus a vermiculatione servat. Sed prior interpretatio est verior, et utrique loco apprime convenit. Nam et incedentium pedibus arena molesta est, et in calidis locis vim habet ἀρτυτικὴν, qua res a corruptione diu servat. Itaque in Africae arenis cadavera condiri multi asserunt et pro mumia distrahi. Quin vox חומטון chometon, quamvis non extet in libris Propheticis, videtur esse valde antiqua (1). Et Graecos puto, aspiratione demta, inde deduxisse ἄμαθον, et, cum προθέσει, ψάμαθον. Quorum utrumque in vetustissimis scriptoribus, Homero puta et aliis, in arenae notione reperitur. Ita tamen, ut ἄμαθον Grammatici velint arenam esse μεσόγειον, mediterraneam, et ψάμαθον παραθαλασσίαν, seu littoralem. Sic fere distinguunt Hesychius, Suidas, alii.

For the more recent Hebrews, Solomon, Kimchi [=David Kimḥi (Radak), Sefer ha-Shorashim], Pomarius [=David de Pomis, Ṣemaḥ David (Venice 1587)] and Aquinas [=Philippe d'Aquin, Dictionarium hebraeo-chaldaeo-talmudico-rabbinicum (Paris 1629)], חומט chomet is the limax [slug]. Nor do those Spaniards mean anything else who in Kimchi render it in the vernacular as כרבר. For one should read כר״כול caracol, which is what they call the slug. This is followed by Arias [=Benito Arias Montano, the interlinear Latin of the Antwerp Polyglot], Münster, Pierre Robert, the Genevans and the English, who here too have limax [slug]. The ancients, however, thought otherwise. For to the Greeks and to Jerome חומט chomet is σαῦρα [saura], a lizard. And in the printed Arabic versions it is حربا [ḥirbā] chirba, the chameleon; in the manuscript ones سام ابرص [sām abraṣ] sammabras, the stellio. Nor does the Syriac ܚܘܠܡܛܐ [חולמטא] chaulmata mean anything else here, according to Gabriel Sionita [=translator of al-Idrīsī's geography into Latin (Geographia Nubiensis, Paris 1619)], whom I prefer to believe rather than Battista Ferrari, who renders it: a bird having a back marked with black and purple feathers; plainly ἀπροσδιονύσως [aprosdionysōs]. For the subject here is not birds but creeping things of the earth. Therefore, like the preceding ones, חומט chomet also seems to belong to the lizard kind. Nor is the origin of the word obscure. For to the Talmudists חומטון chometon is sand. Hence in the tractate On the Sabbath, ch. 5, fol. 54a (where the Talmudists discuss why in 1 Reg. IX, 13 Hiram, king of the Tyrians, called ארץ כבול, the land of Chabul, the district which Solomon had given him), R. Nachman, son of Isaac, says: col. 1076 ארץ חומטין היתה אמאי קרו לה כבול דמשתרגה בה כרעא עד כבלא It was a land of chometon; hence they called it the land of chabul, because in it the leg sank down to the ankle. And Kimchi, citing this passage in his Lexicon under the root כבל, though with slightly altered words, explains חומטון chometon as חול chol, that is, sand. So for them land of chometon is land of sand, or sandy land. Baal Aruch [=the ʿArukh of Nathan ben Jehiel of Rome] [=Nathan ben Jehiel of Rome, the ʿArukh] likewise explains the whole passage thus: פי׳ שרגליו של אדם משתקעות בה מחמת החול כאילו הן כבולין לכן אינה עושה פירות It means that a man's feet sank into it (the land) because of the (abundance of) sand, as if they had been II, 501 fettered, and that it was therefore unfruitful. In the Gloss on the Talmud, the land of chometon is land מלחה ומתבקעת, salty and cracked. And in ch. 2 of the same tractate, fol. 31a, חומטון for the Glossator is ארץ מלחה ומשמרת את הפירות מהתליע, salty soil, which preserves fruits from worms. But the former interpretation is the truer, and suits both passages exceedingly well. For sand is both troublesome to the feet of those walking, and in hot places has a ἀρτυτικὴν [artytikēn] power, by which it preserves things from corruption for a long time. Thus many assert that in the sands of Africa corpses are embalmed and sold as mummy. Indeed the word חומטון chometon, although it does not occur in the Prophetic books, seems to be very ancient R1. And I think the Greeks, dropping the aspiration, derived from it ἄμαθον [amathon], and, with a prefix, ψάμαθον [psamathon]. Both are found in the most ancient writers, Homer for instance and others, in the sense of sand. Yet the Grammarians hold that ἄμαθον [amathon] is μεσόγειον [mesogeion], inland sand, and ψάμαθον [psamathon] παραθαλασσίαν [parathalassian], or shore sand. So, roughly, Hesychius, the Suda and others distinguish them. ¶

Itaque, ut חימטון chometon est arena: sic חומט chomet est lacerti species, quae in arenis degit. Et videtur intelligi Arabum حلكا chulaca, vel لحكا lucha, et luchaca; de qua Alcamus, الحلكا دويبة تغوص في الرمل او ضرب من العظا كالحلكا Alchulaca, animalculum, quod in arena mergitur: aut lacertae species, quae etiam chulaca vocatur. Idem, اللحكا دويبة زرقا تشبه العظا Luchaca, animalculum coeru- leum, lacertae simile. Damir, لحلكا دويبة شبيهة بالعغناة تغوص في الرمل Alchulaca, animalculum, lacertae simile, quod in arenis immergitur. Alazhari, اللحكا دويبة كانها سمكة تكون في الرمل فاذا احست الانسان دارت في الرمل وغاصت فيه Luchaca, animalculum, in arena degens, ut piscis (in aqua); quod, cum sentit hominem, in arena volvitur, atque in ea immergitur. Et Ibnosacith, اللحكا دوبة شبيهة بالعظاية وقوايم خفيفة Luchaca, animalculum, lacertae simile, pedibus levissimis. Asseidalanius et Arruvianus, دويبة مثل الاصبع تجري في الرمل ثم تغوص col. 1077 فيه وهذا يقوي قول الجوهري انه مقلوبة من الحلكا II, 502 Animalculum est digitale, quod per arenas currit, et in iis demergitur. Id confirmat dictum Giauhari, Nomen luchaca inversum esse ex chulaca. Rursus Damir, العرب تسيها بنات النقا لانها تسكن نقيات Arabes etiam appellant filias tumuli arenae; quia in arenae tumulis degit.

Therefore, just as חימטון chometon is sand, so חומט chomet is a species of lizard that lives in the sands. And it seems that the Arabs' حلكا [ḥlkā] chulaca, or لحكا [lḥkā] lucha, and luchaca, is meant; of which Alcamus [=al-Qāmūs al-muḥīṭ, the Arabic dictionary of al-Fīrūzābādī, which Bochart cites like an author] says: الحلكا دويبة تغوص في الرمل او ضرب من العظا كالحلكا [al-ḥlkā duwaybba taghūṣu fī al-raml aw ḍarb min al-ʿaẓā ka-al-ḥlkā] Alchulaca, a little animal that plunges into the sand; or a species of lizard, which is also called chulaca. The same: اللحكا دويبة زرقا تشبه العظا [al-lḥkā duwaybba zarqā tushbihu al-ʿaẓā] Luchaca, a little blue animal, like a lizard. Damir [=al-Damīrī, Ḥayāt al-ḥayawān]: لحلكا دويبة شبيهة بالعغناة تغوص في الرمل [lḥlkā duwaybba shabīha bi-al-ʿghnāt taghūṣu fī al-raml] Alchulaca, a little animal like a lizard, which plunges into the sands. Alazhari: اللحكا دويبة كانها سمكة تكون في الرمل فاذا احست الانسان دارت في الرمل وغاصت فيه [al-lḥkā duwaybba ka-annahā samaka takūnu fī al-raml fa-idhā aḥassat al-insān dārat fī al-raml wa-ghāṣat fīhi] Luchaca, a little animal living in the sand like a fish (in water); which, when it senses a man, rolls about in the sand and plunges into it. And Ibnosacith: اللحكا دوبة شبيهة بالعظاية وقوايم خفيفة [al-lḥkā dwba shabīha bi-al-ʿaẓāya wa-qawāyim khafīfa] Luchaca, a little animal like a lizard, with very light feet. Asseidalanius and Arruvianus: دويبة مثل الاصبع تجري في الرمل ثم تغوص [duwaybba mithl al-iṣbaʿ tajrī fī al-raml thumma taghūṣu] col. 1077 فيه وهذا يقوي قول الجوهري انه مقلوبة من الحلكا [fīhi wa-hādhā yuqawwī qawl al-Jawharī annahu maqlūba min al-ḥlkā] II, 502 It is a little animal the size of a finger, which runs through the sands and plunges into them. This confirms the statement of Giauhari [=al-Jawharī, al-Ṣiḥāḥ] that the name luchaca is inverted from chulaca. Again Damir: العرب تسيها بنات النقا لانها تسكن نقيات [al-ʿarab tsyhā banāt al-naqā li-annahā taskunu nqyāt] The Arabs also call them daughters of the sand-hill, because it lives in sand-hills. ¶

Sed et lacerti communis una species est ἀμμώδης. Damir capite العظاة Aladha, seu De Lacerta, وهي انواع كثيرة منها الابيض والاحمر والاصغر وكلها منقطه بالسواد والوان بحسب مساكنها فان منها ما يسكن الرمال ومنها ما يسكن قريبا من الما والعشب Earum multae sunt species: quaedam albae, quaedam rubrae, quaedam flavae; quae omnes sunt nigro distinctae. Et colores illis sunt pro ratione loci, quem occupant. Aliae enim in arenis degunt: aliae prope aquas et in herbosis locis. Et Alazhari, هي دويبة ملسا تعدوا وتترد كثيرا تشبه سام ابرص الا انها احسن منه وتسي العظاه وشحمة الارض وشحمة الرمل Est animal molle, multum cursitans, et ultro citroque commeans, simile τῷ sammabras, seu stellioni, nisi quod eo est pulchrius. Vocatur aladha, et Adeps terrae, et adeps arenae. Vides, lector, tam hanc lacertam, quam alteram, minus vulgarem, de qua supra, ab arena nomen ha-bere, aeque ac Hebraeum חומט chomet. Itaque חומט chomet vel alterutrum, vel utrumque significat (2).

But there is also one species of the common lizard that is ἀμμώδης [ammōdēs]. Damir, in the chapter العظاة [al-ʿaẓāh] Aladha, or On the Lizard: وهي انواع كثيرة منها الابيض والاحمر والاصغر وكلها منقطه بالسواد والوان بحسب مساكنها فان منها ما يسكن الرمال ومنها ما يسكن قريبا من الما والعشب [wa-hiya anwāʿ kathīra minhā al-abyaḍ wa-al-aḥmar wa-al-aṣghar wa-kulluhā munaqqaṭa bi-al-sawād wa-alwān bi-ḥasab masākinihā fa-inna minhā mā yaskun al-rimāl wa-minhā mā yaskun qarīban min al-mā wa-al-ʿushb] There are many species of them: some white, some red, some yellow; all of them are spotted with black. And their colours correspond to the place they occupy. For some live in the sands, others near waters and in grassy places. And Alazhari: هي دويبة ملسا تعدوا وتترد كثيرا تشبه سام ابرص الا انها احسن منه وتسي العظاه وشحمة الارض وشحمة الرمل [hiya duwaybba malsā taʿdū wa-tataraddad kathīran tushbih sāmm abraṣ illā annahā aḥsan minhu wa-tusī al-ʿaẓāh wa-shaḥmat al-arḍ wa-shaḥmat al-raml] It is a soft animal, running about a great deal and going back and forth, similar to the sammabras, or stellio, except that it is prettier than it. It is called aladha, and Fat of the earth, and fat of the sand. You see, reader, that both this lizard and the other, less common one discussed above take their name from sand, just as the Hebrew חומט chomet does. Therefore חומט chomet signifies either one of the two, or both R2. ¶

Obiter nota, عظاية vel عضاية in Avicenna pro salamandra poni, et in Abenbitare modo pro salamandra, modo pro sepe, vel lacerta Chalcidica, non sine gravi errore. Utraque enim est venenata. At العظاة veneno caret, ut Arabes diserte docent. Damir, ومن خرافات العرب قالوا ان السوم لما فرق علي الحيوان احتبست العظاة II, 503 عند التفرقة حتي نفد السم واخذ كل حيوان قسطه منه علي قدر السبق اليه فلم يكن لها فيه نصيب وفي طبعها انها عتشي مشيا سريعا ثم تقف ويقال col. 1078 ان ذلك يعرض لها من التذكرة والاسف علي ما فاتها من السم Est ex Arabum Apologis, quod dicunt, quod, cum divisa sunt venena inter animalia, continuit se aladha, dum divisio fiebat, donec venena defecerunt, cum unumquodque animal portionem suam sumsisset, prout ordine primum fuerat. Neque illi fuit sua portio. Proinde, cum illi naturale sit, ut citato gressu incedat, et mox subsistat, ajunt id illi accidere, quia meminit, et moesta est, eo quod defuit ipsi venenum. Ibidem Damir, وتمسي بارض مصر السحلية In terra Aegypti vocatur SUCHLIA, quam Azhari asserit stellioni similem esse, الا انها لا تودي وهي احسن منه nisi quod non est noxia, et est eo pulchrior. Eos igitur falli necesse est, qui hoc animalculum cum salamandra confundunt, aut cum lacerta Chalcidica, quae sunt maxime noxiae.

Note in passing that عظاية [ʿaẓāya] or عضاية [ʿaḍāya] is used in Avicenna [=Ibn Sīnā, al-Qānūn fī l-ṭibb (the Canon)] for the salamander, and in Abenbitar [=Ibn al-Bayṭār] sometimes for the salamander, sometimes for the seps or Chalcidian lizard, not without serious error. For both of these are venomous. But العظاة [al-ʿaẓāh] lacks venom, as the Arabs explicitly teach. Damir: ومن خرافات العرب قالوا ان السوم لما فرق علي الحيوان احتبست العظاة II, 503 عند التفرقة حتي نفد السم واخذ كل حيوان قسطه منه علي قدر السبق اليه فلم يكن لها فيه نصيب وفي طبعها انها عتشي مشيا سريعا ثم تقف ويقال col. 1078 ان ذلك يعرض لها من التذكرة والاسف علي ما فاتها من السم [wa-min khurāfāt al-ʿArab qālū anna al-sūm lammā furriqa ʿalā al-ḥayawān iḥtabasat al-ʿaẓāh ʿinda al-tafriqa ḥattā nafida al-summ wa-akhadha kull ḥayawān qisṭahu minhu ʿalā qadr al-sabq ilayhi fa-lam yakun lahā fīhi naṣīb wa-fī ṭabʿihā annahā ʿatmshī mashyan sarīʿan thumma taqif wa-yuqāl anna dhālika yaʿriḍ lahā min al-tadhkira wa-al-asaf ʿalā mā fātahā min al-summ] It is one of the fables of the Arabs that they say that, when venoms were distributed among the animals, the aladha held back while the distribution was taking place, until the venoms ran out, each animal having taken its portion according to the order in which it had come first. And it got no portion. Accordingly, since it is natural to it to move at a rapid pace and then suddenly stop, they say this happens to it because it remembers and grieves that venom was denied it. In the same place Damir: وتمسي بارض مصر السحلية [wa-tumsī bi-arḍ Miṣr al-suḥliyya] In the land of Egypt it is called SUCHLIA, which Azhari asserts is similar to the stellio, الا انها لا تودي وهي احسن منه [illā annahā lā tūdī wa-hiya aḥsan minhu] except that it is not harmful, and is prettier than it. Those, therefore, must be mistaken who confuse this little animal with the salamander or with the Chalcidian lizard, which are extremely harmful. ¶

Notes

(1) Rosenmüller, II, 501 Hoc dictum in Buxtorfium, quippe qui Talmudicorum חוממון a Graeco vocabulo factum autumat (in Lexico Chald. p. 782). Certe arenae significatio in reliquarum dialectorum cognatarum nulla est usitata.

R1 Rosenmüller, II, 501 This is said against Buxtorf [=Johannes Buxtorf the Elder, Synagoga Judaica and Lexicon Chaldaicum Talmudicum et Rabbinicum], who holds that the Talmudists' חוממון was formed from a Greek word (in the Lexicon Chald. p. 782). Certainly the meaning sand is in no common use in any of the other cognate dialects.

(2) Rosenmüller, II, 502 Sed omnis haec disputatio ea nititur opinione, חומט esse arenam; quo quidem fundamento nil esse potest infirmius, vid. supra not. 1. Apud Chaldaeos verbum חמט denotat, incurvavit, flexit, inflexit se. Unde fortassis Arabibus eruca خُمَّاطٌ est appellata (vid. Golii Lexic. p. 652.), nisi ea potius dicta sit ab eo, quod plantas derodit; est enim خَمَطَ decorticavit arborem. Sed ab inflectendo potuit quoque limax nomen nancisci, qui nonnullis ex eruditis Levit. XI, 30. placuit (vid. Bochart sub hujus Capit. initio). Doceris itaque novo exemplo, quam vana res sit, solis etymologiis elicere significationes eorum nominum, quae animalia designant.

R2 Rosenmüller, II, 502 But this whole discussion rests on the opinion that חומט means sand; and no foundation could be weaker than that, see above note 1. Among the Chaldeans the verb חמט denotes he bent, curved, bent himself. Hence perhaps the caterpillar is called خُمَّاطٌ [khummāṭ] by the Arabs (see Golius [=Jacobus Golius, Lexicon Arabico-Latinum (1653)], Lexic. p. 652), unless it was rather named from the fact that it gnaws plants; for خَمَطَ [khamaṭa] means he stripped the bark from a tree. But from bending the snail could also have got its name, which some of the learned favoured at Lev 11:30 (see Bochart at the beginning of this Chapter). You are thus taught by a new example how vain a thing it is to derive the meanings of names that designate animals from etymologies alone.
