II, 557 CAP. V.

II, 557 Chapter V. ¶

De Columbarum et Turturum sacrificiis.

On the sacrifices of doves and turtledoves. ¶

Iudaei columbas non solum ad esum, sed etiam ad sacrificia exquirebant, quae multorum erant generum. Sic Levit. I, 14. agitur de spontaneo holocausto e turturibus, aut pullis columbarum. Levit. V, 7. qui blasphemantem alium non correxerat, aut ipse temere juraverat, aut immundae rei contactu se polluerat, jubetur offerre par turturum, aut columbarum, unam pro peccato, alteram pro holocausto. Levit. XII, 6. a puerpera, post expletos dies purificationis, Deus agnum anniculum in holocaustum exigit, et pullum columbae, vel turturem, pro peccato; aut, si agnum offerre non possit, duos turtures, aut duos pullos columbarum. Easdem quoque oblationes praescribi videas ei, qui a lepra sanatus est, Levit. XIV, 22. Illi item, qui gonorrhoea laboravit, cum mundatus est a profluvio suo, Levit. XV, 13. 14. Et ἐν τῷ τῆς αἱμοῤῥοούσης κα-θαρισμῷ, Levit. XV, 28. 29. Et in purgatione Naziraei, qui mortui contactu se contaminavit, Num. VI, 10.

The Jews sought doves not only for eating but also for sacrifices, which were of many kinds. Thus Lev 1:14 deals with a voluntary burnt offering of turtledoves or young doves. In Levit. V, 7. one who had not rebuked another who blasphemed, or had himself sworn rashly, or had defiled himself by touching an unclean thing, is commanded to offer a pair of turtledoves or doves, one for a sin offering, the other for a burnt offering. In Lev 12:6. God requires of a woman after childbirth, once the days of her purification are completed, a yearling lamb for a burnt offering and a young dove or a turtledove for a sin offering; or, if she cannot offer a lamb, two turtledoves or two young doves. You may see the same offerings prescribed also for one who has been healed of leprosy, Levit. XIV, 22; likewise for one who has suffered from a discharge, when he has been cleansed of his flux, Levit. XV, 13. 14; and ἐν τῷ τῆς αἱμοῤῥοούσης καθαρισμῷ [en tō tēs haimorrhoousēs katharismō], Levit. XV, 28. 29; and in the purification of the Nazirite who has defiled himself by contact with a dead body, Num. VI, 10. ¶

In quibus sacrificiis nota primo, Deum columbas et turtures caeteris avibus praetulisse, propter puritatem, castitatem, simplicitatem, mansuetudinem et reliquas virtutum umbras, quae in his observatae sunt. 2. Turtures adultos, sed columbarum nonnisi pullos in altari fuisse oblatos, quia turtures in matura, columbae in tenera aetate maxime praestant. 3. Pleraque haec sacrificia nonnisi tenuiorum fuisse, ut expresse observatur Levit. V, 7. et XII, 6. Itaque vel hinc collige, quam pauper fuerit sancta virgo, Christi mater, quae post purificationem non obtulit agnum, sed turtures, aut pullos columbarum, Luc. II, 24. 4. Iam ante Legem hujusmodi sacrificia fuisse in usu, constat ex Abrahami exemplo, qui cum juvenca, capra et ariete, trimis, turturem et גוזל gozal, id est, columbini generis pullum, Deo offerre jussus est. Nempe, ut hoc sacrificium omnia victimarum genera complecteretur, quae postmodum in Lege constituta sunt. Quod puto simplicissimum, quamvis varia mysteria hic multi quaerunt. Et per quinque haec animalia alii Christi dotes: alii eos significari velint, quibus Deus terram Chanaan destinaverat, et virtutes, in iis requisitas: alii denique posterorum Abrahami quis futurus esset status. Per bovem enim, hircum et agnum, quae terrestria sunt animalia, et sub hominum jugo vivere col. 26 solita, designari tempus illud, quo abjecti et humiles et sub Aegyptiorum servitute victuri essent; per turturem, quae solitaria est, Iudaeorum in deserto moram: et per columbam, quae gregalis est, et in cultis locis degit, eorum ha-bitationem in Chanaanitide. 5. Columbarum sacrificia prava quadam κακοζηλίᾳ gentes etiam usurpasse. Propertius libro quarto Elegia ad Lenam, II, 558 Sed cape torquatae, Venus o regina, columbae, Ob meritum ante tuos guttura secta focos.

In these sacrifices note, first, that God preferred doves and turtledoves to other birds because of the purity, chastity, simplicity, gentleness and the other shadows of virtues that have been observed in them. 2. That adult turtledoves, but only young doves, were offered on the altar, because turtledoves are best at a mature age, doves at a tender age. 3. That most of these sacrifices were only those of the poorer people, as is expressly noted in Levit. V, 7. and XII, 6. So gather even from this how poor the holy Virgin, the mother of Christ, was, who after her purification offered not a lamb but turtledoves or young doves, Luc. II, 24. 4. That sacrifices of this kind were already in use before the Law is clear from the example of Abraham, who was commanded to offer to God, together with a three-year-old heifer, she-goat and ram, a turtledove and a גוזל gozal, that is, a young bird of the dove kind: namely, so that this sacrifice might embrace all the kinds of victims that were afterwards established in the Law. This I think the simplest explanation, although many seek various mysteries here. Some would have these five animals signify the gifts of Christ; others, those for whom God had destined the land of Canaan, and the virtues required in them; others, finally, what the condition of Abraham's descendants would be. For by the ox, the goat and the lamb, which are land animals and accustomed to live under the yoke of men, col. 26 they say is signified the time when they would live abased and humble and in servitude to the Egyptians; by the turtledove, which is solitary, the sojourn of the Jews in the desert; and by the dove, which is gregarious and lives in cultivated places, their dwelling in Canaan. 5. That the gentiles too, through a certain perverse κακοζηλίᾳ [kakozēlia], adopted sacrifices of doves. Propertius, book 4, in the Elegy to the Procuress: II, 558 “But take, o queen Venus, the throat of a ringed dove, cut before your hearths for its desert.” ¶

Ovidius lib. 1. Fastorum,

Ovid, book 1 of the Fasti: ¶

Ergo saepe suo conjux abducta marito Uritur in calidis alba columba focis.

“Therefore often the white dove, a wife taken from her husband, is burned on the hot hearths.” ¶

Columbarum tamen esum Veneris mystis fuisse interdictum, colligi videtur ex his Martialis libro 13. Epigr. 66.

Yet the eating of doves seems to have been forbidden to the initiates of Venus, as may be gathered from these lines of Martial, book 13, Epigr. 66: ¶

Ne violes teneras praeduro dente columbas, Tradita si Cnidiae sunt tibi sacra Deae.

“Do not violate tender doves with too hard a tooth, if the rites of the Cnidian Goddess have been handed down to you.” ¶

Quo ritu turtures et columbas Iudaei mactaverint, breviter docet Moses Le-vit. I, 15. 16. 17. et V, 8. et pluribus Talmudici in Zebachim cap. 6. fol. 64. Et Maimonides in libro De sacrificiis offerendis cap. 6. §. 20. 21. 22. 23. et cap. 7. §. 6. 7. 8. unde multa possent elici non usquequaque trita, si id ageretur in praesens. Sed hic notasse sufficiet, Hebraeos alias quidem victimas ferro mactasse, at aves ungue solo, quo, capite versus reflexo, guttur illis discerpebatur. Id Hebrais est מלק, quod Graeci ἀποκνίζειν, id est, ungue lacerare, vel abscindere, Levit. I, 15. et V, 8. quibus solum locis occurrit. Ierosolymitanus עום, et Ionathan חום, quod idem est, ח et ע permutatis. Arabice idem حذم vel خذم quod secare reddunt, et discerpere. Sic خذمت الصقر unguibus discerpsit accipiter, in Alcamus. Avem discerpendi ritum Kimchius in Lexico in voce מלק, ita describit: ואר״זל כיצד מולקין חטאת העוף נותן שתי גפיה בין שתי אצבעותיו ושתי רגליה בין שתי אצבעותיו והיה מותח צוארה על שתי אצבעותיו והיה מולק בצפורן ממול עורף עד שמגיע לסימנים וקוצץ אותן או רובן ואינו מבדיל שנאמר ולא יבדיל אבל בעולה העוף שאמר ומלק את ראשו ולא אמר ולא יבדיל הוא מולק ומבדיל הראש בעצמו הגוף בעצמו Dixerunt magistri nostri beatae memoriae: Quomodo lacerabatur sacrificium pro peccato ex ave? Ponebat (sacerdos) alas ejus inter duos digitos, et pedes inter duos digitos, et extendebat collum super duos digitos suos, et secabat ungue versus cervicem, donec attingeret signa (in jugulo), quae amputabat, aut majorem partem, ita tamen, ut non divideret, quia dicitur Levit. V, 8. „Et non dividet“. Sed in holocausto ex ave, quia dicitur, „Secabit caput ejus“, neque additur, „Et non dividet“, Levit. I, 15. ita secabat, ut caput a trunco corporis separeretur. Haec ille ex Talmudis loco, quem citavimus. Videndus etiam Maimonides, qui hoc esse asserit מעבודות קשות שבמקדש, unum ex operosissimis Sanctuarii cultibus, in libro De offerendis sacrificiis cap. 7. §. 8. (1).

The rite by which the Jews slaughtered turtledoves and doves is taught briefly by Moses in Levit. I, 15. 16. 17. and V, 8., and at greater length by the Talmudists in Zebachim ch. 6, fol. 64, and by Maimonides [=Moses Maimonides (Rambam), Mishneh Torah] in the book On the offering of sacrifices ch. 6, §§ 20, 21, 22, 23, and ch. 7, §§ 6, 7, 8. Much could be drawn from these that is not altogether commonplace, if that were our present business. But here it will suffice to have noted that the Hebrews slaughtered other victims with a blade, but birds with the fingernail alone, by which, with the head bent back, their throat was torn open. In Hebrew this is מלק, which the Greeks render ἀποκνίζειν [apoknizein], that is, to tear with the nail, or to cut off, Lev 1:15 and V, 8., the only places where it occurs. The Jerusalem Targum has עום, and Jonathan [=Targum Jonathan] חום, which is the same, ח and ע being interchanged. In Arabic the same is حذم [ḥadhama] or خذم [khadhama], which they render to cut and to tear apart. Thus خذمت الصقر [khadhamat al-ṣaqr], the hawk tore with its talons, in Alcamus [=al-Qāmūs al-muḥīṭ, the Arabic dictionary of al-Fīrūzābādī]. Kimchi [=David Kimḥi (Radak), Sefer ha-Shorashim], in his Lexicon under the word מלק, describes the rite of tearing the bird thus: ואר״זל כיצד מולקין חטאת העוף נותן שתי גפיה בין שתי אצבעותיו ושתי רגליה בין שתי אצבעותיו והיה מותח צוארה על שתי אצבעותיו והיה מולק בצפורן ממול עורף עד שמגיע לסימנים וקוצץ אותן או רובן ואינו מבדיל שנאמר ולא יבדיל אבל בעולה העוף שאמר ומלק את ראשו ולא אמר ולא יבדיל הוא מולק ומבדיל הראש בעצמו הגוף בעצמו Our masters of blessed memory said: How was the sin offering of a bird torn? He (the priest) placed its wings between two fingers and its feet between two fingers, and stretched its neck over his two fingers, and cut with his nail opposite the nape until he reached the signs (in the throat), which he severed, or the greater part of them, yet so that he did not divide it, because it is said Lev 5:8 „And he shall not divide it“. But in the burnt offering of a bird, because it is said, „He shall wring off its head“, and it is not added, „And he shall not divide it“, Lev 1:15, he cut so that the head was separated from the trunk of the body. So he, from the passage of the Talmud we have cited. See also Maimonides, who asserts that this was מעבודות קשות שבמקדש, one of the most laborious services of the Sanctuary, in the book On the offering of sacrifices ch. 7, § 8 R1. ¶

II, 559 Ne victimae deessent, in prima templi parte, quae פרבר parbar vocabatur 1 Paral. XXVI, 18. vendebantur boves, et oves, et columbae, Ioh. II, 14. Itaque in Maimonidis tractatu De vasis Sanctuarii cap. 7. §. 9. (inter templi praefectos, quos Hebraei vocabant ממוני המקדש, et Graeci στρατηγοὺς τοῦ ἱεροῦ, Luc. XXII, 52.) recensetur הממונה שעל הקינים, praefectus super nidos, id est, super aves. Atque is ita describitur: הדא שפוסקין עמו שימכור הקנים לקורבנות כך וכך בסלע וכל מי שהוא חייב תורים או שני בני יונה יביא דמיהם למקדשי col. 27 וזה הממונה נותן הקנים לבעלי הקורבנות ועושה חשבון עם הגוברים ונותנין לו משלושים יום לשלושים יום, Is est, cum quo paciscuntur, ut ad oblationes aves comparet, has vel illas pro siclo. Et quisquis tenebatur vel turtures offerre, vel pullos columbarum, pretium ad sanctuarium afferebat. Et iste praefectus dabat aves iis, quorum erat offerre, et cum quaestoribus eas aestimabat; et, postquam de pretio convenerat, eas intra triduum dabant. Et paullo post, וכן קן שנמצא פסול או שנפסל קודם שיקרוב נותן אחר תחתיו, Et, si quae avis profana erat, aut profana fiebat (per pollutionem ceremonialem) antequam offerretur, aliam pro ea dabat. Quae ad quaestum a lucripetis et avaris sacerdotibus fuisse excogitata, his verbis docet Hieronymus in Matthaei cap. 21. Iuxta mandata Legis an-gustissimo in toto orbe templo Domini, et de cunctis pene regionibus Iudaeorum illuc populo confluente, innumerabiles immolabantur hostiae, maxime festis diebus, taurorum, arietum, hircorum, pauperibus, ne absque sacrificio essent, pullos columbarum et turtures offerentibus, accidebat plerumque, ut, qui de longe venerant, non haberent victimas. Excogitaverunt igitur sacerdotes, quomodo praedam de populo facerent; et omnia animalia, quibus opus erat ad sacrificia, vendebant, ut et venderent non habentibus, et ipsi rursum empta susciperent. Proinde Christus in hos lucriones jure merito irritatus, evertit eorum cathedras, qui columbas vendebant, Matth. XXI, 12. et Marc. XI, 15. Et columbarum venditoribus dixit, Auferte ista hinc. Ne facite domum patris mei domum emporii, Ioh. II, 16.

II, 559 So that victims should not be lacking, in the first part of the temple, which was called פרבר parbar, 1 Chr 26:18, oxen, sheep and doves were sold, John 2:14. Accordingly in Maimonides' treatise On the vessels of the Sanctuary ch. 7, § 9 (among the officers of the temple, whom the Hebrews called ממוני המקדש and the Greeks στρατηγοὺς τοῦ ἱεροῦ [stratēgous tou hierou], Luke 22:52) there is listed הממונה שעל הקינים, the officer over the nests, that is, over the birds. And he is described thus: הדא שפוסקין עמו שימכור הקנים לקורבנות כך וכך בסלע וכל מי שהוא חייב תורים או שני בני יונה יביא דמיהם למקדשי col. 27 וזה הממונה נותן הקנים לבעלי הקורבנות ועושה חשבון עם הגוברים ונותנין לו משלושים יום לשלושים יום, He is the one with whom they contract that he provide birds for the offerings, so many or so many for a shekel. And whoever was obliged to offer either turtledoves or young doves brought the price to the sanctuary. And this officer gave the birds to those whose duty it was to offer, and valued them with the treasurers; and, once he had agreed on the price, they paid him within three days. And a little later: וכן קן שנמצא פסול או שנפסל קודם שיקרוב נותן אחר תחתיו, And if any bird was found unfit, or became unfit (through ceremonial defilement) before it was offered, he gave another in its place. That these arrangements were devised for gain by greedy and avaricious priests, Jerome teaches in these words on Matthew ch. 21: According to the commandments of the Law, in the temple of the Lord, the most confined in the whole world, with the people flocking there from almost all the regions of the Jews, innumerable victims were sacrificed, especially on feast days: bulls, rams and he-goats, while the poor, so as not to be without a sacrifice, offered young doves and turtledoves. It often happened that those who had come from afar had no victims. The priests therefore devised a way to make a prey of the people: they sold all the animals needed for the sacrifices, so that they might both sell to those who had none and themselves receive back again what had been bought. Hence Christ, rightly and justly angered against these profiteers, overturned the seats of those who sold doves, Matth. XXI, 12. and Marc. XI, 15. And to those who sold doves he said, Take these things hence. Do not make my father's house a house of trade, John 2:16. ¶
