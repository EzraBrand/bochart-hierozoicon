CHAPTER I.

De Hebraeo Columbae nomine.

On the Hebrew name of the dove.

Mundas aves Sacri Scriptores paucis attingunt, una columba excepta, de qua plura habent, quam de reliquis omnibus simul sumtis. Itaque de illa necesse est agere accuratius. Columbam Hebraice יונה jona dici volunt a verbo ינה jana, quod opprimere significat, quasi omnium praedae pateat. Quam in rem poetae proferuntur,

The sacred writers touch on the clean birds only briefly, with the single exception of the dove, about which they say more than about all the others taken together. It is therefore necessary to treat it more carefully. It is held that the dove is called in Hebrew יונה jona from the verb ינה jana, which means to oppress, as if it lay open to the plundering of all. On this point the poets are cited:

Dat veniam corvis, vexat censura columbas.

Censure pardons the ravens and harasses the doves.

Quod videtur longe petitum. Itaque conabor aliquid afferre verosimilius. Ie- rem. XLVI, 16. et L, 16. Hebraea verba מפני חרב היונה Graeci reddunt ἀπὸ προσώπου μαχαίρας Ἑλληνικῆς, a facie gladii Graeci; Hieronymus, a facie gla- dii columbae; Syrus, a facie gladii ܡܣܝܒܐ cruciantis; R. Selomo et Kimchius, האונסת, opprimentis: sed Chaldaeus Paraphrastes, מן קדם חרב סנאה דהיא כחמר מרויא, a facie gladii hostilis, qui est ut vinum inebrians; quasi היונה fiat a יין, id est, a vino, Iod et Vau permutatis. Indidem igitur יונה jona pro co- lumba deduci queat. Quod si sequamur, יונה jona proprie erit οἰνὰς, columbae ferae genus, a vino sic appellatum. 1. Quia, Eustathio auctore, est οἰνωπὸς τὸ χρῶμα, id est, quia vinum, vel uvas maturas colore refert. 2. Quia vindemiae tempore fere apparet. Aristoteles Hist. lib. 8. cap. 3. Οἰνὰς δὲ τοῦ col. 2 φθινοπώρου καὶ φαίνεται μάλιστα, καὶ ἁλίσκεται· Oenas (vel, ut Gaza reddit, vinago) Autumno maxime apparet et capitur. Athenaeus lib. 9. cap. 11. Ἡ δὲ φὰψ, καὶ ἡ περιστερὰ ἀεὶ φαίνονται· ἡ δὲ οἰνὰς φθινοπώρῳ μόνῳ· Palumbus minor et columba semper apparent; sed vinago solum autumno. Sed huic etymo II, 525 repugnat Hebraeorum usus, apud quos יונה jona, vel יון jon non est οἰνὰς, sed columba in genere, et proprie domestica. Itaque columba οἰνὰς a Syris dicta est ܟܪܡܝܘܢ charmion, vel ܟܪܡܝܘܢܐ charmiona, voce composita ex duabus, ܟܪܡ et ܝܘܢ, vel ܝܘܢܐ, quarum illa vitem, haec columbam sonat. Quod ipsissimum fuit nomen unius illustrium illarum Cleopatrae ancillarum. Graece id Χάρμιον scribitur, vel Χαρμιόνη, vel Χαρμιουνώ. Plutarchus in Antonio, Εἴρας ἡ Κλεοπάτρας κουρεύτρια, καὶ Χάρμιον, ὑφ᾽ ὧν τὰ μέγιστα διοικεῖται τῆς ἡγεμονίας· Iras Cleopatrae tonstrix, et Charmion, a quibus maxima imperii negotia administrabantur. Et infra, ubi de morte Cleopatrae, Τῶν δὲ γυναι-κῶν ἡ μὲν Εἴρας λεγομένη πρὸς τοῖς ποσὶν ἀπέθνησκεν· ἡ δὲ Χάρμιον, ἤδη σφαλλομένη, καὶ καρηβαροῦσα, κατεκόσμει τὸ διάδημα περὶ τὴν κεφαλὴν αὐ-τῆς. Mulierum ejus quae vocabatur Iras, ad pedes ejus expiraverat: Charmion autem, jam lapsabunda, et capite gravata, caput illius diademate exornabat. Galenus De theriaca ad Pisonem libri primi capite octavo, Καὶ τοῖς ὀνόμασι λεγό-μεναι Νάειρα, καὶ Καρμιόνη· ἡ μὲν ἀναπλέκουσα τὰς τρίχας εὐπρεπῶς, ἡ δὲ col. 3 ἀποτέμνουσα τὰς ὑπεροχὰς τῶν ὀνύχων εὐφυῶς. Et (Cleopatrae ancillae) Naira et Carmione dictae; quarum illa comam decenter implicabat, haec extremos ungues dextre praecidebat. Tzetzes Chil. 6. Hist. 44.

This seems far-fetched. I shall therefore try to offer something more probable. In Jer 46:16 and L, 16. the Hebrew words מפני חרב היונה are rendered by the Greeks ἀπὸ προσώπου μαχαίρας Ἑλληνικῆς [apo prosōpou machairas Hellēnikēs], from the face of the Greek sword; by Jerome, from the face of the sword of the dove; by the Syriac, from the face of the sword ܡܣܝܒܐ [מסיבא] of the tormentor; by R. Selomo [=Rashi (R. Solomon ben Isaac)] and Kimchi [=David Kimḥi (Radak), Sefer ha-Shorashim], האונסת, of the oppressor. But the Chaldee Paraphrast has מן קדם חרב סנאה דהיא כחמר מרויא, from the face of the hostile sword, which is like intoxicating wine, as if היונה were formed from יין, that is, from wine, with Yod and Vav interchanged. From the same source, then, יונה jona for the dove could be derived. If we follow this, יונה jona will properly be the οἰνὰς [oinas], a kind of wild dove so named from wine. 1. Because, on the authority of Eustathius, it is οἰνωπὸς τὸ χρῶμα [oinōpos to chrōma], that is, because it resembles wine, or ripe grapes, in colour. 2. Because it usually appears at the time of the vintage. Aristotle, Hist. book 8, ch. 3: Οἰνὰς δὲ τοῦ col. 2 φθινοπώρου καὶ φαίνεται μάλιστα, καὶ ἁλίσκεται· [Oinas de tou phthinopōrou kai phainetai malista, kai halisketai] The oenas (or, as Gaza renders it, vinago) appears and is caught chiefly in autumn. Athenaeus book 9, ch. 11: Ἡ δὲ φὰψ, καὶ ἡ περιστερὰ ἀεὶ φαίνονται· ἡ δὲ οἰνὰς φθινοπώρῳ μόνῳ· [Hē de phaps, kai hē peristera aei phainontai; hē de oinas phthinopōrō monō] The smaller wood pigeon and the dove are always to be seen; but the vinago only in autumn. But this etymology II, 525 is contradicted by the usage of the Hebrews, among whom יונה jona, or יון jon, is not the οἰνὰς [oinas] but the dove in general, and properly the domestic one. Accordingly the οἰνὰς [oinas] dove was called by the Syrians ܟܪܡܝܘܢ [כרמיון] charmion, or ܟܪܡܝܘܢܐ [כרמיונא] charmiona, a word compounded of two, ܟܪܡ [כרם] and ܝܘܢ [יון], or ܝܘܢܐ [יונא], of which the former means vine and the latter dove. This was the very name of one of those famous handmaids of Cleopatra. In Greek it is written Χάρμιον [Charmion], or Χαρμιόνη [Charmionē], or Χαρμιουνώ [Charmiounō]. Plutarch in the Antony: Εἴρας ἡ Κλεοπάτρας κουρεύτρια, καὶ Χάρμιον, ὑφ᾽ ὧν τὰ μέγιστα διοικεῖται τῆς ἡγεμονίας· [Eiras hē Kleopatras koureutria, kai Charmion, hyph᾽ hōn ta megista dioikeitai tēs hēgemonias] Iras, Cleopatra's hairdresser, and Charmion, by whom the most important affairs of the realm were managed. And further on, regarding the death of Cleopatra: Τῶν δὲ γυναικῶν ἡ μὲν Εἴρας λεγομένη πρὸς τοῖς ποσὶν ἀπέθνησκεν· ἡ δὲ Χάρμιον, ἤδη σφαλλομένη, καὶ καρηβαροῦσα, κατεκόσμει τὸ διάδημα περὶ τὴν κεφαλὴν αὐτῆς. [Tōn de gynaikōn hē men Eiras legomenē pros tois posin apethnēsken; hē de Charmion, ēdē sphallomenē, kai karēbarousa, katekosmei to diadēma peri tēn kephalēn autēs] Of her women, the one called Iras had died at her feet; Charmion, however, already tottering and heavy-headed, was adorning her head with the diadem. Galen, On theriac to Piso, book 1, chapter 8: Καὶ τοῖς ὀνόμασι λεγόμεναι Νάειρα, καὶ Καρμιόνη· ἡ μὲν ἀναπλέκουσα τὰς τρίχας εὐπρεπῶς, ἡ δὲ col. 3 ἀποτέμνουσα τὰς ὑπεροχὰς τῶν ὀνύχων εὐφυῶς. [Kai tois onomasi legomenai Naeira, kai Karmionē; hē men anaplekousa tas trichas euprepōs, hē de apotemnousa tas hyperochas tōn onychōn euphyōs] And (Cleopatra's handmaids) called Naira and Carmione, of whom the former braided her hair becomingly, the latter skilfully trimmed the tips of her nails. Tzetzes, Chil. 6, Hist. 44:

Τῇ Κλεοπάτρᾳ τῇ σοφῇ, τῇ καὶ ὡραιοτάτῃ, Καὶ τοῖς λοιποῖς δὲ σύμπασι a) πολλῶν ὑπερφερούσῃ, Πρὸς ἐπιμέλειαν τριχῶν, καὶ τῶν ὀνύχων, ἦσαν Χαρμιουνὼ, καὶ Τάειρα τί δε δηλοῦσι τάδε; Σύρον τὸν Τζέτζην βλέπε νῦν, Ἰσμαηλίτην ἅμα, Καὶ ταῦτα διαρθροῦντα σοι ταῖς γλώσσαις ταῖς ἐκείνων. Κατὰ Ἑβραίων γλῶσσάν τε, καὶ τὴν τῶν Σύρων ἅμα, Χαρμὶ δηλοῖ τὴν ἀμπελον, Οὐνὼ περιστεράν δε, Τὴν, ἣν b) φησιν οἱ Ἕλληνες περιστερὰν οἰνάδα, Κατὰ Ἰσμαηλίτας δὲ, τοὺς Ἄγαρ γόνους λέγω, Περιστερὰν τὴν Γάειραν σημαίνουσάν μοι νόει.

Cleopatrae sapienti, et formosissimae, et supra reliquas omnes mulieres longe praestanti, ad curam capillorum et unguium, erant Charmiuno et Taïra. Haec autem nomina quid designant? Syrum vide nunc Tzetzem et Ismaëlitem simul, et haec illorum linguis tibi explicantem. Tam Hebraeorum, quam Syrorum lingua Charmi vitem significat, et Uno columbam, quam Graeci columbam oenadem vocant. At juxta Ismaëlitas, ex Agare oriundos, dico, Taïram mihi columbam significare II, 526 intellige. Ex his collige, in Plutarcho pro Εἴρας, et in Galeno pro Νά-ειρα, rescribendum esse Τάειρας, vel Τάειρα, ut in Tzetze, qui recte quidem scribit hanc vocem, sed male interpretatur. Quippe Arabicum طاير taïr, vel طايرة taira, columbam non significat, sed avem in genere. Unde Chaldaeis טייר tajer est augurari. Neque dividi debuit Charmiuno in Charmi et Uno; sed in כרם charm, et יונא juno. Interim non dubitandum, quin Charmiuno, vel Charmiona vere sit οἰνάς. Itaque יונה jona per se non est οἰνὰς, sed columba quaevis. Quo posito nulla apparet ratio, cur a vino dicta sit יונה jona. Proinde aliud quaerendum. Neque fortasse absurdum erit, ut patriae columbae יונים jonim, quasi Graecae, putentur fuisse appellatae. Graeci enim olim Ἴω-νες Iones dicebantur, ut alibi probavimus: et Hebraice יונים jonim, iisdem literis, quamvis javanim hodie efferant, quia Masorethis ita placuit. Columbas inde appellari, duo potissimum suadent. Unum, quod apud Plinium, libri decimi capite vigesimo nono, Theophrastus tradit, invectitias esse in Asia columbas. Alterum, quod in Graecia columbas fuisse ab omni aevo historia fabularis abunde docet. Sic 1. Dodonaeam columbam ajunt c) ἱζομένην ἐπὶ φηγὸν, αὐδάξασθαι φωνῇ ἀνθρωπίνῃ, ὡς χρεὼν εἴη, μαντήϊον αὐτόθι Διὸς γένεσθαι, fago insidentem, humana voce dixisse, in fatis esse, ut eo loci Iovis oraculum conderetur, quod fuit Graeciae totius longe antiquissimum. 2. Ut a Noacho, sic a Deucalione ferunt d) περιστερὰν, ἐκ τῆς λάρνακος ἀφιεμένην, δήλωμα γενέσθαι, χειμῶνος μὲν εἴσω πάλιν ἐνδυομένην, εὐδίας δὲ, ἀποπτᾶσαν, columbam, ex arca emissam, tempestatis signum fuisse, cum eodem rediit, et serenitatis, cum avolavit. 3. Cleopatra, wise, most beautiful, and far surpassing all other women, had for the care of her hair and nails Charmiuno and Taïra. But what do these names signify? See now Tzetzes, at once a Syrian and an Ishmaelite, explaining them to you in their languages. In the language of the Hebrews as well as of the Syrians, Charmi signifies the vine, and Uno the dove, which the Greeks call the oenas dove. But among the Ishmaelites, I mean those descended from Hagar, understand that Taïra signifies II, 526 the dove to me. From this gather that in Plutarch for Εἴρας [Eiras], and in Galen for Νάειρα [Naeira], one must write Τάειρας [Taeiras] or Τάειρα [Taeira], as in Tzetzes, who indeed writes this word correctly but interprets it wrongly. For the Arabic طاير [ṭāyir] taïr, or طايرة [ṭāyira] taira, does not signify a dove, but a bird in general. Hence for the Chaldeans טייר tajer means to take auguries. Nor should Charmiuno have been divided into Charmi and Uno, but into כרם charm and יונא juno. Meanwhile there is no doubt that Charmiuno, or Charmiona, is truly οἰνάς [oinas]. So יונה jona by itself is not οἰνὰς [oinas], but any dove. This being granted, no reason appears why יונה jona should be named from wine. Therefore something else must be sought. Nor perhaps will it be absurd to think that doves were called by their native name יונים jonim, as if Greek ones. For the Greeks were formerly called Ἴωνες [Iōnes] Iones, as we have proved elsewhere; and in Hebrew יונים jonim, with the same letters, although today they pronounce javanim, because that pleased the Masoretes. Two things chiefly suggest that doves were named from this. One, that in Pliny, book 10, chapter 29, Theophrastus reports that doves were imported into Asia. The other, that mythical history amply shows that there were doves in Greece from all time. Thus 1. they say that the Dodonaean dove c) ἱζομένην ἐπὶ φηγὸν, αὐδάξασθαι φωνῇ ἀνθρωπίνῃ, ὡς χρεὼν εἴη, μαντήϊον αὐτόθι Διὸς γένεσθαι [hizomenēn epi phēgon, audaxasthai phōnē anthrōpinē, hōs chreōn eiē, mantēion autothi Dios genesthai], sitting on a beech tree, said with a human voice that it was fated that an oracle of Jupiter should be founded in that place, which was by far the most ancient of all Greece. 2. As from Noah, so from Deucalion they relate d) περιστερὰν, ἐκ τῆς λάρνακος ἀφιεμένην, δήλωμα γενέσθαι, χειμῶνος μὲν εἴσω πάλιν ἐνδυομένην, εὐδίας δὲ, ἀποπτᾶσαν [peristeran, ek tēs larnakos aphiemenēn, dēlōma genesthai, cheimōnos men eisō palin endyomenēn, eudias de, apoptasan], that a dove, sent out from the ark, was a sign of storm when it returned there, and of fair weather when it flew away. 3. Doves are said to have fed Jupiter, crying as an infant in Crete, with ambrosia brought from Ocean, according to Moero of Byzantium in Athenaeus, book 11,

Nor were the Samaritans free from this superstition, if indeed we believe the Jews. The Talmud, tractate Cholin, ch. 1, fol. 6a, on the Cuthaeans or Samaritans: אמר רב נחמן בר יצחק דמות יונה מצאו להן בראש הר גריזים שהיו עובדין אותה Said Rab Nachman, son of Isaac: they found among them the image of a dove, which they worshipped on Mount Gerizim. The gloss on the Talmudic tractate Avodazera, fol. 26b: אל ימול כותי שמל לשם דמות שנמצאו בראש הר גריזים ועובדין לה עבודה כדאמר בהכל שיחטין ומלו את בניהם לשם יונה זו, A Cuthaean shall not circumcise, for he circumcises in the name of the image which they found on the summit of Mount Gerizim, and they worship it, as is related in the chapter (of Cholin, cited above, which begins) "All slaughter": and they circumcise their sons in the name of this dove. Meor enajim: קרוב בעיני להאמין שהכותיים אשר הושיב מלך אשור ובבל בשומרון לאות ולעד הכנעם באהבה אל מלכם ההוא אשר אות דגלה יונה חבבו אותה והקימוהו ליראתם בראש הר גריזים אשר עם שכם ראש ממלכת שומרון בימים ההם, I am fully persuaded that the Cuthaeans, whom the king of Assyria and Babylonia had deported to Samaria, as a sign and testimony of their love and devotion toward their king, who had the dove as the emblem of his banner, loved the dove, and erected a statue, which they worshipped, on the summit of Mount Gerizim, near the city of Shechem, which was then the capital of the Samaritan kingdom. Tsemach David, in the Chronology of the Second Temple, at the year 881: In the days of R. Meir they found the Cuthaeans' image of a dove on Mount Gerizim, as is stated in the first chapter of Cholin. And he decreed that they should be treated in every respect as gentiles of the land. R1.

Ex his videatur posse confici, tam in Syria, quam in Graecia columbas ab omni aevo nidificasse. Tamen est, quod regeratur, nempe Mosis quidem aetate etiam a plebejis in Israele oblatos fuisse בני יונה, pullos columbarum, ut Lex diserte praecipit: sed, an Abraham idem obtulerit, in quaestionem posse vocari. Neque enim legitur obtulisse תור ובן יונה turturem et pullum columbae, ut jubetur II, 531 in Lege Mosis, sed תור וגוזל turturum et gozal, quae vox non solum significat pullum columbae, proprie dictae, sed et pullum turturis, et palumbi, et liviae, et vinaginis, et avis alia, si qua est, quae ad columbinum genus referatur. Damir in capite De القطا alkata, quomodo vocant Arabes quoddam palumbi genus, يقال القطا والحمام وانواعها امهات الجوازل والجوازل فراخها الواحد جوزل Alkata et columba, et col. 7 aves homogeneae dicuntur matres algavvazil. Et Algavvazil sunt pulli earum a singulari gauzal. Porro جوزل gauzal Arabibus id ipsum est, quod Hebraeis גוזל gozal. Proinde, quia τοῦ gozal sunt variae species, cum pipio praecise significatur, quandoque additur διακριτικῶς, gozal, columbae pullus. Ita in Chal- daeo Cantici paraphraste capitis primi versu decimo quinto, Oculi tui sunt כגוזלין בני יונתא, ut gozalin pulli columbae. Et Vajikra Rabba capite vigesimo secundo de Tito, imperatore, קראו לרופאים ומצאו בו כמו גוזל בן יונה והיה בו משקל שתי לטראות, Vocaverunt medicos, qui (cerebro ejus discisso), in illo in- venerunt tanquam gozal columbae filium, ponderis duarum librarum. A pipitu dicitur gozal, vel gauzal, ut latine pipio. Nam الجزل algezel est pipitus. In Alcamus redditur صوت الحمام vox columbae; nempe pipientis adhuc, et nondum adultae. Itaque falluntur, qui גוזל gozal referunt ad verbum גזל gazal, id est, rapere; quia rapinae est obnoxius. Syri per metathesim ܙܘܓܠ zogal dicunt pro ܓܘܙܠ gozal. Sed Poeni olim gozol. Unde peristereon Punice חציר־גוזול azirgozol, id est, herba pipionis, in Herbario Apuleii capite sexagesimo quinto.

From this it might seem possible to conclude that doves have nested in Syria as well as in Greece from all time. Yet there is an objection to be raised: in the age of Moses, it is true, בני יונה, young of doves, were offered even by common people in Israel, as the Law expressly prescribes; but whether Abraham offered the same can be called into question. For we do not read that he offered תור ובן יונה, a turtledove and a young dove, as is commanded II, 531 in the Law of Moses, but תור וגוזל, a turtledove and a gozal. This word signifies not only the young of the dove properly so called, but also the young of the turtledove, of the wood pigeon, of the livia, of the vinago, and of any other bird that may be assigned to the dove kind. Damir [=al-Damīrī, Ḥayāt al-ḥayawān], in the chapter On القطا [al-qaṭā] alkata, as the Arabs call a certain kind of wood pigeon, writes: يقال القطا والحمام وانواعها امهات الجوازل والجوازل فراخها الواحد جوزل [yuqāl al-qaṭā wa-l-ḥamām wa-anwāʿuhā ummahāt al-jawāzil wa-l-jawāzil firākhuhā al-wāḥid jawzal] "Alkata and the dove, and col. 7 birds of the same kind are called the mothers of the algavvazil. And the algavvazil are their young, from the singular gauzal." Now جوزل [jawzal] gauzal is for the Arabs exactly what גוזל gozal is for the Hebrews. Accordingly, since there are various species of gozal, when a pipio [squab] is meant precisely, there is sometimes added, by way of distinction, "gozal, young of the dove." Thus in the Chaldee paraphrast of the Song of Songs, chapter 1, verse 15: "Your eyes are כגוזלין בני יונתא, like gozalin, young of the dove." And Vajikra Rabba [=Leviticus Rabbah], chapter 22, on the emperor Titus: קראו לרופאים ומצאו בו כמו גוזל בן יונה והיה בו משקל שתי לטראות, "They called the physicians, who (when his skull had been cut open) found in it something like a gozal, a young of the dove, weighing two pounds." It is called gozal, or gauzal, from its peeping, just as in Latin pipio. For الجزل [al-jazl] algezel is pipitus [peeping]. In the Alcamus [=al-Qāmūs al-muḥīṭ, the Arabic dictionary of al-Fīrūzābādī, which Bochart cites like an author] it is rendered صوت الحمام [ṣawt al-ḥamām] the voice of the dove, namely of one still peeping and not yet grown. Therefore those are mistaken who derive גוזל gozal from the verb גזל gazal, that is, to seize, because it is exposed to plunder. The Syrians, by metathesis, say ܙܘܓܠ [זוגל] zogal instead of ܓܘܙܠ [גוזל] gozal. But the Phoenicians of old said gozol. Hence peristereon is in Punic חציר־גוזול azirgozol, that is, herb of the squab, in the Herbarium of Apuleius, chapter 65.

Iam de columbis Semiramidis posset id ipsum regeri. Quippe, licet etiam יונים jonim, id est, columbas domesticas, et quae proprie sic dicuntur, coluerint Syri posteriores in gratiam Semiramidis; sylvestres tamen columbas illi adfuisse, vel ex Ctesia videtur posse colligi. Cum loca, in quibus degebant illae columbae, deserta et petrosa vocet, ἐρήμους, καὶ πετρώδεις τόπους. Proinde nomen Semiramidis, inde sumptum, ab Hesychio explicatur περιστερὰ ὄρειος, columba montana. Quam vocem esse Arabicam, non Syram, ut putatur, alibi docuimus, et scriptam fuisse Serimam, quod διὰ τὴν εὐφωνίαν Graeci mutarunt in Σεμίραμιν. Nempe Arabibus حمام himam est columba, et سراة sera mons, شعرا sera sylva, صحرا sahra desertum: ex quarum vocum una aliqua eum حمام himam juncta factum nomen Serimam pro montis, aut sylvae, aut deserti columba. Qualis est compositio in verbis כרמיון charmion, vel כרמיונא charmiona, de quibus dictum, et חריונים chirjonim, vel דביונים dibjonim, de II, 532 quibus dicturi sumus. Sed haec praecipuum, quod ex historia Semiramidis columbas jam olim fuisse in Syria nullo modo probari potest; quia illius tempus est plane incertum. Nam, si Ctesiae stamus, Scriptori valde fabuloso, Nini uxor fuit Semiramis, cujus regnum incidet in Abrahami tempora. Sed Semiramin alii Belochi filiam faciunt, qui annis plus quingentis Nino posterior fuit. Hinc Eusebius ad numerum 582. qui decimus est a morte Iosuae: Assyriorum octavus decimus Belochus annis XXV. cujus filia Atossa, quae et Semiramis, regnavit cum patre annis XII. Et Photius auctore 186. de Canone Hist. 9. Ὅσα οἱ ἄλλοι περὶ Ἀττόσης Ἀσσυρίου ἀναγράφουσι, ταῦθ᾽ οὗτος εἰς Σεμίραμιν ἀνα-φέρει· Quaecunque alii de Atossa Assyria scribunt, is refert ad Semiramin. Sed, quod mirere magis, si Herodoto credimus libro primo, Semiramis nonnisi πέν-τε γενεαῖς, quinque generationibus, id est, annis ad summum ducentis, Nitocri vetustior fuit, illius Laboniti matre, quem devicit Cyrus. Cum inter Cyrum et Belochum anni plus mille, inter eundem et Ninum anni intercedant mille quingenti, vel sexcenti. Neque vero referri possunt gesta Semiramidis ad Abrahami aetatem, qua regem שנער, seu Babyloniae, regulum fuisse colligere est ex Gen. XIV. Et aeque incertum, quod quidam asserunt, Babyloniae reges columbam col. 8 habuisse pro signo militari: cum vetus auctor nullus citetur, qui hoc referat (2). Et Cyrum verissimile est, postquam Babylonios imperio suo subjecit, eadem usurpasse signa militaria, quae habuerant Medi et Assyrii, antecessores. Eum autem pro signo aquilam habuisse, constat ex his Xenophontis libro septimo Cyropaediae, Ἦν δ᾽ αὐτῷ τὸ σημεῖον ἀετὸς χρυσοῦς ἐπὶ δόρατος μακροῦ ἀνατεταμέ-νος. Erat autem ipsi pro signo aquila aurea, in longa hasta extensa. Quod exinde Persarum reges omnes imitati sunt. Idem Xenophon, Καὶ νῦν δὲ ἔτι τοῦτο τὸ σημεῖον τῷ Περσῶν βασιλεῖ διαμένει· Hoc etiam signum hodieque restat Persarum regi. Curtius libro tertio, ubi de exercitu Darii: Inter haec aquilam auream, pennas extendenti similem, sacraverant. Philostratus in Heroicis capite de Themistocle, Μῆδοι ταῦτα, καὶ Βαβυλὼν μέση, καὶ τὸ σημεῖον τὸ βασίλειον ὁ χρυσοῦς ἐπὶ τῆς πέλτης ἀετός· Medi haec, et Babylon media, et signum militare aurea super hastam aquila. Itaque in Ieremia [XXV, 38. XLVI,16. L,16.] חרב היונה non est gladius columbae, ut quidem puto, sed gladius opprimens, vel devastans, qualis Babyloniorum fuit sub rege Nebucadnetsare (3). Proinde II, 533 de eodem dicit propheta Ierem. XLVI, 14. אכלה חרב סביביך, Te consumsit gladius circumquaque. Sed et Samaritanos columbas coluisse, prorsus est fabulosum. Quae idola coluerint, cum primum ex Assyria in Samariam translati sunt, refertur 2 Reg. XVII, 30. 31. Viri Babylonii fecerunt Succoth-Benoth, Chuthaei fecerunt Nergal, etc. At ibi de columba, aut ejus imagine, ab iis culta, ne γρῦ quidem Sacra Historia. Neque id iis uspiam a Iosepho objectum, qui multis locis acriter in eos invehitur. Videsis Antiquitatum Iudaicarum li-brum nonum, et undecimum in fine; et libri duodecimi caput primum et septimum; et libri decimi tertii caput sextum; et libri decimi octavi caput tertium et quintum; et libri vigesimi caput quintum. Neque ulla ratione potuisset consistere columbae, aut ejus imaginis cultus cum Lege Mosis, quam admittebant. Nam, ut taceam, simulachra omnia toties et tam diserte a Mose prohiberi, quo tandem praetextu ab iis coli potuisset id ipsum animal, quod in sacrificiis ad Deum mactare jubebantur? Tamen haud dubie verum est, quod Xenophon, Ctesias, Lucianus atque alii testes αὐτόπται asserunt, Syros et Assyrios aut columbas ut Deas coluisse, aut saltem ab iis abstinuisse tanquam a re sacra. Sed quo praecise tempore in Syriam, aut Assyriam invecta fuerit haec superstitio, nemo facile dixerit.

Now, the same objection could be raised regarding the doves of Semiramis. For although the later Syrians also worshipped יונים jonim, that is, domestic doves, those properly so called, out of regard for Semiramis, it nevertheless seems possible to infer, even from Ctesias, that wild doves were attendant on her. For he calls the places in which those doves lived desert and rocky, ἐρήμους, καὶ πετρώδεις τόπους [erēmous, kai petrōdeis topous]. Accordingly the name of Semiramis, taken from this, is explained by Hesychius as περιστερὰ ὄρειος [peristera oreios], mountain dove. We have shown elsewhere that this word is Arabic, not Syrian as is supposed, and that it was written Serimam, which the Greeks changed διὰ τὴν εὐφωνίαν [dia tēn euphōnian] into Σεμίραμιν [Semiramin]. For among the Arabs حمام [ḥmām] himam is dove, and سراة [srāh] sera mountain, شعرا [shʿrā] sera forest, صحرا [ṣḥrā] sahra desert; from some one of these words joined with حمام [ḥmām] himam the name Serimam was formed, for dove of the mountain, or of the forest, or of the desert. Such is the composition in the words כרמיון charmion or כרמיונא charmiona, of which we have spoken, and חריונים chirjonim or דביונים dibjonim, of II, 532 which we shall speak. But the chief point is this: from the history of Semiramis it can in no way be proved that doves were already worshipped in Syria in ancient times, because her date is quite uncertain. For if we follow Ctesias, a highly fabulous writer, Semiramis was the wife of Ninus, whose reign would fall in the times of Abraham. But others make Semiramis the daughter of Belochus, who was more than five hundred years later than Ninus. Hence Eusebius at the number 582, which is the tenth year from the death of Joshua: Eighteenth king of the Assyrians, Belochus, for 25 years; whose daughter Atossa, who is also Semiramis, reigned with her father for 12 years. And Photius, on author 186, concerning the Canon, Hist. 9: Ὅσα οἱ ἄλλοι περὶ Ἀττόσης Ἀσσυρίου ἀναγράφουσι, ταῦθ᾽ οὗτος εἰς Σεμίραμιν ἀναφέρει· [Hosa hoi alloi peri Attosēs Assyriou anagraphousi, tauth᾽ houtos eis Semiramin anapherei] Whatever others write about Atossa the Assyrian, he refers to Semiramis. But, what is more surprising, if we believe Herodotus in his first book, Semiramis was older only by πέντε γενεαῖς [pente geneais], five generations, that is, at most two hundred years, than Nitocris, the mother of that Labonitus whom Cyrus defeated. Yet between Cyrus and Belochus more than a thousand years intervene, and between him and Ninus fifteen or sixteen hundred. Nor can the deeds of Semiramis be referred to the age of Abraham, when, as may be gathered from Gen. XIV, the king of שנער, or Babylonia, was a petty prince. And equally uncertain is what some assert, that the kings of Babylonia had the dove col. 8 as a military standard, since no ancient author is cited who reports this R2. And it is most probable that Cyrus, after subjecting the Babylonians to his rule, adopted the same military standards that his predecessors, the Medes and Assyrians, had had. But that he had an eagle as his standard is clear from these words of Xenophon in the seventh book of the Cyropaedia: Ἦν δ᾽ αὐτῷ τὸ σημεῖον ἀετὸς χρυσοῦς ἐπὶ δόρατος μακροῦ ἀνατεταμένος [Ēn d᾽ autō to sēmeion aetos chrysous epi doratos makrou anatetamenos]. His standard was a golden eagle, stretched out upon a long spear. This all the kings of the Persians thereafter imitated. The same Xenophon: Καὶ νῦν δὲ ἔτι τοῦτο τὸ σημεῖον τῷ Περσῶν βασιλεῖ διαμένει· [Kai nyn de eti touto to sēmeion tō Persōn basilei diamenei] This standard still remains even today for the king of the Persians. Curtius, book 3, on the army of Darius: Among these they had consecrated a golden eagle, resembling one spreading its wings. Philostratus in the Heroica, in the chapter on Themistocles: Μῆδοι ταῦτα, καὶ Βαβυλὼν μέση, καὶ τὸ σημεῖον τὸ βασίλειον ὁ χρυσοῦς ἐπὶ τῆς πέλτης ἀετός· [Mēdoi tauta, kai Babylōn mesē, kai to sēmeion to basileion ho chrysous epi tēs peltēs aetos] These things are the Medes, and Babylon in the middle, and the military standard, a golden eagle upon a spear. Therefore in Jeremiah [XXV, 38. XLVI,16. L,16.] חרב היונה is not, as I at least think, the sword of the dove, but the oppressing or devastating sword, such as that of the Babylonians was under King Nebuchadnezzar R3. Accordingly II, 533 the prophet says of the same, Jer 46:14: אכלה חרב סביביך, The sword has devoured round about you. But that the Samaritans also worshipped doves is entirely fabulous. What idols they worshipped when they were first transplanted from Assyria into Samaria is related in 2 Reg. XVII, 30. 31.: The men of Babylon made Succoth-Benoth, the Cuthites made Nergal, etc. But there the Sacred History says not a γρῦ [gry] about a dove, or its image, worshipped by them. Nor was this anywhere charged against them by Josephus, who in many places inveighs sharply against them. See the ninth book of the Jewish Antiquities, and the end of the eleventh; and chapters 1 and 7 of book 12; and chapter 6 of book 13; and chapters 3 and 5 of book 18; and chapter 5 of book 20. Nor could the worship of the dove, or of its image, in any way have been consistent with the Law of Moses, which they accepted. For, to say nothing of the fact that all images are forbidden so often and so expressly by Moses, on what pretext could they possibly have worshipped the very animal which they were commanded to slaughter to God in sacrifice? Nevertheless it is undoubtedly true, as Xenophon, Ctesias, Lucian and other eyewitnesses, αὐτόπται [autoptai], assert, that the Syrians and Assyrians either worshipped doves as goddesses, or at least abstained from them as from something sacred. But at what precise time this superstition was introduced into Syria or Assyria, no one could easily say.
