III, 115 col. 349 CAP. XXXII.

III, 115 col. 349 Chapter 32. ¶

Probatur, עטלף atalleph esse Vespertilionem.

It is proved that עטלף atalleph is the bat. ¶

Ut ab aquila incepit Moses, quae regina est avium, ita in vespertilione desiit, qui naturae est ambiguae, et inter aves et quadrupedes medium locum obtinet. Huc pertinet, quod Philosophus eum scribit ἐπαμφοτερίζειν τοῖς πτη-νοῖς καὶ πεζοῖς, ἀμφωτέρων τε μετέχειν, καὶ οὐδετέρων, De partibus animalium libri quarti capite decimo tertio. Hinc Varro in Agathone,

As Moses began with the eagle, which is the queen of birds, so he ended with the bat, which is of ambiguous nature and holds a middle place between birds and quadrupeds. To this belongs what the Philosopher writes, that it ἐπαμφοτερίζειν τοῖς πτηνοῖς καὶ πεζοῖς, ἀμφωτέρων τε μετέχειν, καὶ οὐδετέρων [epamphoterizein tois ptēnois kai pezois, amphōterōn te metechein, kai oudeterōn], On the Parts of Animals, book 4, chapter 13. Hence Varro in the Agathon: ¶

Quid multa? factus sum vespertilio, neque
In muribus plane, neque in volucribus sum.

Why say more? I have become a bat, and I am neither plainly among the mice nor among the birds. ¶

Et Macrobius Saturnalium libri septimi capite decimo sexto, Exordio volantia universa de ovis prodeunt, excepto uno, quod incertae naturae est. Nam vespertilio volat quidem pellitis alis: sed inter volantia non habendus est, quia quatuor pedibus graditur, formatosque pullos parit et nutrit lacte, quos generat. Itaque ut Samaritani apud Iosephum modo se Iudaeos, modo se ethnicos esse profitebantur, prout res Iudaeorum bene, aut male cedebant, ita in fabulis vespertilio modo se murem, modo se avem esse dictitat, prout e re sua est. Et notum est Panarcis Aenigma, ὅτι βάλοι ξύλῳ τε καὶ οὐ ξύλῳ καθημένην ὄρνιθα καὶ οὐκ ὄρνιθα ἀνήρ τε καὶ οὐκ ἀνὴρ λίθῳ καὶ οὐ λίθῳ, vir non vir (eunuchus) insidentem avem non avem (vespertilionem) ferit ligno non ligno (ferula), aut lapide III, 116 non lapide (pumice): cujus griphi meminere Plato libro quinto De Legibus, Clearchus in libro De griphis, Athenaeus libri decimi capite decimo nono, et Suidas obiter in Νυκτερίδος αἶνος. Miratur vir doctissimus, cur ibi dicatur sedere haec avis, cum non sedeat, sed vel volet, vel appensa petris haereat, aut arborum ramis. Itaque καθημένην mutari vult in πετομένην. Sed non puto quidquam mutandum. Καθήμενος non semper sedentem significat, sed saepe desidentem, manentem, quiescentem. Exempla vide Matth. IV, 16. et IX, 9. et XI, 16. et passim. Res notior est, quam ut probatione egeat. Et Romanis quoque sedere id ipsum est. Itaque de hac eadem ave Symposius Aenigmate vigesimo octavo,

And Macrobius, Saturnalia, book 7, chapter 16: All flying things come forth at the beginning from eggs, except one, which is of uncertain nature. For the bat does indeed fly with skin wings, but it is not to be counted among flying things, because it walks on four feet, and it bears formed young and nourishes with milk those it begets. Therefore, just as the Samaritans in Josephus professed themselves now Jews, now gentiles, according as the affairs of the Jews went well or badly, so in the fables the bat declares itself now a mouse, now a bird, as suits its interest. And well known is the riddle of Panarces, ὅτι βάλοι ξύλῳ τε καὶ οὐ ξύλῳ καθημένην ὄρνιθα καὶ οὐκ ὄρνιθα ἀνήρ τε καὶ οὐκ ἀνὴρ λίθῳ καὶ οὐ λίθῳ [hoti baloi xylō te kai ou xylō kathēmenēn ornitha kai ouk ornitha anēr te kai ouk anēr lithō kai ou lithō], a man not a man (a eunuch) strikes a bird not a bird (a bat) sitting on wood not wood (a fennel stalk), with a stone III, 116 not a stone (pumice). This riddle is mentioned by Plato, Laws, book 5, Clearchus in the book On Riddles, Athenaeus, book 10, chapter 19, and Suidas in passing under Νυκτερίδος αἶνος [Nykteridos ainos]. A very learned man wonders why this bird is there said to sit, since it does not sit, but either flies or hangs suspended from rocks or from the branches of trees. He therefore wants καθημένην [kathēmenēn] changed to πετομένην [petomenēn]. But I do not think anything should be changed. Καθήμενος [Kathēmenos] does not always mean sitting, but often settling, remaining, resting. See the examples Matt 4:16 and IX, 9. and XI, 16. and passim. The matter is too well known to need proof. And for the Romans too sedere [to sit] means exactly this. Thus of this same bird Symposius, Riddle 28: ¶

Sed sedeo in tenebris, nec me committo diebus. col. 350

But I sit in darkness, and do not entrust myself to the days. col. 350 ¶

Hebraice עטלף atalleph, et Chaldaice עטלפא attallepha, cur dicatur, hactenus fuit incertum, sed rem ignotam conabor aperire. In nominibus polysyllabis saepe videas literarum trajectiones: ut in אלגומים algumim, pro אלמוגים almugim; מתלעות mathleoth, pro מלתעות maltheoth; et in Aegyptio CALASIRI ex קרסול karsol. Aliquid hic simile contigisse putaverim, et עטלף atalleph dictum pro עפטלל aphtalel, vel ephtalel, id est, ave tenebrarum. Nam עוף oph est avis: et Hebraeum צל tsel, vel צלל tselal, pro umbra et tenebris, Chaldaice scribitur טול tul, et טלל telall (1). Ita haec avis nomen habuerit naturae suae plane congruum. Nam Graece quoque νυκτερὶς dicitur ἀπὸ τῆς νυκτὸς, quia est ἐκ τῶν νυκτινόμων. Neque alia ratio Romani nominis. Ovidius Metamorphosews libri quarti Fabula duodecima,

Why it is called עטלף atalleph in Hebrew and עטלפא attallepha in Chaldee has hitherto been uncertain, but I shall try to disclose the unknown matter. In polysyllabic names one often sees transpositions of letters: as in אלגומים algumim for אלמוגים almugim; מתלעות mathleoth for מלתעות maltheoth; and in the Egyptian CALASIRI from קרסול karsol. I would think something similar has happened here, and that עטלף atalleph is said for עפטלל aphtalel or ephtalel, that is, bird of darkness. For עוף oph is bird; and the Hebrew צל tsel or צלל tselal, for shade and darkness, is written in Chaldee טול tul and טלל telall R1. So this bird would have had a name entirely fitting its nature. For in Greek too it is called νυκτερὶς [nykteris] from νυκτὸς [nyktos, night], because it is ἐκ τῶν νυκτινόμων [ek tōn nyktinomōn]. Nor is the reason for the Roman name any different. Ovid, Metamorphoses, book 4, Fable 12: ¶

Nocte volant, seroque tenent a vespere nomen.

They fly by night, and take their name from the late evening. ¶

Symposius ubi supra,

Symposius, in the place cited above: ¶

Nox mihi dat nomen, primo de tempore noctis.

Night gives me my name, from the first time of night. ¶

Nonnius, Vespertilio, animal volucre, biforme; dictum, quod vespere se ad volatum praeparat noctis. Isidorus, Vespertilio pro tempore nomen accepit, eo quod, lucem fugiens, crepusculo vespertino circumvolat. Arabes vocant الخفاش alchopphas ab oculorum vitio. Alcamus, الخفاش الوطواط سمي لصغر عينيه وضعف بصره Alchopphas, idem, quod alvvatvvat (vespertilio). Ita vocatur propter oculorum parvitatem et visus debilitatem. III, 117 Nam الخفش alchaphas eidem est صغر العين وضعف البصر خلقة او فصال في الجفون بلا وجع وان يبصر باليل دون النهار وفي يوم غيم دون صاحي parvitas oculi, et visus debilitas naturalis, aut vitium in palpebris sine dolore, aut cum quis noctu plus videt, quam interdiu, et die caliginoso plus quam claro.

Nonius: Vespertilio, a winged creature of two forms; so called because at evening it prepares itself for the night's flight. Isidore: The bat received its name from the time, because, fleeing the light, it flies about in the evening twilight. The Arabs call it الخفاش [al-khuffāsh] alchopphas, from a defect of the eyes. Alcamus [=al-Qāmūs al-muḥīṭ, the Arabic dictionary of al-Fīrūzābādī]: الخفاش الوطواط سمي لصغر عينيه وضعف بصره [al-khuffāsh al-waṭwāṭ summiya li-ṣighar ʿaynayhi wa-ḍaʿf baṣarihi] Alchopphas, the same as alvvatvvat (bat). It is so called because of the smallness of its eyes and the weakness of its sight. III, 117 For according to the same author الخفش [al-khafash] alchaphas is صغر العين وضعف البصر خلقة او فصال في الجفون بلا وجع وان يبصر باليل دون النهار وفي يوم غيم دون صاحي [ṣighar al-ʿayn wa-ḍaʿf al-baṣar khilqatan aw fiṣāl fī al-jufūn bi-lā wajaʿ wa-an yubṣira bi-l-layl dūna al-nahār wa-fī yawm ghaym dūna ṣāḥī], smallness of the eye and natural weakness of sight, or a defect in the eyelids without pain, or when someone sees better by night than by day, and on a cloudy day better than on a clear one. ¶

Extra Legem unicus occurrit in Scriptura locus, in quo de vespertilione agatur. Nempe Esa. II, 20. In illa die projiciet homo idola sua argentea, et ido- la sua aurea, quae ad adorandum sibi fecerat, ad talpas, ולעטלפים, et ad vesper- tiliones: id est, in tenebras detrudet, ut ne amplius superstitiosis hominibus fulgore suo illudant. Quia, ut talpa caeca est, ita vespertilio fugit lucem; quod pluribus docuimus in capite De talpis a).

Outside the Law only one passage occurs in Scripture that deals with the bat, namely Isa 2:20. In that day a man shall cast his idols of silver and his idols of gold, which he had made for himself to worship, to the moles ולעטלפים, and to the bats: that is, he will thrust them down into darkness, so that they may no longer deceive superstitious people with their brilliance. For as the mole is blind, so the bat flees the light, as we have shown at greater length in the chapter On Moles a). ¶

Mahumetanam de vespertilione fabulam pro corollario libet addere. Deum Patrem per verbum aeternum, id est, per Iesum Christum, filium suum, omnia creavisse credimus, a Scriptura ita edocti. Ita enim David Psalm. XXXIII, 6. Verbo domini coeli facti sunt. Et Ioh. I, 3. Omnia per ipsum facta sunt: et sine ipso factum est nihil, quod factum est. Et 1 Cor. VIII, 6. Unus Dominus Iesus Christus, per quem omnia, et nos per ipsum: Et Col. I, 16. Per eum condita sunt omnia, quae in coelis sunt, et quae in terra; visibilia et invisibilia, etc. Omnia per ipsum et in ipso creata sunt. Et Hebr. I, 2. Loquutus est nobis in Filio, quem constituit haeredem omnium, per quem etiam mundum condidit. Hinc Ioh. V, 19. Quaecunque ille (Pater) fecerit, haec et Filius similiter facit. Haec Pro- phetae et Apostoli. At recutiti doctores, praeter vespertilionem, Christum quidquam creasse negant. Quo argumento, ipsi viderint. Nam in Alcorani Surata, cui titulus ال عمران Familia Amrani, quem Mariae, virginis, patrem fuisse col. 351 fingunt, aves se creare dicit Christus: Ad vos, inquit, veni cum signo a Do- mino vestro, اني اخلق لكم من الطين كهية الطير فانفخ فيها فيكون طيرا باذن الله Ego formo vobis ex luto tanquam avis figuram, et flo in eam, et fit avis, Deo sic permittente. Et in Su- rata Almeida, id est, mensa, Deus illum sic alloquitur: وان تخلق من الطين كهية الطير باذني فتنفخ فيها فتكون طيرا باذني Et, cum ex luto avis imaginem formabas permissu meo, et fla- bas in eam, et, me concedente fiebat avis. Sed has scilicet aves vespertiliones III, 118 fuisse, et Iesum Christum nihil praeterea creasse, divinarunt, qui vixerunt post Mahometem; quia ad horum tenebrionum ingenium visa est accommodatior avis tenebricosa. Hinc Alkazuinius, وانما طالب بنو اسرايل عيسي عليه السلام يخلق الخفاش لانه اتم الطير خلقه لان له اذنا واسنان وثديا فاتخذ من الطين Israelis porro filii Iesum, cui bene sit, rogaverunt, ut vespertilionem crearet; quia vespertilio est natura sua perfectissimus, cum habeat aures, et dentes, et mammas: et sumpsit lutum, etc. Et Damir, الخفاش هو الذي خلقه عيسي بن مريم باذن الله Vespertilio est avis, quam Dei permissu creavit Iesus, Mariae filius. Et Ibno Abas, الطير الوطواط الذي نفخ فيها عيسي بن مريم وكان طيرا باذن الله عز وجل Avis watwat (vespertilio), in quam flavit Iesus, Mariae filius, et facta est avis, permissu Dei fortis et gloriosi. Et VVaheb, filius Manbae, لما خلق عيسي عليه السلامّ الخفاش باذن الله كان يطير ما دام الناس ينظرون اليه فاذا غاب عن ابصارهم سقط ميتا الحكمة في ذلك ليبين فعل الخالق من فعل الخالق تعالي Cur Iesus, cui bene sit, permissu Dei, vespertilionem creavit, qui a volatu non cessat, dum illum aspiciunt homines: sed, cum aspicere desinunt, jacet ut mortuus? Ut inde sciremus, creaturae opus quantum differat ab opere creatoris, qui summus est Deus. At, o boni, si Christus vespertilionem creavit demum, postquam e virgine natus est, quomodo illius Moses, Esaias, col. 352 Graeci interpretes, Homerus, Herodotus, Corinna, Plato, Aristoteles, Clearchus, Antigonus, Nicander, Varro, et sexcenti alii ante Christum natum meminerunt? Aut, si in specie, quae jam exstabat, et a summo Deo creata fuerat, nova individua creavit Christus, Mariae filius, Christi opera a primi illius creatoris operibus nihil differunt.

By way of a corollary I should like to add a Muhammadan fable about the bat. We believe that God the Father created all things through the eternal Word, that is, through Jesus Christ, his Son, as Scripture teaches us. For so David says, Ps 33:6: By the word of the Lord the heavens were made. And John 1:3: All things were made through him, and without him nothing was made that was made. And 1 Cor. VIII, 6: One Lord Jesus Christ, through whom are all things, and we through him. And Col. I, 16: Through him were founded all things that are in the heavens and that are on earth, visible and invisible, etc. All things were created through him and in him. And Heb 1:2: He has spoken to us in the Son, whom he appointed heir of all things, through whom also he founded the world. Hence John 5:19: Whatever he (the Father) does, these things the Son also does likewise. So say the Prophets and the Apostles. But the circumcised doctors deny that Christ created anything except the bat. On what grounds, let them see to it. For in the Sura of the Qur'an entitled ال عمران [Āl ʿImrān] The Family of Amran (whom they imagine to have been the father of Mary, the virgin), col. 351 Christ says that he creates birds: I have come to you, he says, with a sign from your Lord: اني اخلق لكم من الطين كهية الطير فانفخ فيها فيكون طيرا باذن الله [innī akhluqu lakum min al-ṭīn ka-hayʾat al-ṭayr fa-anfukhu fīhā fa-yakūnu ṭayran bi-idhn Allāh] I form for you out of clay something like the figure of a bird, and I breathe into it, and it becomes a bird, God so permitting. And in the Sura Almeida, that is, the Table, God addresses him thus: وان تخلق من الطين كهية الطير باذني فتنفخ فيها فتكون طيرا باذني [wa-in takhluqu min al-ṭīn ka-hayʾat al-ṭayr bi-idhnī fa-tanfukhu fīhā fa-takūnu ṭayran bi-idhnī] And when you formed out of clay the image of a bird by my permission, and breathed into it, and by my leave it became a bird. But that these birds were, of course, bats, and that Jesus Christ created nothing besides, was divined by those who lived after Muhammad, III, 118 because a bird of darkness seemed better suited to the temper of these lovers of darkness. Hence Alkazuinius [=al-Qazwīnī, ʿAjāʾib al-makhlūqāt]: وانما طالب بنو اسرايل عيسي عليه السلام يخلق الخفاش لانه اتم الطير خلقه لان له اذنا واسنان وثديا فاتخذ من الطين [wa-innamā ṭālaba banū Isrāyīl ʿĪsā ʿalayhi al-salām yakhluqu al-khuffāsh li-annahu atammu al-ṭayr khalqahu li-anna lahu udhnan wa-asnān wa-thadyan fa-ittakhadha min al-ṭīn] Moreover, the children of Israel asked Jesus, on whom be peace, to create a bat, because the bat is by its nature the most perfect, since it has ears, teeth and breasts; and he took clay, etc. And Damir [=al-Damīrī, Ḥayāt al-ḥayawān]: الخفاش هو الذي خلقه عيسي بن مريم باذن الله [al-khuffāsh huwa alladhī khalaqahu ʿĪsā ibn Maryam bi-idhn Allāh] The bat is the bird which Jesus, son of Mary, created by God's permission. And Ibno Abas: الطير الوطواط الذي نفخ فيها عيسي بن مريم وكان طيرا باذن الله عز وجل [al-ṭayr al-waṭwāṭ alladhī nafakha fīhā ʿĪsā ibn Maryam wa-kāna ṭayran bi-idhn Allāh ʿazza wa-jalla] The bird watwat (the bat), into which Jesus, son of Mary, breathed, and it became a bird by the permission of God, mighty and glorious. And VVaheb, son of Manba: لما خلق عيسي عليه السلامّ الخفاش باذن الله كان يطير ما دام الناس ينظرون اليه فاذا غاب عن ابصارهم سقط ميتا الحكمة في ذلك ليبين فعل الخالق من فعل الخالق تعالي [lammā khalaqa ʿĪsā ʿalayhi al-salām al-khuffāsh bi-idhn Allāh kāna yaṭīru mā dāma al-nās yanẓurūna ilayhi fa-idhā ghāba ʿan abṣārihim saqaṭa mayyitan al-ḥikma fī dhālika li-yubayyina fiʿl al-khāliq min fiʿl al-khāliq taʿālā] Why did Jesus, on whom be peace, by God's permission create the bat, which does not cease flying while men look at it, but, when they stop looking, lies as if dead? So that we might know from this how much the work of a creature differs from the work of the Creator, who is God most high. But, good sirs, if Christ created the bat only after he was born of the virgin, how is it that Moses, Isaiah, col. 352 the Greek translators, Homer, Herodotus, Corinna, Plato, Aristotle, Clearchus, Antigonus, Nicander, Varro, and six hundred others mentioned it before Christ was born? Or, if within a species that already existed and had been created by the supreme God, Christ, son of Mary, created new individuals, then Christ's works differ in no way from the works of that first Creator. ¶
