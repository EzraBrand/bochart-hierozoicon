II, 806 Chapter XIII. col. 214 ¶

De Corvis, qui Eliae panem et carnes afferebant prope torrentem Cerith 1 Reg. XVII, 3.

On the ravens that brought Elias bread and meat near the brook Cherith, 1 Reg. XVII, 3. ¶

Iam de Elia dicendum, cui, latenti ad torrentem Cerith, prope Iordanem הערבים, corvi, a Deo jussi, panem et carnes bis afferebant in dies, 1 Reg. XVII, 3. 4. 5. 6. Quod, quia morosis ingeniis parum credibile visum est, non desunt, qui Hebraeo verbo ערבים orebim negent corvos intelligi. Sic apud Kimchium nonnulli mercatores explicant, ut Ezech. XXVII, 27. ubi ערבי מערבך, id est, qui negotiantur negotia tua. Aliis ערבים sunt Arabes; ut 2 Par. XXI, 16. et Nehem. IV, 1. In Beresith Rabba cap. 33. עיר היא בתחום ביתשאן ושמה ערבו, Urbs est Arabo nomine in vicinia Bethsan (sive Scythopoleos): ut scribit Rabbi Iuda, cujus cives hic dici putantur הערבים. Arabi etiam interpreti ערבים Orebim non sunt corvi, sed populi nomen. Is enim ita habet 1 Reg. XVII, 4. Praecepi لعوربيم Orebimis, ut te nuiriant. Et versu sexto, وكان العوربيم Et fuit, ut Orebimi afferrent ipsi panem et carnem (1). Sed facile est docere, haec interpretamenta nihil habere sani. Utut enim ab Ezechiele II, 807 Tyrii mercatores describantur hac periphrasi ערבי מערבך, qui negotiantur negotia tua: tamen ערבים per se mercatores nusquam significat. Nec Deus in genere dixisset, Praecepi mercatoribus, sed Praecepi mercatoribus hujus, vel illius loci, ut te nutriant. Reliquas versiones refellit loci situs, in quo accidit hoc miraculum. Nam prope Iordanem, ubi latebat Elias, nulli erant Arabes, nulli Orebimi, neque ulla urbs Arabo nomine, quod quidem sciam. Et Arabes Hebraice non dicuntur ערבים Orebim; sed ערבים Arbim: et urbs ערבו Arabo, si quae fuit, quod mihi nondum constat, hujus incolae ערבויים Arabojim (2) vocati sunt, non Orebim. Praeterea Elias, ibi latere jussus, non vere latuisset, si latibuli sui conscios habuisset locorum incolas. Et hae latebrae Achabo, regi, mox patuissent, qui illum magna cura undequaque disquirebat (3).

Now we must speak of Elias, to whom, while he was hiding at the brook Cherith near the Jordan, הערבים, the ravens, at God's command, brought bread and meat twice a day, 1 Reg. XVII, 3. 4. 5. 6. Because this has seemed scarcely credible to captious minds, there are those who deny that ravens are meant by the Hebrew word ערבים orebim. Thus, according to Kimchi [=David Kimḥi (Radak), Sefer ha-Shorashim], some explain it as merchants, as in Ezek 27:27, where we have ערבי מערבך, that is, those who trade in your wares. For others ערבים are Arabs, as in 2 Chr 21:16 and Neh 4:1. In Bereshith Rabba, ch. 33, we read עיר היא בתחום ביתשאן ושמה ערבו, There is a town named Arabo in the vicinity of Bethshan (or Scythopolis), as Rabbi Judah writes; its citizens are thought to be called הערבים here. For the Arabic translator too, ערבים Orebim are not ravens but the name of a people. For he renders 1 Reg. XVII, 4.: I have commanded لعوربيم [li-ʿwrbym] the Orebimites to feed you. And in verse six: وكان العوربيم [wa-kāna al-ʿwrbym] And it came about that the Orebimites brought him bread and meat R1. But it is easy to show that these interpretations have nothing sound in them. For although Ezekiel II, 807 describes the Tyrian merchants with the periphrasis ערבי מערבך, those who trade in your wares, ערבים by itself nowhere means merchants. Nor would God have said in general terms, I have commanded merchants, but rather I have commanded the merchants of this or that place to feed you. The other renderings are refuted by the location of the place where this miracle occurred. For near the Jordan, where Elias was hiding, there were no Arabs, no Orebimites, nor any town named Arabo, as far as I know. Moreover, Arabs are not called ערבים Orebim in Hebrew, but ערבים Arbim; and as for the town ערבו Arabo, if there ever was one (which is not yet established to my satisfaction), its inhabitants would have been called ערבויים Arabojim R2, not Orebim. Furthermore, Elias, ordered to hide there, would not truly have been hidden if the local inhabitants had known of his hiding place. And this hiding place would soon have been disclosed to King Ahab, who was searching for him everywhere with great care R3. ¶

Hic igitur הערבים nihil quidquam sunt, quam veri corvi, quomodo post col. 215 Graecos accipiunt Iosephus, Chaldaeus, Syrus, Hieronymus, plerique ex Hebraeis, et omnes Ecclesiae Doctores, nullo excepto. His Deus praecepisse se dicit. Corvis (inquit) praecepi, ut ibi te nutriant. Nempe brutis etiam praecipere dicitur Deus: ut 2 Paral. VII, 13. Si praecepero locustae, ut exedat terram. Amos IX, 3. Et serpenti praecipiam, ut mordeat eos. Et Ion. II, 10. (al. 11.) Pisci dixit Deus, ut Ionam in terram evomeret. Sed quomodo praecipit etiam rebus inanimatis: puta coelis, Es. XLV, 12. nubibus Iob. XXXVIII, 11. Psal. LXXVIII, 23. Es. V, 6. diluculo, Iob. XXXVIII, 12. gladio, Amos IX, 4. Brutis scilicet, aut nubibus, etc. praecipere est iis uti ad exequenda sua decreta. Recte igitur hic Kimchius, וטעם צויתי ששמתי בלבם להביא לחם ובשר שתי פעמים ביום, Praecepi (corvis), id est, in animum ipsis indidi, ut panem et carnem afferrent bis in die. Sed longe aliter viduae se praecepisse dicit, ut Eliam pasceret, versu nono ejusdem capitis. Illi enim vel verbo expresso praeceperat, vel mentem ejus ad charitatem et pietatem erga Dei servum inflectendo. Notandum porro, plures corvos huic operi destinari, non unum, ut statuit a) Eutychius Alexandrinus; quia non suffecisset unus ad ferendum Eliae necessarium alimentum. Nam in vita Pauli Eremitae apud Sanctum Hieronymum, inter II, 808 quaedam alia, quae fateor me non capere, etiam illud est, quod unus corvus affert panem integrum, qui viris duobus in prandium sufficiat, Antonio nempe, et Paulo. Suspiciunt, inquit, alitem corvum in ramo arboris consedisse, qui, inde leniter subvolans, integrum panem ante mirantium ora deposuit. Post cujus abscessum, Eja inquit Paulus, Dominus nobis prandium misit, etc. Sexa- ginta jam anni sunt, quod accipio dimidii semper panis fragmentum: verum ad adventum tuum militibus suis Christus duplicavit annonam. At hic aliter res se habet. Neque enim unus corvus pluribus, sed plures uni cibum afferunt. Cibus ille fuit panis et caro. Quae seorsim allata fuisse volunt multi veteres, panes scilicet mane, et carnes vespere. Athanasius in Synopsi, Οἱ κόρακες ἔφε-ρον αὐτῷ, διὰ ῥήματα Κυρίου, πρωΐας μὲν ἄρτους, δείλης δὲ κρέα. Theodore- tus in 3. Reg. Quaest. 52. Προσέφερον γὰρ οἱ κόρακες ἄρτους μὲν ἕωθεν, δείλης δὲ κρέα. Augustinus Sermone 146. De tempore, Afferebat servo Dei corvus mane panes, ad vesperam carnes. Eutychius ubi supra, وكان غراب ياتيه في كل يوم بالغداة بخبز وبالعشا بلحم ياكله Et corvus fuit, qui ei quotidie afferebat mane panem, et vespere car- nem, quibus vesceretur. Nimirum ita fert Graeca versio in plerisque exemplaribus: sed et in Complutensi rectius, quod habetur in Hebraeo, Chaldaeo, Syro, Arabico et Romano textu, ἄρτους καὶ κρέας τὸ πρωῒ, καὶ ἄρτους καὶ κρέας τὸ δείλης. Quippe voluit Deus, in summa rerum omnium penuria servo suo utrumque afferri, panem et carnes, ut plenum esset convivium, et sua providentia in illo conservando sic magis eluceret. Eodem pertinet, quod corvi bis in die, mane et vespere, has epulas Prophetae offerebant, prandii scilicet et coenae, statis horis. Utrumque enim apud Iudaeos in usu fuisse, constat ex Luc. XIV, 12. Et meridiani convivii mentio fit Gen. XLIII, 25. Ruth. II, 14. et Act. X, 9. 10.

Here, therefore, the הערבים are nothing other than true ravens, as, after col. 215 the Greeks, they are understood by Josephus, the Chaldee, the Syriac, Jerome, most of the Hebrews, and all the Doctors of the Church without exception. God says that he commanded them: I have commanded the ravens (he says) to feed thee there. God is indeed said to command even brute animals, as in 2 Chr 7:13. If I command the locust to devour the land. Amos 9:3 And I will command the serpent, and he shall bite them. And Jonah 2:10 (var. 11.) God spoke to the fish, that it should vomit out Jonah upon the land. But in the same way he commands even inanimate things: the heavens, for instance, Isa 45:12; the clouds, Job 38:11. Ps 78:23. Isa 5:6; the dawn, Job 38:12; the sword, Amos 9:4. To command brute animals, or clouds, etc., is to use them to carry out his decrees. Kimchi therefore rightly says here: וטעם צויתי ששמתי בלבם להביא לחם ובשר שתי פעמים ביום, I have commanded (the ravens), that is, I have put it into their mind to bring bread and meat twice a day. But it is in a very different way that he says, in the ninth verse of the same chapter, that he commanded the widow to feed Elias. For he had commanded her either by an express word, or by inclining her mind to charity and piety toward the servant of God. It should further be noted that several ravens, not one, were appointed for this task, as a) Eutychius of Alexandria holds; for one would not have sufficed to carry the food Elias needed. For in the life of Paul the Hermit by Saint Jerome, among II, 808 certain other things which I confess I do not understand, there is also this: that a single raven brings a whole loaf, enough for the meal of two men, namely Antony and Paul. They look up, he says, and see that a raven had settled on a branch of the tree, which, flying gently down from there, laid a whole loaf before their astonished faces. After it had gone, Well, said Paul, the Lord has sent us a meal, etc. For sixty years now I have always received a piece of half a loaf; but at your coming Christ has doubled the ration for his soldiers. Here, however, the case is different. For it is not one raven bringing food to several, but several bringing it to one. That food was bread and meat. Many of the ancients hold that these were brought separately: bread in the morning and meat in the evening. Athanasius in the Synopsis: Οἱ κόρακες ἔφερον αὐτῷ, διὰ ῥήματα Κυρίου, πρωΐας μὲν ἄρτους, δείλης δὲ κρέα [Hoi korakes epheron autō, dia rhēmata Kyriou, prōïas men artous, deilēs de krea]. Theodoret on 3 Kings, Question 52: Προσέφερον γὰρ οἱ κόρακες ἄρτους μὲν ἕωθεν, δείλης δὲ κρέα [Prosepheron gar hoi korakes artous men heōthen, deilēs de krea]. Augustine, Sermon 146 De tempore: The raven brought the servant of God bread in the morning, meat in the evening. Eutychius, as cited above: وكان غراب ياتيه في كل يوم بالغداة بخبز وبالعشا بلحم ياكله [wa-kāna ghurāb yaʾtīhi fī kull yawm bi-l-ghadāh bi-khubz wa-bi-l-ʿashā bi-laḥm yaʾkuluhu] And it was a raven that brought him every day bread in the morning and meat in the evening, to eat. This is indeed what the Greek version has in most copies; but the Complutensian has more correctly what is found in the Hebrew, Chaldee, Syriac, Arabic and Roman text: ἄρτους καὶ κρέας τὸ πρωῒ, καὶ ἄρτους καὶ κρέας τὸ δείλης [artous kai kreas to prōi, kai artous kai kreas to deilēs]. For God willed that, amid the utmost scarcity of everything, both bread and meat should be brought to his servant, so that the meal might be complete and his providence in preserving him might shine out the more. To the same purpose is the fact that the ravens brought these meals to the Prophet twice a day, morning and evening, namely dinner and supper, at fixed hours. For that both were customary among the Jews is clear from Luke 14:12. And a midday meal is mentioned in Gen 43:25 Ruth 2:14 and Acts 10:9–10. ¶

Hebraei apud Kimchium anxie quaerunt, unde corvi panes et carnes attulerint. Et alii quidem ex Achabi, alii ex Iosaphati mensa petita fuisse statuunt. Quasi prophetae, in summa frugalitate educato, necesse fuerit habere regales dapes. Dixerim potius, haec prophetae destinata fuisse ab uno aliquo ex illis septem millibus, qui b) genua non flexerant ante Baalem, quem Deus esse col. 216 voluerit tanti arcani conscium. Quamvis sit tutissimum respondere cum auctore Mirabilium Scripturae libri secundi capite decimo quinto, Unde vero eas carnes, et panes ille corvus detulerit, ipse viderit, qui tale officium committebat. Alibi c) huic ipsi Eliae, in deserto esurienti, Dei angelus panem et aquam apponit, quod convenientius fuisse dices, et Θεοπρεπέστερον. Sed et a corvis voluit Deus idem II, 809 officium praestari, ut doceret, omnes creaturas imperio suo subesse (4). At corvus, inquies, immunda est avis, contactuque omnia foedans. Omnino, cum plerumque vescatur cadaveribus, et lex ceremonialis immundis avibus eum accenseat. Sed, quod purificavit Deus, non debet haberi pro immundo, Act. X, 15. Idem Deus, qui hanc legem sanxerat, etiam abrogavit, aut saltem Eliam ab ejus observatione dispensavit ad tempus. Quamvis hujusmodi dispensatione non fuerit opus. Ceremoniarum enim observatio poterat praetermitti, cum urgebat vitae necessitas. Ita Samsoni licuit favum mellis comedere, quem in mortuo leone apes contexuerant; ut ad hunc locum observat Theodoretus. Et Davides esuriens panes τῆς προθέσεως edit sine scrupulo, quos alioqui non licebat edere, nisi solis sacerdotibus, Luc. VI, 4. Ac proinde recte sancitum est, ut Iudaeis, die sabbathi ab hoste provocatis, pugnare licitum esset, et vim vi repellere, 1 Macchab. II, 41. Agebatur enim de vita. Et Sabbathum est propter hominem, non homo propter Sabbathum. Marc. II, 27. Auctor Mirabilium corvos, Eliae servientes, reparasse culpam asserit, in diluvio admissam. Corvis, inquit, ministrare prophetae praecipitur, ut culpam, quam in diluvio commiserat, purgare illa avis videretur, dum ut fidelis minister efficitur Eliae, qui negligens, et fallax erat antea Noae. Chrysostomus in Sermone De Elia vult, Prophetam corvis in eo fuisse similem, quod ut corvus est animal μισότεκνον, ita Elias filios suos, nempe Iudaeos, propter peccata oderat, et, coelo precibus suis clauso, longam famem eis indixerat. Ac Deum proinde prophetae animum, plus nimio exasperatum, emollire voluisse corvorum exemplo; qui, utut immites, et μισότεκνοι, mutato tamen animo, jam prophetae miserebantur, et, ne fame periret, cibos illi subministrabant: quasi Eliae dixisset, Μὴ τοσαύτη παρὰ σοὶ μετὰ Ἰουδαίων μισοτεκνία, ὅση τοῖς κόραξι κατὰ τῶν νεοσσῶν τῶν οἰκείων. ἀλλ᾽ ὅρα νῦν με-ταβεβλημένους τοὺς κόρακας. ὅρα τὴν ἐκείνων, ὦ Ἠλία, φιλανθρωπίαν. οἱ περὶ τοὺς οἰκείους ἀπανθρωποὶ νεοσσοὺς, σοῦ γίνονται ξενοδόχοι φιλότιμοι. Ἄτο-πόν ἐστιν, ὦ Ἠλία, τοὺς μὲν κόρακας μεσίτας γίνεσθαι πρὸς σὲ τῆς παρ᾽ ἐμοῦ φιλανθρωπίας· σὲ δὲ μεσίτην τοῖς Ἰουδαίοις μὴ γίνεσθαι τῆς παρ᾽ ἐμοῦ κατ᾽ II, 810 αὐτῶν τιμωρίας. Αἰδέσθητι τὴν τῶν κοράκων μεταβολὴν, καὶ γένου περὶ τοὺς Ἰουδαίους χρηστός. In Iudaeos filios tuos non tanto odio teneris, quanto corvi in pullos suos. Corvos tamen nunc mutatos vide. Eorum, o Elia, vide benignitatem. Qui in proprios pullos inhumani sunt, erga te munificos hospites se exhibent. Absurdum est, o Elia, quod corvi meae erga te benignitatis sunt mediatores: tu vero pro Iudaeis, quos punio, non intercedis. Corvorum mutationem admirare, et erga Iudaeos esto benignus. Sunt e recentioribus, qui Deum volunt a corvis eo justius hoc officium exegisse, ut prophetam suum alerent, quo majori cura eos alit, cum adhuc pulli a parentibus deseruntur, ut probant ex Iobi, Davidis, atque ipsius Christi verbis, quae aliter explicavimus.

The Hebrews in Kimchi anxiously ask where the ravens brought the bread and meat from. Some hold that they were taken from the table of Ahab, others from that of Jehoshaphat. As if the prophet, brought up in the utmost frugality, had needed royal fare. I would rather say that these things were set aside for the prophet by some one of those seven thousand who b) had not bowed the knee before Baal, whom God col. 216 willed to be privy to so great a secret. Yet it is safest to answer with the author of the Mirabilia Scripturae, book 2, chapter 15: But whence that raven brought that meat and bread, let him see to it who entrusted it with such a task. Elsewhere c) an angel of God sets bread and water before this same Elias when he was hungry in the desert, which you will say was more fitting, and Θεοπρεπέστερον [theoprepesteron]. But God also willed the same II, 809 service to be performed by ravens, to teach that all creatures are subject to his command R4. But the raven, you will say, is an unclean bird, defiling everything by its touch. Quite so, since it mostly feeds on carcasses, and the ceremonial law counts it among the unclean birds. But what God has cleansed must not be held unclean, Acts 10:15. The same God who had enacted this law also abrogated it, or at least dispensed Elias from observing it for a time. Although there was no need of such a dispensation. For the observance of ceremonies could be set aside when the necessity of life pressed. Thus Samson was permitted to eat the honeycomb which bees had built in the dead lion, as Theodoret observes on this passage. And David, when hungry, ate without scruple the bread τῆς προθέσεως [tēs protheseōs], which otherwise it was not lawful to eat except for the priests alone, Luc. VI, 4. And accordingly it was rightly decreed that the Jews, when attacked by the enemy on the sabbath day, might fight and repel force with force, 1 Macc 2:41. For life was at stake. And the Sabbath is for man, not man for the Sabbath. Mark 2:27. The author of the Mirabilia asserts that the ravens serving Elias made amends for the fault committed in the flood. The ravens, he says, are commanded to minister to the prophet, so that that bird might be seen to expiate the fault it had committed in the flood, by becoming a faithful servant to Elias, it which had before been negligent and deceitful toward Noah. Chrysostom, in the Sermon On Elias, holds that the Prophet was like the ravens in this: that as the raven is a μισότεκνον [misoteknon] animal, so Elias hated his children, namely the Jews, for their sins, and, having shut heaven by his prayers, had imposed a long famine on them. And that God therefore wished to soften the prophet's mind, exasperated beyond measure, by the example of the ravens, which, harsh and μισότεκνοι [misoteknoi] though they are, yet with a changed disposition now took pity on the prophet and supplied him with food lest he perish of hunger: as if he had said to Elias, Μὴ τοσαύτη παρὰ σοὶ μετὰ Ἰουδαίων μισοτεκνία, ὅση τοῖς κόραξι κατὰ τῶν νεοσσῶν τῶν οἰκείων. ἀλλ᾽ ὅρα νῦν μεταβεβλημένους τοὺς κόρακας. ὅρα τὴν ἐκείνων, ὦ Ἠλία, φιλανθρωπίαν. οἱ περὶ τοὺς οἰκείους ἀπανθρωποὶ νεοσσοὺς, σοῦ γίνονται ξενοδόχοι φιλότιμοι. Ἄτοπόν ἐστιν, ὦ Ἠλία, τοὺς μὲν κόρακας μεσίτας γίνεσθαι πρὸς σὲ τῆς παρ᾽ ἐμοῦ φιλανθρωπίας· σὲ δὲ μεσίτην τοῖς Ἰουδαίοις μὴ γίνεσθαι τῆς παρ᾽ ἐμοῦ κατ᾽ II, 810 αὐτῶν τιμωρίας. Αἰδέσθητι τὴν τῶν κοράκων μεταβολὴν, καὶ γένου περὶ τοὺς Ἰουδαίους χρηστός. [Mē tosautē para soi meta Ioudaiōn misoteknia, hosē tois koraxi kata tōn neossōn tōn oikeiōn. all᾽ hora nyn metabeblēmenous tous korakas. hora tēn ekeinōn, ō Ēlia, philanthrōpian. hoi peri tous oikeious apanthrōpoi neossous, sou ginontai xenodochoi philotimoi. Atopon estin, ō Ēlia, tous men korakas mesitas ginesthai pros se tēs par᾽ emou philanthrōpias; se de mesitēn tois Ioudaiois mē ginesthai tēs par᾽ emou kat᾽ autōn timōrias. Aidesthēti tēn tōn korakōn metabolēn, kai genou peri tous Ioudaious chrēstos.] Do not hold the Jews, your children, in as great hatred as the ravens hold their chicks. Yet see the ravens now changed. See, O Elias, their kindness. They who are inhuman toward their own chicks show themselves generous hosts toward you. It is absurd, O Elias, that the ravens should be mediators of my kindness toward you, while you do not intercede for the Jews, whom I am punishing. Marvel at the change in the ravens, and be kind toward the Jews. There are some among more recent writers who hold that God the more justly exacted this service from the ravens, that they should feed his prophet, because of the greater care with which he feeds them when, still chicks, they are abandoned by their parents, as they prove from the words of Job, David, and Christ himself, which we have explained otherwise. ¶

Torrentem Cerith, vel Carith, ad quem obtigit hoc miraculum, putant eundem esse cum torrente Kana, seu arundineti, de quo Ios. XVI, 8. et XVII, 9. Quod facile crediderim, et קנה kana, fuisse dictum ab arundinum copia, in quibus latebat Elias. Ut is, de quo summus vates,

The brook Cerith, or Carith, at which this miracle took place, is thought to be the same as the brook Kana, or of the reed-bed, mentioned in Josh 16:8 and XVII, 9. I would readily believe this, and that it was called קנה kana from the abundance of reeds in which Elias lay hidden. Like the one of whom the greatest of poets says, ¶

— — Tu post carecta latebas.

— — You lay hidden behind the sedge. ¶

Et torrentem כרית Cerith, dici a verbo כרה cara, quod ἐνσιτεῖσθαι, id est, pasci, aut vesci, apprime reddunt Graeci interpretes Iob. XL, 25. eo quod ad eum Propheta a corvis per miraculum pastus est. Quae conjectura si vera est, col. 217 prolepsis agnoscenda est in his verbis, Absconde te ad torrentem Cerith. Utut sit, ex sacro textu hoc scimus, hunc torrentem fuisse prope Iordanem, et ad ortum Samariae. Itaque aliud agebat Iosephus, cum Eliam secessisse scripsit εἰς τὰ πρὸς νότον μέρη, ad partes, quae ad austrum vergunt (5). Et multo absurdius Eutychius, hunc Eliae secessum confundens cum ejusdem quadragesimali jejunio in monte Horeb, وهرب ايلياس النبي علي تخوم الاردن الي جبل حوريب وهو طور سينا وسكن في شعب عين وكان يشرب الما من ذلك الشعب Et fugit Elias, propheta, per confinia Iordanis ad montem Horeb, qui est mons Sina: et habitavit ad fontis fissuram, ex qua bibebat aquam. Quibus reliqua subjungit de corvo, panem et carnem afferente.

And the brook כרית Cerith is named from the verb כרה cara, which the Greek translators at Job 40:25 render very aptly by ἐνσιτεῖσθαι [ensiteisthai], that is, to be fed or to eat, because at it the Prophet was miraculously fed by ravens. If this conjecture is true, a prolepsis col. 217 must be recognized in these words: Hide thyself by the brook Cerith. However that may be, from the sacred text we know this much: that this brook was near the Jordan, and to the east of Samaria. So Josephus was not paying attention when he wrote that Elias withdrew εἰς τὰ πρὸς νότον μέρη [eis ta pros noton merē], to the parts that lie towards the south R5. And far more absurdly Eutychius, confusing this retreat of Elias with his forty-day fast on Mount Horeb: وهرب ايلياس النبي علي تخوم الاردن الي جبل حوريب وهو طور سينا وسكن في شعب عين وكان يشرب الما من ذلك الشعب [wa-haraba Īliyās al-nabī ʿalī tukhūm al-Urdunn ilī jabal Ḥūrīb wa-huwa Ṭūr Sīnā wa-sakana fī shiʿb ʿayn wa-kāna yashrabu al-mā min dhālika al-shiʿb] And Elias the prophet fled through the borders of the Jordan to Mount Horeb, which is Mount Sinai, and dwelt by the cleft of a spring, from which he drank water. To these words he adds the rest about the raven bringing bread and meat. ¶
