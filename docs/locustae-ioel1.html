III, 289 col. 464 Chapter IV. ¶

De Locustis quid habeat Ioel capite primo.

What Joel says about locusts in his first chapter. ¶

Hactenus de locustis Aegyptiis, quibus similes Israelitis propheta Ioel gravissimis verbis interminatus, tandem desinit in verba pacis, et Dei nomine iis multa bona promittit. Nostrum igitur est, e Ioele edecumare, et pro viribus interpretari, quaecunque de locustis habet, prout occurrent, et servato Prophetae ordine. Qui, ut attentionem captet, statim ab initio nova et inaudita se dicturum profitetur cap. I, 2. Audite, inquit, hoc senes, et auribus percipite, omnes incolae terrae: An fuerit istud in diebus vestris, et in diebus patrum vestrorum? Primo senes compellat, qui aetatis beneficio plura viderant atque audiverant. Ut Moses Deut. XXXII, 7. Interroga senes, ut tibi dicant. Et Iob. XXXII, 7. Dies loquentur, et numerus annorum docebit scientiam. Propheta igitur significat, locustas, de quibus statim dicturus est, eam cladem intulisse, qualis nulla memoratur ab omni aevo illata. In Iudaea scilicet. Eo enim haec pertinent, Audite hoc, senes; et auribus percipite, omnes incolae terrae, supple col. 465 Iudaicae, de qua hic res est. Sic, cum de Aegyptiis legitur Exod. X, 14. An-tea non fuerant locustae, istis pares, neque postea futurae sunt: haec ad Aegyptum sunt restringenda. Alioqui cum Ioele pugnaret Moses, aut cum Mose Ioel. Caetera enim, quae Hebraei afferunt, sunt minus ad rem, ut infra docebimus.

So far concerning the Egyptian locusts. With locusts like these the prophet Joel threatened the Israelites in the gravest terms, but at last he ends in words of peace and in God's name promises them many good things. It is therefore our task to pick out from Joel whatever he says about locusts and to interpret it as best we can, as each passage occurs and keeping to the Prophet's order. To capture attention, he declares at the very outset that he is about to say things new and unheard of, ch. I, 2: Hear this, he says, you old men, and give ear, all you inhabitants of the land: Has this happened in your days, or in the days of your fathers? First he addresses the old men, who by the benefit of age had seen and heard more. So Moses, Deut 32:7: Ask the old men, and they will tell you. And Job XXXII, 7: Days will speak, and the multitude of years will teach wisdom. The Prophet therefore signifies that the locusts of which he is about to speak wrought such destruction as none recorded in any age. In Judaea, that is. For this is the point of Hear this, you old men; and give ear, all you inhabitants of the land: supply col. 465 of Judaea, which is the subject here. Likewise, when we read of the Egyptians in Exod. X, 14, Before them there were no such locusts as these, nor shall there be after them, this must be restricted to Egypt. Otherwise Moses would conflict with Joel, or Joel with Moses. For the other explanations which the Hebrews offer are less to the point, as we shall show below. ¶

Versu tertio, Haec filiis vestris narrate, et filii vestri filiis suis, et filii ipsorum aetati alteri] Quod Poetae verbis dixeris,

In the third verse, Tell this to your children, and your children to their children, and their children to another generation] Which you might express in the words of the Poet: ¶

Et nati natorum, et qui nascentur ab illis.

And the children's children, and those who shall be born of them. ¶

Neque enim insignia Dei facta sunt oblivione sepelienda, sed eorum memoria ad posteros sedulo propaganda atque in posterum conservanda. Ut vel beneficiis acceptis grati simus, et pleni fiducia, vel poenis illatis trepidi et cauti magis. Hinc David Psal. LXXVIII, 3. etc. Quae audivimus, ac novimus, et patres nostri narraverunt nobis, non celabimus filios eorum, generationem alteram, ut narrent Domini laudes, et virtutem, et mirabilia, quae fecit, etc. Et mandavit patribus nostris, ut filiis suis nota facerent, ut sciat altera generatio, filii qui nascentur, surgentesque enarrent filiis suis. Et ponant in Deo spem suam, neque obliviscantur operum Dei, etc. Vide et Deut. IV, 9. et VI, 6. 20. Ios. IV, 6. etc.

For God's remarkable deeds must not be buried in oblivion; their memory must be diligently passed on to posterity and preserved for the future. Thus we may either be grateful and full of confidence for benefits received, or more fearful and cautious because of punishments inflicted. Hence David, Ps 78:3 etc.: What we have heard and known, and our fathers have told us, we will not hide from their children, another generation, so that they may tell the praises of the Lord, and his might, and the wonders that he has done, etc. And he commanded our fathers to make them known to their children, so that another generation might know, the children yet to be born, and that they might arise and tell them to their children. And that they might set their hope in God, and not forget the works of God, etc. See also Deut 4:9 and VI, 6. 20. Josh 4:6 etc. ¶

Versu quarto, Residuum gazam comedit arbe, et residuum arbe comedit jelek, et residuum jelek comedit chasil.] Hae sunt locustarum quatuor species, ut III, 290 jam ante a) monuimus, quas Hebraeis nominibus hic appellamus, quia Latina non suppetunt. Sic infra capitis secundi versu vigesimo quinto, Et reddam vobis annos, quos comederit ARBE, IELEK, CHASIL et GAZAM. Ubi gazam, quae prima fuerat, postrema nominatur. Quod observandum in Hebraeos, qui has quatuor locustarum species quatuor annis continuis, gazam nempe primo anno, arbe secundo, jelek tertio, chasil denique quarto, Iudaeam devastasse putant. Quod mihi sit parum verisimile; quia, si locusta, gazam dicta, in Iudaeam primo anno fuisset immissa, non poneretur ultimo loco Ioel II, 25. Et in his plagis enumerandis Propheta eundem utrobique servasset ordinem. Praeterea, si singulae species singulis annis assignatae fuissent, non diceretur arbe comedisse residuum gazam, et jelek residuum arbe, et chasil residuum jelek: neque enim hujus anni proventus sunt praecedentis reliquiae, nec quae futuro provenient in hujus anni reliquiis haberi possunt. Itaque, sive annotina fuerit haec locustarum plaga, aut, quod ex Ioel II, 25. videtur recte concludi, in plures annos propagata: non tamen singulas locustarum species singulis annis assignandas, sed omnes eodem anno in Iudaeam puto ingruisse. Ita, ut, uno agmine ex agro aliquo discedente, alterum succederet, praecedentis reliquias, si quae essent, depasturum ac consumpturum: Idque repetitum toties, quoties fuit necesse, ut nihil omnino, quo vel homines, vel bestiae vescerentur, in agro superesset. Nihilo magis probo, quod volunt, Iudaicam hanc plagam ab Aegyptia in eo differre, quod Aegypto unum locustae genus (ארבה arbe nimirum), at Iudaeae quatuor illae species incubuerint. Quamvis enim arbe sumatur εἰδικῶς pro certa locustae specie, cum reliquis opponitur, ut in Ioele et Levit. XI, 21. plerumque tamen γενικῶς usurpatur pro toto genere locustarum. Quomodo sumsit Moses, ubi egit de τῷ arbe, in Aegyptios immisso. Ita, ut etiam ילק jelek, et חסיל chasil, atque aliae locustarum species includerentur. Proinde David de Aegyptiis Psal. LXXVIII, 46. Dedit τῷ chasil proventum eorum, et laborem eorum τῷ arbe. Et Psal. CV, 34. Dixit, et venit arbe et jelek sine numero. Itaque non solus arbe, sed et chasil et jelek in illos a Deo immissi sunt. Merito igitur apud Pisidam Deus in Aegyptios instruit παγγενῆ τὴν ἀκρίδα, locustam omnigenam. Quatuor in Ioele locustarum species, gazam, arbe, jelek et chasil, col. 466 quomodo Chaldaei, Syri atque Arabes efferant, hic dicere nihil attinet. In Graeca et Vulgata hae sunt, ἡ κάμπη, ἡ ἀκρὶς, ὁ βροῦχος, ἡ ἐρυσίβη, eruca, locusta, bruchus, rubigo. Quarum prima et ultima ad locustarum genus non pertinent. Nam ἡ ἐρυσίβη, rubigo, ne animal quidem est, sed morbus segetum. Itaque huic versioni haud quaquam stamus. Nobis enim id persuasissimum III, 291 est, Hebraicis quatuor vocibus quatuor locustarum species significari. Quod uno ore hic asserunt Hebraei omnes: quamvis illae species, quomodo inter se differant, definire non possint.

In the fourth verse, What the gazam left, the arbe has eaten, and what the arbe left, the jelek has eaten, and what the jelek left, the chasil has eaten.] These are four species of locusts, as III, 290 we noted earlier a), which we call here by their Hebrew names because Latin ones are not available. So too below, in chapter 2, verse 25: And I will restore to you the years which the ARBE, IELEK, CHASIL and GAZAM have eaten. There gazam, which had been first, is named last. This should be noted against the Hebrews, who think that these four species of locusts laid waste Judaea in four successive years: the gazam in the first year, the arbe in the second, the jelek in the third, and finally the chasil in the fourth. To me this seems hardly probable. For if the locust called gazam had been sent into Judaea in the first year, it would not be placed last in Joel 2:25. And in enumerating these plagues the Prophet would have kept the same order in both places. Moreover, if each species had been assigned to a single year, it would not be said that the arbe ate what the gazam left, and the jelek what the arbe left, and the chasil what the jelek left. For this year's produce is not the remainder of the previous year's, nor can what will grow in the coming year be counted among this year's remains. Therefore, whether this plague of locusts lasted one year or, as seems rightly concluded from Joel 2:25, extended over several years, I think that the individual species of locusts are not to be assigned to individual years, but that all of them fell upon Judaea in the same year. Thus, as one swarm left a field, another followed, to graze off and consume the remains of the preceding one, if any were left; and this was repeated as often as necessary, until nothing at all remained in the field on which either men or beasts could feed. Nor do I any more approve of their claim that this Judaean plague differed from the Egyptian in that a single kind of locust (namely ארבה arbe) fell upon Egypt, but those four species upon Judaea. For although arbe is taken εἰδικῶς [eidikōs] for a particular species of locust when it is set against the others, as in Joel and Lev 11:21, yet it is mostly used γενικῶς [genikōs] for the whole genus of locusts. So Moses took it when he dealt with the arbe sent upon the Egyptians, so that ילק jelek and חסיל chasil and other species of locusts were also included. Accordingly David says of the Egyptians, Ps 78:46: He gave their produce to the chasil, and their labour to the arbe. And Ps 105:34: He spoke, and the arbe came, and the jelek without number. Thus not the arbe alone, but also the chasil and the jelek were sent by God against them. Rightly, then, in Pisides God arrays against the Egyptians παγγενῆ τὴν ἀκρίδα [pangenē tēn akrida], the locust of every kind. How the Chaldeans, Syrians and Arabs render the four species of locusts in Joel, gazam, arbe, jelek and chasil, col. 466 is of no concern here. In the Greek and the Vulgate they are ἡ κάμπη [hē kampē], ἡ ἀκρὶς [hē akris], ὁ βροῦχος [ho brouchos], ἡ ἐρυσίβη [hē erysibē]: eruca [caterpillar], locusta [locust], bruchus [bruchus], rubigo [blight]. Of these the first and the last do not belong to the genus of locusts. For ἡ ἐρυσίβη [hē erysibē], rubigo [blight], is not even an animal, but a disease of crops. We therefore by no means stand by this version. For we are fully persuaded III, 291 that the four Hebrew words signify four species of locusts. This all the Hebrews assert here with one voice, although they cannot define how those species differ from one another. ¶

Sed hae voces quidquid sonent, gravis agitatur quaestio, nempe, an, quae de locustis hic dicuntur, ad literam sint explicanda. Mysteria enim hic multi quaerunt, quasi non verae locustae intelligantur, sed Iudaeorum hostes. Inter utramque sententiam ita fluctuare videtur Hieronymus, ut ad ultimam magis propendeat. Primo enim haec refert ad quatuor imperia, quae singula successive in Iudaeos insurrexerunt. Erucam, inquit, quae Hebraice גזם gazam, Graece dicitur κάμπη, Hebraei Assyrios interpretantur, Babylonios atque Chaldaeos; qui, de uno orbis climate procedentes, tam decem tribuum, quam duarum, hoc est, Israelitici populi, cuncta vastarunt. Locustam autem Medos interpretantur, et Persas; qui, subverso imperio Chaldaeorum, Iudaeos habuere captivos. Bruchum Macedonas, et omnes Alexandri successores, maximeque regem Antiochum, cognomento Epiphanem, qui instar bruchi sedit in Iudaea, et omnes priorum regum reliquias devoravit. Rubiginem referunt ad imperium Romanum; qui, quarti et ultimi, in tantum oppressere Iudaeos, ut de suis finibus eos pellerent. Haec ille, tanquam ex mente Hebraeorum, qui tamen, ipso fatente, agnoscunt, etiam ad literam contigisse, quae de locustis hic habentur. Iudaei, inquit, putant, in diebus Ioel tam innumerabilem locustarum super Iudaeam venisse multitudinem, ut cuncta complerent; et non dicam fruges, sed ne vinearum quidem et arborum cortices ramosque dimitterent. Sed hanc Iudaeorum sententiam ita refellit in sequentibus, Hoc utrum factum, necne sit, liquido affirmare non possumus. Neque enim Regum et Paralipomenων narrat historia. Quod utique si fuisset, nunquam Scriptura tacuisset. Tantum dicimus, quod sub metaphora locustarum hostium describatur adventus. Deinde, quasi inter utramque sententiam ambiguus; Ni fallor, inquit, videor mihi in hoc Propheta aliquid reperisse. Narratur impietas hostium sub figura locustarum. Et rursus sic de ipsis locustis dicitur, quasi hostibus comparentur: ut, cum locustas legeris, hostes cogites; cum hostes cogitaveris, redeas ad locustas. Videtur Hieronymo Cyrillus assentiri, qui per has quatuor pestes, Salmanassarem, Nabuchodonozorum, An-tiochum et Romanos intelligit. In eodem tamen haec legas; Ἀληθὲς μὲν οὖν τὸ χρῆμα, κἂν εἰ ἐπὶ ἀκρίδος τυχὸν νοοῖτο, βρούχου τε, καὶ κάμπης· Verum quidem hoc est, etiamsi de locusta, bruchoque, et eruca accipiatur. Theodoretus, postquam nonnullos tropice haec interpretari scripsit de quatuor Assyriae regibus, Theglaphalasare, Salmanassare, Sennacheribo et Nabuchodonozoro! Ἐγὼ δὲ, ait, ἀληθῆ μὲν ἡγοῦμαι καὶ ταῦτα· ὑπολαμβάνω δὲ καὶ τὰ κατὰ τὸ ῥη-τὸν νοούμενα τῷ ὄντι γεγενῆσθαι· Ego vero, inquit, vera quidem haec esse censeo; III, 292 sed et quae ad literam intelliguntur, re vera contigisse arbitror. Chaldaeus literalem sensum plerumque amplectitur. Nonnunquam tamen in mysticum delabitur: ut Ioel. II, 25. ubi pro quatuor locustarum speciebus, arbe, jelek, chasil et gazam, habet עממיא ולשניא ושלטוניא ומלכותא פרענות, populos, et linguas, et imperia, et regna vindictae; id est, quibus Deus usus est ad rebellium in populo suo ultionem. Ex Hebraeis commentatoribus Iarchius, Kimchius et Aben Ezra literae mordicus haerent. At his posterior Abarbenel, Hierony- col. 467 mum sequutus, hunc Prophetam venisse asserit, להתנבאות על ממשלת ארבע המלכיות בבל ופרס ויון ורומי, ut prophetaret de dominatione quatuor imperiorum, Babylonici scilicet, et Persici, et Graeci, et Romani. Et a literali sensu usque adeo alienus est, ut, de iis agens, qui ad locustas haec referunt, in ipsa praefatione exclamet, חלילה לי מלהאמין בזה, Absit a me, ut hoc credam: quasi id esset valde absurdum. Sed, cum nihil objiciat, quod non sit futilissimum, non dubito, quin a Propheta verae locustae intelligantur, quae paullo post prophetiam ejus Iudaeam devastaverint. Neque enim hostes infensi solent cum locustis conferri, nisi ratione numeri: ut cum de Madianitis et Amalekitis dicitur Iud. VI, 5. Venerunt quasi locustae multitudine. Et Iud. VII, 12. Diffusi erant in ea valle tanquam locustae multitudine. Et Iudith. II, 2. de copiis Assyriorum; Et multi miscelli, ut locustae, ad eos conveniebant. Et de Trojanis et Aethiopibus, quos Memnon adduxerat, Q. Smyrnaeus libri secundi versu 195.

But whatever these words may mean, a serious question is raised, namely, whether what is said here about locusts is to be explained literally. For many look for mysteries here, as though not real locusts were meant but the enemies of the Jews. Jerome seems to waver between the two opinions in such a way that he leans more towards the latter. For first he refers these things to the four empires which one after another rose up against the Jews. "The caterpillar," he says, "which in Hebrew is called גזם gazam, in Greek κάμπη [kampē], the Hebrews interpret as the Assyrians, the Babylonians and Chaldeans; who, coming from one region of the world, laid waste everything of the ten tribes as well as of the two, that is, of the Israelite people. The locust they interpret as the Medes and Persians; who, after overthrowing the empire of the Chaldeans, held the Jews captive. The bruchus as the Macedonians and all the successors of Alexander, and above all King Antiochus, surnamed Epiphanes, who settled in Judaea like a bruchus and devoured all the remains left by the earlier kings. The blight they refer to the Roman empire; who, fourth and last, so oppressed the Jews as to drive them from their own territory." So he writes, as if from the mind of the Hebrews, who nevertheless, by his own admission, acknowledge that what is said here about locusts also happened literally. "The Jews," he says, "think that in the days of Joel so countless a multitude of locusts came upon Judaea that they filled everything; and left, I will not say the crops, but not even the bark and branches of the vines and trees." But he refutes this opinion of the Jews in what follows: "Whether this happened or not, we cannot clearly affirm. For the history of the Kings and Chronicles does not narrate it. And if it had happened, Scripture would certainly never have kept silent. We say only that under the metaphor of locusts the coming of enemies is described." Then, as if undecided between the two opinions: "If I am not mistaken," he says, "I seem to have found something in this Prophet. The impiety of the enemies is narrated under the figure of locusts. And again, the locusts themselves are spoken of as if they were compared to enemies: so that when you read of locusts you think of enemies; when you have thought of enemies, you return to the locusts." Cyril seems to agree with Jerome, understanding by these four plagues Shalmaneser, Nebuchadnezzar, Antiochus and the Romans. Yet in the same author you may read this: Ἀληθὲς μὲν οὖν τὸ χρῆμα, κἂν εἰ ἐπὶ ἀκρίδος τυχὸν νοοῖτο, βρούχου τε, καὶ κάμπης· [Alēthes men oun to chrēma, kan ei epi akridos tychon nooito, brouchou te, kai kampēs] "This is indeed true, even if it be taken of the locust, the bruchus and the caterpillar." Theodoret, after writing that some interpret these things figuratively of four kings of Assyria, Tiglath-pileser, Shalmaneser, Sennacherib and Nebuchadnezzar, says: Ἐγὼ δὲ ἀληθῆ μὲν ἡγοῦμαι καὶ ταῦτα· ὑπολαμβάνω δὲ καὶ τὰ κατὰ τὸ ῥητὸν νοούμενα τῷ ὄντι γεγενῆσθαι· [Egō de alēthē men hēgoumai kai tauta; hypolambanō de kai ta kata to rhēton nooumena tō onti gegenēsthai] "I indeed consider these things true; III, 292 but I also think that what is understood literally really happened." The Chaldee mostly embraces the literal sense. Sometimes, however, he slips into the mystical: as in Joel 2:25, where for the four kinds of locusts, arbe, jelek, chasil and gazam, he has עממיא ולשניא ושלטוניא ומלכותא פרענות, "peoples, and tongues, and dominions, and kingdoms of vengeance"; that is, those whom God used to take vengeance on the rebels among his people. Of the Hebrew commentators, Jarchi [=Rashi (R. Solomon ben Isaac), called Jarchi by the Christian Hebraists], Kimchi [=David Kimḥi (Radak), Sefer ha-Shorashim] and Aben Ezra [=Abraham ibn Ezra] cling tenaciously to the letter. But Abarbanel [=Isaac Abarbanel (1437–1508), commentary on the Bible], later than these, following Jerome, col. 467 asserts that this Prophet came להתנבאות על ממשלת ארבע המלכיות בבל ופרס ויון ורומי, "to prophesy concerning the dominion of the four empires, namely the Babylonian, and the Persian, and the Greek, and the Roman." And he is so averse to the literal sense that, dealing with those who refer these things to locusts, he exclaims in the very preface: חלילה לי מלהאמין בזה, "Far be it from me to believe this", as though it were utterly absurd. But since he objects nothing that is not most futile, I do not doubt that real locusts are meant by the Prophet, which laid waste Judaea shortly after his prophecy. For hostile enemies are not usually compared with locusts except in respect of number: as when it is said of the Midianites and Amalekites, Iud. VI, 5., "They came like locusts in multitude." And Iud. VII, 12., "They were spread out in that valley like locusts in multitude." And Jdt 2:2, of the forces of the Assyrians: "And many of mixed stock, like locusts, gathered to them." And of the Trojans and the Ethiopians whom Memnon had brought, Quintus Smyrnaeus, book 2, line 195. ¶

Ipsi autem effundebant se, locustis frugivoris pares, quae feruntur ut nubes, aut immodicus imber super latam terram, innumerae, tetram hominibus famem allaturae. Alioqui enim, cum homines locustis comparantur, id ad contemptum pertinet, et πρὸς τὸν ἐξουθενισμόν. Quale illud de Israelitis, cum Anakaeis gigantibus collatis, Num. XIII, 34. Quibus comparati ut locustae videbamur. Et de hominibus prae Deo Es. XL, 22. Qui sedet super gyrum terrae, et incolae ejus sunt quasi locustae. Nec dissimile illud Luciani in Hermotimo, Οἷον μύρμηκας ἀπὸ τοῦ ὕψους ἐπισκοποῦντάς τινας τοὺς ἄλλους, Alios homines tanquam formicas e sublimi contemplantes. Itaque magna imperia cum his animalculis nusquam conferri videas: sed cum leonibus, pardis et ursis, ut Danielis capite septimo. Sed, ut res sit manifestior, jam sequentia dispiciamus.

But they poured themselves forth, like crop-devouring locusts, which are borne along like a cloud, or a heavy rain over the broad earth, countless, bringing a dreadful famine upon men. For otherwise, when men are compared to locusts, this serves to express contempt, and πρὸς τὸν ἐξουθενισμόν [pros ton exouthenismon]. Such is the statement about the Israelites when compared with the Anakim giants, Num 13:34. Compared with them we seemed like locusts. And about men in comparison with God, Isa 40:22. He who sits upon the circle of the earth, and its inhabitants are like locusts. (10) Rosenmüller, III, 303 Sed non secus egisse Bochartum cum Deodatio, quam cum Kimchio, idem ille asserit Pocockius, l. c. „Verum equidem est, inquit, verba ejus citata extare in editione Genevensi in quaternis 1607., scilicet grana esse corrupta da soverchie piogge, per nimiam pluviam, sed inveniuntur in posteriori editione (Genev. 1641. in forma maxima), verba, quae omnino contrarium indicant, videlicet non potuisse progerminare per lestreme ioccita, propter summam siccitatem, ac si ambiguus haereret cum aliis, num haec vel illa illata sit noxa vel pluviarum frequentia, vel raritate nimia, vel, ut placet quibusdam, utramque conjungere, post diuturnos imbres, saevientem siccitatem, seu aestum solis. R10 Rosenmüller, III, 303 But that Bochart dealt no differently with Diodati than with Kimchi is asserted by the same Pococke, loc. cit.: "It is true," he says, "that the words of his that are cited are found in the Geneva quarto edition of 1607, namely that the grains were spoiled da soverchie piogge, by excessive rain; but in the later edition (Geneva 1641, in largest format) words are found which indicate exactly the opposite, namely that they could not sprout per lestreme ioccita, because of extreme drought, as if he remained in doubt with others whether this damage or that was brought on either by the frequency of rains or by their excessive scarcity, or, as some prefer, by joining both, after long-lasting rains, a raging drought, or the heat of the sun.
