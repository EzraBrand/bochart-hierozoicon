III, 119 col. 352 Chapter 33. ¶

Immundarum avium numerus, et generales notae. Harum probibitio quomodo ad mores pertinuerit.

The number of the unclean birds, and their general marks. How their prohibition pertained to morals. ¶

Postquam immundarum avium censum explicare conati sumus, jam de illarum numero et notis aliquid addemus ex Maimonidis libro De cibis vetitis, Verba sunt capitis primi sectione decima quarta, סימני עוף טהור לא נתפרש מן התורה אלא מנה מינין טמאין בלבד ושאר מיני העוף מותרין והמינין האסורין ארבעה ועשרים הן ואלו הן א נשר ב פרס ג עזניה ד׳ דאה והיא הראה האמורה במשנה תורה ה׳ איה והיא הדייה האמורה במשנה תורה ו׳ מין האיה שכן כתוב בה למינה מכלל שהיא שני מינין ז עורב ח זרזיר שכן נאמר בעורב למינו להביא את הזרזיר ט׳ יענה י׳ תחמס יא׳ שחף יב׳ נץ יג׳ ושרנקא והיא מין הנץ שכן כתוב בו למינהו יד׳ כוס טו׳ שלך יו׳ ינשוף יז׳ תכשמת יח׳ קאת יט׳ רחמה כ׳ חסידה כא׳ האנפה כב׳ מין האנפה שכן נאמר בה למינה כג׳ הדוכיפת כד׳ העטלף, Signa mundarum avium non explicantur ex Lege, quae solum immundarum censum instituit, et reliquae species concessae sunt. Species porro prohibitae sunt viginti quatuor. 1. NESER. 2. PERES. 3. OZNIIA. 4. DAA, qui in Deuteronomio dicitur RAA. 5. AIA, qui in Deuteronomio dicitur DAIA. 6. Species τῆς AIA; quia de ea scribitur secundum speciem suam: itaque genus est, cujus duae species. 7. OREB. 8. ZARZIR; quia de OREB dicitur secundum speciem suam, ad designandum ZARZIR. 9. IAANA. 10. THACHMAS. 11. SA-CHAPH. 12. NETS. 13. Et SARNEKA, qui est species NETS; quia de eo scriptum est juxta speciem suam. 14. CHOS. 15. SALACH. 16. IANSUPH. 17. THINSEMETH. 18. KAATH. 19. RACHAMA. 20. HASIDA. 21. ANAPHA. 22. Species altera ANAPHAE; quia de ea dicitur juxta speciem suam. 23. DU- col. 353 KIPHATH. 24. ATALLEPH.

Now that we have tried to set out the roll of the unclean birds, we shall add something about their number and marks from Maimonides' [=Moses Maimonides (Rambam), Mishneh Torah] book On Forbidden Foods. These are the words of chapter 1, section 14: סימני עוף טהור לא נתפרש מן התורה אלא מנה מינין טמאין בלבד ושאר מיני העוף מותרין והמינין האסורין ארבעה ועשרים הן ואלו הן א נשר ב פרס ג עזניה ד׳ דאה והיא הראה האמורה במשנה תורה ה׳ איה והיא הדייה האמורה במשנה תורה ו׳ מין האיה שכן כתוב בה למינה מכלל שהיא שני מינין ז עורב ח זרזיר שכן נאמר בעורב למינו להביא את הזרזיר ט׳ יענה י׳ תחמס יא׳ שחף יב׳ נץ יג׳ ושרנקא והיא מין הנץ שכן כתוב בו למינהו יד׳ כוס טו׳ שלך יו׳ ינשוף יז׳ תכשמת יח׳ קאת יט׳ רחמה כ׳ חסידה כא׳ האנפה כב׳ מין האנפה שכן נאמר בה למינה כג׳ הדוכיפת כד׳ העטלף, The signs of the clean birds are not explained in the Law, which only drew up a roll of the unclean ones, and the remaining species are permitted. The prohibited species, moreover, are twenty-four. 1. NESER. 2. PERES. 3. OZNIIA. 4. DAA, which in Deuteronomy is called RAA. 5. AIA, which in Deuteronomy is called DAIA. 6. A species of the AIA [tēs]; because of it is written after its kind: so it is a genus with two species. 7. OREB. 8. ZARZIR; because of OREB it is said after its kind, to designate ZARZIR. 9. IAANA. 10. THACHMAS. 11. SACHAPH. 12. NETS. 13. And SARNEKA, which is a species of NETS; because of it is written after its kind. 14. CHOS. 15. SALACH. 16. IANSUPH. 17. THINSEMETH. 18. KAATH. 19. RACHAMA. 20. HASIDA. 21. ANAPHA. 22. A second species of ANAPHA; because of it is said after its kind. 23. DU- col. 353 KIPHATH. 24. ATALLEPH. ¶

In quo Maimonidis censu quaedam sunt observanda. Ac primum illud דאה daa, et ראה raa, recte pro eodem haberi. Nam Leviticum cum Deuteronomio conferenti palam constabit, unum pro altero fuisse scriptum, propter similitudinem literarum Daleth et Res. Quod in Talmudico tractatu Cholin recte observat R. Abja fol. 63. a. b. At non perinde verum est, quod illi ipsi volunt, idem esse איה aja et דיה daja; et eam avem, quae in Levitico איה aja dicitur, in Deuteronomio דיה daja dici. Illud, inquam, verum non est, cum Deut. XIV, 13. ambae habeantur האיה והדיה, tanquam diversae aves. Quin etiam extra Legem utrumque nomen occurrit: nempe איה Iob. XXVIII, 7. et דיות III, 120 Es. XXXIV, 15. Proinde illas distinximus: et איה aja nobis aesalo a), דיה daja fuit vultur niger b).

In this roll of Maimonides some things should be noted. First, that דאה daa and ראה raa are rightly taken as the same. For anyone comparing Leviticus with Deuteronomy it will be plain that one was written for the other, because of the similarity of the letters Daleth and Res. R. Abja rightly observes this in the Talmudic tractate Cholin, fol. 63a–b. But it is not equally true, as these same writers would have it, that איה aja and דיה daja are the same, and that the bird called איה aja in Leviticus is called דיה daja in Deuteronomy. That, I say, is not true, since in Deut 14:13 both are found, האיה והדיה, as distinct birds. Indeed both names also occur outside the Law: namely איה in Job 28:7 and דיות III, 120 in Isa 34:15. Accordingly we have distinguished them: for us איה aja was the merlin (aesalon) a), and דיה daja the black vulture b). ¶

Secundo, ex eo, quod quasdam aves prohibet Moses juxta speciem suam, non sequitur, illarum diversas esse species: neque enim Plurali numero dixit, juxta species suas, sed Singulari, juxta speciem suam: nimirum, ut significet totam speciem prohiberi. Et quod in quibusdam diserte dicit, in aliis supplere satis habuit, ut vitaret taedium perpetuae repetitionis horum verborum, juxta speciem suam.

Second, from the fact that Moses prohibits certain birds after its kind, it does not follow that there are different species of them. For he did not say in the plural, after their kinds, but in the singular, after its kind: evidently to signify that the whole species is prohibited. And what he says expressly in some cases, he was content to have supplied in others, so as to avoid the tedium of constantly repeating these words, after its kind. ¶

Tertio, non capio, cur זרזיר zarzir, id est, sturnum, Hebraei velint esse corvi speciem potius, quam cornicem, aut picam, aut graculum et monedulam, quae ad corvi naturam magis accedunt. Tamen Maimonides hoc de suo non affert. Ita enim citat Aruch ex tractatu Cholin fol. 63. זה עורב למינה להביא את הזרזירין, iste corvus dictus est secundum speciem suam ad designandos zarzirin (seu sturnos). Ubi tamen hodie vocem הזרזירין, seu sturnorum, non reperio. Sed in Bava Kama fol. 92. b. proverbium, inde natum, legas, quod tale est, לא לחנם הלך זרזיר אצל עורב אלא מפני שהוא מינו, non frustra corvum adit sturnus, cum sit ejusdem speciei. Et in Beresith Rabba cap. 65. aliquanto contractius, לא הלך זרזיר אצל עורב אלא שהוא מינו, Sturnus corvum non adiret, nisi esset ὁμογενής. Et in Baal Hatturim Cabalistae observant, verba עורב למינו, corvus juxta speciem suam per Gematriam idem valere, quod זרזיר zarzir. Nam in numeris Hebraicis utrumque valet 414. Et in Aquinate זרזיר zarzir definitur עוף שהוא קרוב לצורת העורב, avis, quae corvo specie similis est. Neque dubium, quin זרזיר zarzir sit sturnus. Nam in Glossa Talmudis explicatur אשטו״רניל estournel. Et in Pomario, storno. Et in Masal Kadmoni Fab. 51 duo colloquutores Hebraice vocantur השפן והזרזיר, id est, cuniculus et sturnus, ut ex pictura apparet. Denique Arabicum زرزور zarzour id ipsum significat. Proinde verba Dioscoridis libro secundo, capite De animalium fimo, ψαρὰς ὀρύζῃ τρέφοντες, sturnos oryza alentes, Avicenna libro secundo Canonis capite زبل, id est, De fimo, ita reddidit: الزرزور المتعلف للارز Zarzur oryza pastus: id est, sturnus, non turdus, ut reddiderunt Latini interpretes, errore, in Hispania nato, ubi zorzal est turdus.

Third, I do not understand why the Hebrews want זרזיר zarzir, that is, the sturnus [starling], to be a species of raven rather than the crow, the magpie, the jackdaw or the daw, which come closer to the nature of the raven. Yet Maimonides does not offer this on his own authority. For the Aruch [=the ʿArukh of Nathan ben Jehiel of Rome] cites it thus from the tractate Cholin, fol. 63: זה עורב למינה להביא את הזרזירין, this raven is said to be after its kind in order to designate the zarzirin (or starlings). There, however, I do not find the word הזרזירין, or of the starlings, today. But in Bava Kama, fol. 92b, you may read a proverb that arose from it, which runs as follows: לא לחנם הלך זרזיר אצל עורב אלא מפני שהוא מינו, not for nothing does the starling go to the raven, since it is of the same species. And in Beresith Rabba, ch. 65, somewhat more briefly: לא הלך זרזיר אצל עורב אלא שהוא מינו, the starling would not go to the raven unless it were ὁμογενής [homogenēs]. And in Baal Hatturim [=Jacob ben Asher (d. 1343), commentary on the Torah] the Cabbalists observe that the words עורב למינו, the raven after its kind, are by Gematria equal to זרזיר zarzir, for in Hebrew numerals each amounts to 414. And in Aquinas [=Philippe d'Aquin, Dictionarium hebraeo-chaldaeo-talmudico-rabbinicum (Paris 1629)] זרזיר zarzir is defined as עוף שהוא קרוב לצורת העורב, a bird which is similar in form to the raven. Nor is there any doubt that זרזיר zarzir is the starling. For in the Gloss on the Talmud it is explained as אשטו״רניל estournel; and in Pomarius [=David de Pomis, Ṣemaḥ David (Venice 1587)], storno. And in Masal Kadmoni, Fable 51, the two speakers are called in Hebrew השפן והזרזיר, that is, the rabbit and the starling, as is clear from the picture. Finally, the Arabic زرزور [zarzūr] zarzour means the very same thing. Accordingly the words of Dioscorides in book 2, in the chapter On the dung of animals, ψαρὰς ὀρύζῃ τρέφοντες [psaras oryzē trephontes], feeding starlings on rice, were rendered by Avicenna [=Ibn Sīnā, al-Qānūn fī l-ṭibb (the Canon)] in book 2 of the Canon, in the chapter زبل [zibl], that is, On dung, as follows: الزرزور المتعلف للارز [al-zarzūr al-mutaʿallif li-l-aruzz] Zarzur fed on rice: that is, the starling, not the thrush, as the Latin translators rendered it, by an error that arose in Spain, where zorzal is the thrush. ¶

Minus obvia est vox שרנקא sarneka, quam usurpat Maimonides, pro specie נץ nets, seu accipitris, ut Talmudici שורינקא sorinka, et, quod idem est, בר־חיריא bar-chirja. Verba sic habent in tractatu Cholin ubi supra, ת״ר הנץ III, 121 זה הנץ למינהו להביא את בר־חיריא מאי בר־חיריא אמר אביי שורינקא Tradiderunt magistri nostri, accipitrem dici juxta speciem suam, ut significetur bar-chirja. Quid est bar-chirja? Dixit Abji, Est forinka. Aruch legit col. 354 בר־חוריא bar-churja; et שורניקא sornika. Et Arabice in Giggeio سرنوق surnouk est avis ex genere accipitrum, raptu vivens. Fallor, aut haec eadem est, quae a Damire in uno exemplari vocatur شروق saruk, in altero زرق zurrak: de qua sic Ibno-Saida, طاير يصاد به بين البازي والباشق Avis est aucupatoria, media (id est, mediae magnitudinis) inter Albazi et Albasak, accipitrum species, apud Arabes notissimas. Et Alphera, هو البازي الابيض والجمع الزرارق وهو صنف من البازي لطيف الا ان مزجه احر وايبس ولذلك هو اشد جناحا واسرع طيرانا واقوي اقداما وفيه ختل وخبث وخير الوانه الاسود الظهر الابيض الصدر الاحمر العين Est Albazi (seu accipiter albus). Plurale est zerariko. Est accipitris species elegans, sed calidissimi et siccissimi temperamenti. Ac proinde alis est firmior, volatu velocior, et pedibus fortior. Et in eo est versutia, et malignitas, et coloris praestantia. Dorso enim niger est, albus stomacho, ruber oculo. Ab albedine stomachi a Talmudicis vocatur בר־חוריא, filius albedinis. Videndum, an sit falco albus recentiorum.

Less familiar is the word שרנקא sarneka, which Maimonides uses for a species of נץ nets, or hawk, as the Talmudists use שורינקא sorinka, and, what is the same, בר־חיריא bar-chirja. The words run thus in the tractate Cholin, in the place cited above: ת״ר הנץ III, 121 זה הנץ למינהו להביא את בר־חיריא מאי בר־חיריא אמר אביי שורינקא Our masters have taught that the hawk is said to be after its kind so as to signify bar-chirja. What is bar-chirja? Abji said: It is forinka. The Aruch reads col. 354 בר־חוריא bar-churja, and שורניקא sornika. And in Arabic, in Giggeius [=Antonio Giggei, Thesaurus linguae Arabicae (Milan 1632)], سرنوق [surnūq] surnouk is a bird of the hawk kind, living by prey. I am mistaken if this is not the same bird that Damir [=al-Damīrī, Ḥayāt al-ḥayawān] calls in one copy شروق [shrwq] saruk, in the other زرق [zurraq] zurrak, of which Ibno-Saida says thus: طاير يصاد به بين البازي والباشق [ṭāyir yuṣād bihi bayna al-bāzī wa-l-bāshiq] It is a bird used in fowling, intermediate (that is, of intermediate size) between Albazi and Albasak, species of hawk very well known among the Arabs. And Alphera: هو البازي الابيض والجمع الزرارق وهو صنف من البازي لطيف الا ان مزجه احر وايبس ولذلك هو اشد جناحا واسرع طيرانا واقوي اقداما وفيه ختل وخبث وخير الوانه الاسود الظهر الابيض الصدر الاحمر العين [huwa al-bāzī al-abyaḍ wa-l-jamʿ al-zarāriq wa-huwa ṣinf min al-bāzī laṭīf illā anna mizājahu aḥarr wa-aybas wa-li-dhālika huwa ashadd janāḥan wa-asraʿ ṭayarānan wa-aqwā aqdāman wa-fīhi khatl wa-khubth wa-khayr alwānihi al-aswad al-ẓahr al-abyaḍ al-ṣadr al-aḥmar al-ʿayn] It is Albazi (or the white hawk). The plural is zerariko. It is an elegant species of hawk, but of a very hot and very dry temperament. And therefore it is stronger in its wings, swifter in flight, and stronger in its feet. And in it there is cunning and malice, and excellence of colour. For it is black on the back, white on the breast, red in the eye. From the whiteness of its breast it is called by the Talmudists בר־חוריא, son of whiteness. It should be examined whether it is the white falcon of more recent writers. ¶

Haec sunt immundarum avium nomina, tam quae in Scriptura leguntur, quam quae ex traditione proferunt Iudaeorum magistri. Quorum censum instituere Maimonidi facile fuit: sed, quod a tali doctore exspectabamus, illa interpretari ne conatus quidem est. Et, quod mirere magis, Talmudici doctores, tot saeculis antiquiores, nonnisi pauca explicant, eaque valde ambigue, ita, ut eos appareat in meris palpare tenebris. Et Graecis quoque interpretibus, qui tanto ante plerosque Talmudicos vixerunt, quanto illi ante Maimonidem, calculum hic saepe haesisse docet, quod sibi tam parum constant, et unius avis vocabulum in tam diversas notiones distrahunt. In captivitate scilicet Babylonica Iudaei linguam suam ex parte obliti sunt, et Hebraismi puritas in Syrismum degeneravit. Unde est, quod post reditum ex illa captivitate, vocum aliquot, quarum rarior erat usus, periit vera significatio. In quo numero fuerunt quaedam avium appellationes. Unde Aben Ezra in Levit. XI, 29. 30. ubi reptilium octo genera prohibet Moses, גם אלה השמנה לא נוכל לדעתם גם העופות כי אם מדברי קבלה, Neque haec octo (reptilia), neque etiam aves vere nosse possumus, nisi ex traditionis verbis. Sed haec traditio nusquam extat. Et traditionum auctores Talmudici alio nos amandant, nempe ad peritos aucupes. Ita in III, 122 Cholin fol. 63. b. א״ר יצחק עוף טהור יאכל במסורת נאמן הצייד לומר עוף זה טהור מסר לי רבי א״ר יוחנן והוא שבקי בהן ובשמותיהן, Dixit R. Isaac, Avis munda comeditur per traditionem fidelis aucupis: ita, ut dicas, Haec avis munda est, ut magister (auceps) mihi tradidit. R. Iochanan, Et qui expertus sit in iis, et in earum nominibus. Unde Maimonides ubi supra sectione decima quinta, כל מי שהוא בקי במינין אלו ובשמותיהן ה״ז אוכל כל עוף שאינו מהם ואינו צריך בדיקה ועוף טהור נאכל במסורת והוא שיהיה דבר פשוט באותו מקום שזה col. 355 עוף טהור ונאמן צייד לומר עוף זה התיר לי רבי הצייד והוא שיוחזק אותו צייד שהוא בקי במינין אלו ובשמותיהן Quisquis est exercitatus in illis speciebus (quae prohibitae sunt) et in earum nominibus, is libere vescitur omnibus iis avibus, quae non sunt in illo censu, neque eget ampliore disquisitione. Et avis munda comeditur per traditionem, cum id clare explicatur in illo loco, in quo fuerit avis munda: et ibi est fidelis auceps. Ut dicat (qui comedit), Hanc avem concessit mihi magister auceps. Nempe confirmavit ipsum auceps, in illis exercitatus, et in earum appellationibus. Sed, quia hujusmodi aucupes nusquam reperire est, addit sectione decima sexta, מי שאינו מכירן ואינו יודע שמותיהן בודק בסימנין אלו שנתנו חכמים כל עוף שהוא דורס ואוכל בידוע שהוא מאלו המינין וטמא ושאינו דורס ואוכל אם יש בו אחד משלשה סימנין אלו ה״ז עוף טהור ואלו הן אצבע יתירה או זקף והיא המוראה או שהיה קרקבנו נקלף ביד Qui aves illas non novit, et earum nescit nomina, eas secernit per signa haec, quae sapientes nobis tradiderunt. Omnis avis, quae praedam conculcando dilacerat et vorat, palam est ex illis speciebus, et habetur pro immunda. Sed avis, quae non est rapax et carnivora, pro munda habetur, si in ea reperiatur unum ex his tribus signis. Nempe, 1. Si habeat digitum superfluum (id est, calcar, quale in gallis): 2. aut ingluviem, quae Hebraice murea dicitur: 3. aut si ventriculus manu nudetur (sine cultro). Quae partim petita sunt ex Talmudico tractatu Cholin, partim ex Ionathanis Chaldaica paraphrasi, quae sic habet Levit. XI, 13. וית אלין מיניא תשקצון מן עופא דלית להון ציבעא יתירא ודלית זקפתא ודקורקבניה ליתוהי מקליף, Et has species ex avibus detestamini: quibus non est digitus superfluus, et quae ingluviem non habent, et quibus ventriculus non nudatur. Quod ultimum non capio satis. Tamen extremum hoc signum Géonim tanti faciunt, ut, vel si unicum hoc in ave desit, ejus esum nunquam concedant, ut docet pluribus Maimonides. Sed Iudaeorum coquos consulat, qui hujusmodi nugarum curiosus est.

These are the names of the unclean birds, both those found in Scripture and those which the masters of the Jews put forward from tradition. To draw up a list of them was easy for Maimonides; but he did not even attempt to interpret them, which is what we expected from such a teacher. And, what is more surprising, the Talmudic doctors, older by so many centuries, explain only a few, and those very ambiguously, so that they plainly grope in sheer darkness. That the Greek translators too, who lived as long before most of the Talmudists as these did before Maimonides, were often at a loss here is shown by how little they agree with themselves, and by how they pull the name of a single bird into such different meanings. For in the Babylonian captivity the Jews partly forgot their language, and the purity of Hebrew degenerated into Syriac. Hence, after the return from that captivity, the true meaning of some words whose use was rarer was lost. Among these were certain names of birds. Hence Aben Ezra [=Abraham ibn Ezra] on Levit. XI, 29. 30., where Moses forbids eight kinds of creeping things: גם אלה השמנה לא נוכל לדעתם גם העופות כי אם מדברי קבלה, Neither these eight (creeping things), nor the birds either, can we truly know, except from the words of tradition. But this tradition is nowhere extant. And the authors of the traditions, the Talmudists, send us elsewhere, namely to skilled fowlers. Thus in III, 122 Cholin fol. 63b: א״ר יצחק עוף טהור יאכל במסורת נאמן הצייד לומר עוף זה טהור מסר לי רבי א״ר יוחנן והוא שבקי בהן ובשמותיהן, R. Isaac said: A clean bird is eaten on the tradition of a trustworthy fowler, so that you may say, This bird is clean, as my master (the fowler) handed down to me. R. Iochanan: Provided he is experienced in them and in their names. Hence Maimonides, in the place cited above, section 15: כל מי שהוא בקי במינין אלו ובשמותיהן ה״ז אוכל כל עוף שאינו מהם ואינו צריך בדיקה ועוף טהור נאכל במסורת והוא שיהיה דבר פשוט באותו מקום שזה col. 355 עוף טהור ונאמן צייד לומר עוף זה התיר לי רבי הצייד והוא שיוחזק אותו צייד שהוא בקי במינין אלו ובשמותיהן Whoever is practised in those species (which are forbidden) and in their names may freely eat all those birds which are not in that list, and needs no further examination. And a clean bird is eaten on tradition, when it is clearly established in the place where it is that it is a clean bird; and there a fowler is trustworthy, so that (the one who eats) may say, My master the fowler permitted this bird to me. That is, provided that fowler is confirmed to be practised in those species and in their names. But since fowlers of this kind are nowhere to be found, he adds in section 16: מי שאינו מכירן ואינו יודע שמותיהן בודק בסימנין אלו שנתנו חכמים כל עוף שהוא דורס ואוכל בידוע שהוא מאלו המינין וטמא ושאינו דורס ואוכל אם יש בו אחד משלשה סימנין אלו ה״ז עוף טהור ואלו הן אצבע יתירה או זקף והיא המוראה או שהיה קרקבנו נקלף ביד He who does not know those birds and does not know their names distinguishes them by these signs which the sages have handed down to us. Every bird which tears its prey by trampling on it and devours it is evidently of those species and is held unclean. But a bird which is not rapacious and carnivorous is held clean if one of these three signs is found in it: namely, 1. if it has a superfluous toe (that is, a spur, as in cocks); 2. or a crop, which in Hebrew is called murea; 3. or if its gizzard can be stripped by hand (without a knife). These are taken partly from the Talmudic tractate Cholin, partly from the Chaldee paraphrase of Jonathan [=Targum Jonathan], which reads thus at Lev 11:13: וית אלין מיניא תשקצון מן עופא דלית להון ציבעא יתירא ודלית זקפתא ודקורקבניה ליתוהי מקליף, And these species among the birds you shall detest: those which have no superfluous toe, and which have no crop, and whose gizzard is not stripped. This last I do not well understand. Yet the Geonim make so much of this last sign that, if this one alone is lacking in a bird, they never permit it to be eaten, as Maimonides explains at length. But let whoever is curious about such trifles consult the cooks of the Jews. ¶

Harum prohibitionum ratio tota videtur fuisse mystica. 1. Rapaces aves prohibuit Deus; aquilam puta, ossifragam, milvum, vulturem, accipitrem, corvum, etc. ut a rapina Iudaeos averteret. 2. Nocturnas; puta bubonem, noctuam, vespertilionem: ut abjicerent opera tenebrarum, et se proderent lucis filios. 3. Lacustres et riparias; ut onocrotalum, catarrhactem, pelicanum, ciconiam, III, 123 quarum victus est impurissimus, et upupam, quae in stercore nidulatur: ut ab omni immunditia eos arceret. 4. Struthionem denique, qui a terra non attollitur: ut, terrenis relictis, ad ea tenderent, quae sursum sunt. Quae explicatio nostra non est, sed veterum interpretum. Aristeas, Περὶ ὧν δὲ ἀπη-γόρευται πτηνῶν, εὑρήσεις ἄγριά τε, καὶ σαρκοφάγα, καὶ καταδυναστεύοντα τῇ περὶ ταῦτα δυνάμει τὰ λοιπά· Aves, quae prohibentur, reperies agrestes esse, et carnivoras, et potentia sua tyrannidem in alias exercere. Quod statim ad mores transfert Barnabas in Epistola, quam eximius vir edidit noster Vossius; Οὐδὲ μὴ φάγῃς, φησὶ, τὸν ἀετὸν, οὐδὲ τὸν ὀξύπτερον, οὐδὲ τὸν ἰκτῖνα, οὐδὲ τὸν κόρακα· οὐ μὴ, φησὶ, κολληθήσῃ ἀνθρώποις τοιούτοις, οἵ τινες οὐκ οἴδασι διὰ κόπου καὶ ἱδρῶτος πορίζειν ἑαυτοῖς τὴν τροφὴν, ἀλλ' ἁρπάζουσι τὰ ἀλλό-τρια ἐν ἀνομίᾳ αὐτῶν· Nec manducabis, inquit, aquilam, aut accipitrem, aut corvum: id est, iis te non adjunges; qui victum per laborem et sudorem sibi acquirere nesciunt, sed aliena rapiunt per suam iniquitatem. Quae Clemens Alexandrinus citasse non contentus libro quinto Stromatum, etiam ita imitatur in Paedagogi libri tertii capite undecimo, Οὐδ' ἰκτῖνα, ἢ ὠκύπτερον μαστοφαγῆ, ἢ col. 356 ἀετὸν φάγῃς, φησίν· οὐκ ἐγγιεῖς, φησὶν, τοῖς δι' ἁπαγῆς τὸν βίον πορίζο-μένοις· Milvum non manducabis, aut accipitrem, qui mammas vorat, aut aquilam: id est, ne accedas ad eos, qui ex raptu victum sibi comparant. Origenes in Leviticum Homilia septima, Non manducabis, inquit, haec, quia immundae sunt, aquilam, et vulturem, et caetera his similia. His etenim avibus semper mortuorum corporum cibus est, et ex mortuis cadaveribus vivunt. Omnes ergo, qui hujusmodi vitam gerunt, immundi habendi sunt. Ego puto, et illos in his nominari, qui alienis incubant mortibus, etc. velut mortuorum cadaveribus inhiantes. Sed et ibidem eos, qui, cum in verbis coelestem eruditionem prae se ferant, tamen in factis carnalia et mortua opera praestant, recte vultures et aquilas dici asserit, quae de excelsis ad carnes mortuas et foetidas delabuntur. Et paullo post, Quaedam non tam rapacitatem, quam obscuritatem et tenebras amant. Omnis enim, qui male agit, odit lucem, et non venit ad lucem, ut noctuae et vespertiliones, etc. Novatianus De cibis Iudaicis capite tertio, Quis corvum (cibum faciat)? sed versutas execratur voluntates. Passerem cum interdicit (στρουθὸν voluit, id est, struthionem), intemperantiam coarguit. Quando noctuam, odit lucifugas veritatis. Quando cygnum, cervicis altae superbos. Quando charadrium, garrulam nimis linguae intemperantiam. Quando vespertilionem, quaerentes tenebras noctis simul et erroris. Apud Cyrillum libro nono Contra Iulianum, aquilae, accipitres, catarrhactae, gryphes, et vultures rapaces et avaros designant; corvus versipelles; upupa, noctua et vespertilio, indoctos et tenebriones, qui doctrinae lucem oderunt. Et libro decimo quarto De adoratione idem habet ejusdem argumenti multo plura. Et ibidem non solum upupam, sed et ardeam et charadrium, III, 124 inter nocturnas aves cum noctua et vespertilione recenset, rejici asserens γλαῦκα, καὶ ἐρωδιὸν, καὶ χαραδριὸν, καὶ τὰ ὅμοια αὐτοῦ, ἐπόπα, καὶ νυ-κτερίδα, noctuam, et ardeam, et charadrium, et his similes, upupam et vespertilionem, ut νυκτὸς φίλα, καὶ χαίροντα μὲν τῷ σκότει, καὶ πτήσει ὡς ἡδυτά-την ἐν αὐτῷ ποιεῖσθαι μεμελετηκότα, κατεχθραίνοντα δὲ τὸ ἡλίου φῶς, καὶ τὸ ἐν αὐτῷ τι δρᾷν μὴ ἀνεχόμενα, aves noctis amicas, et tenebris gaudentes, et in iis volare solitas libentius, et a solis lumine abhorrentes, neque in eo quidquam moliri sustinentes. Quod noctuae quidem et vespertilioni convenit, sed upupae, ardeae et charadrio non item. Tamen Cyrillum hac in parte sequitur Procopius Gazaeus in Commentariis in Leviticum. Sed in iis, quae ad naturam pertinent, non semper sacris doctoribus fidendum, quibus majoris momenti curae his minutulis serio vacare non permiserunt.

The reason for these prohibitions seems to have been wholly mystical. 1. God forbade the birds of prey, namely the eagle, the ossifrage, the kite, the vulture, the hawk, the raven, etc., in order to turn the Jews away from robbery. 2. The nocturnal birds, namely the eagle owl, the little owl and the bat, so that they might cast off the works of darkness and show themselves children of light. 3. The birds of lakes and riverbanks, such as the onocrotalus, the catarrhactes, the pelican and the stork, III, 123 whose food is most impure, and the hoopoe, which nests in dung, so as to keep them from all uncleanness. 4. Finally the ostrich, which does not rise from the ground, so that, leaving earthly things behind, they might strive toward the things that are above. This explanation is not ours but that of the ancient interpreters. Aristeas: Περὶ ὧν δὲ ἀπηγόρευται πτηνῶν, εὑρήσεις ἄγριά τε, καὶ σαρκοφάγα, καὶ καταδυναστεύοντα τῇ περὶ ταῦτα δυνάμει τὰ λοιπά· [Peri hōn de apēgoreutai ptēnōn, heurēseis agria te, kai sarkophaga, kai katadynasteuonta tē peri tauta dynamei ta loipa] As for the birds that are forbidden, you will find that they are wild and carnivorous, and by their strength exercise tyranny over the others. Barnabas at once transfers this to morals in the Epistle which our eminent Vossius edited: Οὐδὲ μὴ φάγῃς, φησὶ, τὸν ἀετὸν, οὐδὲ τὸν ὀξύπτερον, οὐδὲ τὸν ἰκτῖνα, οὐδὲ τὸν κόρακα· οὐ μὴ, φησὶ, κολληθήσῃ ἀνθρώποις τοιούτοις, οἵ τινες οὐκ οἴδασι διὰ κόπου καὶ ἱδρῶτος πορίζειν ἑαυτοῖς τὴν τροφὴν, ἀλλ' ἁρπάζουσι τὰ ἀλλότρια ἐν ἀνομίᾳ αὐτῶν· [Oude mē phagēs, phēsi, ton aeton, oude ton oxypteron, oude ton iktina, oude ton koraka; ou mē, phēsi, kollēthēsē anthrōpois toioutois, hoi tines ouk oidasi dia kopou kai hidrōtos porizein heautois tēn trophēn, all' harpazousi ta allotria en anomia autōn] Nor shall you eat, he says, the eagle, or the hawk, or the raven: that is, you shall not join yourself to those who do not know how to earn their living by toil and sweat, but seize what belongs to others through their iniquity. Clement of Alexandria, not content with having quoted this in book 5 of the Stromata, also imitates it thus in the Paedagogus, book 3, chapter 11: Οὐδ' ἰκτῖνα, ἢ ὠκύπτερον μαστοφαγῆ, ἢ col. 356 ἀετὸν φάγῃς, φησίν· οὐκ ἐγγιεῖς, φησὶν, τοῖς δι' ἁπαγῆς τὸν βίον πορίζομένοις· [Oud' iktina, ē ōkypteron mastophagē, ē aeton phagēs, phēsin; ouk engieis, phēsin, tois di' hapagēs ton bion porizomenois] You shall not eat the kite, or the hawk that devours breasts, or the eagle: that is, do not approach those who obtain their living by plunder. Origen, in Homily 7 on Leviticus, says: You shall not eat these, because they are unclean: the eagle, and the vulture, and the rest like them. For these birds always feed on dead bodies, and live off dead carcasses. All, therefore, who lead a life of this kind are to be held unclean. I think that those too are named in these who brood over the deaths of others, etc., as if gaping after the carcasses of the dead. In the same place he also asserts that those who in their words display heavenly learning, yet in their deeds perform carnal and dead works, are rightly called vultures and eagles, which swoop down from the heights upon dead and stinking flesh. And a little later: Some love not so much rapacity as obscurity and darkness. For everyone who does evil hates the light and does not come to the light, like little owls and bats, etc. Novatian, On Jewish Foods, chapter 3: Who (would make food of) the raven? But he execrates crafty dispositions. When he forbids the sparrow (he meant στρουθὸν [strouthon], that is, the ostrich), he rebukes intemperance. When the little owl, he hates those who flee the light of truth. When the swan, the proud with lofty neck. When the charadrius, the excessively chattering intemperance of the tongue. When the bat, those who seek the darkness of night and of error alike. In Cyril, book 9 Against Julian, eagles, hawks, catarrhactae, griffins and vultures signify the rapacious and greedy; the raven, the shifty; the hoopoe, little owl and bat, the ignorant and lovers of darkness, who hate the light of doctrine. And in book 14 On Adoration the same author has much more on the same subject. There too he counts not only the hoopoe but also the heron and the charadrius III, 124 among the nocturnal birds together with the little owl and the bat, asserting that γλαῦκα, καὶ ἐρωδιὸν, καὶ χαραδριὸν, καὶ τὰ ὅμοια αὐτοῦ, ἐπόπα, καὶ νυκτερίδα [glauka, kai erōdion, kai charadrion, kai ta homoia autou, epopa, kai nykterida], the little owl, and the heron, and the charadrius, and those like them, the hoopoe and the bat, are rejected as νυκτὸς φίλα, καὶ χαίροντα μὲν τῷ σκότει, καὶ πτήσει ὡς ἡδυτάτην ἐν αὐτῷ ποιεῖσθαι μεμελετηκότα, κατεχθραίνοντα δὲ τὸ ἡλίου φῶς, καὶ τὸ ἐν αὐτῷ τι δρᾷν μὴ ἀνεχόμενα [nyktos phila, kai chaironta men tō skotei, kai ptēsei hōs hēdytatēn en autō poieisthai memeletēkota, katechthrainonta de to hēliou phōs, kai to en autō ti dran mē anechomena], birds that are friends of the night, delight in darkness, and are accustomed to fly more gladly in it, and that shrink from the light of the sun and cannot bear to undertake anything in it. This indeed fits the little owl and the bat, but not the hoopoe, the heron and the charadrius. Yet Procopius of Gaza follows Cyril on this point in his Commentaries on Leviticus. But in matters pertaining to nature the sacred doctors are not always to be trusted, since cares of greater moment did not allow them to devote serious attention to these trifles. ¶

Placet inprimis Thomae acumen, qui, Hieronymi versionem et ordinem sequutus, ex harum avium singulis aliquid transfert ad mores. In aquila, inquit c), quae alte volitat, prohibetur superbia. In gryphe, qui equis et hominibus infestus est, crudelitas potentum prohibetur. In haliaeeto, qui pascitur minutis avibus, significantur illi, qui pauperibus sunt molesti. In milvo, qui maxime insidiis utitur, significantur fraudulenti. In vulture, qui sequitur exercitum, exspectans comedere cadavera mortuorum, significantur illi, qui mortes et seditiones hominum affectant, ut inde lucrentur. Lege locum, et videbis, eum reliqua pari cura persequi: ita tamen, ut quaedam de avibus afferat minus vera; 1. Puta porphyrionem habere unum pedem latum ad natandum, alterum fissum ad ambulandum: herodionem, (id est, ardeam) cum falcone eundem esse: caladrionem, (id est, charadrium) avem esse garrulam, etc. et quasdam ἀποδόσεις coactas habeat, et vi extortas; qui morbus fuit illorum temporum. Tamen in multis elucet viri illius ingenium, meliore saeculo digni.

Above all I like the acumen of Thomas, who, following the version and order of Jerome, transfers something from each of these birds to morals. "In the eagle," he says c), "which flies high, pride is forbidden. In the gryphon, which is hostile to horses and men, the cruelty of the powerful is forbidden. In the haliaeetus, which feeds on small birds, those are signified who harass the poor. In the kite, which makes the greatest use of ambushes, the deceitful are signified. In the vulture, which follows an army, waiting to eat the corpses of the dead, those are signified who strive after the deaths and seditions of men, so as to profit from them." Read the passage, and you will see that he pursues the rest with equal care. Yet he does so in such a way that he reports certain things about the birds that are not quite true: 1. for instance, that the porphyrio has one foot broad for swimming and the other split for walking; that the herodio (that is, the ardea [heron]) is the same as the falcon; that the caladrius (that is, the charadrius) is a garrulous bird, etc. He also has some ἀποδόσεις [apodoseis] that are forced and extorted by violence, which was the malady of those times. Nevertheless, in many places the talent of that man shines forth, worthy of a better age. ¶
